## Summary

Tricholoma imbricatum (Fr.) P. Kumm. 1871 is an agaric in the family Tricholomataceae, known in English as the matt knight. Sources describe a brown to reddish-brown, often scaly cap and a white spore print. Wikipedia places it on the ground in coniferous forests in Europe and North America. MushroomExpert.Com doubts that North American collections match the European species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the "true" Tricholoma imbricatum is a European species originally described from Scandinavia (Fries, 1815). It adds that the name is regularly applied to North American mushrooms, but that it is doubtful these correspond phylogenetically to the European species. It quotes a DNA survey (Heilmann-Clausen et al. 2017) finding that the included North American sequence is "quite deviant from the two European sequences, and might represent a distinct species." This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes North American collections (the illustrated ones are from Colorado) as mycorrhizal with pines (Pinus). It says they grow alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall, and also over winter in warm climates. It reports the species as widely recorded in North America but much more common from the Rocky Mountains westward. Wikipedia states that it grows on the ground in coniferous forests in North America and Europe. Mushroom World likewise describes coniferous forests, particularly under pine. No source supplied gives a season or host for European collections. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
