## Summary

Fomitopsis pinicola (Sw.) P. Karst. (1881) is a polypore in the family Fomitopsidaceae, known in English as the red-belted conk or red-belted bracket. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a common Eurasian polypore on the wood of conifers and hardwoods, with a shiny orange-red to red band on the cap surface and a thick whitish marginal zone. Recent DNA and morphology studies restrict the name to Eurasian material, with North American collections assigned to other species such as Fomitopsis mounceae and Fomitopsis schrenkii. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

The name is based on Boletus pinicola Sw., published in 1810. Wikispecies records that the original typification was not indicated and that a lectotype, plate 953 of Flora Danica, was designated by Ryvarden in 1991. MushroomExpert.Com states the species was originally described from Sweden. Wikispecies lists homotypic synonyms in Polyporus, Fomes, Trametes, Placodes, Pseudofomes and Ungulina. MushroomExpert.Com reports that studies by Haight and collaborators (2016, 2019) found F. pinicola to be strictly Eurasian, and Wikipedia says DNA analysis supports splitting it into F. pinicola, F. mounceae and F. schrenkii. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia (a single source) says the fruit body is useful as tinder. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of conifers and hardwoods, and also sometimes parasitic on living trees. It causes a brown cubical rot, grows alone or gregariously, is perennial, and is widespread in Europe and Asia. Wikipedia likewise describes it as a brown rot fungus, and reports that the decay affects the sapwood and then progresses into the heartwood of dead trees. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
