## Summary

Leucopaxillus gentianeus (Quél.) Kotl. is a member of the Tricholomataceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes young fresh caps as dark liver brown, with an odor strongly mealy and a bitter taste. Wikipedia describes the species as bitter-tasting and inedible. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described in 1873 as Clitocybe gentianea by the French mycologist Lucien Quélet, and that František Kotlaba transferred it to Leucopaxillus in 1966. MushroomExpert.Com notes that Singer & Smith (1943) documented 10 forms and 'subforms' of Leucopaxillus amarus, the former name for this species, primarily on the basis of taste, degree of pigmentation and size. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, decomposing the litter of conifers, growing scattered, gregariously, or in arcs or fairy rings. It fruits in summer and fall and, in warmer climates, winter. It is described as widely distributed and common in North America from the Rocky Mountains westward and southward into Mexico, and also known from Central America, Australia and Europe. The collections illustrated and described there are from California and Colorado. *[mushroomexpert]*
