## Summary

Clavulinopsis laeticolor (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) R.H. Petersen, known as the golden fairy club or handsome club, is a clavarioid fungus in the family Clavariaceae. Wikipedia describes it as forming slender, cylindrical, yellow fruit bodies that grow on the ground. It was described from Cuba and is treated as part of a species complex that is not yet resolved. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says only that the Latin name translates as 'joyfully colored', and it applies this to the typically orange or bright orangish-yellow fruit bodies. No other supplied source discusses the derivation of the name. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described from Cuba in 1868 by Miles Joseph Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis. Ron Petersen placed it in the genus Clavulinopsis in 1965. E. J. H. Corner treated the species under the name Clavulinopsis pulchra, a taxon originally described from the United States and regarded as part of the C. laeticolor complex. Wikipedia states that the species complex is as yet unresolved. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that the fungus grows singly or in small clusters on the ground, in woodland litter or in agriculturally unimproved grassland, and that it is presumed to be saprotrophic. It says the species grows in woodland in America and Asia, but in Europe generally occurs in agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland such as pastures and lawns. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as presumably saprobic, growing alone, scattered, gregariously or in loose groups under hardwoods or conifers. It says the species is usually terrestrial but occasionally appears on well-rotted, moss-covered stumps. The same source gives the season as summer and fall (also winter in warmer climates) and bases its description on collections from Illinois and Minnesota. iNaturalist, which is derived from Wikipedia, adds that it often fruits among mosses. GBIF's description record lists the substrate as soil and the habit as terrestrial. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc, inaturalist]*
