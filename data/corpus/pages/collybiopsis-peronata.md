## Summary

Collybiopsis peronata (Bolton) R.H. Petersen, in the family Omphalotaceae, is a species of gilled mushroom known as wood woolly-foot. Wikipedia describes it as common in European woods, with a cap about 2 to 6 cm across that is yellowish or reddish brown with a small central umbo. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name peronata indicates the stem is "booted" with a hairy covering below, from the Latin peronatus meaning "rough-booted". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, James Bolton originally described the species as Agaricus peronatus in his 1788 book "An history of fungusses, growing about Halifax". Samuel Frederick Gray allocated it to Gymnopus in 1821. Bulliard described the same species as Agaricus urens in 1791, and Fries placed it in Marasmius in 1836. Paul Kummer gave it the name Collybia peronata in 1871. For many years it was known as either Marasmius urens or Collybia peronata. Wikipedia says the peronatus and urens forms were once treated as separate species, with urens having a lighter-coloured cap, but calls that view outdated. It also says that work culminating in 1997 by Antonín and Noordeloos found Collybia as then defined to be polyphyletic, and that the species is now accepted in Collybiopsis. This account rests on a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as saprobic, growing generally in smaller or larger clumps on leaves or needles in deciduous or coniferous woods, and found from May to December. It calls the species common throughout Europe and says it has also been reported from a few sites in America and Japan. The GBIF descriptions record lists the distribution only as DK, NO and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
