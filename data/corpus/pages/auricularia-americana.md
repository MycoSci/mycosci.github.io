## Summary

Auricularia americana Parmasto & I. Parmasto ex Audet, Boulet & Sirard (2003) is a species in the family Auriculariaceae, with its type locality in Québec. Wikipedia describes its basidiocarps as gelatinous and ear-like, growing on dead conifer wood, and reports it from North America and East Asia. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was originally described in 1987 from Quebec on Abies balsamea but was not validly published until 2003. It says molecular research based on DNA sequences has shown A. americana to be a distinct species, and that it was formerly confused with Auricularia auricula-judae, which grows on broadleaf wood and is confined to Europe. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently reports that research (Looney and collaborators 2013; Wu and collaborators 2015) found the auricula-like North American Auricularia species are not properly placed under the names A. auricula or A. auricula-judae. It treats A. americana in the strict sense as the conifer-growing species that has no medullary zone under the microscope. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes A. americana as a wood-rotting species, typically found on dead attached or fallen wood of conifers. It says the species is widely distributed in North America (primarily in the Northeast, between April and September) and is also known from China and the Russian Far East. A GBIF description record lists Vermont as a distribution. This habitat account rests on a single independent source (Wikipedia) plus a one-word GBIF distribution record. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
