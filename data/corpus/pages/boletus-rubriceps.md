## Summary

Boletus rubriceps D. Arora & J.L. Frank (2014) is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, with its type locality in New Mexico. It is recorded from the United States, Mexico and Canada. Sources place it in the southwestern United States and the southern Rocky Mountains. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet refers to the reddish color of the caps, from Latin ruber ("red") and caput ("head" or "cap"). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was officially described as new to science in 2014, and that the bolete had previously been reported as either Boletus edulis or B. pinophilus. It says molecular analysis showed the species to be sufficiently different from other morphologically similar Boletus species to warrant designation as a distinct species. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) notes that the species appeared on that site from September 2010 to November 2016 as "Boletus cf. pinophilus". Kuo also writes that it seems virtually identical to a reddish-capped version of Boletus edulis he has collected under Scots pine in Finland, which European mycologists often call "Boletus pinophilus". *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it is found in the southwestern United States and southern Rocky Mountains, where it associates with spruce, pine, and sometimes fir. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as found under spruces at high elevations in the Rocky Mountains in late summer and early fall. It states the species is mycorrhizal with Engelmann spruce, and perhaps with other spruces and with firs. It grows alone, scattered, or gregariously, in summer and fall during the monsoon season. The collections Kuo illustrates and describes are from Colorado. He notes that Amanita muscaria is often fruiting nearby, as is Sarcodon imbricatus. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
