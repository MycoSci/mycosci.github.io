## Summary

Tremella aurantia Schwein. (1822), in the family Tremellaceae, was described from North Carolina; Index Fungorum lists Naematelia aurantia as its current name. Wikipedia describes it as producing yellow, frondose, gelatinous fruit bodies up to 15 cm across, and as a parasite of the fungus Stereum hirsutum. It says the species is commonly called golden ear in North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet aurantia means "golden". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first published in 1822 by Lewis David de Schweinitz, based on collections from North Carolina. It says Edward Angus Burt transferred it to Naematelia in 1921, but it remained better known as Tremella aurantia until molecular research based on cladistic analysis of DNA sequences showed Naematelia to be a distinct genus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes Naematelia aurantia as a parasite of Stereum hirsutum, growing on and often completely enveloping the host's fruit bodies. Following its host, fruit bodies are typically found on dead, attached or recently fallen branches of broadleaf trees. The same source gives a mainly north temperate distribution, known throughout North and South America, Europe and northern Asia. This is a single-source account from Wikipedia. No season is stated. *[wikipedia]*
