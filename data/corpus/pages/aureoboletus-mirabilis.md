## Summary

Aureoboletus mirabilis, the admirable bolete, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, placed in Aureoboletus by Halling in 2015. Sources describe a dark reddish-brown cap, yellow pores and a dark reddish-brown stem that is usually reticulate toward the apex. It is reported from hemlock forests of the Pacific Coast of North America, with disjunct records from Asia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet mirabilis as meaning "admirable" or "marvelous". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, William Alphonso Murrill first described the species in 1912 as Ceriomyces mirabilis, based on specimens found in Seattle, Washington. Later that year he moved it to Boletus. Rolf Singer transferred it to Xerocomus in 1940 and to Boletellus in 1945, and Egon Horak placed it in Heimioporus in 2004; Wikispecies lists the same synonyms and dates. Wikipedia reports that Harry Thiers first favoured Boletellus in 1966 and returned it to Boletus in 1975, after finding no roughened spores in California material. It was transferred to Aureoboletus in 2015 on the basis of DNA evidence. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes the species as mycorrhizal and associated with hemlocks, growing alone, scattered or gregariously on well-decayed, mossy hemlock stumps and logs, or on the ground near hemlock stumps. Wikipedia says it grows solitarily, scattered or in small groups on the ground or on well-decayed conifer logs, especially of western and mountain hemlock and occasionally Douglas-fir and western red cedar. Wikipedia states that a mycorrhizal association with hemlock is strongly suspected, but attempts to grow the mycorrhizae in laboratory culture have failed. Both sources give the range as northern California to Alaska; Wikipedia adds the Cascade Range and interior forests such as Manitoba, and says it has also been collected in Japan and Taiwan. The sources differ on season: MushroomExpert.Com gives fall and winter, while Wikipedia gives late summer to autumn. MushroomExpert.Com states that its illustrated and described collection is from Oregon. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
