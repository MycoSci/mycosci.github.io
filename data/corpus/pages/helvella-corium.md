## Summary

Helvella corium (O. Weberb.) Massee, 1895, is a species in the family Helvellaceae, order Pezizales. Wikipedia describes it as a black, cup-shaped fungus with a short stipe, and says it is uncommon despite being collected across Asia, Europe and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it grows on the ground, often near willows, in deciduous or mixed forests. It lists associations with Populus tremuloides, Thuja plicata, Salix species (such as Salix herbacea and Salix glauca), Shepherdia canadensis and Dryas shrubs, and cites Jordan for a preference for sandy soils or dunes. It has also been found on caustic spoil mounds of an alkali factory in Kraków, Poland, and on abandoned uranium tailings in Ontario, Canada. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as probably mycorrhizal, growing in woods, sandy soil or debris areas, often near willows or aspens, from May to August. It gives the range as widely distributed in boreal and montane North America north of roughly the 42nd parallel. Kuo writes that he has not seen it fresh and studied preserved collections from Alaska and northern Michigan. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
