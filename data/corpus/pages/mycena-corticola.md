## Summary

Mycena corticola (Pers.) Gray, 1821 is a species in the family Mycenaceae, with a basionym, Agaricus corticola. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a very small saprobe that grows on bark, with caps usually 3-5 mm across and up to 1 cm. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com cites the name as (Persoon, 1801) Gray, 1821. It notes that Mycena meliigena is probably a synonym and is the name some European authors prefer. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the bark of standing or fallen trees, usually with moss present. On fallen wood it is found early in decomposition, while the bark is still attached. It is frequently found on oaks but also on the bark of other hardwoods or conifers. It grows scattered or densely gregarious. The season is late summer and fall, sometimes spring. That source describes it as common in eastern North America and widely distributed from the Great Plains eastward. This is a single-source description. *[mushroomexpert]*
