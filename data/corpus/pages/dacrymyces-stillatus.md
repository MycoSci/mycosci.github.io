## Summary

Dacrymyces stillatus Nees (1816) is a jelly fungus in the family Dacrymycetaceae, known in English as Common Jelly Spot. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small, yellowish orange, gelatinous blob, usually under a centimeter across, though sometimes disc-like or with brain-like wrinkles. The same source notes that the species as currently defined is variable and might be better thought of as a species complex in need of critical study. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing gregariously on the wood of hardwoods (especially oaks) or conifers, on decorticated (bark-less) sticks or erumpent through still-attached bark. The same source gives the season as spring through fall, or over winter in warm climates. It reports the species as widely distributed in Europe, Asia and Oceania, and in North America from Alaska to Mexico, Florida and the Maritime Provinces. The collections it illustrates and describes are from Illinois. *[mushroomexpert]*
