## Summary

Hygrophorus purpurascens (Alb. & Schwein.) Fr. is an agaric in the family Hygrophoraceae, commonly called the purple waxy cap or veiled purple hygrophorus. Its cap is pink with streaks of purplish red, and mature gills have red spots. MushroomExpert.Com notes a cobwebby, cortina-like partial veil, best seen in the button stage, that separates it from related red-spotting waxy caps. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet purpurascens means "becoming purple". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was originally described as Agaricus purpurascens by Albertini and Schweinitz in 1805, and that Elias Fries transferred it to Hygrophorus in 1838. It also lists Kummer's 1871 Limacium purpurascens as a synonym. Neither source gives the circumstances of the original collection or the reasons for the transfer. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow on the ground in clusters or groups under conifer trees, and that it is a snowbank mushroom commonly found fruiting near the edges of snowbanks or shortly after snowmelt. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in late summer and fall (over winter in warmer climates). It describes the species as fairly widely distributed in northern and western North America and reported from the Appalachian Mountains, and as a conifer associate of montane and northern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
