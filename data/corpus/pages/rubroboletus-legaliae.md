## Summary

Rubroboletus legaliae (Pilát & Dermek) Della Magg. & Trassin. is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, placed in Rubroboletus in 2015. It is recorded in many European countries, and its English vernacular name in the catalog is 'bilious bolete'. Wikipedia describes it as related to Rubroboletus satanas and as uncommon in southern England and elsewhere in Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the name honours the French mycologist Marcelle Le Gal. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia credits the Czech mycologist Albert Pilát with describing Boletus legaliae in 1968. It says Boletus splendidus, as described by Charles-Édouard Martín in 1894, is a synonym. It also says the description of Boletus satanoides was too vague to be ascribed to any actual species. Wikipedia reports that the species was transferred to Rubroboletus in 2015 by Marco Della Maggiora and Renzo Trassinelli, and that a molecular study of the holotype of Rubroboletus spinari showed it to be conspecific with R. legaliae. All of this comes from one source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to Wikipedia, the species grows with oak (Quercus) and beech (Fagus), often on neutral to acid soils. For the UK it says the species commonly occurs in open woodland or parkland with plenty of sun on neutral-to-acidic soil. It describes the species as uncommon in southern England and Europe, and as uncommon to rare in the UK. Wikipedia also says it is considered vulnerable in the Czech Republic and is assessed as 'Vulnerable' by the JNCC in Britain. No fruiting season is stated in any supplied source. These habitat statements come from one source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
