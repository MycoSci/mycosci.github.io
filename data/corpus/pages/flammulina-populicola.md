## Summary

Flammulina populicola Redhead & R.H. Petersen (1999) is a species in the family Physalacriaceae, described from California. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a saprobe on the wood of quaking aspen and other poplars (Populus), with a sticky, orangish brown to yellowish brown cap, a velvety brownish stem and a white spore print. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com says it was originally described from California (Redhead & Petersen 1999). It treats the species as separate from Flammulina velutipes on four grounds: it did not mate with F. velutipes in culture, its spores are proportionally wider and ellipsoid, the terminal elements of its cap skin are club-shaped, and the two species have different DNA signatures. The same source adds that its association with western poplars also separates it ecologically. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the stumps, logs, roots and living wood of poplars (Populus, including quaking aspen and narrowleaf cottonwood). It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, or more commonly in clusters, and often looks terrestrial when it grows from root systems. It fruits in late summer and fall, and is described as common during monsoon season in the Rockies. That source gives its North American distribution as the Rocky Mountains westward, says it is found occasionally in north-temperate Europe (probably as an import with ornamental trees), and notes it is also documented from China. The described and illustrated collections are from Colorado. Wikipedia independently gives wood of quaking aspen and other poplars as the substrate. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
