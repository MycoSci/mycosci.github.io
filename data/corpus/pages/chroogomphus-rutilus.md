## Summary

Chroogomphus rutilus (Schaeff.) O.K. Mill. is a species of fungus in the family Gomphidiaceae, first described as Agaricus rutilus by Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1774 and transferred to Chroogomphus in 1964. Wikipedia describes it as ectomycorrhizal with Pinus species. Sources differ on whether it occurs in North America; see habitat. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described scientifically as Agaricus rutilus by Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1774. It says Orson K. Miller, Jr. transferred it to the genus Chroogomphus in 1964, and that Gomphidius viscidus is an old synonym. A single other source, Mushroom World, adds a note that C. rutilus as traditionally known in North America is now recognised as a different species. That note says molecular studies show the true C. rutilus is confined to Europe, and that North American collections previously assigned to the name are genetically identical with Chroogomphus ochraceus. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fungus lives ectomycorrhizally with Pinus species. Mushroom World describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially pines, and typically found in woodland and forest clearings (single-source description). On range, Wikipedia says it is found in Europe and North America. Mushroom World lists the location as Europe with North America in parentheses, and notes that North American collections traditionally assigned to this name are now regarded as a different species (see discovery_and_naming).
