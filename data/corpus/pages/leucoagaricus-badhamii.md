## Summary

Leucoagaricus badhamii (Berk. & Broome) Singer, 1951, is a species in the family Agaricaceae. The spine records its current name in Index Fungorum as Leucocoprinus badhamii. Wikipedia describes its flesh as staining blood red when cut or bruised, which gives it the common name blushing dapperling. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet badhamii honours the British writer, physician, entomologist and mycologist Charles David Badham, author of the 1847 Treatise on the Esculent Funguses of England. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the common name blushing dapperling comes from the flesh turning blood red when cut or bruised. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Miles Joseph Berkeley and Christopher Edmund Broome first described it in 1854 as Agaricus badhamii. Marcel Locquin reclassified it as Leucocoprinus badhamii in 1943, and Rolf Singer moved it to Leucoagaricus in 1951. Wikipedia says it was often still treated as a Leucocoprinus until 2024, when the genera began to be combined and the name reverted officially to Leucocoprinus badhamii. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only source with habitat text, describes it as a saprotroph on humus-rich ground. It grows in small groups in deciduous and mixed woodland and may be associated with beech. It may also grow on woodchips, which lets it appear outside forest habitats. The same source says it has been found in America, Britain, Europe and Africa but appears to be uncommon. *[wikipedia]*
