## Summary

Morchella elata Fr. (1822) is a species of Morchellaceae, known in English as the black morel. Until 2012 the name was applied broadly to black morels worldwide. Later DNA work restricted it to Europe, and its exact identity is still unsettled. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

The name Morchella elata was proposed by Elias Magnus Fries of Sweden in 1822. DNA analysis in 2011 showed North American black morels to be largely distinct from European species, which restricted the name M. elata to Europe. In 2012 Kuo et al. named many North American black morels that may earlier have been called M. elata. A later study (Richard et al. 2014) suggested that Fries' original description may correspond to the lineage Mel-10, since described from North America as Morchella importuna and later shown to be widespread across continents. The variety M. elata var. purpurescens is now considered to belong to a phylogenetically distinct lineage. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that M. elata fruits in spring on soil. Fries' original description was based on a collection from a fir wood in Sweden and reported the species as rare. Because the true identity of M. elata is not yet fully clarified, its exact ecological preferences and distribution remain unclear. *[wikipedia]*
