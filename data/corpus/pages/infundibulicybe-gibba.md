## Summary

Infundibulicybe gibba (Pers.) Harmaja, 2003, is a gilled mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae, known in English as the common funnel or funnel clitocybe. Wikipedia describes it as common in European woods, and MushroomExpert.Com describes a pale pinkish tan cap that becomes deeply vase-shaped, with crowded gills running down the stem and a white spore print. It is also recorded in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet gibba comes from the Latin adjective "gibbus", meaning "humped" or "gibbous". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Christiaan Hendrik Persoon described the species in 1801 as Agaricus gibbus, when gilled mushrooms were generally all assigned to Agaricus. Paul Kummer placed it in Clitocybe in 1871. In 2003 Harri Harmaja created the genus Infundibulicybe for some of the larger members of the former Clitocybe and designated I. gibba as its type species. MushroomExpert.Com adds that the species was originally described from Denmark. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a gregarious saprobic mushroom that grows on soil in deciduous or, less commonly, coniferous woods, from summer to autumn, and that sometimes forms fairy rings. MushroomExpert.Com likewise describes it as saprobic, growing scattered or gregariously, primarily under hardwoods (especially oaks) but sometimes reported under conifers. It fruits in summer and fall, or over winter and spring in warm climates. Wikipedia calls it very common throughout Europe and says it occurs in North America and Japan. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as widely distributed in boreal and temperate Europe, widespread in North America from Alaska to Newfoundland and Labrador and south into Mexico, and sometimes reported from Asia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
