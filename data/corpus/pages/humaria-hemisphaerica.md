## Summary

Humaria hemisphaerica (F.H. Wigg.) Fuckel, 1870, is a cup fungus in the family Pyronemataceae, recorded in English as Brown-haired White Cup and Glazed Cup. Its cups have a white to whitish or pale bluish inner surface and a brown outer surface covered in stiff brown hairs. Sources describe it as occurring in Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin word hemisphaericum, meaning half a sphere. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described in 1780 by the German mycologist Friedrich Heinrich Wiggers as Peziza hemisphaerica. Elias Magnus Fries placed it within the Lachnea series, a name he applied to cup fungi with hairy apothecia. In 1870 Leopold Fuckel transferred it to the genus Humaria. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fungus grows year-round on North America's west coast and is common in the Pacific Northwest, and that further east it fruits from July to October. It usually grows alone, but also appears scattered or grouped, on the ground and sometimes on decaying wood. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as widely distributed in North America, growing on soil and less frequently on well-rotted wood. It describes the fungus as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, sometimes growing on their well-decayed deadwood, alone or gregariously, in summer and fall. GBIF lists distribution records for Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
