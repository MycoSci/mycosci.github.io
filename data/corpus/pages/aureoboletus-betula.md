## Summary

Aureoboletus betula (Schwein.) M. Kuo & B. Ortiz (2020) is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the shaggy-stalked bolete. Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com report it under oaks or in mixed pine-oak woods, primarily in the southern Appalachians. MushroomExpert.Com describes a sticky reddish-to-yellow cap, a non-bruising yellow pore surface that becomes slightly greenish with age, and a deeply, coarsely reticulate stem. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet 'betula' means birch. It says the epithet does not imply a preference for growing under birch trees. It refers instead to birch's shaggy bark, which the stipe of this species is reminiscent of. This is a single-source statement, and no independent source discusses the derivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Lewis David de Schweinitz first described the species in 1822 as Boletus betula. Wikipedia says it was reclassified many times, including as Heimioporus betula by Egon Horak in 2004. It also says Michael Kuo and Beatriz Ortiz-Santana moved it to Aureoboletus in 2020. MushroomExpert.Com lists Austroboletus betula, Boletellus betula, Boletus betula and Heimioporus betula as previous names. It adds that recent DNA results (Kuo & Ortiz-Santana 2020) support placement in Aureoboletus. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks (and perhaps other hardwoods and/or pines), especially in mixed pine-oak woods. It grows alone or scattered in summer and fall. It is described as fairly widely distributed from Michigan and Pennsylvania to the southeastern United States and Mexico, but more common in the southern Appalachian Mountains. The collections MushroomExpert.Com illustrates and describes are from Ohio. Wikipedia gives a narrower account: under oaks, or in mixed woods of pine and oak, primarily in the southern Appalachians, from July to September. The two accounts are worded very similarly, so treat them as one line of evidence, not two confirmations. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
