## Summary

Rhodofomes cajanderi (P. Karst.) B.K. Cui, M.L. Han & Y.C. Dai (2016) is a bracket fungus in the family Fomitopsidaceae, commonly called the rosy conk. Wikipedia describes it as a perennial shelf fungus with a rose-pink pore surface. MushroomExpert.Com describes a tough polypore with a pink pore surface and caps that are often fused together. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the name "rosy conk" is used because of the species' rose-colored pore surface. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com credits the species to P. Karsten (1904), with the combination in Rhodofomes by B. K. Cui, M. L. Han & Y. C. Dai (2016). It says the species was originally described from Finland. It lists Fomitopsis cajanderi and Fomitopsis subrosea as synonyms. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the fungus as saprobic on the deadwood of conifers (and, rarely, hardwoods) and sometimes parasitic on living trees, causing a brown cubical rot. It says the fungus grows alone or gregariously and is perennial. It gives the distribution as northern Europe, Russia, China and Japan, and as widespread in North America and the Caribbean. The collections it illustrates and describes are from California and Georgia. Wikipedia separately says the fungus causes a brown pocket rot in various conifer species, is widespread in western North America with more prevalence in southern climates, and has a particular preference for higher-altitude spruce forests. These are single-source statements from two different sources, and they are not identical in wording or scope. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
