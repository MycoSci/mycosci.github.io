## Summary

Lactarius controversus Pers. (family Russulaceae) is a large, funnel-capped milk cap that exudes white milky latex when damaged. Sources describe it as distinguished mainly by rosy markings on the cap, often in concentric rings, and pinkish-buff gills. It is recorded in Europe and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that in Europe, including Britain, it usually grows with Salix on heaths and moors and is uncommon, and that in North America it grows with aspen, poplar and willow (noted in the Sierra Nevada and New Mexico). MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with Populus (especially quaking aspen and big-toothed aspen) and Salix, growing alone, gregariously or sometimes in dense troops, in summer and fall, across northern and montane North America. A GBIF-hosted description lists specimens from Idaho, Montana and Wyoming collected under Populus tremuloides, with dated collections from July to September. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
