## Summary

Clitopilus geminus (Paulet) Noordel. & Co-David is a species in the family Entolomataceae (order Agaricales), and Index Fungorum currently lists it as Rhodocybe gemina. Wikipedia gives it the recommended English name tan pinkgill and describes fleshy, agaricoid fruit bodies that are cream when young and brownish when mature. The Clitopilus placement dates from a 2009 molecular study, and Wikipedia states that later research placed the species within Rhodocybe instead. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally named Hypophyllum geminum by the French mycologist Jean-Jacques Paulet in 1793 and was transferred to Rhodocybe in 1987. Before that it was commonly called Rhodocybe truncata, based on Agaricus truncatus of Jacob Christian Schäffer (1774). Wikipedia says no original specimen of Schäffer's species exists and that its description is so ambiguous that some authorities referred it to Hebeloma. Rhodocybe truncata is now considered a nomen dubium and is no longer used. In 2009, on the basis of DNA-sequence analysis, Co-David et al. included the species in an expanded concept of Clitopilus. Wikipedia states that subsequent research showed Rhodocybe to be a monophyletic genus to which the species belongs. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a saprotrophic species that generally grows in woodlands, both broadleaved and occasionally coniferous, and also in grassland and scrub. This is a single-source statement, and no host tree, substrate or season is given. *[wikipedia]*
