## Summary

Tylopilus badiceps (Peck) A.H. Sm. & Thiers is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, recorded from North America. It was described in 1900 as Boletus badiceps by Charles Horton Peck and transferred to Tylopilus in 1971 by Smith and Thiers. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was described in 1900 as Boletus badiceps by Charles Horton Peck and transferred to Tylopilus in 1971 by Alexander H. Smith and Harry Delbert Thiers. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) notes that Peck considered the "truncate or beveled margin of the pileus" an important feature, but Kuo finds the folded cap edge present only about half the time, and says Tylopilus ferrugineus can show it too. Kuo also says Smith and Thiers (1971) regarded the species as "one of the most easily recognized species in the genus" because of its cap-surface structure, but he finds that structure more variable than they did. Kuo describes the name as applying, under current (and possibly outdated) concepts, to eastern North American collections with the features he lists. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall, and widely distributed in eastern North America; the collections he illustrates and describes are from Illinois. He describes it as a woodland species, in contrast to Tylopilus ferrugineus, which he says is almost always found in grassy areas under oaks in lawns or parks. GBIF lists a distribution record for Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
