## Summary

Naematelia encephala (Pers.) Fr., 1818, is a species in the family Naemateliaceae, known in the UK as conifer brain. According to Wikipedia, it produces pink, brain-like, gelatinous fruit bodies and is parasitic on the fungus Stereum sanguinolentum. It is recorded from north temperate regions. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet encephala means "brain", with reference to the shape and colour of the basidiocarps. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Per Wikipedia, Tremella encephala was first published in 1801 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon, based on an earlier description by Carl Ludwig Willdenow, who had described the species from Germany as Tremella encephaliformis. In 1818 Elias Magnus Fries selected it as the type species of Naematelia, a genus he proposed for fungi with gelatinous basidiocarps that have a hard or compact core. Robert Bandoni showed in 1961 that this central core is the remains of the host fungus, Stereum sanguinolentum. This account comes from a single independent source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes Naematelia encephala as a parasite of Stereum sanguinolentum, growing on and often completely enveloping the host's basidiocarps. Following its host, the fruit bodies are typically found on dead, attached or recently fallen branches of conifers. The same source gives a north temperate distribution, known throughout North America and Northern Eurasia, and also recorded from Australia. The GBIF description record lists distribution entries for Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
