## Summary

Agaricus abruptibulbus Peck (1905) is a species in the family Agaricaceae. Sources describe it as a whitish, almond-scented Agaricus with a bulbous stem base, and a cap that turns yellow when bruised or with age. The name has been applied inconsistently, and some authors treat it as a synonym of Agaricus silvicola. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Charles Horton Peck first named the species Agaricus abruptus in 1900, then renamed it A. abruptibulbus. His stated reason was that Fries had earlier named a species Agaricus abruptus in the subgenus Flammula, which was later raised to genus rank as Flammula abruptus. It was unclear whether the name Agaricus abruptus would stay available. MushroomExpert.Com, following Kerrigan (2016), says Peck's concept and type collection conflated three separate species, and that the name has since been applied inconsistently. According to Wikipedia, some American authors consider the species synonymous with A. silvicola, and some European authors have synonymized it with A. essettei. Wikipedia quotes Trudell and Ammirati (2009): "The name A. abruptibulbus has been applied to forms with bulbous stipe bases, but variation in stipe shape is so great that the use of this name has been largely abandoned." MushroomExpert.Com, following Kerrigan (2016), says DNA sequencing and micromorphology have clarified the picture somewhat, and it treats the species as distinct. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes the species as saprobic, growing gregariously in hardwood forests or in urban locations under ornamental Norway spruce, and fruiting in fall. It cites Kerrigan (2016) as documenting it from Pennsylvania, New York and Washington; the collections Kuo illustrates and describes are from Illinois. Wikipedia lists reports from New York, Mississippi, Quebec, Canada, Germany, China and India. GBIF's description record gives a distribution of Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
