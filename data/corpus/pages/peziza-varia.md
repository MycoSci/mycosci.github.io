## Summary

Peziza varia (Hedw.) Alb. & Schwein., 1805, is a cup fungus in the family Pezizaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a brown, bald upper surface that is often wrinkled near the centre and a whitish, minutely fuzzy under surface. It says the species is widely distributed in Europe, Oceania and North America. Wikipedia states it is nonpoisonous but inedible. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com says the species was originally described from Sweden (Hedwig 1789) and lectotypified from Denmark, Sweden and England (Hansen et al. 2002). It also says DNA-informed studies indicate several potential lineages, so Peziza varia may include several phylogenetic species. The same source says the name Peziza repanda has often been applied to large specimens on rotting logs, that repanda is taxonomically troubled, and that Peziza cerea and Peziza micropus may represent the same mushroom. Wikipedia lists P. repanda, P. cerea and P. micropus as synonyms. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (single source) reports growth alone or gregariously on well-decayed hardwood and, less often, conifer logs. It also reports terrestrial growth in woody debris near stumps, and occasional occurrence on dung or manure, in woodchips, or in damp basements. It says the species is possibly mycorrhizal despite appearing on deadwood, and is typically found in colder weather (spring and fall in temperate regions), sometimes in summer. The collections it illustrates and describes are from California, Colorado, Illinois, Michigan and Missouri. iNaturalist (a declared derivative of Wikipedia) calls it a common European fungus associated with rotting wood. *[mushroomexpert, inaturalist]*
