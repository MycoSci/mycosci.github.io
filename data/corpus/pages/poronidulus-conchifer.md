## Summary

Poronidulus conchifer (Schwein.) Murrill, 1904, is a polypore in the family Polyporaceae and the only species in the monotypic genus Poronidulus. The fruit body starts as a small cup-like structure, and a bracket- or kidney-shaped cap later develops as an extension of the cup. The cup is described as a splash-cup for dispersing asexual spores. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia says the generic name Poronidulus combines the Ancient Greek word πόρος ("pore") with the Latin word nidulus ("small nest"). It refers to the superficial similarity of the cup-shaped fruit bodies to those of the genus Nidularia. No source discusses the derivation of the species epithet. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the genus Poronidulus was circumscribed by the American mycologist William Alphonso Murrill in 1904. MushroomExpert.Com says the species is often called Trametes conchifer in field guides, and lists Trametes conchifer and Polyporus conchifer as synonyms. It cites Schweinitz, 1822 and Murrill, 1904 as the naming references. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods (primarily elms), annual, and causing a white rot. It grows in clusters on branches, logs and stumps from spring through fall, and is described as widely distributed from the Great Plains eastward. Wikipedia describes it as found in North America. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
