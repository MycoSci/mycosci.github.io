## Summary

Mycorrhaphium adustum (Schwein.) Maas Geest., 1962, is a toothed polypore in the family Steccherinaceae (order Polyporales), with a basionym published by Schweinitz. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small toothed mushroom with a pale cap, a margin that blackens, and teeth on the underside, found on hardwood sticks and small logs in eastern North America. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that the epithet adustum means "charred" or "burned", and links it to the blackening cap margin and the gray to black bruising of the cap and underside. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com cites the original description to Schweinitz (1822) and says it was described from North Carolina. The current combination is credited to Maas Geesteranus (1962). The same source lists Steccherinum adustum as a synonym and says Bjerkandera adusta is a different mushroom, with minute pores rather than teeth, and not a synonym. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to MushroomExpert.Com (a single source): saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods, growing alone or in clusters on sticks or small logs rather than larger logs or stumps, in summer and fall. It is described as widely distributed in North America east of the Great Plains and reliably documented from the Caribbean, Central America, Brazil, and Russia. The same source says records from eastern Asia probably represent Mycorrhaphium subadustum. The illustrated and described collections are from Illinois. GBIF's description record lists only a distribution entry ('NO; Vermont'). *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
