## Summary

Peniophora incarnata (Pers.) P. Karst. (1889) is a species of the family Peniophoraceae, order Russulales, known in English as the rosy crust. It is a resupinate, crust-like species that grows on the surface of bark. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as membranous and up to 200 μm thick, with an orange or red to reddish-brown, smooth hymenial surface. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that in Scandinavia it grows on a range of deciduous hosts and less often on coniferous trees, and that it is sometimes parasitised by the yellow brain fungus (Tremella mesenterica). A GBIF specimen record describes a collection from China (Ailaoshan National Nature Reserve, Yunnan) on a fallen trunk of an angiosperm in a broad-leaved forest, noted as white rot, collected 16 January 2018. These are the only habitat statements in the supplied sources; the Wikipedia statement is a single-source claim, and the GBIF entry is one specimen record. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
