## Summary

Tulosesus impatiens (Fr.) D. Wächter & A. Melzer (2020) is a species in the family Psathyrellaceae, first described by Fries in 1821 as Agaricus impatiens. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as having buff caps up to 4 cm across on slender whitish stems up to 10 cm tall, and reports the species from Europe and North America. GBIF's description record lists only a distribution of Denmark and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1821 as Agaricus impatiens by Elias Magnus Fries in Systema Mycologicum. Lucien Quélet transferred it to Coprinarius in 1886 and to Coprinus a couple of years later. In 1936 Robert Kühner placed it in Pseudocoprinus, a genus he segregated from Coprinus for species without deliquescent gills; he later changed his mind about separating the two genera. Also in 1936, Gillet transferred it to Psathyrella. Jakob Emanuel Lange published Coprinellus impatiens in 1938. The species was popularly known as Coprinus impatiens until a large-scale phylogenetic analysis in 2001 split Coprinus into smaller genera and confirmed the placement in Coprinellus. In 2020 Dieter Wächter and Andreas Melzer reclassified many Psathyrellaceae species on the basis of a phylogenetic analysis, and the species became Tulosesus impatiens. All of this comes from a single source, Wikipedia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

It states that fruit bodies grow solitarily, or rarely in small bundles, on forest litter in deciduous forests, especially those dominated by beech. This is a single-source description (Wikipedia).
