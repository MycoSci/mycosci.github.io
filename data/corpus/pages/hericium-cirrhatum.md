## Summary

Hericium cirrhatum (Pers.) Nikol. (1950) is a member of the Hericiaceae, known in English as the Tiered Tooth or Tiered tooth fungus. Wikipedia describes it as a saprotrophic, stemless, bracket-like fungus whose spines usually hang in tiers like icicles. Mushroom World describes cream-coloured bracket-like caps with a warty, short-spined upper surface and more pendulous spines beneath. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the generic name Hericium refers to the fertile spines found in the group and means 'pertaining to a hedgehog', and that the spines also gave rise to the species name cirrhatum, which it translates as 'having tendrils'. This derivation comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows on dead standing hardwood trees, fallen wood or tree stumps, such as beech (Fagus sylvatica), in old established deciduous woodlands. It also lists records on sycamore (Acer pseudoplatanus), birch (Betula spp.), ash (Fraxinus spp.), oak (Quercus robur) and elm (Ulmus spp.), and says it is found from July to November in Britain. Mushroom World independently describes it as growing solitarily on the dead wood of a variety of broad-leaf trees, favouring birch, and gives its location as Europe. Wikipedia describes it as nowhere common, present in southern England (particularly the New Forest) and in parts of central and southern mainland Europe, and reports that it was removed from the Red Data List in 2006. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
