## Summary

Mycena cinerella (P. Karst.) P. Karst. 1879, the mealy bonnet, is a small species in the family Mycenaceae, recorded in GBIF occurrence data from many European countries and from North America. Wikipedia describes it as a small grayish saprobic mushroom that grows in groups on needles under pine and Douglas fir, and calls it inedible. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet cinerella means "somewhat ashy color". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the mushroom is commonly known as the "mealy bonnet" and that, as the common name suggests, it smells mealy. It describes the odor and taste of crushed or chewed flesh as strongly farinaceous (mealy, similar to raw potatoes). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Petter Karsten first called it Agaricus cinerellus in 1879 and transferred it to Mycena the same year. In 1936 Jakob Emanuel Lange referred it to the genus Omphalia. Hintikka described Mycena cineroides as a distinct species in 1963, citing a narrowly acute cap lacking brownish or yellowish tones, decurrent gills and two-spored basidia. Intermediate forms have since been found, and some authorities, such as Maas Geesteranus, believe M. cineroides should be treated as a synonym of M. cinerella. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes M. cinerella as a saprobic fungus that decomposes leaf litter and similar detritus. Its fruit bodies grow in groups on needles under pine and Douglas fir, typically in late summer and autumn. Wikipedia lists US collections from Michigan, Washington, Oregon and California, and European collections from Great Britain, Norway, Poland and Sweden. The GBIF description record lists distribution in Belgium (Flemish, Walloon and Brussels-Capital regions), Denmark, Norway and Sweden.
