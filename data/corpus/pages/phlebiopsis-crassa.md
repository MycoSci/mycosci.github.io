## Summary

Phlebiopsis crassa (Lév.) Floudas & Hibbett (2015) is a species in the family Phanerochaetaceae (order Polyporales). MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a crust fungus that is purple when fresh and young, spreading across sticks and logs in North American hardwood forests. A 2020 phylogenetic study reports that P. crassa is a morphological species complex containing three distinct lineages. *[mushroomexpert, lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic and possibly parasitic on recently fallen hardwood with the bark still attached, causing a white rot of the heartwood. It grows in solitary or gregarious patches in fall. Kuo calls it apparently widely distributed in North America but absent or rare on the West Coast, and his described collections are from Illinois. Separately, GBIF-held specimen records list collections from Yunnan Province, China, from trunks and a fallen branch of angiosperms, including one from the trunk of Camellia japonica, and one record is noted as white rot in a broad-leaved forest. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
