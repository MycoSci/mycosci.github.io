## Summary

Phlebiopsis gigantea (Fr.) Jülich 1978 is a member of the Phanerochaetaceae (Polyporales), recorded in GBIF from many countries, mainly in Europe and North America. The supplied literature describes it as a white-rot fungus that colonizes freshly exposed conifer sapwood and rapidly colonizes cut pine stumps, stems and branches. It is mainly discussed in the supplied sources as a forestry biological-control agent and as a subject of wood-decay genomics. *[lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Cultural and culinary history

The supplied sources record forestry and industrial uses, not culinary ones. A 2000 paper reports that P. gigantea had been used in Europe for nearly 40 years to control root and butt rot of conifers caused by Heterobasidion annosum. It is applied to stump surfaces at felling and competes with H. annosum for the woody resource. Three products based on it are described: PG Suspension (UK), PG IBL (Poland) and Rotstop (Finland). In each case the isolates were selected from the wild and screened for competitive ability before formulation. A separate 1997 study inoculated red pine (Pinus resinosa) logs with the fungus. It reported reduced pitch content, easier bark removal and inhibition of blue-stain fungi, and concluded that this could benefit pulp and paper production. *[lit_2, lit_3]*

## Habitat and ecology

Sources describe it as a white-rot fungus that colonizes freshly exposed conifer sapwood (PLoS Genetics 2014, tested on loblolly pine wood) and rapidly colonizes cut stumps, stems and branches of pine (Applied and Environmental Microbiology 1997). It is reported on both pine and Norway spruce stumps in European forestry use. A GBIF description record separately lists specimens examined from Yunnan Province, China, described as white rot on trunks of angiosperm in a broad-leaved forest. No source supplied here gives a fruiting season. *[lit_1, lit_2, lit_3, gbif_desc]*
