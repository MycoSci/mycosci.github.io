## Summary

Gloeophyllum sepiarium (Wulfen) P. Karst. 1882, the rusty gilled polypore (also listed as Conifer Mazegill), is a member of the Gloeophyllaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a wood decay fungus that causes a brown rot. The catalog records it from many countries, including Sweden, the United States, Norway, Finland and Denmark. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows on dead conifers, both in the wild and on lumber, and that it is found throughout North America. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of conifers and occasionally hardwoods, causing a brown rot. It grows alone or gregariously, appears in woods but is not infrequently found on lumber in urban settings, and fruits in summer and fall (and over winter in warm climates). Kuo gives it as widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia says the fruiting body grows for only one year and produces spores in late summer and autumn. Kuo describes it as annual or reviving to be perennial. A GBIF specimen record from Yunnan Province, China, notes an angiosperm dead tree as the substrate. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
