## Summary

Panaeolus papilionaceus (Bull.) Quél. 1872 is a small brown mushroom in the family Bolbitiaceae, known in English as the Petticoat Mottlegill or bell-capped mottlegill. Wikipedia describes it as very common and widely distributed, growing on dung, and as the type species of the genus Panaeolus. Identifying features given by MushroomExpert.Com include a black spore print, mottled gray and black gills, white toothlike partial veil fragments hanging from the cap margin, and no ring on the stem. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports it occurring singly, gregariously, or caespitosely on cow or horse dung, on moose droppings, and in pastures. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone to gregariously in the dung of horses and cows, and common and widely distributed in North America in spring, summer, and fall (and over winter in warmer climates). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
