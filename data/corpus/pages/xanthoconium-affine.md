## Summary

Xanthoconium affine (Peck) Singer is a bolete in the family Boletaceae. Charles Horton Peck first described it as Boletus affinis in 1873, and Rolf Singer transferred it to Xanthoconium in 1944. MushroomExpert.Com describes a brown to yellow-brown capped bolete with whitish pores that age to yellowish brown and a bright yellow-brown spore print. It is recorded from hardwood forests of eastern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Charles Horton Peck first described the species as a Boletus in 1873 and that Rolf Singer placed it in Xanthoconium in 1944. MushroomExpert.Com notes that it was formerly known as Boletus affinis and that two varieties have been described. Var. maculosus has a cap spotted with whitish or pale yellow spots, and var. reticulatum has a conspicuously reticulate stem. No source supplied says from what material it was described or why it was transferred. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, especially oaks and beech, and perhaps rarely with conifers. It grows alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall, and is widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains, south to Mexico. The collections that site illustrates and describes are from Michigan, Illinois, and Kentucky. Wikipedia separately states that it is found under oak and beech in eastern North America from June to September. GBIF's description record lists a distribution of Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
