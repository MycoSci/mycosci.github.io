## Summary

Entoloma strictius (Peck) Sacc. (1887) is a member of the family Entolomataceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a small to medium-sized, brownish, bell-shaped cap that changes color as it dries, a proportionally long stem that is often slightly twisted, and gills that are white before turning pink. The same source notes that several entolomatoid species fit this description, so identification is uncertain. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com cites the name as Peck (1870) with Saccardo's 1887 treatment, and says Peck's original type collection was made on the "[g]round in groves and their borders" in Albany, New York. Kuo lists Nolanea stricta, Entoloma strictior and Rhodopyllus strictior as synonyms. Hesler (1967) recorded two varieties, var. isabellinus (buff to yellowish colors) and var. cystidiosum (prominent cheilocystidia), but Noordeloos (1988) doubts that the latter is closely related to E. strictius. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously under oaks, beech, and other hardwoods in summer and fall. Kuo considers it probably widely distributed in eastern North America; his own collections are from Illinois, Tennessee, and Kentucky. The GBIF descriptions record distribution entries for Biobío Region, Los Ríos Region, and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
