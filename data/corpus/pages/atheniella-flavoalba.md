## Summary

Atheniella flavoalba, known in the UK as the Ivory Bonnet, is a small agaric with a conical to convex, ivory-white to yellowish-white cap up to about 1.5 cm across and a thin, tubular stem up to 8 cm long. It was described by Fries as Agaricus flavoalbus and is currently placed in Atheniella (Mycenaceae) by Redhead et al. (2012). The descriptive details come from a single independent source cluster (Wikipedia and its derivative). *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the epithet flavoalba as "yellow-white", a compound of the Latin adjectives flavus ("yellow") and alba ("white"). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described as Agaricus flavoalbus by Elias Magnus Fries in 1838 and assigned to Mycena by Lucien Quélet in 1872, although its inamyloid basidiospores and non-dextrinoid hyphae are anomalous for that genus. Rolf Singer moved it to Hemimycena (1938) and Marasmiellus (1951), and later considered it a Mycena again in his 1986 work. The 2016 European monograph is quoted as calling it "not a proper Mycena" while retaining it there pending further research. Wikipedia says recent DNA-based analysis showed it is not closely related to Mycena and belongs in Atheniella. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow scattered to densely gregarious in mossy pastures, in needle beds under conifers, or on humus in oak woods, during the autumn. It describes the species as common in old grassland in Europe and less common in North America, though sometimes occurring in large quantities in certain localities. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*
