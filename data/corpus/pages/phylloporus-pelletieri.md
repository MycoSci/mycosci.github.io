## Summary

Phylloporus pelletieri (Lév.) Quél. is a species of Boletaceae, commonly known as the golden-gilled bolete. Wikipedia describes it as having gill-like lamellae on the underside of the cap instead of the pores usual in the Boletales. A GBIF description record lists its distribution as global, with entries for Flanders, Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species forms mycorrhizal relationships with broadleaved trees such as beech and with coniferous trees such as fir or pine, and that fruit bodies are produced in summer and autumn. It describes the species as rare but widely distributed in Europe, reaching into Asia. It inhabits broadleaf or coniferous forests in montane or sub-alpine regions, where it is associated with acidic or sandy soils.
