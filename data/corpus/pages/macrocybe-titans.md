## Summary

Macrocybe titans (H.E. Bigelow & Kimbr.) Pegler, Lodge & Nakasone is a species in the family Callistosporiaceae, known in English as the American titan. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as probably the largest gilled mushroom in North America, with a cap that can measure over a yard across, and says it occurs in the southeastern United States and Mexico. The same source, Wikipedia and a GBIF description give it a wider range in Central America, the Caribbean and South America. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com says the genus name Macrocybe is Latin for "huge head". No supplied source discusses the derivation of the species epithet. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the species was described as Tricholoma titans in 1980 by Howard E. Bigelow and J. W. Kimbrough and reclassified in Macrocybe in 1998. MushroomExpert.Com adds that the original description was based on material from grassy areas in northern Florida. That source also reports that Pegler and collaborators (1998) found the Costa Rican form to be unusual: it grows from active Atta ant gardens and has pseudocystidia on the gill edges as well as the faces. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as apparently saprobic. It grows alone or, more often, gregariously or in loose clusters, in grassy or sandy areas or in ground disturbed by landscaping, usually in the year after the disturbance. In Central America it is reported growing from ant colonies. That source gives the season as fall and winter and the North American range as Mexico to Texas, Florida and the Carolinas. It adds that the species is also found in Central America, the Caribbean and South America, and that the illustrated collections are from Florida and South Carolina. A GBIF description says it has normally been collected in disturbed environments of the Neotropics, and that in Colombia it had been collected only in the departments of Antioquia and Santander before a first record for Tolima. Citing Karlsen-Ayala and Smith (2020), MushroomExpert.Com reports a hypothesis that the species was introduced to Florida from the Caribbean and has been spreading in the southeastern United States, either naturally by spore dispersal or with human help through woodchips and soil movement. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
