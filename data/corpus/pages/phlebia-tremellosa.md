## Summary

Phlebia tremellosa (Schrad.) Nakasone & Burdsall, 1984, is a species in the family Meruliaceae, known in English as jelly rot. Wikipedia describes it as a common and widely distributed wood-decay fungus on rotting hardwood and conifer wood. MushroomExpert.Com describes an effused-reflexed fruiting body with a translucent, orangish to pinkish spore-bearing surface that is elaborately wrinkled and pocketed, with a whitish, woolly upper edge where a cap is developed. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was originally described in 1794 by Heinrich Adolf Schrader as Merulius tremellosus. Nakasone and Burdsall transferred it to Phlebia in 1984, placing Merulius in synonymy. MushroomExpert.Com says this transfer was made on the basis of morphology and culture studies. It adds that DNA data from Moncalvo and collaborators (2002) suggest Phlebia is probably polyphyletic, but that P. tremellosa and the type species P. radiata belong to a central group that would remain in a more restricted Phlebia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a white rot species growing on stumps, fallen branches and logs of both hardwoods and conifers, and reports it from Asia, Europe, North Africa, North America and South America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and causing a white rot, growing alone to gregariously and sometimes in overlapping clusters. It says the fungus is found primarily on the dead wood of hardwoods but also reported on conifer wood. It is annual, fruiting in late spring, summer and fall (over winter in warmer climates), and widely distributed in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
