## Summary

Hebeloma cylindrosporum Romagn. (1965) is a species in the family Hymenogastraceae, with its type locality in France. Wikipedia describes it as an ectomycorrhizal fungus associated with Pinus pinaster. It also reports laboratory studies of how the fungus responds to copper, cadmium and arsenic. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet combines 'Cylindr-', derived from Greek and meaning cylindrical, with '-sporum', derived from Latin and meaning it has a spore. It also says the fungus gets its name from its cylindrically shaped spores. This is a single-source statement, and the same page is the origin of the iNaturalist text. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Pearson had found the mushroom in South Africa in 1948. This account comes from a single encyclopedia source; no primary literature was supplied. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes H. cylindrosporum as an ectomycorrhizal species 'associated with Pinus pinaster', forming a Hartig net with the roots of the pine. It reports the fungus from sandy soils with no or very little organic matter, mostly in coastal sand dune ecosystems along the Atlantic south-west coast of France. Wikipedia says it has mainly been found in Europe, and also in Africa and Temperate Asia. It also says that in a laboratory the fungus can associate with other hosts, namely Larix laricina, Dryas integrifolia and Quercus acutissima. The GBIF description record lists its distribution as DK, NO, Madeira Island and SE. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
