## Summary

Gyromitra infula (Schaeff.) Quél., 1886, is a species of false morel in the family Discinaceae, now placed by some authors in Paragyromitra. Its dark reddish-brown cap becomes saddle-shaped at maturity. It is recorded across the Northern Hemisphere, including the United States, Canada and much of Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet as from the Latin infula, a heavy band of twisted wool worn by Roman officiants at sacrifices. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Jacob Christian Schäffer first described the fungus in 1774 as Helvella infula. Lucien Quélet transferred it to Gyromitra in 1886, and Jean Boudier moved it to Physomitra in 1907. It is now classified by some authors in the newly erected genus Paragyromitra. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered or gregariously on well-decayed conifer wood, or terrestrially near stumps and woody debris. It is widely distributed in North America and more common in northern and montane areas. It fruits in late summer and fall, but in coastal California it fruits in winter and spring. Wikipedia adds that it is found in or near coniferous woodland, often on rotten wood, and also on packed ground such as roadsides and campgrounds. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
