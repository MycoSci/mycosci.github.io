## Summary

Lactarius subdulcis (Pers.) Gray, 1821, in the family Russulaceae, is known as the mild milkcap or beech milk cap. Wikipedia describes it as brown, with crowded gills, a thin layer of flesh in the cap, and plentiful white milk that does not stain fabric yellow. The same source associates it with beech in broad-leaved woodland. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin sub 'under' and dulcis 'sweet', after the milk's delayed sweet taste. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Christian Hendrik Persoon first described the species as Agaricus subdulcis in 1801, and Samuel Frederick Gray placed it in Lactarius in 1821 in The Natural Arrangement of British Plants. Wikispecies lists Agaricus subdulcis Pers. 1801 as the basionym, with later combinations in Galorrheus and Lactifluus. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as mycorrhizal and as found in Europe, in broad-leaved woodland, especially on the floor at the base of beech trees. It gives the season as late summer to late autumn. The mushrooms grow individually or in small groups. Wikipedia also reports them in fields, generally in large batches, with groups of over a hundred not uncommon. This is a single-source description (Wikipedia; the iNaturalist text is a derivative of it). *[wikipedia]*
