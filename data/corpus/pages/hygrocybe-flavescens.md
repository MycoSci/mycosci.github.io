## Summary

Hygrocybe flavescens (Kauffman) Singer, 1951, is a waxy cap in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the Golden Waxy Cap. It was described from Michigan. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a bright yellow to orangish yellow waxy cap with a sticky, convex to flat cap and a yellow stem. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both state that the species was described from Michigan. MushroomExpert.Com credits Charles Kauffman (1906) with the original description and Singer (1951) with the combination in Hygrocybe, and lists Hygrophorus flavescens as a synonym. The same source says the European species Hygrocybe chlorophana may be the same as this North American species. If so, chlorophana would be the preferred name because it is more than 100 years older. The two were traditionally separated by stem stickiness, which that source calls a variable and unreliable character. It reports that a 2013 DNA study (Lodge and collaborators) found clear phylogenetic separation between a North Carolina collection identified as H. flavescens and a Danish collection of H. chlorophana. Whether the North Carolina collection represents H. flavescens as Kauffman originally conceived it in Michigan is described as unresolved, and MushroomExpert.Com says further study is required. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com, a single-source account, gives the precise ecological role as uncertain. It describes the species as appearing in woods under hardwoods or conifers, frequently but not exclusively under coast redwood on the West Coast and under beech in eastern North America. It grows scattered or gregariously, from spring through fall, or in winter in warmer climates. That source describes it as apparently widely distributed in North America, and its described collections come from California, Pennsylvania and Québec. Wikipedia states only that it can be found in various forests and woodlands. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
