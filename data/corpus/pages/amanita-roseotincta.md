## Summary

Amanita roseotincta (Murrill) Murrill, 1914, is an agaric in the family Amanitaceae. Wikipedia describes it as found in pine and mixed pine-hardwood woodlands in North America. It says the fruiting bodies have an off-white cap covered with powdery orange veil remnants that quickly fade in color, a bulb-shaped base, a fragile ring on the stipe, and white gills. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet refers to the rose or pink (Latin roseus) color or tinge (Latin tincta) of the universal veil remnants. It also notes that Murrill's original prefix rosei- used the genitive stem, while the roseo- form takes the ablative, which is more common in Latin compound names describing color-tingeing. This derivation comes from a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by the American mycologist William Alphonso Murrill in 1914 as Venenarius roseitinctus, from material collected by Mrs. F. S. Earle in Mississippi. Murrill transferred it to Amanita later the same year. Saccardo's Sylloge fungorum formalized it as Amanita roseo-tincta in 1925. Wikipedia says the International Code of Nomenclature, applicable to fungi since 2011, standardized the orthography to the ablative prefix without a hyphen, so Amanita roseotincta is the accepted name in Index Fungorum. It also reports that R. E. Tulloss considers Amanita komarekensis a synonym. Nothing beyond this single source was available on the reasons for the transfers. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species occurs in pine and mixed-pine forests, singly and in groups. It gives the range in the United States as New Jersey south to Florida and as far west as Texas. The same source says Amanita guzmanii from southern Mexico is a suspected synonym pending research, which may extend the range farther south. This is single-source information (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
