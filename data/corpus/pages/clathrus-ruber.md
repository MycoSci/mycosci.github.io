## Summary

Clathrus ruber P. Micheli ex Pers. (1801) is a species of the family Phallaceae, known in English as the red cage or latticed stinkhorn. Its fruit body is a hollow, lattice-like sphere of red to orange spongy arms, coated on the inside with a foul-smelling spore slime. It arises from a whitish egg-like stage. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the generic name Clathrus derives from Ancient Greek κλειθρον, 'lattice', and that the specific epithet is Latin ruber, 'red'. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the English names 'latticed stinkhorn', 'basket stinkhorn' and 'red cage' allude to the fruit body's round or oval hollow shape with interlaced, latticed branches. It also reports that locals of the Adriatic hinterland in the former Yugoslavia called it 'witch's heart' (veštičije srce / vještičino srce), and that in parts of rural France it is called cœur de sorcière. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was illustrated by Conrad Gesner in 1560, who mistook it for a marine organism, and later appeared in Gerard's 1597 Great Herball and Clusius's 1601 work. Wikipedia says Pier Antonio Micheli first described it scientifically in 1729 and gave it its current name. The GBIF descriptions record Micheli's 1729 plate as the lectotype. Wikipedia adds that American authors once used the name Clathrus cancellatus, and that the name Clathrus ruber is the correct one because Persoon's 1801 Synopsis Methodica Fungorum, which used the epithet ruber, is sanctioned under the nomenclatural code. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the German Mycological Society named C. ruber its 2011 'Mushroom of the Year'. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the fungus as saprobic, growing alone or clustered near woody debris, in lawns, gardens, cultivated soil and mulch beds. It says the species is considered native to southern and central continental Europe, Macaronesia, North Africa and western Asia, and has probably been introduced elsewhere, often through imported mulch. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) gives the fruiting season as spring through fall, or over winter in warm climates. Wikipedia says that in North America the species can be found from October to March. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as common throughout the Mediterranean and as a 'regular' in San Francisco's Golden Gate Park. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
