## Summary

Entoloma porphyrophaeum (Fr.) P. Karst. is a species of agaric (gilled mushroom) in the family Entolomataceae, with the recommended English name Lilac Pinkgill. Wikipedia describes it as having a European distribution, occurring mainly in agriculturally unimproved grassland, and reports that it is assessed as globally "vulnerable" on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species. Wikipedia also notes that North American reports exist, but that at least some of them represent a distinct species, Entoloma canadense. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

According to Wikipedia, the epithet porphyrophaeum is derived from Ancient Greek πορφύρα ("purple") and φαιός ("dusky"). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described by the Swedish mycologist Elias Magnus Fries in 1857 as Agaricus porphyrophaeus, and that the Finnish mycologist Petter Adolf Karsten transferred it to the genus Entoloma in 1879. ## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the Lilac Pinkgill as rare but widespread in Europe. Like many other European pinkgills, it is said to occur in old, agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns), and it is described as typical of waxcap grasslands.
