## Summary

Boletus auripes, the butter-foot bolete, is a species of bolete in the family Boletaceae, described by Charles Horton Peck in 1898 from material collected in New York. It is a mycorrhizal species recorded from eastern North America and eastern Asia. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet auripes means "golden yellow foot", and that the species is commonly known as the "butter-foot bolete". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Charles Horton Peck described the species in 1898 and collected the type specimen at Port Jefferson, New York. In 1945 Rolf Singer proposed the variety Boletus auripes var. aureissimus, based on Ceriomyces aureissimus W.A. Murrill (1938); that taxon is now regarded as the distinct species Boletus aureissimus. Wally Snell later considered B. crassipes, another Peck species, to be conspecific with B. auripes, but Index Fungorum and MycoBank do not recognize that synonymy. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both sources describe B. auripes as mycorrhizal with hardwoods. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) gives oaks or laurel as hosts and says it grows alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall, east of the Rocky Mountains. Wikipedia says it fruits singly, scattered, or in groups on the ground under broadleaf trees, especially oak and beech. It adds that the species has also been recorded with mountain laurel (Kalmia latifolia), and that fruiting runs between June and November. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
