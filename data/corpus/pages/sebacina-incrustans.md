## Summary

Sebacina incrustans (Pers.) Tul. & C. Tul., 1871, is a member of the family Sebacinaceae (order Sebacinales). MushroomExpert.Com describes it as forming a thin, waxy, whitish crust that engulfs plant stems, leaves and twigs; it is a jelly fungus, but its consistency is not particularly jelly-like. GBIF's description record lists it from Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (a single source) describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, growing in amorphous masses on the ground and engulfing leaves, twigs and plant stems. It fruits in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. The same source says it is widely distributed in North America from the Great Plains eastward and southward into Mexico, but apparently absent from western North America, and is also known from Europe, Asia and Oceania. The collections it illustrates and describes are from Illinois. *[mushroomexpert]*
