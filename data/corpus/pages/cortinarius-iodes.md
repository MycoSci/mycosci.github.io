## Summary

Cortinarius iodes (Berk. & M.A. Curtis, 1853) is a species in the family Cortinariaceae, with its type locality in South Carolina. Wikipedia describes it as a slimy-capped, purple mushroom whose cap develops yellowish spots and whose gills shift from violet to rusty brown with age. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a species of eastern North American oak forests. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia, the only source that discusses the name, states that the specific epithet iodes means "violet-like". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Berkeley and Curtis described the species in 1853 and that the type collection was made by Henry William Ravenel in South Carolina. Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com say Ammirati and Bigelow (1984) treated Cortinarius heliotropicus, named by Peck, as the same species; because C. iodes is the older name, it takes priority. The two sources give different dates for Peck's name (1914 in Wikipedia, 1905 in MushroomExpert.Com). Wikipedia adds that Index Fungorum and MycoBank list no synonyms for C. iodes, and that it is placed in subgenus Myxacium. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as mycorrhizal with deciduous trees, particularly oaks. Wikipedia says fruit bodies grow singly but more often scattered or in groups under hardwoods, in humus and litterfall, with typical habitats including bog edges, swampy areas and hummocks, and fruiting usually from July to November. MushroomExpert.Com gives summer and fall fruiting, or over winter along the Gulf Coast, and a range east of the Great Plains in North America. Wikipedia says it is common in eastern North America and rare in the Pacific Northwest. A GBIF record describes a Colombian collection as terrestrial, on soil. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
