## Summary

Peziza domiciliana Cooke (1877) is a cup fungus in the family Pezizaceae, known in English as the domicile cup or home cup. Two independent sources, Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com, describe it as growing in buildings and on building materials such as plaster and concrete. MushroomExpert.Com notes that other Peziza species could conceivably grow in such settings, so precise identification requires microscopic analysis. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia (a single-source account), Mordecai Cubitt Cooke described the species in 1877 from specimens sent to him that had been found growing on the walls, ceilings and floors of a house in Edinburgh that had been partially destroyed by fire. The same source reports its transfer to Aleuria by McLennan & Halsey in 1936 and to Galactinia by Gamundi in 1960. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes fruit bodies growing singly, in groups or in clusters on plaster, sand, gravel and coal-dust in cellars, caves and greenhouses, and also on rotten wood, drywall/plasterboard and plaster in homes, damp cellars and basements. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously indoors or outside in garages, concrete rubble, coal bins and sand, often appearing through cracks in concrete or masonry. Kuo also lists sandstone, crumbling building remains, carpets, plaster and shower stalls, gives the season as year-round, and says it is widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia lists Europe, North America, South America (Argentina), Deception Island in Antarctica and the eastern Himalayas. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
