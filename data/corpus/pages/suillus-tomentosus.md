## Summary

Suillus tomentosus (Singer, Snell & E.A. Dick, 1960) is a bolete in the family Suillaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as one of only a few blue-staining Suillus species, with an orangish yellow, finely velvety cap, and as associated with two-needle pines. The sources supplied here place it mainly in western North America and the upper Midwest. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with two-needle pines, especially lodgepole pine and jack pine, growing scattered or gregariously in summer and fall (also in winter in coastal California). It says the species was originally described from Colorado and is widely distributed in western North America, the northern Midwest and montane Mexico, and also reported from Central America. Wikipedia says it is commonly found in the Rocky Mountains of Idaho and the Pacific Northwest and is less common in the lake states. It gives fruiting as summer in the Rockies and autumn along the Pacific coast and in the lake states, growing singly or scattered in mixed forests, commonly under lodgepole or other two-needle pines and rarely under jack pines. Wikipedia also reports that it forms tuberculate ectomycorrhizae with lodgepole pine (Pinus contorta var. latifolia). *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
