## Summary

Ramaria botrytis (Pers.) Bourdot is a coral fungus in the family Gomphaceae, order Gomphales, with occurrence records in many countries across several continents. Wikipedia gives the common names clustered coral, pink-tipped coral mushroom and cauliflower coral. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe a stout whitish base with densely packed, short branches whose tips are pink to purplish, giving a cauliflower-like look when young. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet botrytis derives from the Greek βότρυς (botrus), meaning "bunch of grapes". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first named Clavaria botrytis by Persoon in 1797. It says Fries sanctioned the genus name Clavaria in 1821 and treated Ramaria as a section of Clavaria, and that Donk designated R. botrytis the type species of Ramaria in 1933. It lists Gotthold Hahn's 1883 Corallium botrytis and Pearson's Clavaria botrytis var. alba as obsolete synonyms. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes R. botrytis as ectomycorrhizal with broadleaf trees, particularly beech. It says records with conifers probably represent similar species. MushroomExpert.Com instead says it grows under hardwoods or conifers, so the two sources differ on hosts. Both describe fruit bodies growing on the ground alone, scattered or in groups. Wikipedia adds that they grow among leaves in woods, can form fairy rings, and commonly fruit near the edges of melting snowbanks in spring. For North America, Wikipedia gives October–January on the West Coast and July–September elsewhere. MushroomExpert.Com gives summer and fall, plus winter and spring in warm climates, and calls it widely distributed in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
