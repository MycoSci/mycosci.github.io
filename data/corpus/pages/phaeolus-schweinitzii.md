## Summary

Phaeolus schweinitzii (Fr.) Pat., 1900, is a polypore in the family Laetiporaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a fungal plant pathogen, and MushroomExpert.Com calls it a classic "butt rot" fungus of conifers. Its fruiting body may appear terrestrial when it grows from the roots or base of the host tree. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that P. schweinitzii is named after Lewis David de Schweinitz, a Pennsylvania-born Moravian minister and important early American mycologist. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia describes it as an excellent natural source of green, yellow, gold, or brown dye, depending on the material dyed and the mordant used. It says this is what the common name "dyer's polypore" refers to. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says P. schweinitzii causes butt rot on conifers such as Douglas-fir, spruce, fir, hemlock, pine, and larch, and that the fruiting bodies appear in late summer or fall. It describes the species as native to Eurasia and identified as an exotic species in New Zealand, Australia, and South Africa. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as parasitic on the roots and heartwood of living conifers and saprobic on deadwood, usually found near the base of the trunk and appearing terrestrial. That source says it is common on Douglas-fir in the West, white pine in eastern North America, and loblolly pine in the South of the United States. It also says the species is widely distributed in Europe, North America, Central America, Asia, and Oceania, and was originally described from Sweden. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
