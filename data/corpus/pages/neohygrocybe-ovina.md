## Summary

Neohygrocybe ovina (Bull.) Herink, 1958 is a waxcap in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the Blushing Waxcap. Wikipedia reports that its gills and flesh turn pinkish red when bruised, and that it is assessed as globally "vulnerable" on the IUCN Red List. MushroomExpert.Com describes a dry, black cap and stem. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the recommended English name "blushing waxcap" was given because the gills and flesh turn pinkish red when bruised. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described from France in 1793 by Jean Baptiste Bulliard as Agaricus ovinus, and Robert Kühner transferred it to Hygrocybe in 1926. Wikipedia adds that DNA-based research found it to be a distinct species that does not belong in Hygrocybe sensu stricto, and that it was moved to Neohygrocybe, as Josef Herink had already proposed on morphological grounds in 1958. MushroomExpert.Com also gives France (Bulliard 1793) as the place of original description. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species has a European distribution and occurs in old, agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns). MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) likewise reports it in grasslands in Europe, but says that in North America it is found in mixed hardwood/conifer woods. Kuo describes it as growing scattered to gregariously in summer and fall, and says its precise ecological role is uncertain. This seasonal and North American habitat information comes from that one source. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
