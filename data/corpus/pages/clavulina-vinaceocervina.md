## Summary

Clavulina vinaceocervina, the dark-tipped coral, is a coral fungus in the family Hydnaceae, combined in Clavulina by Corner in 1950. Wikipedia describes it as around 5 cm high, reddish-brown to fawn with reddish-pink tips, with a short trunk and irregular, blunt or prong-like branchlets. It is recorded from Australia, New Zealand, Mexico and Costa Rica. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the name as a combination of Latin "vinaceo", meaning wine-coloured, and Latin "cervina", meaning of or pertaining to a deer. This derivation comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described as Clavaria vinaceo-cervina by the Australian mycologist John Burton Cleland in 1931. E. J. H. Corner reclassified it in Clavulina in 1950. Wikipedia also lists two varieties, var. vinaceocervina and var. avellanea. No supplied source says what material Cleland described it from or why it was transferred. *[wikipedia]*
