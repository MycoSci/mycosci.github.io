## Summary

Psilocybe cubensis (Earle) Singer is a species in the family Hymenogastraceae, formerly placed in Stropharia. Wikipedia describes it as a psilocybin mushroom whose principal active compounds are psilocybin and psilocin. It describes it as pan-tropical, and GBIF records place it across the Americas, Africa, Asia and Australia. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name Psilocybe is derived from the Ancient Greek roots psilos and kubê and translates as "bare head". It says cubensis means "coming from Cuba" and refers to the type locality published by Earle. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia reports that a common Thai name, "Hed keequai", translates as "mushroom which appears after water buffalo defecates". It gives regional names including gold top or golden top in Australia, and "Golden teacher" in South Africa. It does not narrate how those names arose. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Franklin Sumner Earle first described the species as Stropharia cubensis in Cuba in 1906. Patouillard identified it as Naematoloma caerulescens in Tonkin in 1907, and Murrill called it Stropharia cyanescens near Gainesville, Florida in 1941. Rolf Singer moved it into Psilocybe and divided it into three varieties. The nominate variety usually had a brownish cap, var. cyanescens from Florida a pale cap, and var. caerulescens from Indochina a more yellowish cap. The later synonyms were assigned to P. cubensis. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that Singer noted psychoactive properties in this species. It also reports that psychoactive mushroom use in Australia grew rapidly between 1969 and 1975. A 1992 paper reported locals and tourists in Thailand consuming P. cubensis and related species in mushroom omelets, particularly on Ko Samui and Ko Pha-ngan. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia describes indoor cultivation on grain spawn and bulk substrates such as coir, vermiculite and gypsum. It also describes small-scale "cake" methods such as PF-Tek and "Uncle Ben's Tek". It credits Terence and Dennis McKenna's 1970s grower's guide with making the species particularly well known for ease of cultivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states the species is found on cow (and occasionally horse) dung, sugar cane mulch or rich pasture soil. It says mushrooms appear from February to December in the northern hemisphere and November to April in the southern hemisphere, and that in Asia it grows on water buffalo dung. A GBIF description of Argentine material records the basidiomata as coprophilous on cattle and horse dung. A GBIF description of Colombian material records dung as the substrate, growing solitary or gregarious. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
