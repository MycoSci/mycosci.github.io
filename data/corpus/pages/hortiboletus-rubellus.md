## Summary

Hortiboletus rubellus (Krombh.) Simonini, Vizzini & Gelardi, commonly known as the ruby bolete, is a member of the family Boletaceae. It is a small bolete with a reddish cap and stipe and yellow pores, and its flesh stains blue when cut or bruised. Until 2015 it was known as Boletus rubellus. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet rubellus is Latin for "somewhat red". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was described by the German mycologist Julius Vincenz von Krombholz in 1836 as Boletus rubellus. The name Boletus versicolor (Rostk.), published in 1844, is now a synonym because it postdates Krombholz's name. The species was at one time placed in Xerocomus, and some texts still treat it that way. It was transferred to the new genus Hortiboletus in 2015, following molecular evidence that it is genetically dissimilar to Boletus. Wikipedia also notes that conflicting information on pore size in notable publications may indicate more than one species is involved, in both Britain and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports it in deciduous woodland in autumn. It describes the species as uncommon to rare in southern England, occurring largely with oak (Quercus), and also present in Europe and the eastern United States and Canada. It adds that a similar species noted as "cf versicolor" has been collected in Victoria, southeastern Australia. Mushroom World, a separate source, says it is commonly found in oak-dominated habitats and gives its location as Europe and the eastern United States. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
