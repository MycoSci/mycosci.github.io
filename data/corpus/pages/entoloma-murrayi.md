## Summary

Entoloma murrayi (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Sacc. & P. Syd. is a species in the family Entolomataceae, known in English as the yellow unicorn Entoloma or unicorn pinkgill. It is described as a small yellow mushroom with a conical cap and a sharp central umbo. Occurrence records in the GBIF descriptions source include Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

According to Wikipedia, the specific epithet murrayi honors the original collector, Dennis Murray of Massachusetts. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the common names "yellow unicorn Entoloma" and "unicorn pinkgill" refer to the characteristic sharp umbo at the top of the cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Per Wikipedia, Miles Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis originally described the species in 1859 as Agaricus murrayi, from collections made in New England, calling it "An extremely pretty species". Pier Andrea Saccardo transferred it to Entoloma in 1899. Wikipedia lists later transfers to Rhodophyllus (Singer, 1942), Noleana (Dennis, 1970) and Inocephalus (Rutter and Watling, 1997). MushroomExpert.Com also lists Inocephalus murraii, Nolanea murraii, Entoloma cuspidatum and Rhodopyllus murraii as synonyms, and notes "Entoloma murraii" as a common misspelling. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes E. murrayi as saprobic, with fruit bodies growing singly or in small groups on the ground in litterfall or humus, or in moss, in wet coniferous and deciduous forests, fruiting in summer and autumn. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously under hardwoods or conifers in swamps and wet woods, in summer and fall, in eastern North America and southward to Central America. Wikipedia gives the North American range as eastern Canada (Atlantic Maritime Ecozone), the eastern United States (Maine south to Alabama and west to the Great Lakes) and Mexico. It adds Central and South America, Southeast Asia, Jamaica and the Dominican Republic. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
