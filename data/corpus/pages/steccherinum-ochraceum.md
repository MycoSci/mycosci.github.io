## Summary

Steccherinum ochraceum (Pers. ex J.F. Gmel.) Gray, 1821, is a hydnoid fungus in the family Steccherinaceae, known in English as the ochre spreading tooth. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a toothed mushroom with a surface of tightly packed, tiny spines that can resemble a cap-less orange polypore or a crust fungus. The same source describes it as a widely distributed decomposer of hardwood deadwood. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was originally described as Hydnum ochraceum by Johann Friedrich Gmelin in 1792, and later transferred to the genus Steccherinum by Samuel Frederick Gray in 1821. No supplied source gives the type material or the reasons for the transfer. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods and, rarely, conifers. It grows alone or gregariously on sticks, logs or stumps and causes a white rot. It fruits from late spring through fall, or over winter in warmer climates. In the Midwest, the same source calls it fairly common in oak woods on fallen sticks and small logs in late spring and early summer. It is described as widely distributed in North America, but more common east of the Rocky Mountains. A GBIF-hosted materials-examined record from Ailaoshan National Nature Reserve, Yunnan, China, describes white rot in a broad-leaved forest, with specimens taken from trunks of angiosperm. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
