## Summary

Exidia glandulosa (Bull.) Fr. is a jelly fungus in the family Auriculariaceae. Wikipedia describes it as having gelatinous, brownish-grey to black, top-shaped fruit bodies that grow singly or in groups on dead wood. It is described there as a common wood-rotting species in Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was originally described from France as Tremella glandulosa by Bulliard in 1789, and placed in Exidia by Fries in 1822. Fries modified the species concept to include a second, effused, coalescing species, so the name Exidia glandulosa served for both until Neuhoff separated them in 1936. Neuhoff then applied the name E. glandulosa to the effused species and used E. truncata for Bulliard's original. Donk pointed out this error in 1966. The same source says recent DNA-based research found two turbinate species in Europe: E. glandulosa, with a hirsute undersurface, and E. truncata, with a punctate undersurface. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a wood-rotting species typically found on dead attached branches of broadleaf trees, especially oak and occasionally hazel, beech and alder. It says the species can colonize living or recently dead wood and persists for some time on fallen branches and logs. Wikipedia says fruit bodies are normally produced in autumn, and that the species' distribution outside Europe is uncertain. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on recently fallen hardwood sticks and branches, especially oak, and says it is commonly encountered in spring and again in fall, and is widely distributed in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
