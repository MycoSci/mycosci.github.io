## Summary

Helvella lacunosa Afzel. (1783) is an ascomycete in the family Helvellaceae, known in English as the elfin saddle. Wikipedia describes it as having an irregularly folded grey-to-black cap and a fluted, ribbed stem. MushroomExpert.Com treats the true species as European, and says that similar mushrooms in North America long called by this name are now thought to belong to several other species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet lacunosa is a Latin adjective meaning "with holes". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that John Lightfoot wrote of the fungus in his 1777 Flora Scotica under the name Helvella mitra, and that Adam Afzelius formally described it in 1783. The GBIF description record says the type was studied by Abbott & Currah (1997), and reproduces a neotype designation from Sweden (Lapland, Vindeljallets naturreservat, under Betula nana, Salix sp. and Dryas sp., 20 Aug. 2010, K. Hansen, K. Gillen & I. Olariaga, KH. 10.97). MushroomExpert.Com instead says that Skrede et al. (2017) clarified the species and designated Schaeffer's 1763 illustration as the type. The supplied sources do not reconcile these two accounts of the type. MushroomExpert.Com also quotes Nguyen et al. (2013) as estimating that at least eight species in the USA and Canada, and at least three in Mexico, had been called H. lacunosa. It says two of these were described as Helvella dryophila and Helvella vespertina, and that the remaining North American species are unnamed. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

The supplied sources differ by region and are not reconciled here. Wikipedia states that the species occurs under pine, oak and Douglas fir and nearby parkland and lawns, often on burnt ground, with fruiting bodies in late summer and autumn (recorded in winter in California). MushroomExpert.Com, describing Michigan collections of one of the North American species it says probably does not match the true European species, says the mushroom is probably mycorrhizal. It says these grow alone, scattered or gregariously in woods under conifers, often on disturbed ground, in eastern North America usually in wet, mossy areas in cold conifer bogs, in summer and fall. For the European species, MushroomExpert.Com says it is more common in montane and boreal regions. The GBIF description record notes the Swedish neotype collection was on the ground under Betula nana, Salix sp. and Dryas sp., and separately records a Colombian collection under Quercus humboldtii. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
