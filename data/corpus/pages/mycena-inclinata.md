## Summary

Mycena inclinata (Fr.) Quél. (1872) is a species in the family Mycenaceae, known in English as the clustered bonnet. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as a saprobic mushroom that grows in clusters on decaying hardwood wood. MushroomExpert.Com adds that its presence in North America is debatable, because North American collections have been fitted to European descriptions. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus inclinatus by Elias Magnus Fries in 1838 and was given its current name by Lucien Quélet in 1872. MushroomExpert.Com gives the same authorship and dates (Fries, 1838; Quélet, 1872). Wikipedia also says E.J.H. Corner described two varieties, kinabaluensis and subglobospora, from Borneo (Malesia) in his 1994 publication. It reports that a molecular study of orchid-associated fungi found the species closely related to M. aurantiomarginata, M. crocata and M. leaiana. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says M. inclinata and M. galericulata are both European species, and that North American mycologists have long fitted their collections to European descriptions. Kuo suggests DNA studies might show the North American material to be genetically distinct. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes M. inclinata as a saprobic fungus that grows in dense groups or clusters on decaying hardwood logs and stumps, especially oak and chestnut, in spring and autumn. It says the fungus is occasionally found on a living tree. MushroomExpert.Com independently describes it as saprobic on the well decayed wood of hardwoods, usually in dense clusters but sometimes alone or scattered. Its season is given as spring and fall, or over winter in warmer climates. For North America, MushroomExpert.Com gives a range east of the Rocky Mountains with occasional West Coast reports, while noting that the identity of North American material is debatable. Wikipedia lists the range as Europe, the Canary Islands, North Africa, East Siberia, Japan, Malesia, Turkey, New Zealand and eastern North America, and calls the species generally rare along the Pacific Coast. These regional statements come from Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com respectively and are not corroborated by a third independent source. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
