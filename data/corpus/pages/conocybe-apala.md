## Summary

Conocybe apala (Fr.) Arnolds, published in 2003, is a small agaric in the family Bolbitiaceae with a pale, conical cap and a thin, hollow, very fragile stem, and it has been recorded in many countries. Sources place it in grassy areas across Europe and North America. The fruit bodies are short-lived; MushroomExpert.Com notes they often collapse completely by mid-day. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the basionym Agaricus apalus was described by the Swedish mycologist Elias Magnus Fries in 1818, and MushroomExpert.Com likewise gives Sweden as the place of original description (Fries 1818). According to Wikipedia, the species was later placed in Bolbitius as B. albipes by G.H. Otth (1871), then as Pluteolus apalus by Lucien Quélet (1886), as Galera hapala (or Galera apala) by Pier Andrea Saccardo (1887), as Bolbitius apalus by Costantin and Dufour (1891), and as Derminus apalus by Paul Hennings (1898). Everhardus Johannes Maria Arnolds reclassified it as Conocybe apala in 2003. Wikipedia adds that it was until recently commonly called Conocybe lactea or Conocybe albipes; MushroomExpert.Com lists C. lactea, C. albipes and C. crispa as synonyms. Neither source gives the reasons for the transfers. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes C. apala as a saprobe of rich soil and short grass, such as pastures, playing fields, lawns and meadows, and also rotting manured straw. It says the fruit bodies appear singly or in small numbers, commonly in humid, rainy, overcast weather, and that they can appear on dewy sunny mornings but do not persist once the dew evaporates. Wikipedia gives a fruiting season from spring to autumn and a distribution across Europe and North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing scattered or gregariously in grassy areas (lawns, meadows, pastures), or in woodchips or compost. It gives the season as summer and fall, especially in muggy weather, and says the species is widely distributed in Europe and North America. The collections MushroomExpert.Com illustrates and describes are from Illinois. The two sources differ slightly on season and substrate. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
