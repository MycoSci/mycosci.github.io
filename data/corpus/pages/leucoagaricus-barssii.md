## Summary

Leucoagaricus barssii (Zeller) Vellinga is a lepiotoid mushroom in the family Agaricaceae, with the English vernacular name smoky dapperling. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a whitish cap that is fibrillose and becomes fibrillose-scaly, white free gills, a large ring on the stem and a white spore print. Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as known mainly from the West Coast of North America and Europe. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first classified as Lepiota barssii by the American mycologist Sanford Myron Zeller in 1934 and was reclassified as Leucoagaricus barssii by Else C. Vellinga in 2000. Wikipedia also states that Leucoagaricus macrorhizus, the type species of Leucoagaricus, was reclassified as Leucoagaricus barssii. It adds that in 2024 the species was moved to Leucocoprinus barssii by the Italian mycologists Vincenzo Migliozzi and Giuseppe Donato. MushroomExpert.Com lists Lepiota barssii, Lepiota/Leucoagaricus pinguipes and Leucoagaricus macrorhizus as synonyms and says the species was originally described from Oregon. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (single-source) describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in landscaping areas, near waste places, on disturbed ground and in woodchips, or in woods or back dunes, in summer and fall. It calls the species a denizen of urban locations and back dunes, often growing alone on disturbed soil. The same source says it is found in California and the Pacific Northwest in North America and is also present in Europe and Australia. Wikipedia reports it as widespread but rarely recorded in the United Kingdom, and says observations appear to be uncommon in Europe. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
