## Summary

Rhodocollybia butyracea (Bull.) Lennox, 1979, is a species in the family Omphalotaceae, known in English as the buttery collybia, butter cap or greasy toughshank. Sources describe it as a saprobic decomposer of conifer litter, usually under pines. Its DNA suggests that more than one species may be included under the name. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com gives the basionym authority as Bulliard, 1792, with the Lennox 1979 combination, and notes that Collybia butyracea is a former name. It also reports that DNA analysis by Mata and collaborators (2004) found at least two genetically distinct populations within R. butyracea, and that further research may show more than one species is involved. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe the species as saprobic, decomposing conifer litter, usually that of pines (Pinus). Wikipedia adds that it occasionally grows on hardwood litter. MushroomExpert.Com gives the season as late summer and fall, and also winter and spring in warmer climates. Both describe it as widely distributed in North America. MushroomExpert.Com's described collections are from Colorado and Illinois. It also reports that a 2004 study by Mleczko, working on Scots pine rootlets in Poland, proposed the fungus is mycorrhizal. Later researchers (Tedersoo et al., 2010) suggested that study's methods may have led to an inaccurate conclusion and argued it is a saprobe. The mycorrhizal proposal is reported by a single source. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
