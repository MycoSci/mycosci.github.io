## Summary

Bolbitius coprophilus (Peck) Hongo, 1959, is a member of the Bolbitiaceae; the spine lists Pluteolus coprophilus as a synonym. MushroomExpert.Com describes young specimens as having white-stained-pink, egg-shaped caps that soon turn pinkish brown to dull grayish brown, and says the stem develops pink shades from the apex downward. Under the microscope it has large spores flattened at one end, with a pore. GBIF distribution records list it from Austria, Belgium, Germany, Denmark, France, Great Britain, the Netherlands, Norway and Sweden. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species epithet, coprophilus, means "dung loving." *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was originally described from New York (Peck 1894), and credits the combination in Bolbitius to T. Hongo, 1959. It gives no further account of the material or the reasons for the transfer. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, decomposing dung or nitrogen-rich mulch, and says it is also found on compost, straw and woodchips. It reports that the species is usually found in urban settings, growing alone or gregariously, in summer and fall (and over winter in warm climates). It is described as widespread in Europe and North America, and the illustrated collection is from Illinois. The GBIF distribution record lists Austria, Belgium, Germany, Denmark, France, Great Britain, the Netherlands, Norway and Sweden. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
