## Summary

Xerocomellus porosporus (Imler ex Watling) Šutara, 2008, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the sepia bolete. Wikipedia describes it as a small mushroom with tubes and pores rather than gills beneath the cap. GBIF holds occurrence records from many countries, mostly in Europe. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, this bolete was described as Xerocomus porosporus in 1958 by Louis Imler (1900–1993), founder of the Antwerp Mycological Circle. The current name Xerocomellus porosporus dates from a 2008 publication by the Czech mycologist Josef Šutara. Wikipedia says Šutara studied the morphological characters of this and closely related boletes in detail, and that the placement has since been supported by DNA studies. This account comes from a single independent source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it appears occasionally in autumn, growing singly or in small groups in mixed deciduous woods, particularly with oak, hornbeams and beech. The same source calls it widespread in northern temperate zones but somewhat rare in Europe. This is a single-source claim; no second independent source was available. *[wikipedia]*
