## Summary

Lactarius aurantiacus (Pers.) Gray, 1821 is a species of mushroom in the family Russulaceae, commonly called the orange milkcap. Wikipedia notes that this English name also refers to other species, such as Lactarius subflammeus. Wikipedia describes it as a mycorrhizal milkcap with a convex, slightly depressed cap 1 to 5 cm across, colored from vibrant orange to light orangish brown, that produces milky latex when cut or bruised. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Agaricus testaceus var. aurantiacus Pers. 1801. It lists later combinations in Agaricus (J.Otto, 1816), Galorrheus (Kummer, 1871) and Lactifluus (Kuntze, 1891). It writes the accepted name as Lactarius aurantiacus (Pers.: Fr.) Gray 1821 'ss. str.', explicitly excluding the sense used by Moser, Neuhoff and Marchand. It states that the name as used by Neuhoff, M.M. Moser and A. Marchand corresponds to Lactarius mitissimus, so literature using those authors' concept may not refer to the same species. This is a single-source account (Wikispecies), and it does not say why the transfers were made. *[wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is mainly found in Europe, with sightings in parts of Asia and North America, and that it grows alone or in small groups. It is reported to grow in forests on acidic soils near pine, spruce and sometimes birch, forming a mycorrhizal relationship with one or more of the surrounding trees. This is a single-source account (Wikipedia; the iNaturalist text is a copy of its opening lines). *[wikipedia]*
