## Summary

Hypomyces lactifluorum (Schwein.) Tul. & C. Tul., family Hypocreaceae, is a parasitic ascomycete, not a mushroom in its own right. It grows on other mushrooms, chiefly Russula, Lactarius and Lactifluus species, and covers them in a hard, pimply, bright orange coating. The covered host is what is commonly called the lobster mushroom. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as distributed across Canada, the United States and Mexico. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the common name 'lobster mushroom' refers to the visual similarity of affected mushrooms to a lobster, and describes the reddish orange colour as resembling the outer shell of a cooked lobster. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was originally described from North Carolina by von Schweinitz in 1822, with the combination credited to Tulasne & Tulasne in 1860. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

A 2014 ethnobiological survey of 197 residents of the Sierra Tarahumara in Chihuahua, Mexico, found Hypomyces lactifluorum among five species the respondents preferred. Wikipedia reports that lobster mushrooms are commercially marketed and have been available at markets in Oregon. *[lit_3, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com reports it as parasitic on Russula, Lactarius and Lactifluus, especially Russula brevipes, Lactarius deceptivus and other members of the Lactarius piperatus group. It gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. Wikipedia describes it in wooded areas, often near Russula brevipes or Lactarius in conifer forests, in particular under ponderosa pine in the American Southwest and the Pacific Northwest. GBIF lists a distribution record for Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
