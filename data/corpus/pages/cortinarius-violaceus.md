## Summary

Cortinarius violaceus (L.) Gray, 1821, is a species in the family Cortinariaceae, known in English as the Violet Webcap or violet cort, and recorded from Europe, North America and Asia. Wikipedia describes it as a dark purple, scaly-capped webcap and notes that it is the type species of the genus Cortinarius. MushroomExpert.Com adds that it is the only Cortinarius with prominent, clearly defined cystidia on its gills. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet violaceus refers to the deep violet colour of the cap. It also gives Gray's 1821 rendering, Cortinaria violacea, as "the violet curtain-stool". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Linnaeus named the species Agaricus violaceus in his 1753 Species Plantarum. Lamarck treated it as a variety of Amanita araneosa in 1783, and Persoon placed it in Section Cortinaria of Agaricus in 1801. Gray established the genus Cortinarius in 1821 and recorded the species as Cortinaria violacea. Wünsche described it as Inoloma violaceum in 1877, and Kuntze called it Gomphos violaceus in 1898, a treatment Wikipedia says most biologists did not accept. Clements and Shear designated it the type species of Cortinarius in 1931. Wikipedia also reports that pine-associated populations have been treated as a separate species, C. hercynicus, or as a subspecies. Moser separated them in 1967 and Brandrud made hercynicus a subspecies in 1983. A 2015 molecular study, on limited testing, found no genetic or ecological difference between the two. MushroomExpert.Com likewise says that in its author's experience the differences are not consistent. A GBIF nomenclatural note records that Brandrud selected a neotype from Sweden, but that original elements exist which, under Art. 9.11, preclude selecting a neotype. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that in Europe the species grows in deciduous woodland in autumn, especially among oak, birch and beech, and occasionally with conifers. It has also been recorded on treeless heathland with bracken, and it is said to favour acidic soil. In Nordic countries the hosts named there are Betula pubescens, B. pendula and Populus tremula, and rarely Fagus sylvatica. In Greenland it grows with Betula pubescens. Wikipedia says that in North America it favours conifers and is more common in old-growth forest in the Pacific Northwest. Fruit bodies occur singly or in small groups, often near rotting wood, and can form fairy rings. MushroomExpert.Com likewise describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers, fruiting in fall, and often reported in old-growth conifer forest near rotting logs in western North America. Its illustrated collections are from Michigan and Oregon. Mushroom World describes limestone and chalk-rich woodland, mainly under beech and other broadleaf trees, fruiting in late summer and autumn. That differs from Wikipedia's statement that the species favours acidic soil. The Linnaean protologue, as quoted in GBIF's description records, gives the habitat as "Habitat ad margines Sylvarum", the margins of woods. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, gbif_desc]*
