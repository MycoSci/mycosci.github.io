## Summary

Lepiota magnispora Murrill (1912), the yellowfoot dapperling, is a species of Agaricaceae. Sources describe a saprotrophic lepiotoid mushroom with a dry, scaly cap, free gills and a white spore print. It closely resembles Lepiota clypeolaria, and its North American range is uncertain because of confusion with similar species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described by William Murrill in 1912 and was previously considered the same species as Lepiota clypeolaria. It says L. magnispora is more brightly colored and has larger spores. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) also notes confusion with L. clypeolaria, and says L. magnispora has brighter, yellower colors and longer spores. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as saprotrophic and found in a wide variety of habitats, growing under both hardwoods and conifers, including western redcedar. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing scattered, gregariously, or in clusters in forest litter under hardwoods and conifers. It gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in coastal California, and says the illustrated and described collections are from California and Colorado. Both sources say its distribution in North America is uncertain because of similar species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
