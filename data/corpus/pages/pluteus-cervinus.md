## Summary

Pluteus cervinus (Schaeff.) P. Kumm. is a gilled mushroom in the family Pluteaceae, known in English as the deer shield, fawn mushroom or deer mushroom. Sources describe it as growing on dead wood, with free gills that are white at first and turn pink as the spores ripen. GBIF holds occurrence records from dozens of countries. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species epithet cervinus means "deer-like" and refers to the colour of the cap, which Jacob Christian Schäffer's original 1774 description called "rehfarbig". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia attributes the original description to Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1774. MushroomExpert.Com gives the same 1774 Schaeffer authority and a 1871 Kummer combination, and lists Pluteus atricapillus as a synonym. MushroomExpert.Com also reports a 2014 DNA study (Justo et al.) that supports several cryptic species within the traditional concept of P. cervinus. It says morphology cannot always separate them, so records under this name may not all refer to the same narrowly defined species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes P. cervinus as saprotrophic on rotten logs, roots, stumps, sawdust and other wood waste. It calls it common in Europe (April to December) and eastern North America, and rare and possibly introduced in western North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods and, less often, conifers, occasionally appearing terrestrial but arising from buried deadwood. It fruits from spring through fall, is common in eastern North America from about the 45th parallel southwards, and is also recorded from the San Francisco Bay area. MushroomExpert.Com adds that it is not generally found on woodchips in urban areas, which appears to differ from Wikipedia's mention of sawdust and wood waste. Mushroom World lists rotting stumps and woody debris of broad-leaf and, very occasionally, coniferous trees. One paper reports it as a first record for West Bengal, India, collected at Birati. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, lit_2]*
