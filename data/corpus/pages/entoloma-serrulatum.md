## Summary

Entoloma serrulatum (Fr.) Hesler is a species of pink-spored mushroom in the family Entolomataceae, known in English as Blue Edge Pinkgill and Charmer. Wikipedia describes it as found across North America, while MushroomExpert.Com says it was originally named from Europe and that the North American version may be a separate species or group of species. MushroomExpert.Com singles out the black-edged (marginate) gills as the feature that distinguishes it from most other blackish, bluish gray or purplish gray entolomatoid mushrooms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that Entoloma serrulatum was originally named from Europe, and that the North American version may be a separate species or group of species. It lists Leptonia serrulata, Eccilia atrides and Rhodopyllus serrulatus among the synonyms. Wikipedia adds that DNA analysis suggests the name may cover multiple species, even in the Pacific Northwest alone. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states the species is found in both grassy areas and forests and is saprophytic. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says the European populations grow in grassy areas, while the North American version is found in woodland areas. It describes the ecology as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in woods, often in damp areas, occasionally fruiting from moss-covered wood, in late summer and fall (or over winter on the West Coast). The collection MushroomExpert.Com illustrates and describes is from a tanoak forest in California. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
