## Summary

Geopyxis carbonaria (Alb. & Schwein.) Sacc., 1889, is a cup fungus in the family Pyronemataceae, originally described as Peziza carbonaria. The sources describe small, goblet-shaped fruitbodies on a short stem that appear on burned ground, sometimes in great numbers. It is recorded from Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet carbonaria derives from the Latin word for "charcoal". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the fungus was first described from Europe in 1805 by Albertini and Schweinitz as Peziza carbonaria, and that Saccardo transferred it to Geopyxis in 1889. MushroomExpert.Com gives the original locality as Upper Lusatia (in present-day Germany and Poland). Wikipedia further reports that Peziza lepida, described by Berkeley and Curtis in 1860 from collections made in Japan, was synonymized with G. carbonaria by Rifai in 1968, an opinion it says Pfister corroborated about a decade later. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as appearing in burned northern and montane conifer forests, gregariously or in loose clusters, beginning the year after the burn and fruiting from spring through fall. Wikipedia likewise describes it on burned soil or charcoal, and reports fruitbodies produced from 16 to 139 weeks after a forest fire in coniferous areas, with most in the first year after a burn. Wikipedia reports that in North America it occurs from Alaska south, excluding the Gulf Coast region, appearing March to June on the West Coast and April to September elsewhere, and that it is also found in Europe and was first reported from Turkey in 2010. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe the fungus as saprotrophic/saprobic and facultatively biotrophic; Wikipedia reports an ectomycorrhizal association with Norway spruce (Picea abies). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
