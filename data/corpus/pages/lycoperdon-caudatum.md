## Summary

Lycoperdon caudatum J. Schröt. (1889) is a puffball in the family Lycoperdaceae, with its type locality in Germany. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small-to-medium, pear-shaped, sort-of-spiny puffball, 4–6 cm high and 3–5 cm wide, whose most distinctive feature is microscopic: spores borne on a pedicel (a tail) of 2–20 µm. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was first named Lycoperdon pedicellatum by Peck (1873), after its pedicellate (tailed) spores. Peck's name is invalid because it had already been used for a completely different mushroom. The site's reference line credits the valid name to J. Schröter, 1889. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in woods under hardwoods or conifers. It is usually associated with woody debris, or even grows from well-decayed deadwood. It sometimes appears in spring but is usually found in fall. It is described as widely distributed in North America and more commonly collected east of the Rocky Mountains; the illustrated and described collection is from Illinois. The GBIF description record lists distribution as Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
