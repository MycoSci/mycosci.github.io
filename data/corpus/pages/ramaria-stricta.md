## Summary

Ramaria stricta (Pers.) Quél. is a coral fungus in the family Gomphaceae. Its fruit body is branched, with mostly upright, nearly parallel branches, and is up to about 10 cm tall according to Wikipedia. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says the branches bruise and discolor purplish brown. Kuo also says the name probably covers a group of similar species that have not yet been separated. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Clavaria stricta by Christian Hendrik Persoon, and Lucien Quélet transferred it to Ramaria in 1888. No source supplied here says why it was transferred. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as lignicolous, growing on dead wood, stumps, trunks and branches of both deciduous and coniferous trees. It says the species is common in late summer and fall in coniferous forests of the Pacific Coast and Rocky Mountains. It also says fruit bodies can form in "log lines" where decaying wood is buried near the surface or is well decomposed. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as growing from dead, sometimes buried, wood of conifers and sometimes hardwoods, from early summer through fall. Kuo says it is more common from the Rocky Mountains westward in North America. Kuo calls the ecology uncertain: a wood-inhabiting Ramaria could be mycorrhizal or saprobic. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
