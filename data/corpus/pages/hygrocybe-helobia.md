## Summary

Hygrocybe helobia (Arnolds) Bon, the garlic waxcap, is a gilled mushroom in the family Hygrophoraceae. Wikipedia describes it as a European grassland species that fruits in autumn and is assessed as globally "near threatened" on the IUCN Red List because its habitat is declining. The same source describes small fruit bodies with a dry, finely scaly, bright scarlet cap and a possible garlic smell. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1974 by the Dutch mycologist Eef Arnolds as Hygrophorus helobius and was later moved to the genus Hygrocybe. The same source says recent DNA-based research places it within Hygrocybe sensu stricto. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is widespread throughout Europe and typically grows in old, unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns), producing fruit bodies in autumn. It describes the species as typical of waxcap grasslands, a habitat declining because of changing agricultural practices.
