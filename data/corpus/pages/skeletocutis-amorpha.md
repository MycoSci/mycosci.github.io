## Summary

Skeletocutis amorpha (Fr.) Kotl. & Pouzar is a poroid fungus in the family Incrustoporiaceae. Wikipedia describes it as the type species of the genus Skeletocutis. It gives the fruit bodies as effused-reflexed (crust-like with the edges curled out into rudimentary caps) or, more rarely, completely crust-like. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries first described the fungus in 1815 as Polyporus amorphus. It has since acquired an extensive synonymy. Wikipedia says the Czech mycologists František Kotlába and Zdeněk Pouzar transferred it to Skeletocutis in 1958. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only independent source with habitat text, describes it as widely distributed and found in Africa, Australia, China and Europe. It says the fungus causes a white rot in the dead wood of various species of the pine family, particularly pine but also fir, larch and spruce. It says the fungus rarely grows on hardwoods such as alder, beech and oak. *[wikipedia]*
