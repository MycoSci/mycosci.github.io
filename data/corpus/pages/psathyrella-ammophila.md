## Summary

Psathyrella ammophila (Durieu & Lév.) P.D. Orton 1960 is a species in the family Psathyrellaceae, commonly called the dune brittlestem. Wikipedia describes it as growing on sand dunes near marram grass and as found throughout Europe, with a few records elsewhere. ## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name Psathyrella is a diminutive of Psathyra, from the Greek psathuros, meaning "friable". It states that the epithet ammophila comes from the Greek words for sand (ammos) and lover, a reference to the mushroom's sand dune habitat. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the common name "brittlestem", used for many Psathyrella species, relates to the fragile nature of the cap and stem. It does not give a separate account of how "dune brittlestem" itself arose. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1868 by Michel Charles Durieu de Maisonneuve and Joseph-Henri Léveillé, in Durieu de Maisonneuve's work on the natural history of Algeria (Exploration scientifique de l'Algérie: Sciences naturelles, botanique), under the name Agaricus ammophilus. The same source says it was identified and described a further five times under different names until 1960, when P.D. Orton made the combination now in use. This account comes from one source and gives no detail on the material examined or the reasons for the transfers. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that P. ammophila grows primarily on sand dunes near marram grass, feeding saprotrophically on the decaying roots. It says the mushroom is most often found in stable, established dunes and dune slacks inland, and can sometimes be found near the shoreline within the littoral zone. It gives the Northern Hemisphere growing season as generally May to November, with mushrooms growing singly or in clumps. The same source describes the distribution as wide but sparse across Europe, with limited coastal records outside Europe in Algeria, New Zealand and Canada. GBIF's distribution entry lists Belgium (including the Flemish Region), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. This is single-cluster evidence: the iNaturalist entry copies Wikipedia. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
