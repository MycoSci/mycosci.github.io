## Summary

Gliophorus irrigatus (Pers.) A.M. Ainsw. & P.M. Kirk, 2013, is a species of waxcap in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the slimy waxcap. Wikipedia describes it as widespread in Europe, typically in grassland, and reports that DNA sequencing has shown North American records to refer to several similar species. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet comes from Latin "irrigatus" (watered or bedewed), with reference to the viscid coating of the fruit bodies. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described in 1801 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon as Agaricus irrigatus, was later combined in a number of genera, and was transferred to Hygrocybe in 1976. It says molecular research published in 2011 showed that it did not belong in Hygrocybe sensu stricto, and it was moved to Gliophorus in 2013. The 2013 Gliophorus treatment in the GBIF-supplied text says Persoon gave no collection details and that no original specimen, drawing or painting survives at Leiden or Uppsala. A neotype was therefore selected from a 2006 collection by David Boertmann in Northwest Jutland, Denmark. The same text says its authors found no evidence that G. irrigatus and G. unguinosus are separate species and that G. irrigatus has priority. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the slimy waxcap is distributed in Europe and the Caucasus, as far east as Georgia, and that like most other European waxcaps it grows in old, unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns). The neotype from Denmark was collected in dry grassland grazed by sheep. The GBIF-supplied text gives the distribution as Europe including the UK and southern Scandinavia, based on eDNA sequences and sequenced specimens, with a single eDNA occurrence in far eastern Canada. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
