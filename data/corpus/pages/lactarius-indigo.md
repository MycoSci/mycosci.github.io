## Summary

Lactarius indigo (Schwein.) Fr., 1838, the indigo milk cap, is an agaric in the family Russulaceae. The fruit body is blue, fading to a paler grayish or silvery blue with age. When cut or broken it exudes indigo-blue latex that slowly turns green in air. It is recorded mainly from eastern and southern North America, Mexico and Central America, and as far south as Colombia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet indigo is derived from the Latin word meaning 'indigo blue'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described in 1822 as Agaricus indigo by the American mycologist Lewis David de Schweinitz. Elias Magnus Fries transferred it to Lactarius in 1838. Otto Kuntze called it Lactifluus indigo in his 1891 Revisio Generum Plantarum, but others did not adopt the change. Hesler and Smith (1960) treated it as the type species of subsection Caerulei. In 1979 they instead placed it in subgenus Lactarius, based on latex colour and the colour changes after exposure to air. MushroomExpert.Com reports that DNA work by Nuytinck and collaborators (2006, 2007b) supported separating L. indigo from other members of section Deliciosi. That work included only two L. indigo specimens, and it was inconclusive on the status of var. diminutivus. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes L. indigo as a mycorrhizal fungus that grows on the ground, scattered or in groups, in both deciduous and coniferous forests. It also states that the species is commonly found in floodplain areas that have been recently submerged. MushroomExpert.Com gives the ecology as mycorrhizal with oaks and pines, growing alone, scattered or gregariously, in summer and fall. It describes the range as fairly widely distributed in North America from the northeast to the southwestern United States, Texas and Mexico, but apparently absent from the Pacific Northwest, the West Coast and the northern Rocky Mountains. Wikipedia adds that it is most common along the Gulf Coast, Mexico and Guatemala, and that it has been reported in Costa Rica (in oak-dominated forest) and Colombia. It also reports that in Europe it has so far been found only in southern France, and that it has been collected in China and India. In Mexico, Wikipedia notes associations with Mexican alder, American hornbeam, American hophornbeam and Liquidambar macrophylla. In Guatemala it associates with smooth-bark Mexican pine and other pine and oak species. A GBIF descriptive record for Colombia lists it as mycorrhizal, on Quercus humboldtii. For a study in the subtropical forests of Xalapa, Mexico, Wikipedia reports maximal fruiting during the rainy season, June to September. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
