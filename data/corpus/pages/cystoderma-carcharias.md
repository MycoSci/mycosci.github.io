## Summary

Cystoderma carcharias (Pers.) Fayod, described in 1889 as a transfer from Persoon's Agaricus carcharias, is recorded across Europe, North America and temperate Asia. Wikipedia describes a small agaric with a pale pink-tinged cap up to 6 cm across, a persistent ring on the stem, and a strong unpleasant odour. MushroomExpert.Com describes the cap as yellow-orange to brownish orange. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states the epithet carcharias is probably derived from the Greek karcharos, meaning sharp, pointed or jagged, and notes that karcharias is translated as shark. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Christian Hendrik Persoon first described the species as Agaricus carcharias in 1794, and that Victor Fayod assigned its current name in 1889. MushroomExpert.Com notes an epitype was designated from Sweden (Saar et al. 2009). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a litter saprotroph found singly or in groups on soil among grass or moss, frequently under conifers (notably spruce), in late summer and autumn, and also reports it on Macquarie Island. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered or gregariously under conifers in late summer and fall (over winter in California), widely distributed in Europe and in North America, primarily in northern and montane areas. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
