## Summary

Astraeus pteridis (Shear) Zeller, the giant hygroscopic earthstar, is a false earthstar in the order Boletales. Wikipedia describes it as larger than the European A. hygrometricus, reaching 5 to 15 cm or more when expanded, with rays that open in humid conditions and close in arid ones. Its source record is thin: a few encyclopedia lines and two papers on triterpene chemistry and mycorrhizal synthesis. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Cornelius Lott Shear described the species in 1902 as Scleroderma pteridis, and that Sanford Myron Zeller transferred it to Astraeus in 1948. It also reports that a 2013 molecular phylogenetic study applied the name A. pteridis to the larger Astraeus of the Pacific Northwest region of North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

A Planta Medica paper (2009) states the species is known only from western North America, occurring alone or in groups on the ground in forests of conifers, with which it forms mycorrhizal associations. Wikipedia reports it has also been found in the Canary Islands, Madeira and Argentina, and says it may be widely distributed or have been translocated. In a separate laboratory study (Canadian Journal of Botany, 1981), A. pteridis formed ectomycorrhizae with Alnus glutinosa, A. incana, A. rhombifolia and A. sinuata in pure culture syntheses; this is an experimental result, not a field host record. *[lit_2, wikipedia, lit_3]*
