## Summary

Mycena leptocephala (Pers.) Gillet, 1876, known as the nitrous bonnet, is a species of the family Mycenaceae. Wikipedia describes small, fragile mushrooms with conical grayish caps 1–3 cm across, gray widely spaced gills, a white spore print and a distinctive bleach-like odor; MushroomExpert.Com likewise describes a strong bleach-like odor and gives a cap size of 1–4 cm. It is recorded across Europe, North America and parts of Asia, and from many other countries. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet leptocephala derives from the Greek λεπτος (leptos, 'thin') and κεφαλη (kephale, 'head'), and refers to the delicate cap. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first named Agaricus leptocephalus by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1800 and was transferred to Mycena in 1876 by the French mycologist Claude-Casimir Gillet. The same source says that Mycena chlorinella (Singer, 1936) was reduced to synonymy in a 1980 publication by the Dutch Mycena specialist Maas Geesteranus, and that the species is placed in section Fragilipedes of Mycena. MushroomExpert.Com's reference line gives the same basionym authority and date, (Persoon, 1800) Gillet, 1876. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes M. leptocephala as saprobic, with fruit bodies scattered to gregarious on fallen sticks and on needle carpets under conifers. It says they are rather common in early summer and again in autumn. MushroomExpert.Com independently describes it as saprobic on conifer debris, growing scattered to gregariously on the ground, in spring and fall (or over winter in warmer climates). It calls the species widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia lists records from North America (Canada, the western and southeastern United States), Venezuela, Western Australia, several European countries, and parts of Asia including India, Korea and China. It also lists Arctic and alpine regions such as Iceland, Greenland and the Murmansk region. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
