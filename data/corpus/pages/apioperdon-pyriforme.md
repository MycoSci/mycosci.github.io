## Summary

Apioperdon pyriforme (Schaeff.) Vizzini (2017) is a puffball in the family Lycoperdaceae, known in English as the pear-shaped puffball or stump puffball. Wikipedia describes it as a saprobic fungus that grows on decaying wood and is the only species in the genus Apioperdon. It was long placed in Lycoperdon, and Lycoperdon pyriforme is a synonym. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet pyriforme is Latin for "pear-shaped". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the fungus was first described by Jacob Christian Schaeffer in 1774. In 2001, DNA evidence compiled by Dirk Krüger and other mycologists suggested that Lycoperdon was polyphyletic, with this species differing most from the rest of the group. Morphological differences, including the presence of rhizomorphs and a preference for wood, supported that finding. A 2003 publication moved the species to Morganella. In 2008 Larsson and Jeppson revisited the phylogeny of the Lycoperdaceae with broader sampling and kept it in Lycoperdon. It was renamed Apioperdon pyriforme in 2017, based on phylogenetic and morphological differences. A 2023 molecular study of Lycoperdaceae (Journal of Fungi) also recovered Apioperdon as a distinct clade. *[wikipedia, lit_2]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as growing on decaying wood. It gives the European season as July to December and the season in western North America as fall to winter. The iNaturalist taxon text is a declared derivative of Wikipedia, so it is not independent evidence. It adds that the species emerges in autumn and is common and abundant on decaying logs of both deciduous and coniferous wood. The GBIF description record lists a distribution of Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. A 2023 survey in the Journal of Fungi reported the species as a new addition to the Lycoperdaceae of Israel. *[wikipedia, inaturalist, gbif_desc, lit_2]*
