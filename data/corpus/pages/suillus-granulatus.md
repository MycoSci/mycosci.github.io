## Summary

Suillus granulatus (L.) Roussel is a pored mushroom in the family Suillaceae, known in English as the weeping bolete or granulated bolete. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe a sticky orange-brown cap, small pale yellow pores that exude milky droplets when young, and a stem with no ring. Both sources place it as a mycorrhizal associate of pines. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that Suillus is an ancient term for fungi derived from the word for "swine". It also states that granulatus means "grainy" and refers to the granular dots on the upper part of the stem. Both statements come from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Carl Linnaeus first described the species in 1753 as a Boletus, and that Henri François Anne de Roussel gave it its current name by transferring it to Suillus. Wikipedia adds that the name S. lactifluus ("oozing milk") was formerly applied to forms in which the granular dots are inconspicuous and do not darken with age. Wikipedia says the eastern North American mushroom once thought to be that species has been confirmed as the rediscovered Suillus weaverae. MushroomExpert.Com says the white-pine-associated mushroom called "Suillus granulatus" in North American field guides is S. weaverae, and that S. lactifluus is probably best treated as a synonym of S. weaverae. MushroomExpert.Com also says the true S. granulatus was originally described from Sweden by Linnaeus. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that S. granulatus grows with Pinus on both calcareous and acidic soils, sometimes in large numbers, and that it is native to the Northern Hemisphere. It also says the species has been introduced to South Africa, Hawaii, Australia, New Zealand, Argentina, Chile and the Falkland Islands, and is associated with Japanese red pine (Pinus densiflora) in South Korea. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with 2-needled pines, including Scots pine. It says the species grows alone or gregariously in woods or in urban locations near pines, from summer through fall, and is widespread and common in Europe and Asia. For North America, MushroomExpert.Com says the species is apparently present, probably as an introduction, in Minnesota with red pine, and possibly with other 2-needled pines. It says the prevalence and range of the true species in North America have not been established. The habitat details on this page come from these two sources. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
