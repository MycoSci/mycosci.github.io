## Summary

Crepidotus applanatus (Pers.) P. Kumm. 1871 is a species in the family Crepidotaceae, first described by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon in 1796. Sources describe it as a stemless, fan-shaped, pleurotoid mushroom with a brown spore print that grows on dead hardwood. Separating it from similar Crepidotus species generally requires microscopic study. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states it was first described in 1796 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon and renamed by Paul Kummer in 1871. MushroomExpert.Com adds that it was originally described from Europe (Persoon 1796) and neotypified from Scotland (Senn-Irlet 1995). It also notes that the current species concepts for C. applanatus, C. malachius and C. pini are fairly recent (Jančovičová et al. 2022) and may not match older field guides. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing gregariously or (more commonly) in overlapping clusters on dead hardwood stumps and logs in summer and fall. It reports the species as widespread in Eurasia, widely distributed in North America east of the Great Plains, and present in the Pacific Northwest. The illustrated collections are from Illinois. Wikipedia likewise gives deciduous wood as the substrate. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
