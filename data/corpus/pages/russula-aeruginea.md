## Summary

Russula aeruginea Lindblad ex Fr. (1863) is a species of Russulaceae with the English names Green Brittlegill and grass green russula, and the type locality is Sweden. Sources describe an evenly green cap with a creamy to yellowish or cream-yellow spore print. MushroomExpert.Com, whose account is based on North American material, describes it as widely distributed there, and Wikipedia describes it as widely distributed in northern temperate regions. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet aeruginea is derived from the Latin aeruginus, referring to the tarnished color of copper. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia gives the first description as Elias Magnus Fries's 1863 work Monographia Hymenomycetum Sueciae. Wikispecies cites the same work, volume 2, page 198, and credits the name to Fries alone. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow on the ground in woods, in troops in leaf litter or in grass. It describes the species as ectomycorrhizal with birch and also found under conifers, particularly pine and spruce. It gives fruiting as July to November in Europe and late summer to autumn in North America. MushroomExpert.Com (North America) describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall. Mushroom World says it grows alone or in scattered groups on soil under birch. The sources differ somewhat in the hosts they name. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
