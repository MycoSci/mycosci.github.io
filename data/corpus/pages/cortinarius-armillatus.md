## Summary

Cortinarius armillatus (Fr.) Fr. 1838 is a webcap in the family Cortinariaceae, recorded in Europe and North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with birch and identifies it by the two to four bright orange-red bands encircling its swollen-based stem. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com reports that a 2011 investigation by Niskanen and collaborators of about 1,000 armillatus-like specimens from northern Europe found support for six separate species. Three of these are known to occur in North America: Cortinarius armillatus, C. luteo-ornatus and C. paragaudis. The same source says C. armillatus is distinguished by orange-red rather than pinkish or wine-red bracelets, comparatively long, ellipsoid, thick-walled spores, and an exclusive association with birch rather than conifers. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that when the species is used to dye cloth, it gives pink without added metals, yellow with tin, green with copper and olive shades with iron. This is a single-source claim. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as mycorrhizal with birch, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall, and widely distributed in northern North America. It cautions that the birch association can be hard to assess in mixed northern forests. Wikipedia reports it from August to September under birch in North America (especially the northeast) and in Europe. The sources differ on forest type: iNaturalist describes it as usually found in moist coniferous forests, especially spruced ones, into October. Mushroom World describes it growing solitary or clustered on soil in mixed hardwood-conifer forests, especially spruced ones. The sources also differ on frequency in North America: MushroomExpert.Com calls it a common mushroom in northern forests where birch appears, while iNaturalist says it grows rarely in North America but is common in Europe. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, inaturalist, mushroomworld]*
