## Summary

Amanita calyptroderma G.F. Atk. & V.G. Ballen (1909) is a species in the family Amanitaceae, with California as its type locality. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a white-spored mushroom with an orange-brown to yellowish-brown cap bearing a thick white universal-veil patch, a skirt-like ring on the stem, and a sac-like volva at the base. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species occurs in conifer forests of the western United States. It forms mycorrhizae with madrone (Arbutus menziesii) in the southern part of its range (Central California northwards to Washington). In the northern part of its range (Washington to southern Canada), it says the preferred host is Douglas-fir (Pseudotsuga menziesii). It gives the fruiting season as fall, winter and spring. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently describes it as mycorrhizal with Douglas-fir in northern Oregon, Washington and British Columbia, and primarily with Pacific madrone in southern Oregon and northern California. It reports the mushroom growing alone, scattered or gregariously, in fall and winter. The two sources differ slightly on where the host switch occurs and on whether spring fruiting occurs. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
