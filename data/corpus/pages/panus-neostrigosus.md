## Summary

Panus neostrigosus Drechsler-Santos & Wartchow (2012) is a species in the family Panaceae (order Polyporales), known in English as the hairy oyster mushroom. Wikipedia describes it as found nearly worldwide in moderate environments. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a wood-decaying, gilled polypore with a densely hairy cap and a frequently off-center stem. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia explains the species epithet neostrigosus as "new strigosus": neo- meaning "new", and strigosus taken from Lentinus strigosus, the taxon on which the new name was based. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the name Panus neostrigosus was formally described in 2012 as a new taxon, to resolve taxonomic confusion between Lentinus strigosus and Lentinus lecomtei, which had been accepted as distinct species and later treated as one taxon. It adds that some authors accept this approach and others do not. MushroomExpert.Com says the epithet strigosus was problematic because of competition from an entirely different "Panus strigosus" named by Berkeley & Curtis in 1859 (now generally known as Pleurotus levis), and that Drechsler-Santos and collaborators (2012) provided the new name to clear up the confusion. MushroomExpert.Com also lists Panus rudis, Lentinus rudis and Panus lecomtei as synonyms, and notes that Pegler (1983) synonymized the first two with Panus/Lentinus strigosus. It says the species was originally described from Sweden (Fries 1838) and from Pennsylvania (Berkeley & Curtis 1859). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes P. neostrigosus as saprobic on the wood of recently dead hardwoods, causing a white rot, usually growing gregariously or in clusters, from spring through fall (and over winter in warm climates). It reports the species as widely distributed in Europe and North America, and also reported from the Caribbean, Central America, South America, Africa, Asia and Oceania; its illustrated collections came from Colorado, Illinois and Ohio. Wikipedia independently describes it as a general saprotroph on decomposing or recently burned wood, in cultivated areas such as amid stubble, in places exposed to the sun, and in tropical forests. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
