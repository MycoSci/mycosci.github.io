## Summary

Lactarius torminosus (Schaeff.) Pers., the woolly milkcap, is a species of Russulaceae in the order Russulales. A single encyclopedia cluster (Wikipedia and its derivative) describes it as a large agaric with a pinkish, zonate, shaggy-margined cap and white, unchanging acrid latex. That same cluster reports that it is highly irritating to the digestive system when eaten raw, and that some authors call it outright poisonous. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Per Wikipedia, the specific epithet torminosus means "tormenting" or "causing colic", in reference to the gastrointestinal distress associated with consuming the raw mushroom. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Jacob Christian Schäffer first described the species in 1774 in the genus Agaricus, and that Samuel Frederick Gray transferred it to Lactarius in 1821. It also states that L. torminosus became the type species of Lactarius in 2011, after molecular studies led to a reorganization of Russulaceae genera. This is a single-cluster account. The spine gives the authorship as (Schaeff.) Pers. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that, despite the toxicity reports, the mushrooms are prepared in Finland, Russia and other northern and eastern European countries by parboiling, soaking in brine for several days, or pickling, and are valued for their peppery taste. It also reports that they are harvested for commercial sale in Finland. This is a single-cluster report and is not a preparation recommendation. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a mycorrhizal species that fruits on the ground, scattered or in groups, in mixed forests. It is reported in association with birch (Betula) and hemlock (Tsuga), and sometimes with aspen (Populus) in North America. It is also reported in urban settings near birch trees. A Scottish field study found it more likely in older than in younger birch woodland. Mushroom World independently describes it as mycorrhizal with birch and often found in open, grassy sites. Wikipedia gives the range as northern temperate and boreal climates, sometimes subarctic, recorded from North Africa, northern Asia and Europe, and common in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
