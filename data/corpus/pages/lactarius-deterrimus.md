## Summary

Lactarius deterrimus Gröger (1968) is a species of Russulaceae, known in English as false saffron milkcap or orange milkcap. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe orange caps that develop green stains with age or injury, and orange latex that turns reddish to maroon after about 10 minutes or more. Both describe it as mycorrhizal with spruce. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet deterrimus is Latin, the superlative of "dēterior" ("less good"), meaning "the worst, the poorest". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was not validly described until 1968, by the German mycologist Frieder Gröger, even though it is one of the commonest fungi in Central Europe. Before that it was treated as a variety of L. deliciosus (var. piceus, Smotlacha). After L. semisanguifluus was described by Heim and Leclair in 1950, this fungus was referred to under that name. L. fennoscandicus was separated from L. deterrimus in 1998 by Verbeken and Vesterholt and treated as a separate species. Wikispecies lists Lactarius deliciosus var. deterrimus (Gröger) Hesler & A.H. Sm. (1979), L. deliciosus var. piceus Smotl. (1947) and var. piceae Vassilkov (1948) among the synonyms, and also lists L. semisanguifluus sensu Lange (1940) and sensu Neuhoff (1956) as taxonomic synonyms. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with spruces, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall, throughout Europe wherever spruces occur. Wikipedia states that a strict mycorrhizal specificity with Norway spruce was traditionally assumed, and that in 2006 an arbutoid mycorrhiza with bearberry (Arctostaphylos uva-ursi) was reported. Wikipedia also describes it as common in spruce-fir and spruce-moorland forests, spruce forests and plantations, and in young spruce stands 10 to 20 years old. It also reports the fungus in European beech and oak-hornbeam forests, forest edges, clearings and parkland. Wikipedia says fruit bodies appear from late June to November, usually August to October, and from July to November in the UK. It says the fungus probably favours calcareous soil but has been found on nearly every soil type, and that heavily eutrophic soils are unsuitable. Mushroom World says it grows under conifers, particularly spruce. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
