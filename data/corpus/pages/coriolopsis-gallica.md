## Summary

Coriolopsis gallica (Fr.) Ryvarden, 1973, is a polypore in the family Polyporaceae, known in English as the brownflesh bracket. Wikipedia describes it as a fungus growing on decaying wood that degrades wood components, mainly lignin, which makes it a white-rot fungus of biotechnological interest. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a bracket with a densely hairy cap, gray to gray-brown angular pores and rusty brown flesh. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows on decaying wood and is not associated with any plant disease. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo), the only source giving detail, describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of willows and cottonwoods, and occasionally other hardwoods, causing a white rot. The same page also says it is parasitic on apple trees in orchards, at least in Colorado, citing E. C. Smith (1930); this conflicts with Wikipedia's statement. That page describes it as growing alone or gregariously, annual, appearing in summer and fall (and over winter in warm climates). It is described as widely distributed in North America but apparently absent from the southeastern United States. These habitat details come from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
