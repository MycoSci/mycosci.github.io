## Summary

Paragalactinia succosa (Berk.) Van Vooren (2020), the Yellowing Cup, is a cup fungus in the family Pezizaceae. MushroomExpert.Com treats it as a species group whose flesh contains a juice that stains fingers and cup surfaces bright yellow. GBIF's description record lists it for Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the species was described as Peziza succosa in the 19th century from Great Britain (Berkeley 1841) and was recently combined into Paragalactinia (van Vooren 2020). The same source says European authors separate it from the very similar P. succosella on morphology (P. succosella has smaller mature fruiting bodies, up to 2 cm across, and slightly smaller spores that are mostly uniguttulate during development rather than biguttulate). Michael Kuo reports that his North American collections do not fit these European concepts, and he quotes Pfister and collaborators (2022) as calling P. succosa and P. succosella a complex awaiting resolution. He suggests the North American members of the group may be as yet unnamed. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes the group as probably mycorrhizal with hardwoods. It says the fungus grows gregariously or in small clusters on bare soil or in moss, often in damp areas and along trails, from spring through fall. Kuo describes it as widely distributed as a species group in Europe, North America and South America, and also reported from India. His illustrated collections are from Illinois and Ohio. The hardwood association is stated only as 'probably' and comes from this one source. A GBIF description record separately lists Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
