## Summary

Geastrum rufescens (Pers., 1794), the rosy earthstar, is a species in the family Geastraceae, with its type locality in Germany. MushroomExpert.Com describes a fairly large earthstar measuring up to about 8 cm across with the arms spread open. Its 6–8 arms are pinkish to pink and thick-fleshed, and the mouth of the spore case is indistinct rather than grooved or puckered. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Sources differ in detail, and each is a single-source statement. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously under hardwoods or conifers, usually appearing in fall, winter and spring, and widely distributed in North America; the collections it illustrates are from Illinois and Indiana. Wikipedia states that it is found in Europe, North America (including Mexico) and Japan, where it typically grows at the base of old oak stumps. Mushroom World gives a summer-to-fall season and says it is often found near decaying hardwood stumps in light soil. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
