## Summary

Onnia tomentosa (Fr.) P. Karst. (1889) is a species of fungus in the family Hymenochaetaceae, commonly called the woolly velvet polypore, velvet rosette or tomentosus root rot. Sources describe it as a root-associated fungus of conifers, chiefly spruce, that causes a white rot. Wikipedia treats it as a plant pathogen, and MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on buried roots or wood. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states the species was originally described from Sweden and lists Inonotus tomentosus and Pelloporus tomentosus as synonyms. Wikipedia states it was formerly known as Inonotus tomentosum (Fr.) Teng until molecular phylogenetic analysis led to major revisions in the classification of the Hymenochaetaceae. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states it is frequently found in coniferous forests at higher altitudes, often growing in large groups, and is rather rare at lower altitudes. It says the fungus causes tomentosus root rot, primarily in spruce, and that in Canada it attacks most commercially important conifers, including white spruce. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on the buried roots or wood of conifers, primarily spruces but occasionally pines, causing a white rot. It says the fungus is annual and usually grows alone or gregariously on the ground while attached to tree roots. It fruits in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates, and is widely distributed in Europe, Asia and North America. The two sources differ in framing: Wikipedia calls it a plant pathogen and MushroomExpert.Com calls it saprobic. MushroomExpert.Com's illustrated collection is from Pennsylvania. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
