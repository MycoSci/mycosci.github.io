## Summary

Caloscypha fulgens (Pers.) Boud. is a cup fungus in the family Caloscyphaceae (order Pezizales), and the only species in the monotypic genus Caloscypha. It has an orange interior and outer surfaces that discolor blue to green when bruised or old. Its known common names include golden cup and spring orange peel fungus. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet fulgens as meaning "bright colored" and the genus name Caloscypha as meaning "beautiful cup". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the fungus's distinctive orange-yellow color earned it the common names "spring orange peel fungus", "golden cup" and "dazzling cup". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1822 as Peziza fulgens and has since been placed in several different genera. Wikispecies lists the basionym as Peziza fulgens Pers., Mycologia europaea 1: 241 (1822), and the current combination as Caloscypha fulgens (Pers.) Boud., Histoire et classification des Discomycètes d'Europe: 54 (1907). Its homotypic synonyms include Pseudoplectania, Aleuria, Scypharia, Otidella, Barlaea, Plicariella, Detonia and Lamprospora combinations. Wikispecies also lists Geniculodendron pyriforme G.A. Salt (1974) as a heterotypic synonym. Wikipedia identifies this name with the asexual (conidial) stage of the fungus. Wikipedia adds that the family Caloscyphaceae was described in 2002 to contain the monotypic genus Caloscypha; from 1968 the genus had been placed in the Pyronemataceae. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says that in North America the species is usually found on the ground in forest litter (duff) near or under conifers, fruiting in early spring after snow melt. It reports North American occurrence only between March and July, and describes it as widespread in the Rocky Mountains and the Pacific Northwest. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently describes it as found under conifers across North America, much more frequently west of the Rockies. That source says it grows gregariously or in clusters in duff on the ground, usually in low-lying, wet areas such as conifer bogs, and in the west often where mountain snowbanks have recently melted. It gives the season as spring and early summer. Both sources describe an asexual stage that acts as a pathogen of conifer seeds. Wikipedia names dormant Sitka spruce (Picea sitchensis) seeds, and MushroomExpert.Com says spruce and pine seeds, usually within decaying cones. Wikipedia also lists collections from Britain, Japan, Sweden, the Netherlands and Turkey. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
