## Summary

Dissoderma odoratum (Cool) I. Saar & Thorn is a species in the family Squamanitaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a strong-smelling species with small purple sporocarps that parasitises the sporocarps of Hebeloma mesophaeum. It is mostly recorded in Europe, with a single North American record from Washington state, and is described as very rare. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was first described by Catharina Cool in 1918 as Lepiota odorata, from material found in the Netherlands in 1915. It was placed in Squamanita as Squamanita odorata in 1946, when Emil J. Imbach described that genus. In 2022 Squamanita was split because phylogenetic analysis showed the genus to be paraphyletic, and the species was moved to Dissoderma. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species grows in clusters on the sporocarps of Hebeloma mesophaeum, so its habitat depends on the presence of that host. The host is described as usually growing with pine, spruce, beech, willow or birch, in habitats including deciduous and coniferous forests, sand dunes, parks and gardens. A significant part of observations come from human-modified areas such as path edges and lawns. In Switzerland the species was recorded at the same place for more than 13 years. Wikipedia reports that it is not known whether the fungus also grows on other Hebeloma species. *[wikipedia]*
