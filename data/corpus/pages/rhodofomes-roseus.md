## Summary

Rhodofomes roseus (Alb. & Schwein.) Kotl. & Pouzar (1990) is a polypore in the family Fomitopsidaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a tough perennial with a pink pore surface and convex to hoof-shaped caps, and both it and Wikipedia say it causes a brown cubical rot. MushroomExpert.Com treats it as very similar to Rhodofomes cajanderi and gives ways to tell the two apart. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Boletus roseus Alb. & Schwein., published in Consp. fung. in 1805, and MushroomExpert.Com says the species was originally described from Germany. Wikispecies lists later transfers to Polyporus (Fries, 1818), Fomes (Fries, 1849), Fomitopsis (P. Karst., 1881), Trametes (P. Karst., 1881), Placodes (Quélet, 1886), Scindalma (Kuntze, 1898) and Ungulina (Patouillard, 1900), before Kotlaba & Pouzar placed it in Rhodofomes in 1990. It also lists Polyporus rufopallidus Trog (1832) and its later combinations as heterotypic synonyms. The supplied sources do not say why any of these transfers were made. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as saprobic on the deadwood of conifers and, rarely, hardwoods. It says the species is also sometimes parasitic on living trees, causes a brown cubical rot, and grows alone or gregariously. The same source says that in many areas (the Rocky Mountains, the West Coast, the Alps) it is more likely to appear on spruce wood at higher elevations, though this is not always the case. It describes the range as widely distributed in Europe, and in North America from Canada through Mexico, especially in northern and montane areas including the Appalachians. It also reports the species from the Caribbean, Central America, Asia and Oceania. Wikipedia describes it as growing in western North America, most often in spruce forests, and states that it grows exclusively on dead wood, with a preference for logs of Picea, Pseudotsuga or Populus. This conflicts with MushroomExpert.Com on whether it is ever parasitic on living trees. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
