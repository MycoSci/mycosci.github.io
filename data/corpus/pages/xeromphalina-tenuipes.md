## Summary

Xeromphalina tenuipes (Schwein.) A.H. Sm. (1953) is a species in the family Mycenaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small mushroom with orangish to yellowish brown caps 2.5–4.5 cm across and a finely fuzzy, golden orangish stem that darkens to reddish brown or brown from the base upward, with distinctive microscopic features (pileocystidia and a turf of caulocystidia on the stem). *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) states that the species was originally described from Pennsylvania (Schweinitz 1832). The supplied sources give no account of the material or of later transfers between genera. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on the deadwood and woody debris of hardwoods, growing scattered to gregariously or in loose clusters. It is often found on fallen logs and also in terrestrial troops alongside the logs and woody debris, in late spring and early summer. That source gives its distribution as widely across North America east of the Rocky Mountains and southward through Mexico, and also reports it from the Caribbean, Central America, South America, Africa and eastern Asia; its illustrated collections are from Indiana and Ohio. A GBIF-hosted record for a Colombian collection (Tolima, 3000–3100 m a.s.l.) gives the substrate as wood and the habit as solitary, grouped or cespitose. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
