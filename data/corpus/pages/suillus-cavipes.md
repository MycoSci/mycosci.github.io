## Summary

Suillus cavipes (Klotzsch) A.H. Sm. & Thiers 1964 is a bolete in the family Suillaceae, known in English as the Hollow Bolete. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a Eurasian species found under European larch (Larix decidua), with a dry, densely hairy brown cap, a yellow pore surface, and a stem that is almost always hollow when mature. The same source reports DNA support for the North American material usually called by this name being a separate species, Suillus ampliporus. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the epithet cavipes as Latin for 'hollow foot' and says it refers to the hollow stem. MushroomExpert.Com likewise glosses cav-ipes as 'hollow-stem', in reference to the stem being almost always hollow when mature. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com cites the name to J. F. Klotzsch (1835) and A. H. Smith & H. D. Thiers (1964), and says the species was originally described from, and neotypified from, Austria. It lists Boletinus cavipes as a synonym and mentions a yellow form, Suillus cavipes aureus, under European larch that is otherwise identical. It also says that although 'Suillus cavipes' appears in many North American field guides, Nguyen and collaborators (2016) provide DNA support for the North American species, associated with tamarack and western larch, being a separate species, best named Suillus ampliporus. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (a single source) describes it as mycorrhizal with larch (Larix), especially Larix decidua, growing alone or gregariously in fall. It gives the distribution as northern and montane Eurasia, wherever the host trees occur, and the illustrated collection as from Alpine Italy. Wikipedia separately states that it is found in Europe and North America and is associated with larch in the Pacific Northwest. MushroomExpert.Com disputes whether North American material belongs to this species (see discovery_and_naming). *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
