## Summary

Phaeocollybia christinae (Fr.) R. Heim, 1931, is a species in the family Hymenogastraceae, known in English as Christina's rootshank. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as having a brownish cap with a pointed umbo and a long stem that extends deeply into the soil. It reports the species from Europe and eastern North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that Fries named the species after his wife. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described in 1838 by the Swedish mycologist Elias Magnus Fries as Agaricus christinae, and Roger Heim transferred it to Phaeocollybia in 1931. This account comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow scattered or in groups, deeply rooted in moss or leaf litter, and that it typically grows in sandy soil. It says the species is suspected to be mycorrhizal and usually appears under conifer trees, particularly spruce, in Europe and eastern North America. The GBIF description record lists a distribution of Denmark, Norway and Sweden.
