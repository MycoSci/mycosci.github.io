## Summary

Cortinarius cyanites Fr. (1838) is a species in the family Cortinariaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a Cortinarius of the subgenus Phlegmacium with a violet-toned, slimy cap, purple flesh that turns blood red when cut or bruised, and a bulbous pale purple stipe. It is found in mixed coniferous and deciduous forests in northern and central Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name is derived from the Ancient Greek cyanos, "dark blue". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Elias Magnus Fries described the species as Cortinarius cyanites in his 1838 Epicrisis Systematis Mycologici seu Synopsis Hymenomycetum. It places the species in subgenus Phlegmacium, section Cyanites. A 2014 genetic analysis found that C. subcyanites and C. pseudocyanites lay within the concept of C. cyanites. The same analysis found three distinct lineages, and two new species, C. boreicyanites and C. violaceorubens, were described from them. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology
