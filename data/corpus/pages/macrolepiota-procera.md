## Summary

Macrolepiota procera (Scop.) Singer, 1948, the parasol mushroom, is a basidiomycete in the family Agaricaceae. Wikipedia describes it as having a large, prominent fruiting body resembling a parasol. GBIF holds occurrence records for it from many countries, mostly in Europe, and MushroomExpert.Com notes that North American collections going under this name may represent other, undescribed species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that in some parts of Europe the species is known as the "snake's hat" or "snake's sponge" because of its scales. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the fungus was first described in 1772 by the Italian naturalist Giovanni Antonio Scopoli as Agaricus procerus, and that Rolf Singer transferred it to Macrolepiota in 1948. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) adds that several North American "parasol mushrooms" go under the name of this Eurasian species. It says many, if not all, of them are undescribed and unnamed, and it asks readers to help document them. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as fairly common on well-drained soils, found solitary or in groups and fairy rings in pastures and occasionally in woodland. It calls the species widespread in temperate regions of Eurasia and possibly North America, and says further research is needed to confirm whether North American specimens are the same species. For the United Kingdom it gives a fruiting period of July through November. Mushroom World gives growth on soil in open grassy places and in mixed woods, solitary, scattered or clustered. MushroomExpert.Com describes North American "parasol mushrooms" as tending to grow alone or scattered in late summer, in woods or at their edges, or in pastures, often on trails and other disturbed ground. Kuo says these may not all be the same species as M. procera, so that account may not apply to it in the strict sense. A GBIF description record lists the habit as solitary and the substrate as soil. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
