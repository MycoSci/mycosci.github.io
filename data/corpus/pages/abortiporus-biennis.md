## Summary

Abortiporus biennis (Bull.) Singer, 1944, commonly called the blushing rosette, is a species in the family Podoscyphaceae (order Polyporales). GBIF holds occurrence records from about 40 countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes two distinct forms, a conventional brown-capped polypore and a gnarled "aborted" form made up mostly of irregular pores. In the supplied literature it also appears as a laboratory organism: an oxalate oxidase was identified in its mycelial extracts, and it has been used in fungal pretreatment of willow sawdust and in submerged fermentation for exopolysaccharide production. *[mushroomexpert, lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Discovery and naming

The basionym is Boletus biennis Bull., 1790. MushroomExpert.Com notes at least 55 taxonomic synonyms for Abortiporus, including many species and genus names. It suggests this is probably at least partly because the two forms look so different and are hard to reconcile with each other and with other polypores. It also suggests that the species produces both sexual spores and asexual chlamydospores, which led to separate names for anamorphic forms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to MushroomExpert.Com (a single source), the species is saprobic on the wood of hardwoods and occasionally conifers. It grows alone or gregariously around the bases of stumps and living trees. It causes a white rot in deadwood and a white trunk rot in living wood. It fruits in summer and fall (also winter and spring in warm climates) and is described as widely distributed in North America. The collections illustrated and described there are from Illinois and Missouri. *[mushroomexpert]*
