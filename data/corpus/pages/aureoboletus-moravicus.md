## Summary

Aureoboletus moravicus (Vacek) Klofac, 2010, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, commonly called the tawny bolete. Wikipedia describes it as an uncommon species found in Europe, listed as vulnerable on some European Red Lists and considered critically endangered in the Czech Republic. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described as Boletus moravicus by Václav Vacek in 1946 and was transferred to the genus Aureoboletus by Wolfgang Klofac in 2010. Wikispecies lists Klofac's 2010 treatment of the genus (Österreichische Zeitschrift für Pilzkunde 19: 133–174) as the reference for the combination, and lists Xerocomus moravicus (Vacek) Herink (1964) and Boletus leonis D.A. Reid (1966) among the synonyms. No supplied source explains the reasons for the transfer. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Single-source account (Wikipedia; iNaturalist reproduces its opening text). Wikipedia states the species is confined to warm-temperate deciduous woodland on volcanic (andesitic) substrates with very acidic to mildly acidic soils (pH 3.9–6.3). It reports Slovak populations in the Štiavnica Mountains, the Kremnica Mountains and the Zvolenská kotlina basin, at 210–518 m elevation, in xerophilous oak-dominated stands (Quercus cerris, Q. petraea, Q. robur), often mixed with hornbeam (Carpinus betulus) and occasionally beech (Fagus sylvatica). Fruiting bodies are said to appear singly or in small groups from July to October, typically on south- or south-west-facing slopes in thermophilous forest. Wikipedia also says the species is known only from scattered localities in central and southern temperate Europe and is red-listed or legally protected in several countries, including Slovakia, the Czech Republic, Denmark, Bulgaria, Switzerland, Croatia and Austria. *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*
