## Summary

Polyozellus multiplex (Underw.) Murrill, 1910, is a member of the Thelephoraceae, with records in the US, Canada, Japan, Korea, China, Russia and Estonia. Sources describe its fruit bodies as clusters of vase- or spoon-shaped caps in blue, purple and blackish tones, and it is called the blue chanterelle even though it is not placed in Cantharellus. Compounds isolated from it, including polyozellin, kynapcin-12 and polyozellic acid, have been studied in laboratory assays. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name is derived from the Greek poly, 'many', and oz, 'branch', and that the epithet multiplex means 'in many pieces', referring to the compound nature of the fruiting body. This derivation comes from that one source only. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Cantharellus multiplex Underw. 1899, published in Underwood's paper 'A new Cantharellus from Maine' (Bulletin of the Torrey Botanical Club 26(5): 254). It lists the combination in Polyozellus by Murrill (1910), and other combinations Craterellus multiplex (Underw.) Shope 1938 and Thelephora multiplex (Underw.) S.Kawam. 1954. It also lists Phyllocarbon yasudai Lloyd 1921 as a heterotypic synonym. Wikipedia states that the genus was long treated as monotypic, and that recent molecular work (Wikipedia places the split in 2018) divided the species complex into five species, so the name now applies to only one of them. *[wikispecies, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially spruces and firs, in low, wet areas. It says the fungus usually grows in dense, fused clusters in northern and montane North America (from the Pacific Northwest to Maine, and in the Rocky Mountains), in summer and fall. Wikipedia describes an ectomycorrhizal species in coniferous woods under spruce and fir, more frequent at higher elevations and most often encountered in summer and fall. Wikipedia also says North American collections are still being re-catalogued after the split into five species. The MushroomExpert.Com page (2015) predates that split, so it is unclear which of these ecology and range statements apply to P. multiplex in the strict sense. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
