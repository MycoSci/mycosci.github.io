## Summary

Inocybe praetervisa Quél. (1883) is a small yellow-brown mushroom in the family Inocybaceae. Wikipedia describes it as distinguished within Inocybe by its nodulose spores and a marginate bulb at the stem base. It is recorded from Europe, North America and Asia. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet praetervisa as coming from a Latin word meaning "overlooked". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Quélet first described the species in the first volume of Bresadola's 1883 Fungi tridentini. Schröter moved it to Astrosporina in 1889. That genus is no longer recognised, and Astrosporina praetervisa is now treated as a synonym. Its nodulose spores once defined Astrosporina, and I. praetervisa was that genus's type species. A 2016 type study in Mycologia examined three authentic and topotypic specimens in the Bresadola herbarium and found that the name I. praetervisa has often been misapplied. It designated an epitype in support of a lectotype, and it treated I. rivularis as a later synonym of I. praetervisa. Another species, I. phaeocystidiosa, is named there as the one most often misdetermined as I. praetervisa. *[wikipedia, lit_2]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes I. praetervisa as ectomycorrhizal, growing on the ground in mixed, deciduous or even coniferous woodland, and typically favouring beech. It fruits solitarily or in trooping groups in late summer and through autumn, and it is described as not commonly encountered. Wikipedia gives its range as Europe, Asia and North America. A 2018 study in Mycoscience states that I. praetervisa occurs in mountainous mixed coniferous forests in Southern Europe and is not confirmed from Northern Europe. That study was written before, and treats differently, the species delimitation of the 2016 Mycologia study. Because the name has often been misapplied (see discovery_and_naming), older habitat and distribution statements may refer to other species of the group. *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_2]*
