## Summary

Tricholoma album (Schaeff.) P. Kumm., the white knight, is an all-white mushroom in the genus Tricholoma, family Tricholomataceae. It has a white cap and gills and a whitish stipe with no ring. It is recorded across Europe, and has been reported from India, while its presence in North America is unconfirmed. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the generic name derives from the Greek trichos 'hair' and loma 'hem', 'fringe' or 'border', and that the specific epithet is the Latin adjective albus 'white'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described as Agaricus albus by Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1774, was reclassified as Gyrophila alba by Lucien Quélet in 1886, and was given its current binomial by Paul Kummer in 1871. The same source says Marcel Bon named the variety Tricholoma album var. thalliophilum for mushrooms that stain blue-green with thallium oxide and sulfoformol, and that some later authors have questioned the taxonomic value of that characteristic. It also notes that the species is placed in section Lasciva, with an older classification placing it in section Inamoena. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is found across Europe, with fruit bodies appearing between August and December in association with oak (Quercus), with which it forms a mycorrhizal relationship. It can be found growing in sizeable fairy rings. The same source says its presence in North America has not been confirmed and that it was reported from India in 2010. GBIF lists distribution records for Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
