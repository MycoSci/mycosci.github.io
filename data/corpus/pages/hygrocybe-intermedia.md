## Summary

Hygrocybe intermedia (Pass.) Fayod is a gilled mushroom in the family Hygrophoraceae, given the recommended English name fibrous waxcap. According to Wikipedia, it has a European distribution and occurs mainly in agriculturally unimproved grassland. Threats to that habitat have led to it being assessed as globally "vulnerable" on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described from Italy in 1872 by Giovanni Passerini as Hygrophorus intermedius, and that Victor Fayod transferred it to the genus Hygrocybe in 1889. The same source reports that molecular (DNA-sequence cladistic) research has shown H. intermedia to be distinct and to belong in Hygrocybe sensu stricto. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as widespread but generally rare throughout Europe, and says that, like other European waxcaps, it typically occurs in old, agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns). The same source notes that recent research suggests waxcaps in general are neither mycorrhizal nor saprotrophic but may be associated with mosses; this is stated about waxcaps as a group, not specifically about this species. The GBIF description record lists its distribution as Belgium (Flemish and Walloon Regions), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. Both the grassland and the European-range statements come from a single independent source cluster (Wikipedia); the GBIF record gives distribution only. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
