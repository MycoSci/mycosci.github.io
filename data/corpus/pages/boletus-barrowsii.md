## Summary

Boletus barrowsii Thiers & A.H. Sm. (1976) is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as Barrow's Bolete and White King Bolete, with a type locality in Arizona. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as an impressive, whitish member of the Boletus edulis group with a robust, finely reticulate stem and a non-bruising pore surface. Its flesh is white and does not turn blue when sliced, and it is associated with Pinus ponderosa. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the English name "white king bolete" refers to the species' pale-coloured cap. No supplied source explains the name "Barrow's Bolete". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was described in 1976 by the American mycologists Harry D. Thiers and Alexander H. Smith. Their material was a specimen collected near Jacob Lake, Arizona, on August 21, 1971, by the amateur mycologist Charles "Chuck" Barrows, who had studied the mushroom in New Mexico. It had previously been held to be a white colour form of B. edulis. Wikipedia also reports that a 2010 molecular study found B. barrowsii to be sister to a lineage that gave rise to B. quercophilus of Costa Rica and B. nobilissimus of eastern North America. This account rests on a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as ectomycorrhizal, found under Pinus ponderosa (ponderosa pine) inland and under Quercus agrifolia (coast live oak) closer to the west coast. It reports that fruit bodies appear after rain and are more abundant if the rain comes in early autumn rather than later in the year. Wikipedia gives the range as Arizona and New Mexico, also Colorado, west into California and north to British Columbia. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently describes it as mycorrhizal with ponderosa pine, and occasionally with spruces, in the Southwest and Colorado, and with coast live oak on the West Coast. It says the fruit bodies grow alone, scattered or gregariously. Kuo gives the season as summer and fall (monsoon season) in the Southwest, and fall and winter on the West Coast. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
