## Summary

Pholiota aurivella (Batsch) P. Kumm., the golden pholiota or golden scalycap, is a species of Strophariaceae. It is described as having a bright to golden yellow, viscid cap with darker scales, and it grows in clusters on wood. Wikipedia reports that most field guides list it as inedible. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Discovery and naming

A 1998 paper in Czech Mycology re-examined the delimitation of Pholiota aurivella and Pholiota adiposa, based on fresh and herbarium material from the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Sweden, Finland and Austria. It concluded that the true P. aurivella grows on Salix, with a subviscid to dry, matt cap surface and thick dark scales. It designated a new neotype for P. aurivella in place of the one chosen by Jacobsson, which conflicted with Fries' protologue. It also found the holotype of Pholiota cerifera identical with P. aurivella. This is a single-paper treatment, and other sources supplied here (Wikipedia, Mushroom World) describe the species' growth on wood more broadly. *[lit_2]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species grows in clusters on live or dead trees, is found in native forest of New Zealand, southern Canada and the United States, and is frequently found in the American West and Southwest, especially in late summer and fall. Mushroom World gives a similar account, saying it typically grows on decaying wood such as stumps or fallen trees, in temperate regions of Europe and North America. The 1998 Czech Mycology paper reports that the true P. aurivella, as it redefines the species, grows on Salix. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, lit_2]*
