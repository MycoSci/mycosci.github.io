## Summary

Hypholoma capnoides (Fr.) P. Kumm. (1871), the conifer tuft, is a member of the Strophariaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as fruiting in clusters on conifer stumps and logs, with a yellow-brown to cinnamon cap, a purplish brown spore print, and no ring on the stem. Wikipedia reports it from North America, Europe and Asia. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing in clusters or gregariously on or near decaying conifer logs, especially the wood of pines and Douglas-fir, and often common in pine plantations after trimming operations. The same source calls it typically a fall or winter mushroom that can also be encountered in spring, and says it is widely distributed in North America; its illustrated and described collections are from Michigan, Illinois and Québec. Wikipedia separately says it grows in clusters on decaying wood, for example in tufts on old tree stumps, in North America, Europe and Asia. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
