## Summary

Phallus multicolor (Berk. & Broome) Cooke, 1882, is a stinkhorn in the family Phallaceae, placed in the order Phallales. Wikipedia describes it as similar to Phallus indusiatus but with a more brightly coloured cap, stipe and indusium, and usually smaller. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a "netted stinkhorn" with a lemon yellow head, visible once the brown spore slime has been removed, and a yellow skirt. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com notes that two varieties have been described, the typical variety and var. laeticolor. They are putatively separable by the length and colour of the indusium and by the colours of the stem and volva. That source says the differences do not appear to be consistent. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously in woods and in urban settings such as gardens and woodchip beds. It gives the fruiting season as year-round and the range as Australia, New Zealand and Hawaii. It adds that the species is also reported from Asia, although those records may represent Phallus cinnabarinus. Its illustrated specimens were photographed in Australia. Wikipedia lists Australia, Guam, Sumatra, Java, Borneo, Papua New Guinea, DRC, Tobago, Hawaii and New Caledonia, and says it is typically found in tropical or subtropical climates. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
