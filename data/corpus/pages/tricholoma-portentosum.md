## Summary

Tricholoma portentosum (Fr.) Quél. is a species in the family Tricholomataceae, listed in this catalog under the common names Coalman and Sticky Gray Trich. Wikipedia describes it as a grey-capped mushroom of the genus Tricholoma, found in woodlands in Europe and North America. It gives the cap as 3–11 cm across, sticky when wet, dark grey with darker streaks perpendicular to the margin, with white gills and a white spore print. GBIF's description record lists only distribution locations (including Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Madeira Island and Vermont). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name Tricholoma comes from the Ancient Greek θρίξ (trix, τριχός trichos), 'hair', and λῶμα (lôma), 'fringe', and refers to the fibrils on the caps of many species in the genus. It states that the species epithet portentosum comes from the Latin portentosus, meaning marvellous or prodigious. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described as Agaricus portentosus by Elias Magnus Fries in 1821 and was later placed in the genus Tricholoma by Lucien Quélet. Wikispecies also lists Agaricus portentosus Fr., 1821 as a synonym. Wikipedia adds that at least three varieties have been described: var. album with an all white cap, var. lugdunense with a paler cap, and var. boutevillei with a very dark cap, which it says is the form growing with oak and beech. No supplied source describes the type material or explains the later transfers. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only supplied source with habitat text (iNaturalist is a declared derivative of it), states that the fruit bodies appear in late autumn in coniferous woodland in Europe and North America. It describes the species as ectomycorrhizal, most commonly associated with Pinus sylvestris but also sometimes with oak (Quercus) or beech (Fagus) on sandy soils. Wikipedia also reports a decline in the Netherlands since the 1980s, where it is now rare, and describes it as uncommon in Britain. *[wikipedia]*
