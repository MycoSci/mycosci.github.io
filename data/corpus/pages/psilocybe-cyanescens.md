## Summary

Psilocybe cyanescens Wakef. (1946) is a species in the family Hymenogastraceae, with the type locality given as Great Britain. Wikipedia describes it as a potent psychedelic mushroom, commonly called the wavy cap, whose main psychedelic compounds are psilocybin and psilocin. It is recorded across North America, Europe, New Zealand and parts of west Asia, and it grows mainly on wood chips and mulch. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Elsie Wakefield published the formal description in 1946 in the Transactions of the British Mycological Society, based on a specimen she had recently collected at Kew Gardens, and that she had begun collecting the species as early as 1910. It also reports that an epitype from Hamburg, Germany was designated in 2012. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species grows today primarily on wood chips, especially in and along the perimeter of mulched plant beds in urban areas. It can also grow on other lignin-rich substrates, and it does not typically grow on mulch made from bark. Fruiting depends on a drop in temperature. In the San Francisco Bay Area this means late October to February, and elsewhere it generally occurs in fall at 10–18 °C. In the United States it occurs mainly in the Pacific Northwest, south to the San Francisco Bay Area. Wikipedia also lists New Zealand, Western Europe, Central Europe and parts of west Asia (Iran). Its natural habitat is stated to be unknown. A 2018 German study (lit_3) calls it an invasive synanthropic species in Germany and attributes its rapid spread to human activity and urban mulch use. *[wikipedia, lit_3]*
