## Summary

Helvella solitaria P. Karst. (1871) is a cup fungus in the family Helvellaceae. Wikipedia describes a cup with a dark greyish-brown to brownish-black spore-bearing surface on a hollow, whitish to pale grey, ribbed stalk. MushroomExpert.Com describes a grayish brown to brown cup on a whitish, deeply ribbed stem. Both sources place it in Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Petter Adolf Karsten first appeared to name the species as Peziza solitaria in 1869, a name later ruled an illegitimate homonym. He validly described it as Helvella solitaria in 1871. The holotype was collected at Mustiala, Finland, on 21 September 1866 and is held at the Finnish Museum of Natural History (H). An epitype from a 1942 collection in Stadsskogen, Uppsala, Sweden, was later designated to stabilise the name. Bizzarri and Saccardo transferred it to Lachnea solitaria in 1876. Bresadola's Helvella queletii (1882) is listed as a later synonym, and Wikipedia says it was recombined as Acetabula queletii by J. Benedix in 1962. MushroomExpert.Com adds that several phylogenetic species have recently been separated from H. solitaria. These are China's Helvella taiyuanensis (no morphological differences) and Europe's resurrected Helvella platypodia (putatively smaller and more gray). It says more separations are possible once North American collections are studied more fully. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a boreal and temperate distribution in coniferous and mixed forests, from lowland to alpine zones, with collections up to about 2,400 m elevation. It says the fungus fruits on the ground, on mineral-rich soils beneath shrubs such as willows and in dryas-dominated fellfield associations. It has also been recorded on grassy verges and on roadside substrates under trembling aspen. Wikipedia gives the fruiting season as early summer into autumn. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says it is probably mycorrhizal and grows alone or gregariously under hardwoods or conifers, often near well-rotted wood. He gives the season as spring through fall, or over winter in warm climates, but says he most often collects it in late spring or early summer. That page describes North American records from Alaska and the Maritime Provinces through Mexico, with records also from Central America and Asia. Wikipedia lists confirmed European records from Iceland, Norway, Sweden, Switzerland and France, extending east to Georgia (Caucasus) and northern Russia, and reports a sequenced North American collection from Alberta, Canada. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
