## Summary

Pseudoboletus parasiticus (Bull.) Šutara, 1991, the parasitic bolete, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae. Sources describe it as growing on the earthball Scleroderma citrinum. It is recorded in Europe and in eastern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com says the species was originally described from France. It says Šutara (1991) placed it in its own genus, Pseudoboletus, because its stem surface lacks a caulohymenium: no basidia or cystidia occur in patches on the stem, even directly below the pore surface. The same source says DNA studies (Nuhn et al. 2013, Wu et al. 2016) have since supported this placement. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows on Scleroderma citrinum earthballs in eastern North America from July to September. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as apparently parasitic on Scleroderma citrinum. That source reports it most often in eastern hemlock bogs where the host is growing, alone or in small clusters, in summer and fall. It calls the species widespread in Europe and distributed fairly widely east of the Great Plains in North America. MushroomExpert.Com also notes that researchers (Richter & Bruhn 1989) grew mycorrhizae in culture between this species and Pinus resinosa (red pine), which raises the possibility that it is mycorrhizal, and that the details of its parasitism are not well understood. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
