## Summary

Leucoagaricus meleagris (Gray) Singer 1951 is a lepiotoid species in the family Agaricaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as an uncommon mushroom that appears in woodchips and compost beds in tightly packed clusters, with dark brown cap centers and fine brown scales over a whitish ground color. Wikipedia lists the species under the newer name Macropsalliota meleagris. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that the epithet meleagris is Greek for "guinea hen" (μελεαγρις), and that it is unclear why Sowerby thought the mushroom looked like a guinea hen. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia reports that when Samuel Frederick Gray moved the species to Gymnopus in 1821, he suggested the common name "Turkey-fowl naked-foot". MushroomExpert.Com separately proposes "Lady Arden's hot-bed guinea hen". That name is built from the epithet's meaning and from Sowerby's account of receiving specimens from Lady Arden. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both credit James Sowerby with the original description as Agaricus meleagris in 1799. Sowerby wrote that he was obliged to Lady Arden for specimens found in a hot-bed on 24 May 1798. According to MushroomExpert.Com, Sowerby's description is very brief and relies on an illustration in place of a full text description. Wikipedia lists the later transfers: Gymnopus (Gray, 1821), Lepiota (Saccardo, 1887), Hiatula (Singer, 1936), Leucocoprinus (Locquin, 1945), Leucoagaricus (Singer, 1949), and Macropsalliota (Yang, Lin & Yang, 2024). It also lists Mastocephalus biornatus (Kunze, 1891), a name it says was not accepted. MushroomExpert.Com adds that, according to Vellinga (2000), Lepiota sanguiflua, described from Florida by Murrill in 1945, is likely the same species. These transfer details come from Wikipedia alone. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing in clusters and gregariously in woodchips or compost, in summer and fall. It says the species was originally described from England, is widely distributed but uncommon in Europe and North America, and is also reported from Asia, Oceania and South America. The collections MushroomExpert.Com illustrates and describes are from Ohio. Wikipedia says it grows in small groups and tufts in autumn. It notes that the early observations came from greenhouses and bark beds in hothouses. More recent records are from woodchips in England and Skåne, Sweden, and from greenhouses in Warsaw, Poland. Wikipedia calls it widespread but rarely recorded in the United Kingdom. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
