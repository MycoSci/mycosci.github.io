## Summary

Tubaria furfuracea (Pers.) Gillet is a small brown-spored agaric in the family Tubariaceae, with a cap 1–4 cm across and whitish veil remnants on the cap margin when fresh. Persoon described it in 1801 as Agaricus furfuraceus, and Gillet transferred it to Tubaria in 1876. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a typical "little brown mushroom" that probably requires microscopic study to identify with confidence. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Christiaan Hendrik Persoon first described the species in 1801 as Agaricus, and that Claude-Casimir Gillet transferred it to Tubaria in 1876. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) notes that, depending on the mycologist consulted, Tubaria anthracophila, T. hiemalis and T. romagnesiana are synonyms. It also states that no thorough contemporary study of the mushrooms worldwide called "Tubaria furfuracea" has been published to the author's knowledge. Such a study might reveal cryptic species and misidentifications, and Kuo suggests treating the name as a species group. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (a single-source account) describes it as saprobic and gregarious, usually on deadwood or woody debris. It is often found in urban settings in mulch, wood chips or disturbed ground, and also in woods on or around decaying logs and stumps. It is more likely to appear in winter or spring but also occurs in summer and fall. The same source calls it widespread in Eurasia, North America and Oceania, and reports it from Central America, South America and southern Africa. *[mushroomexpert]*
