## Summary

Gymnopilus luteofolius (Peck) Singer is a large, widely distributed mushroom with a purplish-red scaly cap, yellow gills that turn rusty brown, a bright rusty orange spore print and a bitter taste. Wikipedia describes it as found throughout North America, and a 2011 Mexican survey reported it as a new record for Mexico. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_2]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that it was first described as Agaricus luteofolius by Charles Horton Peck in 1875, renamed Pholiota luteofolius by Pier Andrea Saccardo in 1887, and given its current name by Rolf Singer in 1951. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as growing in dense clusters on dead hardwoods and conifers in North America, where it is common, appearing generally from June to October, or September to March on the West Coast. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of conifers, including commercial lumber and wood chips, and occasionally reported on hardwood. It grows in loose clusters or densely gregariously, in summer and fall or over winter in warm climates, and Kuo's described collections are from Illinois and North Carolina. A 2011 survey of macrofungi from Temascaltepec, Estado de México, reported it as a new record for Mexico. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_2]*
