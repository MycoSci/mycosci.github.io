## Summary

Pleurotus nebrodensis (Inzenga) Quél. (1886) is a fungus in the family Pleurotaceae. According to Wikipedia, it was declared critically endangered by the IUCN in 2006 and grows only on limestone in northern Sicily. The same source describes it as creamy white to yellow, 5 to 20 cm across, with extremely angled gills and a cap surface that breaks apart at maturity. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the first record of the mushroom was in 1866 by the Italian botanist Giuseppe Inzenga, who named it Agaricus nebrodensis. In 1886 the French mycologist Lucien Quélet transferred it to the genus Pleurotus. The same source reports that recent research shows P. nebrodensis to be closely related to, but distinct from, Pleurotus eryngii, which also occurs in the Mediterranean Basin and is also associated with plants in the family Apiaceae. This account rests on a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states that the fungus is being grown like a crop to reduce the strain on the wild population, in tunnels covered by black nets. This is a single-source statement, and no cultivation method is given. *[wikipedia]*
