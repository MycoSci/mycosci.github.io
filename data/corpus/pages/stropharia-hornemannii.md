## Summary

Stropharia hornemannii (Fr.) S. Lundell & Nannf., 1934 is a species of agaric fungus in the family Strophariaceae, called Conifer Roundhead in the site catalog. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a large Stropharia with a sticky, reddish brown to purple-brown or brown cap, a thick scaly stem and a dark purplish brown spore print. Wikipedia lists the alternative common names luxuriant ringstalk and lacerated stropharia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

According to Wikipedia, the specific epithet hornemannii honours the Danish botanist Jens Wilken Hornemann, who made the first scientifically documented collections of the species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia calls it primarily a boreal species centred in Northern Europe and particularly common in Scandinavia. It quotes Fries describing it as "ubiquitous in our coniferous forests on the ground, rarer on trunks, throughout autumn". Wikipedia's account of the first French collection (1971, Massif Central) describes over 50 fruiting bodies in a gently sloping fir-dominated forest, growing mainly on decaying wood fragments buried in humus and moss, with collections in September and October. Wikipedia adds that it is most often found on the ground and only occasionally directly on decaying wood. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered or in clusters on duff and woody debris in boreal and montane forests, often fruiting from well-decayed wood, in summer and fall, and widely distributed in northern and montane North America. Its illustrated and described collections are from Colorado and Québec. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
