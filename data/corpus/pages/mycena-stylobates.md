## Summary

Mycena stylobates (Pers.) P. Kumm. is a species in the family Mycenaceae (order Agaricales), known in English as the bulbous bonnet. Wikipedia describes it as a small mushroom with whitish to gray fruit bodies and bell-shaped caps up to 15 mm across. The same source gives North America and Europe as its range and lists it as inedible. This is a single-source description, because the iNaturalist text is a copy of Wikipedia. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first named Agaricus stylobates by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1801, and Elias Magnus Fries sanctioned that name. Paul Kummer transferred it to Mycena in 1871, when he raised many of Fries' "tribes" to the rank of genus. It was also placed in Basidopus by Franklin Sumner Earle in 1909 and in Pseudomycena by Karel Cejp in 1930. Wikipedia says both of those genera have since been subsumed into Mycena. The same source says Mordecai Cubitt Cooke called it the "discoid Mycena" in his 1871 Handbook of British Fungi. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow scattered or in groups on oak leaves or coniferous needles, in spring and summer or early autumn, and that the species is common during warm, wet seasons. It reports that Alexander H. Smith collected it in Tennessee, Michigan, Idaho and Washington (USA) and in Nova Scotia and Ontario (Canada). It also lists Europe, including Britain, Denmark, Germany, Norway, Poland, Romania, Scotland, Serbia and Sweden. Wikipedia adds that Australian records are, in Cheryl Grgurinovic's 2003 conclusion, "best regarded as erroneous". The GBIF descriptions record only a distribution list (Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden). All habitat detail comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
