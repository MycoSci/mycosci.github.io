## Summary

Retiboletus griseus (Frost) Manfr. Binder & Bresinsky is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the Grey Bolete. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a gray, drab bolete with a coarsely and widely reticulate stem, fairly common in eastern North America's oak-hickory forests. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described scientifically in 1878 by the American botanist Charles Christopher Frost, and that it was transferred to Retiboletus in 2002. MushroomExpert.Com lists Boletus griseus as a previous name. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it can be found under oak trees in eastern North America from June to September. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and other hardwoods, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously from early summer through fall. It gives the distribution as widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains and sometimes reported in the Southwest, and says the collections it illustrates and describes are from Illinois. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
