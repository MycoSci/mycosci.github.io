## Summary

Auricularia cornea Ehrenb. (1820) is a species in the family Auriculariaceae, order Auriculariales. Wikipedia states it was originally described from Oahu, Hawaii, and that it is widely distributed in southern Asia, Africa, Australasia, the Pacific Islands and South America. GBIF holds occurrence records from a wide range of countries. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia describes the species as one of several gelatinous fungi known as wood ear, wood fungus, ear fungus or tree ear fungus, a name alluding to their rubbery, ear-shaped fruitbodies. It also gives local names: pepeiao in Hawaii (meaning "ear"), hakeke among Māori in New Zealand, and taingang daga or tenga ng daga ("rat's ears") in the Philippines. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Christian Gottfried Ehrenberg described the species in 1820 from Hawaii (Oahu). Bernard Lowy accepted it as a distinct species in his 1952 world monograph of Auricularia, and molecular work based on cladistic analysis of DNA sequences later confirmed it as distinct. Wikipedia says it was formerly often misidentified as A. polytricha, and that this misidentification remains widespread, at least in Japan. Wikispecies records that a lectotype (Ehrenberg's Tab. 19, fig. 9) was designated by Popov in 2014. It lists Exidia cornea, Hirneola cornea and Auricula cornea as homotypic synonyms, and Auricularia reticulata L.J.Li as a heterotypic synonym (nom. illeg.). *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*
