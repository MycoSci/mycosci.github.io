## Summary

Tuber lyonii Butters (1903) is a truffle in the family Tuberaceae, described from Minnesota and recorded from the United States, Canada and Mexico. It is native to North America and fruits underground in mycorrhizal association with trees, chiefly oaks and hickories including pecan. Sources give it the common names pecan truffle and American brown truffle. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the name "pecan truffle" comes from the species being most commonly found in pecan orchards, in association with the pecan tree, and notes that pecan is not its only symbiont. This is a single-source account. No source explains the origin of the alternative name "American brown truffle". *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia gives the range as northern Mexico (Nuevo León and Tamaulipas) north into Québec, and from the eastern seaboard west to the southern Rockies. It reports the species most commonly with Carya (hickories and pecans) and Quercus (oaks), and occasionally with Corylus (hazels), Castanea (chestnuts) and basswood. It describes well-managed pecan orchards, particularly along the edges of herbicide strips, as one productive habitat. Fruit bodies usually sit in the top few inches of soil and sometimes push up through it. Fruiting is described as late summer into fall, and as continuing through winter to as late as February in Florida and southern Georgia.

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and hickories, especially pecan, growing alone or gregariously underground, often in disturbed-ground and urban settings. It gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates, and the distribution as primarily southeastern but reported throughout eastern North America and in New Mexico. The collection Kuo illustrates and describes is from Texas. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
