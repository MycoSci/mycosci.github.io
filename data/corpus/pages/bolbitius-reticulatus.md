## Summary

Bolbitius reticulatus (Pers.) Ricken, 1911, is a species in the family Bolbitiaceae (order Agaricales), catalogued under the common name Netted Fieldcap. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as growing on wood or woody debris in Europe and North America, with a sticky, fragile, deeply lined cap and a cinnamon-brown spore print. ## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com says the species epithet reticulatus refers to the prominently veined-reticulate cap surface that both grayish and purple-brown forms sometimes develop. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com says the species was originally described from Europe. It also says varieties including aleuriatus and pluteoides have been named over the years, and that these taxa are sometimes elevated to species rank. It reports that recent DNA-based research (Malysheva et al. 2015) supports a wide species concept for B. reticulatus, without subdividing it into the traditionally accepted varieties and forms. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on wood or woody debris, including woodchips. It is said to decompose the deadwood of hardwoods: decaying logs in woodland settings and woodchips in urban settings. It grows alone or scattered, in summer and fall (and over winter in warm climates). The source calls it widespread in Europe and North America, and its illustrated and described collections are from Illinois and Missouri. The GBIF description record lists distribution as Belgium (including the Brussels-Capital and Flemish Regions), Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
