## Summary

Ampulloclitocybe clavipes (Pers.) Redhead, Lutzoni, Moncalvo & Vilgalys (2002) is a gilled mushroom in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the club foot. Wikipedia describes it as a species from Europe and North America with grey-brown caps, yellowish decurrent gills and a bulbous stalk. MushroomExpert.Com notes that it is not easy to identify with certainty without a microscope, since many clitocyboid mushrooms look alike to the naked eye. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet derives from the Latin clava, 'club', and pes, 'foot'. MushroomExpert.Com says the bulbous stem gives the species its name (clavipes), but adds that the stem is not always as bulbous as field guide photos suggest, especially in older specimens. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus clavipes by Persoon in 1801. Kummer transferred it to Clitocybe in 1871, and Bigelow improperly designated it the type species of Clitocybe in 1965. Quélet placed it in Omphalia in 1886. Redhead and colleagues proposed the genus Ampulloclitocybe because the species was only distantly related to Clitocybe proper. Harmaja independently proposed Clavicybe around the same time. Wikipedia says Ampulloclitocybe was published on 5 November 2002 and Clavicybe on 31 December 2002, and that Harmaja conceded Ampulloclitocybe had priority. Wikipedia also reports that Orton described Clitocybe squamulosoides in 1960 as a slender, large-spored relative, but that the differences are inconsistent and intermediate forms exist, so it is considered indistinguishable from A. clavipes. MushroomExpert.Com independently states that DNA studies placed this mushroom and its close relatives far from the bulk of Clitocybe species, in the Hygrophoraceae. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is found in conifer and deciduous forests, particularly under beech, and that in northern Europe the fruit bodies appear from August to November. It describes the species as widespread and abundant across Northern Europe and the British Isles. In North America it says the species is common under pine plantations in the east and less common in the Pacific Northwest. MushroomExpert.Com says the species is treated as saprobic by some authors and mycorrhizal by others. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, primarily under conifers but sometimes under hardwoods. It describes the species as originally described from Europe, widely distributed throughout Europe and Asia, and also found in Australia. In North America it says the species is widely distributed but apparently absent or very rare in the lower Rocky Mountains and the southwestern United States. These two sources differ in emphasis on conifer versus deciduous hosts, and each is a single-source account. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
