## Summary

Amanita jacksonii Pomerl. (1984) is a species in the family Amanitaceae. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a reddish-orange to red-capped Amanita with yellow gills and a large, white, sacklike volva. Wikipedia gives its range as extending from the Province of Quebec, Canada, to at least the State of Hidalgo, Mexico. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species was named in honor of Canadian watercolorist and mycological illustrator Henry A. C. Jackson. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was given its current name in 1984 by Canadian mycologist René Pomerleau, and that it was first described from specimens found in Quebec. Wikispecies cites the publication as Pomerleau, R. 1984, 'A propos du nom scientifique de l'orange américaine', Le Naturaliste Canadien 111(3): 329–330. MushroomExpert.Com says it was originally described from Québec and cites 'Pomerleau 1980'. It adds that the North American versions of the European Amanita caesarea are numerous and confusing, that their taxonomy is not completely established, and that virtually identical cryptic phylogenetic species occur in the lower Midwest, Texas, the Four Corners region and Mexico. Some of these have been officially named and others have only temporary names. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and pines, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall. That description is of the A. jacksonii group, which it says is widely distributed east of the Great Plains and in Mexico, from Québec to Hidalgo. Its illustrated collections came from Missouri, North Carolina and Texas. Mushroom World, a separate aggregator, says it grows solitary or in small clumps on soil in hardwood forests. Wikipedia gives a range from the Province of Quebec to at least the State of Hidalgo, Mexico, and says it has been observed as far south as Cayo District, Belize. *[mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, wikipedia]*
