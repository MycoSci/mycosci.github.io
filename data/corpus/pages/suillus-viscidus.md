## Summary

Suillus viscidus (L.) Roussel, 1796, commonly called the sticky bolete, is a bolete in the family Suillaceae. Wikipedia describes it as an ectomycorrhizal associate of larch that is uncommon to rare in Europe and also occurs in Japan. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that Suillus viscidus forms an ectomycorrhizal association specifically with larch (Larix), so its distribution is limited by the range of the host tree. It reports the species from throughout Europe, where it is considered uncommon to rare, and from Japan. It is said to occur in the same habitat as Suillus grevillei and Suillus tridentinus, with fruiting bodies found in groups among grass under larch from summer to autumn. This habitat account comes from a single independent source (Wikipedia; the iNaturalist text is a copy of it). The GBIF description record lists only distribution entries: Belgium (including the Brussels-Capital, Flemish and Walloon Regions), Estonia, Great Britain, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
