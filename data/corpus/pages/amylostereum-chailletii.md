## Summary

Amylostereum chailletii (Pers.) Boidin is a crust fungus in the family Echinodontiaceae (order Russulales), known in English as the powdered duster. It was originally described by Persoon in 1822 as Thelephora chailletii and was moved to Amylostereum by Boidin in 1958. Wikipedia states that it causes a white rot, especially in spruce and fir species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species causes a white rot, especially in spruce and fir species. A 1987 Plant Pathology paper describes it as a sapwood decay fungus introduced by the woodwasp Urocerus gigas into larch already damaged by the bark beetle Ips cembrae and the fungus Ceratocystis laricicola. A 1983 Science paper describes it as the fungal symbiont occurring in the wood on which larvae of the woodwasp Sirex cyaneus feed. A 1998 New Phytologist study of isolates from Lithuania, Sweden, Denmark and Great Britain reports that its population structure fits spread by airborne basidiospores, and that only a small fraction of isolates were local clones spread by Urocerus gigas. GBIF's distribution record lists Belgium (Flemish Region), Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. No supplied source gives a fruiting season. *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_2, lit_3, gbif_desc]*
