## Summary

Artomyces pyxidatus (Pers.) Jülich 1982 is a coral fungus in the family Auriscalpiaceae (Russulales). Its branch tips are described as crown-like, each with a shallow cup-like depression ringed by points. It is recorded across North America, Europe and Asia, among other regions. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet pyxidatus means "box-like" and refers to the crown-like shape of the branch tips. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com says the species is better known as Clavicorona pyxidata, a former name. It reports that DNA studies have upheld Jülich's proposal that the morphological differences are large enough to place this species and its close relatives in a separate genus, Artomyces. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously on the dead wood of hardwoods, especially aspens, tulip trees, willows and maples. It gives the season as spring, summer and fall, and the range as eastern North America, the Rocky Mountains and Mexico, with the fungus absent or rare on the West Coast. Wikipedia states that in eastern North America it appears on decaying wood from June to September. A 2025 study from Uttarakhand, India reports it in association with Quercus leucotrichophora. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, lit_3]*
