## Summary

Clitocybe rivulosa (Pers.) P. Kumm. (1871) is placed in the Tricholomataceae, and Wikipedia describes it as a small white funnel-shaped toadstool of grassy places. Wikipedia, the only source with descriptive text, calls it poisonous and says it contains potentially deadly levels of muscarine. GBIF distribution records in the supplied data cover Belgium, Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the surface of the cap can develop concentric rings of cracks with age, and that the species epithet rivulosa refers to this fissuring. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the name 'sweating mushroom' derives from the symptoms of poisoning (SLUDGE syndrome). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that it was first described as Agaricus rivulosus by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1801 and classified as Clitocybe rivulosa by Paul Kummer in 1871. It also says the species was reclassified to its current name after a phylogenetic study by Zheng-Mi He and colleagues in 2023. Wikipedia adds that the very similar Clitocybe dealbata is sometimes regarded as part of the same species, in which case the name rivulosa takes precedence. This history comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it is found in grassy habitats in summer and autumn in Europe and North America, where it can often form fairy rings. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*
