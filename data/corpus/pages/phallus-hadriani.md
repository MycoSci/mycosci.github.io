## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet hadriani honours the Dutch botanist Hadrianus Junius (1512–1575), who wrote a pamphlet on stinkhorn mushrooms in 1564. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by the French botanist Étienne Pierre Ventenat in 1798 and sanctioned by Persoon in his 1801 Synopsis Methodica Fungorum. Nees von Esenbeck called it Hymenophallus hadriani in 1817, which is now a synonym. Wikipedia also lists later synonyms: Phallus iosmus (Berkeley, 1836) and Phallus imperialis (Schulzer, 1873). Kuo (MushroomExpert.Com, 2021) argues that the name's application is unsettled. In North America it is often used for a Phallus impudicus-like form with a pink to purple volva and egg. In Europe it is currently applied to a coastal, dune-inhabiting species with a pink volva and larger spores than P. impudicus. Kuo says Ventenat's protologue emphasizes a non-reticulated cap and a stem spotted with gray, whereas all contemporary concepts involve a reticulated head. He concludes that none of the modern applications of the name matches the protologue, and that Ventenat's description is a better match for something like Phallus campanulatus or Phallus ravenelii. This is a single-author interpretation. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes P. hadriani as saprobic. It says the species typically grows in public lawns, yards and gardens, usually in sandy soils. In North America it is commonly associated with decomposing tree stumps or their roots. In Great Britain its distribution is described as more or less restricted to coastal dunes. In Poland it is reported to avoid humid, humic forest soils and to live in association with xerophilous grasses and the black locust (Robinia pseudoacacia). In Australia it is thought to be introduced, probably on woodchip mulch used in gardening and landscaping. GBIF's description record lists distribution only, for Belgium (Flanders), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. These habitat statements come from Wikipedia alone. Kuo notes that the name is applied to different concepts in different regions, so the habitat reports may not all refer to the same organism. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc, mushroomexpert]*
