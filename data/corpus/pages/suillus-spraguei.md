## Summary

Suillus spraguei (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Kuntze, 1898, is a bolete in the family Suillaceae, known in English as the painted suillus. Sources describe it as mycorrhizal with eastern white pine and recorded across eastern North America and parts of eastern Asia, with a disjunct distribution. Its cap is dry and covered with pinkish to brick-red scales, over yellow pores and a stem with a whitish to grayish ring. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet spraguei honors the collector Sprague, and that pictus (the epithet of the synonym Suillus pictus) means "painted" or "colored". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia notes that the name "eastern painted Suillus" is used to contrast this species with the "western painted Suillus" (Suillus lakei). It does not narrate how the other common names arose. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the first specimen was collected in New England in 1856 by Charles James Sprague, and Miles Joseph Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis published a formal description as Boletus spraguei in 1872. Wikipedia says Charles Horton Peck published Boletus pictus in 1873 and that Rolf Singer reported in 1945 that this name was an illegitimate homonym. It adds that Otto Kuntze had transferred the taxon to Suillus in 1898 and that the name Suillus spraguei was officially adopted in 1986. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) gives a different account of the synonym. It says Peck described Boletinus pictus as new in 1889 but cited Boletus spraguei as an earlier name for the same mushroom, and it calls this a nomenclatural mistake. MushroomExpert.Com also says the species was originally described from "New England" (Berkeley & Curtis 1872). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe S. spraguei as mycorrhizal with eastern white pine, found throughout the natural range of the host tree. Wikipedia adds that the fungus is host-specific in natural soils to five-needled (white) pines, and lists Korean pine, Chinese white pine, Siberian dwarf pine and Japanese white pine as hosts of Asian populations. It says the fruit bodies grow on the ground and, in North America, appear from as early as June to as late as October. MushroomExpert.Com says late summer and fall, growing alone or gregariously, and reports its illustrated collections from Kentucky and Michigan. In a Japanese field study of a Korean pine stand cited by Wikipedia, the mushrooms appeared mostly from August to November and tended to grow in clumps. For distribution, Wikipedia gives China, Japan, Korea and Taiwan in Asia. In North America it gives eastern Canada (Nova Scotia) south to the Carolinas and west to Minnesota, plus Mexico (Coahuila and Durango). It also reports introductions to Europe (Germany, Netherlands). MushroomExpert.Com likewise mentions rare occurrence under imported eastern white pine in Europe. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
