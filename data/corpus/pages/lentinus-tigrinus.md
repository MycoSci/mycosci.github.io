## Summary

Lentinus tigrinus (Bull.) Fr., 1825, is a species in the family Polyporaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a fairly small gilled mushroom that DNA studies place in the polypores, with small dark brown scales on the cap and a navel-like central depression. The same source describes it as a saprobe on hardwood, usually in floodplains. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com calls the original French name "L'agaric tigré" (the tiger agaric). It presumes that the brown scales on the whitish cap reminded Bulliard of a tiger, and says this is an inference rather than something Bulliard stated. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, Bulliard first described the species from France in 1782 as "L'agaric tigré", and Fries treated it in 1825. In North America a form with malformed gills, which looks as if covered by a partial veil or Hypomyces, is common. Kauffman (1918) called it a "monstrous form" and Smith (1979) called it an "abortive stage". Morgan (1895) considered it a separate species that deserved its own genus ("Lentodium squamulosum"). Later mating studies (Rosinski & Robison, 1968) found the normal and gasteroid forms completely intercompatible. Grand (2004) found clear DNA separation between North American and non-North American (Eurasian and Australian) populations, which can nonetheless still mate in petri dishes. All of this comes from a single source. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, or more often gregariously to loosely clustered on the wood of hardwoods. It is usually found in floodplains on wood subject to regular wetting by streams and rivers, and in North America it is found on riverbank trees such as willows, cottonwoods and silver maples. That source gives spring through fall as the season, or over winter in warmer climates. Bulliard's original description is quoted as "in summer and fall in woods on old, rotten trees and more commonly on elms". The same source says it is widespread in Europe. In North America it is widely distributed from the Great Plains eastward and in the Southwest, and it is also found in the Caribbean, Central America, South America and Asia. The described and illustrated collections are from Illinois and Missouri. All habitat statements come from this single source. *[mushroomexpert]*
