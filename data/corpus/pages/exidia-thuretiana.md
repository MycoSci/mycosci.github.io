## Summary

Exidia thuretiana (Lév.) Fr. is a jelly fungus in the family Auriculariaceae, known in English as white brain. Wikipedia describes its fruit bodies as white and gelatinous with brain-like folds, growing on dead branches of broadleaf trees in Europe. That source treats the species under the name Descidia thuretiana, following molecular work. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet "thuretiana" compliments the botanist Gustave Thuret, owner of the Château de Rentilly, in whose grounds the species was first collected. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first found growing on beech in France and was described in 1848 by Joseph-Henri Léveillé as Tremella thuretiana. Fries transferred it to Exidia in 1874. Donk preferred the name Exidia albida (Huds.) Bref. for it, but most later authors have followed Reid in treating E. albida as a nomen dubium, a name of uncertain application. Molecular research based on DNA sequences found that the species does not belong in Exidia sensu stricto and placed it in the newly described genus Descidia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a wood-rotting species, typically found on dead attached or fallen branches. It was originally recorded on beech and frequently occurs on that substrate. It is also known from other broadleaf trees and shrubs, including oak, hazel, ash and apple. The same source says it typically fruits in autumn and winter and is widely distributed in Europe, North Africa and northern Asia, and that it has been collected from Greenland. The GBIF description record lists a distribution for Belgium (Flemish and Walloon Regions), Denmark and Norway. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
