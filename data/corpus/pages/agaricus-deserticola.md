## Summary

Agaricus deserticola G. Moreno, Esqueda & Lizárraga (2010), commonly called the gasteroid agaricus, is a species of fungus in the family Agaricaceae. According to Wikipedia, its fruit bodies are secotioid: the cap does not fully expand and no true gills develop, with spore-producing tissue called a gleba instead. The same source describes it as found only in southwestern and western North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Per Wikipedia, the species was first described as Secotium texense by Berkeley and Curtis in 1873, from specimens sent from western Texas. Massee moved it to Gyrophragmium in 1891. Murrill listed it in Gymnopus in 1916, without explaining the transfer. In 1943 Zeller created the genus Longia for it. In 1945 Zeller replaced that name with Longula, because Longia had already been used for a genus of rusts. A 2004 phylogenetic study showed a close relationship with Agaricus, and the species was moved there. The resulting name, Agaricus texensis, was unusable because Berkeley and Curtis had already used it in 1853 for a different taxon. Moreno and colleagues therefore published the replacement name Agaricus deserticola in 2010. All of this comes from a single independent source (Wikipedia; the iNaturalist entry is a declared derivative of it). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only independent source, describes the species as saprobic, growing singly to sometimes more numerous, at low elevations and typically in sandy soil. It gives the usual habitats as drylands, coastal sage scrub and desert ecosystems, and says it also grows in lawns and fields. The same source says the range is restricted to southwestern and western North America, where it fruits throughout the year, typically during or following cold, wet weather. It cites Zeller for a range from central Texas west to San Diego County, California, and north to Josephine County, Oregon. It also reports collections in the northwestern Mexican states of Sonora, Chihuahua and Baja California, and says the mushroom was once common in the San Francisco Bay area before land development reduced its preferred habitats. *[wikipedia]*
