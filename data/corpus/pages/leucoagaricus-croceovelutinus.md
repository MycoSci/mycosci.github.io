## Summary

Leucoagaricus croceovelutinus is a species of mushroom-producing fungus in the family Agaricaceae. It was described in 1972 by Marcel Bon and Jacques Boiffard as Leucocoprinus croceovelutinus and moved to Leucoagaricus by Bon in 1976. Wikipedia describes it as a small dapperling with thin white or pale yellow flesh that discolours red within a minute of exposure, and a 3–6 cm cap with scattered red-brown scales. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was described in 1972 by the French mycologists Marcel Bon and Jacques Boiffard as Leucocoprinus croceovelutinus. Bon reclassified it in 1976 as Leucoagaricus croceovelutinus. The variety Leucocoprinus croceovelutinus var. diversisporus was described in 1990 by the British mycologist Derek Reid and is now considered a synonym. The source does not say why the species was transferred between genera. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the specimens studied by Bon and Boiffard were found on humus under deciduous trees near the Le Veillon beach near Talmont Saint Hilaire, France. Derek Reid suggested a possible association with Robinia, Quercus and Ligustrum trees, based on the limited recorded collections then available, which came from France, the Netherlands and Hungary. This association is a suggestion from a single source, not an established host relationship. Wikipedia also notes a record from Bulgaria, and the GBIF distribution listing gives DK, NO and SE. No supplied source states a fruiting season. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
