## Summary

Cystodermella adnatifolia (Peck) Harmaja, 2002, is a member of the family Agaricaceae. Per MushroomExpert.Com, it has a dry, orange-red to reddish orange cap 2–4 cm across, covered when fresh with small granular scales, and white attached gills and a white spore print. GBIF's distribution record lists Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that the epithet adnatifolia refers to the adnate, or attached, gills. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, Charles Peck originally described the species in Lepiota, from New York, and regarded it as an outlier in that genus because its gills are attached to the stem rather than free. Peck listed four lepiotas with attached gills (adnatifolia, granosa, rugosoreticulata and amianthina), all of which that source says are now placed in Cystoderma or Cystodermella. The same source gives Cystoderma adnatifolia and Cystoderma granulosum var. adnatifolium as synonyms. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously under conifers in summer and fall. It was originally described from New York and is described as fairly widely distributed in Europe and North America; the collections illustrated and described on that page are from Illinois and Italy. GBIF's distribution record lists DK, NO and SE. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
