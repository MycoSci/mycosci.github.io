## Summary

Calvatia sculpta (Harkn.) Lloyd, 1904, the sculpted puffball, is a puffball in the family Lycoperdaceae, originally described as Lycoperdon sculptum. Its fruit body is described as pear- or egg-shaped and covered in large pyramidal or polygonal warts, reaching roughly 8 to 15 cm tall. It is recorded mainly from mountainous western North America, with a 2008 report from a Brazilian dune. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1885 by Harvey Willson Harkness as Lycoperdon sculptum, from fruit bodies found at about 6,000 to 8,000 feet (1,800 to 2,400 m) in the Sierra Nevada. Harkness thought the species differed so much from known species that it was almost worthy of generic rank, but he placed it in Lycoperdon. His type collections were reportedly destroyed in the fires following the 1906 San Francisco earthquake. In 1904 Curtis Gates Lloyd moved it to Calvatia because its deeply colored capillitial threads resembled those of Calvatia caelata. Wikispecies confirms the 1885 basionym and the 1904 combination. A 1935 Mycologia article reports that University of California herbarium specimens labeled Calvatia sculpta had been confused with a different puffball, and that over half of the material examined showed an excessively branched capillitium. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only independent source with habitat text, says the species grows solitarily or in small groups in forest duff. It is typically associated with coniferous forests at high elevations, above about 750 m (2,500 ft), on western mountains such as the Sierra Nevada and the Cascade Range. It gives United States records from California, Oregon, Washington and Idaho, calls the species uncommon, and says it fruits in spring, summer and fall during wet weather. The same source reports a 2008 find on sandy soil in Natal Dunes State Park, Rio Grande do Norte, Brazil, associated with roots of Eugenia brasiliensis. It notes that the Brazilian population has not been compared genetically with North American specimens. GBIF also lists a distribution record for Brazil. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
