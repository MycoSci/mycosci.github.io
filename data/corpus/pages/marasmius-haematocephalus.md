## Summary

Marasmius haematocephalus (Mont.) Fr., known as the purple pinwheel, is a species in the family Marasmiaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a small mushroom with striated purple caps that grows on leaf litter. It was long thought to be pantropical, but molecular data now suggest that the name covers a species complex. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

The GBIF-hosted description text states that the epithet means "blood red head", from Greek haimatos and kephali, and refers to the "rubro-sanguineo" pileus in the protologue. *[gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described from fallen leaves in Brazil as Agaricus haematocephalus by Montagne in 1837, and Fries transferred it to Marasmius in 1838. Collectors later applied the name to similar-looking specimens from around the world. Wikipedia reports that molecular data showed these collections to be a species complex, and that the "true" M. haematocephalus is currently believed to be limited to South America. Several new species from Brazil and Australia have been split from the complex. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows on leaf litter. The GBIF-hosted description text records the collection substrate as close-growing basidiomata on dried eudicotyledonous petioles in forest litter. Wikipedia says the type specimen was collected in Brazil and that the species in the strict sense is now believed to occur only in South America. It adds that members of the species complex have been collected in the southeastern USA, China, India and South Africa. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
