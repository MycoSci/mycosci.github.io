## Summary

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially pines, and identifies it by its orange-brown colors, collapsing cottony ring and strong mealy odor. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the original description of Armillaria focalis (Fries, 1838) did not say whether the cap was sticky or dry. Armillaria zelleri was named by Stuntz and Smith (in Smith, 1949), who argued that its sticky cap distinguished it from Armillaria focalis, which they took to have a dry cap. MushroomExpert.Com says contemporary European concepts of the species (now known as Tricholoma focale) require a sticky cap, which removes the stated justification for A. zelleri as a separate species. It reports that several North American authors (Ovrebo, 1980; Shanks, 1994, 1997) have therefore reduced Armillaria zelleri to synonymy with Tricholoma focale. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes T. focale as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially pines. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, in fall or, in warmer climates, over winter. It is described as fairly widely distributed in northern and montane North America, and the illustrated and described collections are from Colorado and Québec. The same source notes that collections from California, Sweden and Denmark were grouped together in an ITS-based DNA survey (Heilmann-Clausen and collaborators 2017), which it takes to suggest a transcontinental distribution. Mushroom World, a separate source, describes it as growing scattered or in small trooping groups in coastal locations where there are pine trees. *[mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
