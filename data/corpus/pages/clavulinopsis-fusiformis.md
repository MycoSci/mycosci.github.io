## Summary

Clavulinopsis fusiformis (Sowerby) Corner is a clavarioid fungus in the family Clavariaceae, with the recommended UK English name golden spindles. Wikipedia reports that it was described from England and is part of an unresolved species complex, so records from outside Europe are uncertain. It forms cylindrical, bright yellow, densely clustered fruit bodies. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet fusiformis is derived from Latin and means "spindle-shaped". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1799 by the English botanist and mycologist James Sowerby from collections made on Hampstead Heath in London. E.J.H. Corner transferred it to Clavulinopsis in 1950. Wikipedia adds that initial molecular work, based on DNA sequence analysis, indicates C. fusiformis belongs to a complex of related species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes fruit bodies growing in large, dense clusters on the ground and presumed to be saprotrophic. In Europe it is said to occur generally in agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns), and elsewhere in woodland (or woodland litter). Wikipedia also reports it as one of the dominant macrofungi in a Fargesia spathacea-dominated community forest in China at 2,600–3,500 m. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as presumably saprobic, growing in dense clusters with fused bases or occasionally gregariously, in woods under hardwoods or conifers and sometimes in grass, in summer and fall, and more common in the northern half of North America. A GBIF description record lists the substrate as soil. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
