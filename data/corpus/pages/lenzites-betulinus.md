## Summary

Lenzites betulinus (L.) Fr., 1838, is a species in the family Polyporaceae (order Polyporales). MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small bracket fungus whose fuzzy, zoned cap resembles Trametes versicolor or Trametes hirsuta from above. Underneath it has true gills rather than pores. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states the species was originally described from Sweden (Linnaeus 1753), with the Fries 1838 combination. It also says Lenzites betulina is an orthographic variant of the same name. It reports that some DNA studies (Tomsovsky et al. 2006, Justo & Hibbett 2011, Carlson et al. 2014) placed L. betulinus in a group with Trametes gibbosa, separate from many other Trametes species. Depending on how Trametes and related genera are treated, it says the name Trametes betulina might reasonably be applied to this fungus. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods, occasionally reported on conifer wood. It is annual and grows alone or in overlapping clusters on logs and stumps, producing a straw-colored white rot of the sapwood. It fruits in summer and fall. The same source calls it widely distributed in Eurasia, Oceania, Africa and the Americas. Its illustrated and described collections come from Kentucky, Ohio and Texas. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*
