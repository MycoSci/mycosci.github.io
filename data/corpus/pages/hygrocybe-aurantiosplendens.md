## Summary

Hygrocybe aurantiosplendens R. Haller Aar. (1954) is a waxcap in the family Hygrophoraceae, described from Switzerland and known in the UK as the orange waxcap. Wikipedia describes it as a mainly European grassland species that is assessed as globally vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both say that North American material called by this name may not be the same species as the European one. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet comes from the Latin aurantius (orange) plus splendens (shining). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both say the species was originally described from Switzerland in 1954 by R. Haller (of Aarau, per MushroomExpert.Com). Wikipedia reports that DNA-sequence (cladistic) analysis places it within Hygrocybe sensu stricto. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says the name is often used, following Largent (1985), for West Coast North American material that he thinks matches the original concept poorly (too red, too convex, too smooth-stemmed, with non-constricted spores). In his view the eastern North American material appears closer to the European concept, and a thorough contemporary study of the species group is needed. Wikipedia likewise says the American ecotypes may represent distinct species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says that in Europe the species typically grows in old, nutrient-poor, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns) and fruits in autumn. It says the British Isles and Scandinavia appear to have the greatest abundance, and that the species is rare to uncommon throughout its range. It also lists Finland, Iceland, Western Russia and high elevations in Southern Europe. Wikipedia says the American form is more typical of woodlands. In eastern North America it is reported sporadically from Maine south to Florida and west to northern Wisconsin. On the West Coast it is largely restricted to coastal regions from northern Washington to central California. MushroomExpert.Com describes a collection from Illinois growing scattered or gregariously under hardwoods in summer, and says the precise ecological role is uncertain and the North American distribution is uncertain. Wikipedia describes the species as typical of waxcap grasslands, a declining habitat, and lists it as globally vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
