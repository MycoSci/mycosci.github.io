## Summary

Thelephora anthocephala (Bull.) Fr. is a coral fungus in the family Thelephoraceae. It was first described by Bulliard in 1786 as Clavaria anthocephala and transferred to Thelephora by Fries in 1838. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as coral-like, with tough-fleshed, gray branches that begin to flatten at their tips, arising from a thickened, stem-like base. That description comes from a single source. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was originally described as new to science in 1786 by the French botanist Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard, who placed it in the genus Clavaria. Elias Fries transferred it to Thelephora in his 1838 work Epicrisis Systematis Mycologici. MushroomExpert.Com adds that the North American version is probably not the same as the true European version described from France, and that Thelephora species are still defined mainly by species concepts from Corner (1968). It notes that the few species-level DNA studies in the genus point to hidden species and considerable morphological variability within species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes the North American form as mycorrhizal with oaks and other hardwoods. It grows alone, gregariously, or in small clusters on the ground in summer. That source calls it widespread and common in North America east of the Rocky Mountains, and occasionally reported in the Pacific Northwest. The described collection is from Illinois. The same source cautions that the North American version may not be the true European species. This habitat account rests on that one source. GBIF's description record separately lists a distribution of Belgium (Flemish and Walloon Regions), Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont, without habitat detail. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
