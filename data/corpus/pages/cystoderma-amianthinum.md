## Summary

Cystoderma amianthinum (Scop.) Fayod, 1889, is a small orange-ochre to yellowish-brown gilled mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae, commonly called the earthy powdercap or saffron parasol. Sources describe it as growing in damp mossy grassland, coniferous forest clearings and on wooded heaths, and as widespread in Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia says the generic name Cystoderma is roughly translated as 'blistered skin' and is probably a reference to the appearance of the pellicle (cap skin). No source discusses the derivation of the species epithet. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was first noted by Giovanni Antonio Scopoli, who called it Agaricus amianthinus in 1772, and that the genus name Cystoderma was erected by Victor Fayod in 1889. MushroomExpert.Com says it was originally described from Slovenia (Scopoli 1772). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia and Mushroom World (one independent cluster) describe it in damp mossy grassland, in coniferous forest clearings, or on wooded heaths. Wikipedia adds mossy woodland, amongst grass or bracken, sometimes with willow, and often on acidic soils. MushroomExpert.Com independently describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered or gregariously, usually in moss under conifers, in late summer and fall (over winter in California). It gives the range as widely distributed in Europe and North America, primarily in northern and montane areas, and also documented in Central America and Oceania. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, mushroomexpert]*
