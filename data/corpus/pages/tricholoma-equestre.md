## Summary

Tricholoma equestre (L.) P. Kumm. (1871), the man on horseback or yellow knight, is a gilled fungus in the family Tricholomataceae. It is described as forming ectomycorrhiza with pine trees. Although long eaten and sold in European markets, it has been linked to poisoning cases in Europe, including reports of deaths, and a 2020 systematic review cited by Wikipedia suggests not harvesting or consuming it. *[wikipedia, inaturalist, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific name equestre as meaning 'of or pertaining to horses' in Latin, and derives the generic name Tricholoma from the Greek trichos 'hair' and loma 'hem', 'fringe' or 'border'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Linnaeus described it in Species Plantarum (1753) as Agaricus equestris, which predates Persoon's Agaricus flavovirens (1793); the epithet equestris therefore takes precedence over flavovirens. Paul Kummer placed it in Tricholoma in 1871 in Der Führer in die Pilzkunde. Wikispecies lists the basionym Agaricus equestris L. (1753) and several homotypic and heterotypic synonyms, including Tricholoma auratum and Tricholoma flavovirens. MushroomExpert.Com notes that a global assessment combining molecular and morphological methods is 'strongly needed' because of 'considerable cryptic diversity' in the group, and Wikipedia reports molecular research suggesting multiple species on the West Coast of North America were identified as T. flavovirens. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia (a single source) states the species was for a long time highly regarded as one of the tastier edible species and was sold in European markets. It also says medieval French knights allegedly reserved it for themselves, leaving the bovine bolete (Suillus bovinus) for the peasants. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with pines, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in poor, sandy soil in summer and fall. It says the species was originally described from Sweden, is widely distributed in Europe and North America, and is reported from Central America and Asia, with illustrated collections from California, Colorado and Michigan. Mushroom World gives a similar account of pine association and sandy soil in summer and fall. Wikipedia says it is especially abundant in France and Central Portugal. *[mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, wikipedia]*
