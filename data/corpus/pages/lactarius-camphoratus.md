## Summary

Lactarius camphoratus (Bull.) Fr. is a small, dull reddish-brown milkcap in the family Russulaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a distinctive odor compared to maple syrup, burned sugar or curry, which becomes stronger when the mushroom is dried. It also describes watery white milk and a cap 1-4 cm across. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia's article on the candy caps says L. camphoratus is gathered and sold in the United Kingdom and Yunnan, China, and that the candy caps are used primarily as a flavoring, often in dried form. The same article cites Marchand as reporting that some individuals use L. camphoratus as part of a pipe tobacco mix. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers or hardwoods, growing alone, scattered or gregariously, often in moss, in spring, summer and fall, and widely distributed in eastern North America. It notes that the North American concept may not be the same genetic entity as the original French collection. *[mushroomexpert]*
