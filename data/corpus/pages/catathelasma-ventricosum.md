## Summary

Catathelasma ventricosum (Peck) Singer, 1940, is placed in the family Biannulariaceae and is recorded in the US, Canada, Japan, China and several other countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a large whitish-capped gilled mushroom with gills running down the stem and a flaring double ring, growing under conifers in northeastern North America. The other supplied literature consists of laboratory studies on cultured mycelia and on fruiting-body production. *[mushroomexpert, lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com notes that the species is also written "Catathelasma ventricosa". Its author says the concept is well characterized by northeastern North American collections but shifts westward, where authors include browner-capped, mealy-odored collections and risk confusion with Catathelasma imperiale. He restricts C. imperiale to the West and treats a whitish cap and non-mealy odor as the defining features of C. ventricosum "for now". This is a single-source account of the taxonomic concept, not a history of the original description. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Cultivation

A 2013 study in the Journal of Microbiology and Biotechnology reports using C. ventricosum to produce fruiting bodies and lignocellulosic enzymes on a supplemented substrate. Adding Mn2+, Cu2+ and aromatic compounds raised laccase activity and productivity, and inoculating Penicillium decumbens into the substrate after 20 days of C. ventricosum growth gave the highest values. This is one research report, described by its authors as the first of its kind, and is not a published cultivation protocol. *[lit_2]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com states that it grows alone or scattered on the ground under conifers in fall, in northeastern North America, and is reported from the Rocky Mountains and the West Coast. The same source calls it "presumably mycorrhizal", so that is not a confirmed ecological role. This is a single-source account, and the author doubts that the species as he defines it occurs in the Rocky Mountains and westward. *[mushroomexpert]*
