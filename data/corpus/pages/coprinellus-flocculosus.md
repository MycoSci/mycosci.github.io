## Summary

Coprinellus flocculosus (DC.) Vilgalys, Hopple & Jacq. Johnson is a species of agaric fungus in the family Psathyrellaceae, with Coprinus flocculosus as a synonym. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe a coprinoid mushroom with a shaggy whitish veil on a yellowish to brownish-yellow cap, a black spore print, and gills that liquefy with age. MushroomExpert.Com notes that microscopic examination was needed to identify its collection, because many similar coprinoids share the macroscopic features. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, gregariously, or in clusters in fertilized soil, woodchips, old straw, sawdust and similar material, or in woods, especially from woody debris. It gives the season as spring through fall, or year-round in warm climates or indoors, and calls the species widespread in Europe, Oceania and North America. The collection MushroomExpert.Com illustrates came from Ohio and appeared in a potted plant growing in commercial potting mulch. Wikipedia separately says it can be found in wood debris throughout North America. GBIF's description record lists a distribution of Belgium (Flemish Region), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
