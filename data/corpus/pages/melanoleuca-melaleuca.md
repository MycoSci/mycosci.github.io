## Summary

Melanoleuca melaleuca (Pers.) Murrill (1911) is a mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae and the type species of the genus Melanoleuca. Wikipedia describes it as difficult to distinguish from related species, because it is variable, because the taxonomic criteria used have proved variable, and because authorities disagree on how the species should be defined. ## Etymology

Wikipedia states that both the genus and species names come from the same Ancient Greek words for black (μέλας / melas) and white (λευκόν / leukon). No other supplied source discusses the derivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species name originates with Persoon's 1801 Synopsis Methodica Fungorum, where it was published as Agaricus melaleucus. Patouillard's genus name Melanoleuca (1897) was based on it. Patouillard had first named the genus Melaleuca in 1887, with M. vulgaris as type species, and changed it to Melanoleuca in 1897. Melanoleuca has since been declared a nomen conservandum. Wikipedia says Murrill devised the combination Melanoleuca melaleuca in a 1911 article in Mycologia. It also notes that Species Fungorum treats M. vulgaris as a synonym of M. polioleuca, not of M. melaleuca. Wikipedia reports that some treatments of the genus define M. melaleuca by the absence of cheilocystidia. It adds that molecular work by Vizzini et al. indicates these cystidia can be present or absent within the same Melanoleuca species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is widely distributed, known from Asia, Africa, Europe, North America and Oceania. It says the species can be found in grassy places in woods, roadsides and heathland. It also says that, like all Melanoleuca, it is saprophytic on organic litter and is not associated with particular types of tree. The GBIF description record lists distribution entries for Denmark, Norway and Sweden only. No supplied source gives a fruiting season. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
