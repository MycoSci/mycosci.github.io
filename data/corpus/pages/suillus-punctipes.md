## Summary

Suillus punctipes, commonly known as the spicy suillus, is a bolete in the family Suillaceae. Charles Horton Peck described it in 1878 as a species of Boletus, and Rolf Singer transferred it to Suillus in 1945. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as an associate of eastern white pine in eastern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the American mycologist Charles Horton Peck described the fungus in 1878 as a species of Boletus, from material collected at Gansevoort, New York. Peck gave its distinguishing features as "its rhubarb-colored stem thickened at the base and the brownish color of the young hymenium". Rolf Singer transferred it to Suillus in 1945. MushroomExpert.Com likewise gives the original description as Peck 1878 and the combination in Suillus as Singer 1945, and says it was originally described from New York. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (a single field-guide source) describes the species as mycorrhizal with eastern white pine, growing alone or gregariously in summer and fall. It says the species can be found throughout the natural range of the host tree, roughly from Minnesota to Maine and the Carolinas, and is distributed in Midwestern and northeastern North America. The collections illustrated and described there are from Kentucky. Wikipedia separately states that the species has been recorded from Taiwan, and a GBIF distribution entry lists Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
