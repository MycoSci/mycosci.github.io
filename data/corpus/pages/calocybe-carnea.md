## Summary

Calocybe carnea (Bull.) Donk, 1962, is a species in the family Lyophyllaceae. It is a small pink-capped mushroom with white gills and a white spore print, and the sources describe it growing in grassy places such as lawns and meadows. It is recorded from Europe and North America, and MushroomExpert.Com also reports it from Oceania. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet carnea is derived from the Latin for "flesh-coloured". It also states that the genus name is derived from the Ancient Greek kalos, "pretty", and cubos, "head". This derivation comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the species was originally described as Agaricus carneus by the French mycologist Pierre Bulliard in 1792. It says the species has been through many name changes since. MushroomExpert.Com likewise gives the original description as Bulliard 1792, from France. Its author, Michael Kuo, regards Calocybe persicolor as a synonym. He notes that the separating characters depend on which author is consulted. He cites Kalamees (2018), who treats the two as distinct, and Fries (1821), whose original description conflicts with some of those characters. He also cites Læssøe & Petersen (2019) and Breitenbach & Kränzlin (1991), who doubt that they should be separated. He adds that a single-gene ITS study (Bellanger et al. 2015) suggests they may be the same. Wikipedia notes that a similar species, Rugosomyces persicolor, is sometimes recorded as a variety or variant of C. carnea. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes C. carnea as growing in grassy meadows, fields and on lawns, appearing in spring, summer and early autumn, usually after rain. It says the species is very rarely found in woods unless there is a grassy clearing, and that fruiting bodies often appear on fertilized farmland. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, gregariously, or in small clusters in grassy areas. He says it is usually found in cultivated or disturbed ground and occasionally in woods, in summer and fall. Kuo calls it widespread in Europe and in North America north of Mexico, but apparently not common in North America; he has seen it once in 30 years of collecting. He also reports it from Oceania, and his described collection is from a lawn in northern Michigan in 2007. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
