## Summary

Hygrophorus russula (Schaeff. ex Fr.) Bataille is a species of the waxy cap family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the pinkmottle woodwax. MushroomExpert.Com and Wikipedia both describe a russula-like mushroom with a reddish to pinkish, often streaked or spotted cap and firm, non-crumbly flesh. It is reported from Europe and North America, and Wikipedia also lists Russia, Japan and Korea. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name is derived from its reddish coloration, which is reminiscent of members of the genus Russula. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Jacob Christian Schäffer described the species as Agaricus russula in 1774, and that Claude Casimir Gillet placed it in the genus Tricholoma in 1878. MushroomExpert.Com gives the original description as from Bavaria (Schaeffer 1774). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and other hardwoods, growing scattered or gregariously, sometimes in fairy rings or arcs. It gives the season as late summer and fall, and also over winter in warmer climates, and calls the species widespread in Europe and North America. Its illustrated collections are from Illinois and Kentucky. Wikipedia says that in eastern North America it appears under oak from August to October, and later on the West Coast. Wikipedia adds that it is more common in the east of the continent than the west, and that it can be abundant some years, especially after rainfall, sometimes appearing in arcs or fairy rings. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
