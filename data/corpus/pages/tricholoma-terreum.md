## Summary

Tricholoma terreum (Schaeff.) P. Kumm. (1871) is a mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae, known in English as the grey knight or dirty tricholoma. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe a grey-capped species with a white spore print and no ring, associated with conifers in Europe and North America. MushroomExpert.Com cautions that several species fit the same general description and that microscopic features should be checked for a confident identification. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the name 'grey knight' comes from the species' discoloured gills. This is a single-source statement, and no source given here explains the origin of 'dirty tricholoma'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the fungus was first described as Agaricus terreus by Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1762, and as Agaricus myomyces by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1794, and that Paul Kummer gave it its current binomial in 1871. MushroomExpert.Com cites the Schäffer name as 1774, so the two sources disagree on that year. Wikipedia says almost all modern sources treat Tricholoma myomyces as a synonym of T. terreum, with exceptions: Bon and Courtecuisse separate T. myomyces for lowland mushrooms with white gills and a fleecy cap, and Moser separated it on the basis that the gills should go yellow. MushroomExpert.Com says North American treatments have generally used the name myomyces for a species with a cortina-like veil, as opposed to a terreum without one. It adds that European authors have shown the veil to be inconsistent and not correlated with other morphological features. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports fruiting bodies under conifers, particularly pine and spruce, in Europe from late summer to late autumn (September–December). It gives August to December for much of North America, and slightly later on the West Coast. Wikipedia also says the species may arise in parks near these trees, may grow in fairy rings, and generally occurs in quite densely populated groups that are not bunched. It states the species has been encountered under introduced pines in Australia and New Zealand, and has been recorded under exotic Pinus radiata plantations in Australia. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially pines but also spruce, fir and Douglas-fir, and more rarely with hardwoods, at least in Europe. It lists growth alone, scattered or gregarious, from spring through fall, in Europe and North America, and says its illustrated collections come from Italy, Colorado and Kentucky. The two sources give different fruiting seasons for Europe. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
