## Summary

Scleroderma michiganense (Guzmán) Guzmán 1970 is an earthball in the family Sclerodermataceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as an eastern, hardwood-associated species with a thick rind, a smooth to finely scaly surface that bruises slightly reddish and turns red with KOH, large reticulate spores, and clamp connections in the rind hyphae. The same source says the very similar Scleroderma hypogaeum is associated with conifers in western North America. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to MushroomExpert.Com, it is mycorrhizal with oaks and possibly other hardwoods. It is usually found in dry, sandy soil, growing gregariously or scattered, in summer and fall. The same source describes it as widely distributed in North America east of the Rocky Mountains, from Canada to the Gulf Coast, but most often reported from the upper Midwest. Its illustrated and described collections are from Michigan and Wisconsin. All of this comes from a single source. *[mushroomexpert]*
