## Summary

Cyptotrama asprata (Berk.) Redhead & Ginns, 1980, is a saprobic species of mushroom in the family Physalacriaceae. Sources describe a small fungus with a bright yellow to orange cap that is covered with spiky or granular tufts when young. It is widely distributed in tropical regions and is recorded in GBIF from dozens of countries. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described from Ceylon by Miles Joseph Berkeley in 1847. Later collections were described under other names, including Lentinus chrysopeplus from Cuba, Agaricus sabriusculus and Agaricus lacunosa from New York, Collybia lacunosa from Michigan, and Omphalia scabriuscula from Connecticut. Wikipedia reports, citing Redhead and Ginns's 1980 article, that the species has been given 28 names and placed in 14 genera. MushroomExpert.Com separately lists Collybia lacunosa and Cyptotrama chrysopepla as synonyms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes C. asprata as a saprobic fungus growing on the decaying wood of deciduous and coniferous trees. It lists hosts including white fir (Abies concolor), sugar maple (Acer saccharum) and other maples, grey alder (Alnus oblongifolia), beech (Fagus), spruce (Picea), ponderosa pine (Pinus ponderosa) and other pines, poplar (Populus) and oak (Quercus). MushroomExpert.Com likewise describes it as saprobic on deadwood of hardwoods or conifers, growing scattered to gregariously. Wikipedia says that in temperate North America specimens are typically collected from July through September. MushroomExpert.Com gives the season as summer, or over winter in warmer climates. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as widely distributed in eastern North America, also reported from Texas and the Southwest, and apparently absent in the northwest and on the West Coast. Wikipedia describes a pantropical distribution, with collections from Australia, southeastern Canada, China, Costa Rica, India, Hawaii, New Zealand, Japan and the Russian Far East, and says it is absent from Europe and northwestern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
