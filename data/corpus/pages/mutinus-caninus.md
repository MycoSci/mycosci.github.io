## Summary

Mutinus caninus (Schaeff.) Fr., the dog stinkhorn, is a member of the Phallaceae. Sources describe it as a small, slender, phallus-shaped stinkhorn with orange colouring and a dark spore-slime-covered tip, widespread in Europe and with few verified records in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name Mutinus refers to a Roman phallic deity, Mutinus Titinus, and that caninus means "dog-like" in Latin. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states the species was originally described from Germany (Schaeffer 1774) and gives the Fries 1849 citation. Wikipedia reports that Sanford Myron Zeller described an albino form, M. caninus var. albus, from collections in Oregon, and that Edward Angus Burt first mentioned it in the scientific literature in 1896. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously in gardens and cultivated areas or in woods, in summer and fall, and widespread in Europe. It states the species is not common in North America and may be limited to Ohio, where it is documented. The author reviewed online North American reports and judged them to be other species. Wikipedia describes it as usually found in small groups in leaf litter, on wood debris or on wooded roadsides, in both deciduous and coniferous woods, from summer to late autumn, and lists it as occurring in Europe, Britain and eastern North America. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
