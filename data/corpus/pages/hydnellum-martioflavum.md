## Summary

Hydnellum martioflavum (Snell, K.A.Harrison & H.A.C.Jacks.) E.Larss., K.H.Larss. & Kõljalg is a tooth fungus in the family Bankeraceae, recorded in Europe and North America. Wikipedia describes it as having fleshy, yellowish-brown to purplish-brown caps up to 10 cm across with downward-pointing spines underneath, growing in association with coniferous trees. The same source lists it as vulnerable in Switzerland. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described by Wally Snell, Kenneth A. Harrison and Henry Jackson in 1962 as Hydnum martioflavum, from collections made in Quebec and Nova Scotia, Canada. The GBIF record gives the basionym as Hydnum martioflavum, published in Lloydia 25: 161 (1962), with a holotype from Ste Anne de la Pocatière, Quebec, collected by H.A.C. Jackson and W.H. Snell on 13 September 1954 (BPI 259438). Wikipedia says Rudolph Arnold Maas Geesteranus transferred it to Sarcodon in 1964 and regarded his own Sarcodon armeniacus, described the year before, as a synonym. The GBIF record shows the combination in Hydnellum made by Larsson, Larsson and Kõljalg. Wikipedia adds that molecular phylogenetic analysis found it most closely related to H. caeruleum. The Wikipedia text is a single source; the GBIF record gives only the protologue details. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the fungus grows in association with coniferous trees and that the original Canadian collections, from Quebec and Nova Scotia, were made under spruce and balsam fir. The same source says its distribution in Europe more or less follows the distribution of pine trees, that its northern limit in Norway is Troms, and that it is considered vulnerable in Switzerland. These statements come from a single source (Wikipedia); no season or substrate is given. *[wikipedia]*
