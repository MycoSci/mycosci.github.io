## Summary

Phellodon niger (Fr.) P. Karst., published in 1881 and known in English as the black tooth, is a tooth fungus in the family Thelephoraceae. Wikipedia describes it as found in continental Europe and North America, and notes that molecular work suggests the North American populations are a genetically distinct but similar species. GBIF distribution records list Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Elias Magnus Fries described the species in 1815 as a Hydnum. Petter Karsten circumscribed the genus Phellodon in 1881 with three original species, and P. niger (first published with the epithet "nigrum") is the type species. Lucien Quélet's 1886 Calodon niger is listed as a synonym. Comparison of ITS sequences from United Kingdom collections with collections from the Southern United States showed 92–93% similarity, which Wikipedia says suggests the North American populations are a different species. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the fruitbodies are used to make a gray-blue or green dye. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that P. niger forms ectomycorrhizae with Norway spruce (Picea abies). Stable carbon-13 isotope analysis gave a metabolic signature close to that of saprotrophic fungi, which Wikipedia says may indicate it can obtain carbon from sources other than a tree host. It is reported from continental Europe, where it is widespread, and from North America. A preliminary British red-list assessment considers it rare, and it is considered vulnerable in Switzerland. All of this comes from a single independent source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
