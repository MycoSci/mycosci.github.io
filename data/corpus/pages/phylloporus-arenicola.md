## Summary

Phylloporus arenicola A.H. Sm. & Trappe (1972), the western gilled bolete, is a species in the family Boletaceae with its type locality in Oregon. It is recorded from the United States and Canada. Sources describe it as a mycorrhizal associate of pines that grows in sandy coastal soil and sand dunes of the Pacific Northwest, and MushroomExpert.Com calls it rare and known primarily from the Oregon coast. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet arenicola means "growing in sand". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was described as new to science in 1972 by Alexander H. Smith and James Trappe, and that Smith made the type collection near Pacific City, Oregon, in November 1970. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes fruit bodies growing singly, scattered, or in groups on the ground in sand dunes, in a mycorrhizal association with pine trees. It gives records from California, Oregon and Washington, and a West Coast fruiting period of November to May. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with pines, including lodgepole pine and bishop pine, usually growing alone in sandy coastal soil in winter, in coastal Oregon and coastal northern California. Kuo notes that his account is based partly on cited literature, because he did not collect the illustrated specimen himself. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
