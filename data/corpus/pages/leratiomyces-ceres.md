## Summary

Leratiomyces ceres (Cooke & Massee) Spooner & Bridge, 2008, is a member of the Strophariaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a mushroom with a bright red to orange cap and a dark purple-brown spore deposit, usually found growing gregariously on wood chips. MushroomExpert.Com describes a reddish-orange to brownish-orange cap and a dark purple-gray spore print. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the name Agaricus ceres was created in 1888 by Cooke and Massee for the white-stemmed species, then reclassified as Psilocybe ceres (1891) and Leratiomyces ceres (2008). It reports that the name Stropharia aurantiaca was used extensively but incorrectly for this mushroom. According to Wikipedia, Richard Fortey found in 2004 that the illustration defining that name shows L. squamosus var. thraustus, not L. ceres. MushroomExpert.Com says the mushroom was called Stropharia aurantiaca until DNA studies began to break up the stropharioid mushrooms. It describes a 2008 paper by Bridge and collaborators that supports two groups within the old Stropharia, and says Spooner and collaborators arrived at Leratiomyces for the second group. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it is usually found growing gregariously on wood chips, and that it is common on wood chips and lawns in North America, Europe, Australia and New Zealand. MushroomExpert.Com (single-source detail) describes it as saprobic, growing scattered or gregariously in woodchips or sawdust, lawns and gardens, fruiting fall through spring, and found from coastal California to British Columbia. MushroomExpert.Com states that the described collections are from California. It also says the species appears to be a landscaping "import" but that its origin is difficult to determine, and that it is found with increasing regularity in Europe. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
