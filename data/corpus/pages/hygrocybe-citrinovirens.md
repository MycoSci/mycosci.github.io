## Summary

Hygrocybe citrinovirens (J.E. Lange) Jul. Schäff. 1947, the Citrine Waxcap, is a gilled fungus in the family Hygrophoraceae. Wikipedia describes it as having a European distribution, occurring mainly in agriculturally unimproved grassland, and reports it as assessed globally as "vulnerable" on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species because of threats to that habitat. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described from Denmark in 1923 by the mycologist Jakob Emanuel Lange as Camarophyllus citrinovirens. Julius Schäffer transferred it to the genus Hygrocybe in 1947. Wikipedia also reports that molecular research based on DNA sequence analysis has shown it to be distinct and to belong in Hygrocybe sensu stricto. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as widespread but generally rare throughout Europe, with the largest populations in the north west of the continent. It states that, like other waxcaps, it occurs in old, agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns). This is a single-source account (Wikipedia; the iNaturalist entry is a declared derivative of it). *[wikipedia]*
