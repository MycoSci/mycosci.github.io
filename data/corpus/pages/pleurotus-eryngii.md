## Summary

Pleurotus eryngii (DC.) Quél. 1872 is a species in the family Pleurotaceae. Wikipedia describes it as an Old World species and as a species complex, with varieties distinguished by their associated host plants in the carrot family. The site catalog records it from many countries across Europe, Asia and North Africa, with fewer records from the Americas. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name derives from the fungus growing in association with the roots of Eryngium campestre or other Eryngium plants (English names 'sea holly' or 'eryngo'). This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia treats P. eryngii as a species complex and lists several described varieties, each tied to a different host plant: var. eryngii (DC.) Quél. 1872 with Eryngium; var. ferulae (Lanzi) Sacc. 1887 with Ferula communis; var. elaeoselini Venturella, Zervakis & La Rocca 2000 with Elaeoselinum asclepium; var. thapsiae Venturella, Zervakis & Saitta 2002 with Thapsia garganica; and var. tingitanus Lewinsohn 2002 with Ferula tingitana. It adds that molecular studies show P. nebrodensis to be closely related to, but distinct from, P. eryngii. No supplied source narrates the original description or the circumstances of the later transfers. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states that the species is widely cultivated and commercially produced, and gives Korea, where it was introduced in 1995, as an example. This is a single-source statement, and no cultivation method is described in the supplied sources. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia gives the natural range as extending from the Atlantic Ocean through the Mediterranean Basin (including North Africa) and Central Europe into Western Asia and India. It states that, unlike other Pleurotus species, which are primarily wood-decay fungi, the P. eryngii complex are also weak parasites on the roots of herbaceous plants in the carrot family (Apiaceae). Wikipedia also states that it grows in association with members of that family, including Eryngium species, and lists Ferula, Ferulago, Cachrys, Laserpitium and Diplotaenia among other associated genera. Wikipedia is the only source for these statements. No supplied source gives a fruiting season. *[wikipedia]*
