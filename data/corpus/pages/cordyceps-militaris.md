## Summary

Cordyceps militaris (L.) Fr. is a species in the family Cordycipitaceae, recorded from many countries including the Netherlands, United Kingdom, United States, Sweden and Japan. It is an insect-parasitic fungus whose orange to red club-shaped fruiting bodies grow from buried pupae or larvae. The Wikipedia entry says it is used in traditional Chinese medicine. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was originally described by Carl Linnaeus in 1753 as Clavaria militaris, and that it is the type species of the genus Cordyceps. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that in Asia the fruiting body is cooked as a mushroom in dishes such as chicken soup, pork bone soup and hot pot, that it is approved as a novel food in China, and that it is used in traditional Chinese medicine. The same source says it is marketed as a dietary supplement but lacks sufficient scientific evidence for safety or effectiveness. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states that C. militaris can be cultivated on a variety of media, including silkworm pupae, rice and liquid nutrition, and that it is cultivated in China, Vietnam, Taiwan and Indonesia. It also states that crops can be affected by pathogenic molds that parasitize and kill the fungus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as parasitic on buried larvae and pupae of insects, primarily moths and butterflies, growing alone or gregariously in summer and fall. It says the insect may be buried in the ground or in well-decayed wood, and that the species is widely distributed in North America but apparently more common east of the Rocky Mountains. Wikipedia describes it as spread throughout the Northern Hemisphere, with fruiting bodies appearing in Europe from August to November. A GBIF description record gives the substrate as lepidopteran larvae. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
