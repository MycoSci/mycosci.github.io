## Summary

Caloboletus radicans (Pers.) Vizzini, the rooting bolete or whitish bolete, is a large ectomycorrhizal bolete in the family Boletaceae. Wikipedia describes it as found in Europe under broad-leaved trees, fruiting in summer and autumn, with a pale buff or greyish-white cap, yellow pores and a stout stipe, and staining intensely blue when handled or cut. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin radic- 'root', referring to the fungus's rooting stipe. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the fungus was first described by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1801, who placed it in the genus Boletus. In 2014 Alfredo Vizzini transferred it to the new genus Caloboletus, based on molecular phylogenetic data. Wikipedia lists Boletus albidus as a later synonym. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it fruits during warm spells in summer and early autumn and forms ectomycorrhizal associations with a wide range of broad-leaved trees, including oaks (Quercus), beech (Fagus), hornbeam (Carpinus), chestnut (Castanea) and lime (Tilia). It is reported to grow on both calcareous (chalk) and acidic soils in southern England, where Wikipedia calls it common, and across much of Europe. *[wikipedia]*
