## Summary

Cuphophyllus virgineus (Wulfen) Kovalenko is a species of gilled mushroom in the family Hygrophoraceae, recommended in the UK as the snowy waxcap. Wikipedia describes a largely north temperate distribution, with fruit bodies typically produced in autumn. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small to medium-sized, whitish, waxy cap with a dry stem and gills that run down the stem, and notes that the cap sometimes develops brownish or yellowish shades over the center. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet comes from Latin "virgineus" (= pure white). This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1781 by the Austrian mycologist Franz Xaver von Wulfen as Agaricus virgineus. It was later combined in a number of different genera and transferred to Hygrocybe in 1969, before being moved to Cuphophyllus. Wikipedia reports that molecular research published in 2011, based on cladistic analysis of DNA sequences, found that it does not belong in Hygrocybe sensu stricto. Hygrocybe nivea, described by Scopoli in 1772, was sometimes distinguished by smaller, more slender fruit bodies but is now regarded as a synonym. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo, 2022) likewise says H. nivea was reduced to synonymy by Arnolds (1986). It adds that synonymies and taxa separated on minor differences abound, and quotes Boertmann (2000): "[t]he complex of taxa related to [Cuphophyllus virgineus] is badly in need of a revision." Kuo says no revision had been published in the 22 years since, to his knowledge. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Two independent sources describe habitat, and their wording differs by region. MushroomExpert.Com (Kuo) describes it as growing scattered or gregariously in grasslands in Europe, or in disturbed-ground areas in woods under hardwoods or conifers in North America. Kuo gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in warmer climates, and states that its precise ecological role is uncertain. Wikipedia describes it as growing in old, unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns) in Europe but in woodland elsewhere, with fruit bodies typically produced in autumn. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
