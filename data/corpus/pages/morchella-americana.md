## Summary

Morchella americana Clowez & Matherly (2012) is a species of fungus in the family Morchellaceae, with its type locality in Michigan. Wikipedia describes it as a North American species, common east of the Rocky Mountains, with a cone-shaped, honeycomb-ridged cap about 3–10 cm tall and a whitish, hollow stipe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet americana refers to the species' occurrence in North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was described as new to science in 2012 (Clowez and Matherly). It also states that in 2014 Richard et al. clarified its taxonomic status, retaining the name Morchella americana of Clowez and Matherly (2012) over M. esculentoides. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states the species is found throughout North America except the Gulf Coast, and is common east of the Rocky Mountains in a range stretching from Ontario south to Texas, Arkansas, Alabama, Georgia and South Carolina. In western North America it is said to typically occur under hardwood, especially cottonwood in river bottoms, or with apple trees or ashes in forest, riparian or urban settings. Wikipedia also reports that the phylogenetic species 'Mes-4' (M. americana) has been found in Turkey, France and Germany, but is suspected of having been introduced to Europe from North America. A GBIF description record lists a distribution of Vermont.
