## Summary

Caloboletus calopus (Pers.) Vizzini, 2014, is a species of bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the bitter beech bolete, bitter bolete or scarlet-stemmed bolete. It was first described as Boletus calopus by Persoon in 1801 and is the type species of Caloboletus. Wikipedia reports that it is not considered edible because of an intensely bitter taste that persists after cooking. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific name is derived from the Greek καλος/kalos ('pretty') and πους/pous ('foot'), referring to the brightly coloured stipe. It adds that the German name, Schönfußröhrling or 'pretty-foot bolete', is a literal translation. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was originally published as Boletus olivaceus by Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1774, but that name is unavailable because it was later sanctioned for another species. Gmelin's 1792 synonym Boletus lapidum is also illegitimate. Persoon described it as Boletus calopus in 1801. It was later moved to Dictyopus by Quélet (1886) and to Tubiporus by Maire (1937). Molecular phylogenetics showed it was only distantly related to the type species of Boletus, and Alfredo Vizzini transferred it in 2014 to the new genus Caloboletus, with C. calopus as the type species. The basionym and synonym citations are also listed in Wikispecies. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to Wikipedia, this is an ectomycorrhizal species that grows in coniferous and deciduous woodland, often at higher altitudes and especially under beech and oak. Fruit bodies occur singly or in large groups. In Northern Europe it is reported on chalky ground from July to December. Wikipedia also lists North America's Pacific Northwest and Michigan, and records from Turkey, Pakistan, China, Korea and Taiwan. It notes that Miller and Watling (1968) suggested the typical form does not occur in the United States, while other authors reached the opposite conclusion. This is a single-source account (Wikipedia; the iNaturalist text is derived from it). *[wikipedia]*
