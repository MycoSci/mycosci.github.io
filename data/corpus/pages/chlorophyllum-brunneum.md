## Summary

Chlorophyllum brunneum (Farl. & Burt) Vellinga, 2002, is a member of the family Agaricaceae. It is one of three closely related species known as the shaggy parasol, along with C. rhacodes and C. olivieri. The shaggy parasol species are found in North America, Europe and Southern Africa, and C. brunneum is also found in Australia. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was originally described as Lepiota brunnea by Farlow & Burt in 1929, from material collected in a Massachusetts greenhouse. Wikipedia reports that the taxon, formerly treated as Macrolepiota rhacodes var. brunneum, was raised to species rank as Chlorophyllum brunneum. The Australian Systematic Botany checklist (2003) gives C. brunneum as the correct name for the species often referred to as M. rachodes in Australia. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (single source) describes C. brunneum as saprobic. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously in gardens, waste places and lawns, or in greenhouses, and sometimes forms fairy rings. Fruiting is in summer and fall, or over winter in coastal California. It is described as common on the West Coast of North America and in the Denver area, and rare in northeastern North America. The collections illustrated and described there are from California. *[mushroomexpert]*
