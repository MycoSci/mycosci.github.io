## Summary

Tricholoma saponaceum (Fr.) P. Kumm. 1871 is a gilled mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae, with a distinctive soap-like odour and flesh that can stain pinkish to orange, especially at the stem base. GBIF records it across Europe and North America, among other regions. Sources suggest the name may cover a species complex, particularly in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet saponaceum is derived from the Latin meaning 'of or pertaining to soap'. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the English names soap-scented toadstool, soapy knight and soap tricholoma, along with the French Tricolome à odeur de savon and the German Seifenritterling, all relate to the mushroom's soapy scent. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries first described the species in 1818 as Agaricus saponaceus, and Paul Kummer placed it in Tricholoma in 1871. Wikipedia also notes that the variety ardosiacum, with a dark blue-grey cap, was described by Giacomo Bresadola, and that the species may represent a complex of two or more species. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) states that, under the strictest current definition (Christensen & Heilmann-Clausen 2013), T. saponaceum is a European species originally described from Sweden. Those authors separate two further European species from the saponaceum group: T. rapipes and T. sudum. Kuo considers that the North American material placed under this name almost certainly involves several species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a terrestrial mushroom of coniferous and deciduous woodland in Europe and North America, more often under conifers in North America, fruiting from late summer to late autumn. It lists spruce, live oak, tanoak and madrone as associates in the western United States, and oak in central and southern Europe. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers, growing alone, gregariously or in clusters, in summer and fall, or in winter and spring in warmer areas. For the strictly defined European species, it gives an association with hardwoods, possibly but not certainly with conifers as well. Its North American collections come from tanoak forests in California, spruce-fir forests in Colorado and oak-hickory forests in Illinois. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
