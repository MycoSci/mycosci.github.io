## Summary

Mycena polygramma (Bull.) Gray, 1821, commonly called the grooved bonnet, is a species in the family Mycenaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a small, pale gray-brown mushroom with a broadly conical cap and pinkish gills, found in small troops on stumps and branches, and as bioluminescent. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states the specific epithet is derived from the Greek πολύς meaning "many" and γραμμή, or "line". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the French mycologist Jean Bulliard first called it Agaricus polygrammus in 1789, and that Elias Magnus Fries later sanctioned that name in his Systema Mycologicum. Samuel Frederick Gray transferred it to Mycena in 1821, when he raised many of Fries' sub-generic divisions to genus level. Wikipedia lists Agaricus chloroticus, described by Junghuhn in 1830, as the only known taxonomic synonym. The spine lists more synonyms, so that statement is Wikipedia's own. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to Wikipedia, fruit bodies grow in groups or small clusters on stumps, branches, twigs or buried wood. They grow under hardwoods, particularly oak, maple and basswood, and occasionally on coniferous trees. In North America it has been collected in North Carolina, Massachusetts, New York and Michigan, where it fruits from June to October. Wikipedia calls it rare in North America and common in Europe, including Great Britain. This is a single-source account, since the iNaturalist text is a copy of Wikipedia. *[wikipedia]*
