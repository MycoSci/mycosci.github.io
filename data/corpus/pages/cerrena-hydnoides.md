## Summary

Cerrena hydnoides (Sw.) Zmitr. (2001) is a species of Polyporales in the family Cerrenaceae. Some sources treat it under the name Hexagonia hydnoides, and its generic placement is disputed. GBIF holds occurrence records from the Americas, Africa, Australia and France. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia, a single source, says the name refers to the species' distinctive dark hairy bristles on the cap, with hydnoides meaning 'relating to Hydnum', a genus of toothed fungi. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia, a single source that treats the species as Hexagonia hydnoides, says researchers dispute whether it belongs in Cerrena or Hexagonia. It reports that a study published in 2001 reassigned the species to Cerrena as Cerrena hydnoides, and that Species Fungorum lists that as the accepted name, though not all modern literature follows it. It also reports that a 2013 phylogenetic study of Polyporales genera suggested moving the species to Pogonomyces. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, a single source, describes the species as saprobic, growing on dead wood, particularly hardwood, in tropical and subtropical forests. A 2025 inventory in Reserva Ecologica Arenillas, El Oro, Ecuador, lists Cerrena hydnoides among species found in both the dry forest and the salt flat ecosystems. A GBIF description record cites a specimen from Honda, Tolima, Colombia, collected by F. W. Pennell. *[wikipedia, lit_1, gbif_desc]*
