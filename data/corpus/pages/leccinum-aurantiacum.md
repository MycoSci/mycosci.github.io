## Summary

Leccinum aurantiacum (Bull.) Gray, 1821, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae. Wikipedia describes it as having a large, characteristically red-capped fruiting body. Both Wikipedia and Mushroom World note that material recorded from North America under this name may not be the same species as the European one. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species fruits in summer and autumn in forests in Europe and North America, and that the association with its host tree is mycorrhizal. In Europe it has traditionally been associated with poplar. Wikipedia also reports it among oak and other deciduous trees, including beech, birch, chestnut, willow and Tilia, and says it is not known to associate with conifers in Europe. According to Wikipedia, North American records from coniferous forests may actually be L. vulpinum, and records from deciduous forests have been attributed to other North American species such as L. insigne and L. brunneum, so L. aurantiacum may be absent from North America altogether. Mushroom World, which gives its location as Europe, says it typically grows solitary or in small scattered groups on soil under aspen. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
