## Summary

Rhizina undulata Fr. (1815) is a fungus in the family Rhizinaceae, order Pezizales, known as the doughnut fungus or pine firefungus. Wikipedia describes it as having crust-like, dark purple-brown fruit bodies attached to the ground by root-like rhizoids. It grows on burned ground or conifer debris and is parasitic on conifer seedlings, and it has caused considerable damage to tree plantations. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet undulata means "wavy" or "undulating". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was first described in 1774 as Helvella inflata by the German polymath Jacob Christian Schäffer, and it acquired its current name in 1815 through Elias Magnus Fries's publication. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a widespread fungus that grows on burned soil or conifer debris and prefers acidic soil. Its spores are activated by the heat of fire. It is regarded as saprobic but can also attack conifers parasitically, and it is a known cause of root disease in conifer plantations where slash has been burned after clearcutting. Wikipedia reports it from northern Asia, Europe and North America, and states that it has been introduced into southern Africa. Mushroom World gives a similar account of clustered growth on burned soil or conifer debris, and gives a season of early summer to early autumn. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
