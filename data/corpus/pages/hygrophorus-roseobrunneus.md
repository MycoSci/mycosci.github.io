## Summary

Hygrophorus roseobrunneus Murrill (1916) is a waxy cap in the family Hygrophoraceae, described from California and recorded mainly in North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes a sticky, pinkish-tan to brown cap 2.5–6 cm across, white gills, a white stem with a granular-mealy apex, and a white spore print. The species is listed there as mycorrhizal with oaks. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species epithet roseobrunneus means "rose-brown", and notes that the color of the species is fairly variable, ranging from pinkish to tan, brown, pinkish brown, or pale brownish. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com gives Murrill 1916 as the original description, from California. It lists Hygrophorus roseibrunneus (spelled with an i) as a synonym. Its author considers the West Coast and eastern North American versions the same species under current definitions, but writes that it would not surprise him if they were distinct phylogenetic species. This is one author's opinion, not an established finding. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the species is mycorrhizal with oaks and grows scattered or gregariously in late fall and winter. In the Midwest and eastern North America it is often one of the last mycorrhizal mushrooms of the year, appearing in November and December under oaks. On the West Coast it appears under live oaks in fall and winter. The source describes it as widespread in North America, and its illustrated and described collections are from Illinois and Ohio. *[mushroomexpert]*
