## Summary

Ganoderma lucidum (Curtis) P. Karst. (1881) is the type species of Ganoderma, recorded in Polyporaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a red-colored, laccate species growing on decaying hardwood. The same source says the name has long been applied to many different Ganoderma species, and that products sold as 'reishi' or 'G. lucidum' often contain other species, so identification under this name is unreliable. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the genus name Ganoderma as derived from Greek ganos (γάνος, 'brightness, sheen', hence 'shining') and derma (δέρμα, 'skin'), and the epithet lucidum from Latin lucidus, 'light, bright, clear'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Karsten first described the genus Ganoderma in 1881 and included only one species, G. lucidum (Curtis) Karst. The species had previously been called Boletus lucidus Curtis (1781) and then Polyporus lucidus (Curtis) Fr. (1821). Patouillard later revised the genus to include all species with pigmented spores, adhering tubes and laccate-crusted cuticles, giving 48 species in his 1889 monograph. Wikipedia reports that molecular phylogenetic analyses place G. lucidum closer to the North American G. tsugae and G. oregonense than to G. sichuanense (= G. lingzhi), and that samples labeled G. lucidum fell into five of six clades in one study. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states that the species is cultivated using intensive technology in Japan, Korea and China, and in some South American countries. It cites a 2020 study on cultivation on side-streams and waste, and says fruiting bodies form less well on softwoods than on other woods such as aspen. The same source notes that G. lucidum can still degrade Pinus sylvestris wood. This is a single-cluster account, and the same source warns that cultivation kits and products labeled G. lucidum often contain other species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as growing on decaying hardwood trees. A GBIF-hosted description reports that in Europe basidiocarps grow mostly on oaks but also on other trees, across 24 woody plant genera. It often colonises old deciduous trees in parks and botanical gardens but occurs mainly in forests. Another GBIF-hosted record, in Spanish, gives a habit of gregarious to cespitose, a substrate of dead or living trunks in sunny places, tropical rainforest habitat, and an altitude of 500-1000 (units not stated). Because the name has been applied to several species, these records may not all refer to the same taxon. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
