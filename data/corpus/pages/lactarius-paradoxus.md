## Summary

Lactarius paradoxus Beardslee & Burl. (1940) is a milkcap in the family Russulaceae, originally described from Florida and recorded in the United States, Canada, Puerto Rico, the Dominican Republic, Mexico and Slovenia. Sources describe a blue to gray cap, a dark red latex, and staining green with age, and record it from eastern and southern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says the Greek root of "paradoxus" simply means "odd". In his view the name fits because the species looks like an indigo-subpurpureus combination, depending on how you hold it. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, Henry Beardslee and Gertrude Burlingham named the species in 1940. They commented that seen from above it looks like Lactarius indigo, while the underside closely resembles Lactaria subpurpurea (now Lactarius subpurpureus). MushroomExpert.Com states it was originally described from Florida. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with pines, especially loblolly and long-leaf pine. It grows alone or gregariously, often in grassy areas near the host trees. That source gives the season as summer and fall and says it is fairly widely distributed in eastern North America, more common in the lower Appalachians and the southeastern states. It is also recorded from the Caribbean and the Gulf Coast of Mexico. Wikipedia separately says it fruits from early fall to late winter in the southern and eastern United States, in grass and under pines, and is mycorrhizal with pine and oak. The two sources give different fruiting seasons. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
