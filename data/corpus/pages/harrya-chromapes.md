## Summary

Harrya chromapes (Frost) Halling, Nuhn, Osmundson & Manfr. Binder, 2012, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the yellowfoot bolete or chrome-footed bolete. Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe a pinkish young cap, pink to reddish scabers on the stem, and a chrome-yellow stem base. It was long placed in Boletus, Leccinum and Tylopilus before the genus Harrya was created for it in 2012. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet chromapes is Latin for "yellow foot". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Charles Christopher Frost first described the species as Boletus chromapes in his 1874 catalogue of the bolete fungi of New England, in which he published 22 new bolete species. Rolf Singer later placed it in Leccinum (1947) because of the scabrous dots on the stipe. Smith and Thiers (1968) treated it as a Tylopilus, giving greater weight to the pinkish-brown spore print. In 2012 molecular analysis of large-subunit ribosomal DNA and translation elongation factor 1α showed it belonged to a distinct lineage in the Boletaceae, and the genus Harrya was circumscribed with H. chromapes as the type species alongside the newly described H. atriceps. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently notes that field guides usually list the species as Tylopilus chromapes or Leccinum chromapes, and that a new genus was erected to hold it and its rarely collected Costa Rican relative H. atriceps. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes H. chromapes as an ectomycorrhizal species whose fruit bodies grow singly to scattered on soil. In North America it is usually found in forests containing conifers, Betulaceae and oak, from eastern Canada south to Georgia and Alabama and west to Michigan and Mississippi, and it is also recorded from Mexico. Wikipedia gives the fruiting season as late spring to late summer. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with a wide variety of hardwoods and conifers in diverse ecosystems, growing alone, scattered or gregariously, and widely distributed east of the Great Plains, in Texas and in Mexico. It gives the season as summer and fall, and also over winter on the Gulf Coast. Wikipedia adds that in Costa Rica the species associates with oak, and that in Asia it is known from India (West Bengal), Taiwan, Japan and China, where it associates with trees of the beech and pine families. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
