## Summary

Phyllotopsis nidulans (Pers.) Singer, the mock oyster or orange oyster, is a fungus in the family Phyllotopsidaceae. Its fruit body is an orange, hairy, fan-shaped cap with crowded orange gills and little or no stem. It is described as often having a foul odor and grows on dead wood. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet nidulans means "partly encased or lying in a cavity". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Christian Hendrik Persoon first described the species in 1798 as Agaricus nidulans, and that it is the type species of the genus Phyllotopsis. MushroomExpert.Com notes that the species has traditionally been placed in the Tricholomataceae, but DNA studies have not supported this. It says Lodge and collaborators (2013) informally placed it in the "basal Hygrophoroid clade", and that its placement is still being worked out. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes P. nidulans as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods and conifers, often fairly recently dead wood with the bark still attached. It is said to cause a white, stringy rot and to grow gregariously or in overlapping clusters. The fruiting season given is fall and spring, or over winter in warm climates. The same source describes it as widely distributed in North America, with illustrated collections from Illinois, Kentucky, Arkansas, Québec and California. Wikipedia describes a wider distribution in temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere, with a range that extends north to Alaska and includes Costa Rica and Korea. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
