## Summary

Terana coerulea (Lam.) Pers. is a crust fungus in the family Phanerochaetaceae, commonly called the cobalt crust fungus. Wikipedia describes it as saprobic and resupinate, with a dark blue fruiting body 2–6 mm thick that is velvety or waxy when moist and crusty and brittle when dry. It was chosen as fungus of the year for 2009 by the German Mycological Society. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia (a single-source account), the species was first described in 1779 by Jean-Baptiste Lamarck under the name Byssus caerulea. The same source says the genus name Terana was devised by Michel Adanson in 1763 for similar crust fungi, and that Parmasto proposed the genus Pulcherricium in 1968 for this one species, although Terana is the better-established name. It also notes that both the spellings coerulea and caerulea are found frequently in the literature. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it is usually found in warm, damp hardwood forests on the undersides of fallen logs and branches of deciduous trees, and that it usually grows on dead deciduous wood, often ash, maple, oak and hazel. The same source describes a worldwide distribution in warmer climates, with reports from Asia, Africa, New Zealand, Eastern North America, the Canary Islands, Europe, Taiwan, Thailand and Turkey. The GBIF description record lists distribution entries for Biobío Region, DK, NO and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
