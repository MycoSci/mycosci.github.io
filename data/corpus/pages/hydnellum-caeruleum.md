## Summary

Hydnellum caeruleum (Hornem.) P. Karst. is a stipitate toothed fungus in the family Bankeraceae (order Thelephorales), with a recorded distribution across Europe, North America and temperate Asia. Sources describe a bluish-capped mushroom with an orange stem and blue-zoned flesh, mycorrhizal with conifers and in some regions with hardwoods. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia credits Jens Wilken Hornemann with the original description (1825) and Petter Adolf Karsten with the transfer to Hydnellum in 1879. It lists Hydnum cyaneotinctum (Orris Island, Maine, 1903) and Hydnellum/Hydnum/Sarcodon alachuanum (Alachua, Florida, 1940) as synonyms. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) treats H. alachuanum and H. cyaneotinctum as synonyms only 'for the time being'. It describes them as eastern North American, hardwood-associated forms that may be resurrected if DNA analysis finds several genetically distinct species within H. caeruleum. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the fungus is prized by mushroom dyers and can yield blue, green and brown dyes depending on the mordant used. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as ectomycorrhizal and often found in humus beneath conifers, commonly in pine and spruce ecosystems. It also notes a Scottish study in which the fungus was associated with Arctostaphylos uva-ursi at a treeless site. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers in most areas, but also with live oaks and other hardwoods in coastal California and on the Gulf Coast. It grows alone or gregariously, in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates, and is widely distributed in North America. GBIF distribution records list Flanders, Denmark, Norway, Madeira, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
