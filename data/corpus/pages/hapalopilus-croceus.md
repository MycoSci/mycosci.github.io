## Summary

Hapalopilus croceus (Pers.) Donk, 1933, is a polypore in the family Phanerochaetaceae, known in English as the Orange Polypore. It is a bright orange, bracket-forming species recorded on oak wood, and Wikipedia reports it as globally rare and assessed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a medium-sized orange polypore with angular pores and a KOH reaction that turns dark purple on all parts. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Christian Hendrik Persoon originally described the species in 1796 as Boletus croceus, and Marinus Anton Donk transferred it to Hapalopilus in 1933, giving the name in current use. ## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a white-rot polypore that specialises in decaying coarse, veteran oak wood and, more rarely, Castanea wood. It says the core range is in Europe, where the species is confined to old, large-stemmed oaks, and that confirmed records also come from eastern North America, China and Japan. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and parasitic on oak wood, causing a white rot of the heartwood. It grows alone or in small groups in summer and fall and is widely distributed east of the Great Plains in North America; the illustrated collection is from Ohio. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
