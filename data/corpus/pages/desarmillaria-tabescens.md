## Summary

Desarmillaria tabescens (Scop.) R.A. Koch & Aime, 2017, is a species in the family Physalacriaceae, known in English as the ringless honey fungus or ringless honey mushroom. Wikipedia, which treats it under the name Armillaria tabescens, describes it as a plant pathogen whose mycelium is bioluminescent. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (under the name Armillaria tabescens) describes a pathogen of woody plants that attacks trees already stressed or wounded. Reported hosts include fir, oak, poplar, eucalyptus and almond, and it was recorded most often on fruit and ornamental trees in one Albanian survey. It is described as occurring in warm, dry regions. In Europe it tends to be in southern areas, with records from south-east England, France, Hungary, Italy, Portugal and Greece, and rarely Germany and Switzerland. It has been found from sea level to 1,300 m. Fruiting bodies are described as abundant when the soil is moist, and it is said to fruit in September before Armillaria mellea and Armillaria gallica. In Japan it is described as very rare. Mushroom World, a single source, gives the distribution as Europe and North America. It describes fruiting from late summer to autumn in dense clusters around the bases of hardwood trees, particularly oaks, and on buried roots or stumps. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
