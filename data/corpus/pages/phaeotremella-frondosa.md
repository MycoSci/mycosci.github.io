## Summary

Phaeotremella frondosa (Fr.) Spirin & Malysheva, 2018 is a jelly fungus in the family Tremellaceae; Tremella frondosa is a synonym. Sources describe it as producing brown, leafy-lobed, gelatinous fruit bodies and as a parasite of Stereum fungi on the wood of broadleaf trees. It is recorded from many countries in North America, Europe and Asia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet "frondosa" means "leafy", with reference to the shape of the fruit bodies. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Tremella frondosa was first published in 1822 by Elias Magnus Fries. It was not clearly distinguished from Tremella foliacea until DNA-sequence-based molecular research showed that the name Tremella foliacea covered several similar but distinct species. Phaeotremella frondosa is the name adopted for the widespread species on broadleaf trees, while Phaeotremella foliacea is restricted to conifers. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) attributes the current treatment to Spirin and collaborators (2018). It notes that many field guides list this species as "Tremella foliacea". It also cautions that the 2018 study relied mainly on European collections and only a few North American ones (from British Columbia), so further changes are possible. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes P. frondosa as a parasite of Stereum species, including S. hirsutum and S. rugosum. It grows on the host's hyphae in the wood rather than on the host's fruit bodies. Fruit bodies are typically found on dead, attached or recently fallen branches of broadleaf trees. MushroomExpert.Com names Stereum ostrea and Stereum complicatum as hosts, on the deadwood of hardwoods. It gives the season as spring through fall, or over winter in warmer climates, and describes the species as widely distributed in North America; its illustrated collections are from Illinois. Wikipedia gives the range as North America, Europe and northern Asia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
