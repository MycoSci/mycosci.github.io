## Summary

Aureoboletus russellii (Frost) G. Wu & Zhu L. Yang (2016) is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, formerly placed in Boletellus. Wikipedia describes it as having a coarsely shaggy stem and a velvety cap that cracks into patches with age. It is recorded from eastern North America and parts of Asia. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet russellii honors the American botanist and Unitarian minister John Lewis Russell, the first collector of the species. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first named Boletus russellii by Charles Christopher Frost in 1878, from collections made in New England. Edouard-Jean Gilbert transferred it to Boletellus in 1931, and Gang Wu and Zhu L. Yang transferred it to Aureoboletus in 2016. The supplied sources do not give the reasons for the transfers. MushroomExpert.Com also lists Boletus russellii and Boletellus russellii as previous names. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as growing singly or scattered on the ground in association with oak, hemlock and pine in eastern North America, with a range from eastern Canada south to Central America and west to Michigan and southern Arizona. It also reports the species from Asia, including Korea and Taiwan. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and other hardwoods, also sometimes reported under conifers. It says the fruit bodies grow alone, scattered or gregariously, in summer and fall. Its stated range is North America east of the Rocky Mountains, from the northeast to the upper Midwest and southeastern United States, through Texas into Mexico, and also in southern Arizona. The MushroomExpert.Com collections that were illustrated and described are from Illinois and Michigan. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
