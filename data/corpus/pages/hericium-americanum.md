## Summary

Hericium americanum Ginns (1984) is a tooth fungus in the family Hericiaceae, originally described from Pennsylvania. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as North America's only Hericium species that combines long spines with a branched fruiting body. Wikipedia reports that it was designated Vermont's state mushroom on May 7, 2024. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes the species as saprobic and possibly parasitic. It grows alone or gregariously on dead hardwood logs and stumps, or from the wounds of living hardwoods, and is also sometimes found on conifer wood. It fruits in late summer and fall. Kuo reports it as widespread in North America east of the Great Plains, especially north of the 38th Parallel (roughly Interstate 70), and also recorded from Central America. He states that western North American reports online probably represent misidentifications. Wikipedia describes it as commonly found on decaying trees in the northern United States and Canada, and says it grows exceptionally well in temperate deciduous forests. Wikipedia also says that in the Pacific Northwest it can be found in October and November, which conflicts with Kuo's statement about western reports (see disagreements). Mushroom World describes it as growing on dead broadleaf trees and on fallen trunks and branches, and as perhaps initially parasitic on dying broadleaf trees. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
