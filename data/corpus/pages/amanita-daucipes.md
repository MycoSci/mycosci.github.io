## Summary

Amanita daucipes (Sacc.) Lloyd, 1898, is a species in the family Amanitaceae, recorded in the United States and Mexico. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a large Amanita with orangish to pinkish-brown veil warts on the cap and a large basal bulb. Both also describe a strong, unpleasant odor. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet daucipes means "carrot foot". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the shape of the large basal bulb, with its blunt short rooting base, is suggestive of the common names carrot-footed lepidella, carrot-foot amanita and turnip-foot amanita. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes A. daucipes as mycorrhizal, growing solitary or scattered on the ground in mixed coniferous and deciduous forests, especially those dominated by oak. It lists hickory (Carya) and birch (Betula) as other associated trees. It names Maryland, North Carolina, New Jersey, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Tennessee, Virginia, West Virginia, Kentucky and Texas as states of occurrence. It also notes a predilection for disturbed soil such as roadsides, and says the southern end of the range extends to Sonora, Mexico. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks, often found in mixed woods where oaks and conifers both occur. It says the mushrooms grow alone, scattered or sometimes gregariously in summer and fall, and are most common in the Appalachian Mountains. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
