## Summary

Russula claroflava Grove (1888) is a member of the family Russulaceae, described from Great Britain. The supplied sources describe a yellow-capped species whose surfaces turn grey with age or bruising. They place it with birch in damp to boggy sites in Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin clarus 'bright' or 'clear', and flava 'light yellow'. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was described in 1888 by William Bywater Grove, based on a specimen from Sutton Park in the English Midlands. Wikispecies gives the original reference as Grove, W.B. 1888, 'Wayside Notes', The Midland Naturalist 11:265-266. It also lists Russula ochroleuca var. claroflava (Grove) Cooke 1890 among the synonyms. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species appears in summer and autumn, usually with birch (Betula) or aspen (Populus), on heaths and moors. It prefers damp places near ponds or lakes, often occurring in sphagnum, and is occasionally found in drier places. Wikipedia gives its range as Britain, northern Europe and North America. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes the species as mycorrhizal with various birches, growing scattered or gregariously in summer and fall in northern and montane Europe and North America. Kuo notes that European treatments insist on a birch association. He cites Bills and Miller (1984) for the Appalachian form also associating with red spruce and Fraser fir. The collection he illustrates and describes is from North Carolina, associated with red spruce at about 6600 feet. Mushroom World describes it as growing with birch in very damp or boggy woodland. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
