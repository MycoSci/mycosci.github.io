## Summary

Osteina obducta (Berk.) Donk, 1966, is a polypore in the family Dacryobolaceae (Polyporales), and it is the type species of Osteina. Sources describe it as a brown-rot fungus on the wood of conifers, with fruit bodies that become extremely hard when dry. GBIF holds occurrence records from many countries across North America, Europe and Asia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the fungus was first described by Berkeley in 1845 from "British North America", which it glosses as what is now the United States from the Mississippi River eastward. Wikipedia states that Marinus Anton Donk circumscribed the genus and species in 1966, making Osteina obducta the type species. MushroomExpert.Com lists Polyporus zelleri, Polyporus osseus and Oligoporus obductus as synonyms. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of conifers, rarely reported on the deadwood of hardwoods. It causes a brown rot and grows alone or gregariously, usually in overlapping clusters, and is annual. It is described as fairly widely distributed in North America but more commonly reported from montane and northern areas, and as also known from central Europe, China and Japan. The collections illustrated there are from Colorado. Wikipedia likewise states that it causes a brown rot in gymnosperm wood. The GBIF description record lists distribution as NO; SE; Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
