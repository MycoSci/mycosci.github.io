## Summary

Lepiota clypeolaria, the shield dapperling or shaggy-stalked Lepiota, is a mushroom in the family Agaricaceae, originally described by Bulliard and transferred to Lepiota by Kummer in 1871. Wikipedia describes it as a common species with a brownish cap, a shaggy stipe with a collapsed, sheathing ring or ring zone, and spindle-shaped spores, widely distributed in northern temperate zones. Wikipedia considers it poisonous. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described in 1789 as Agaricus clypeolarius by the French mycologist Jean Baptiste François Bulliard, and that Paul Kummer transferred it to Lepiota in 1871. MushroomExpert.Com notes that the name has been widely applied to a brownish, shaggy-stemmed lepiotoid mushroom, and that DNA research by Vellinga (2000, 2001a) found at least two species matching that description: the 'true' L. clypeolaria and L. magnispora. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that fruit bodies grow singly or in small groups on the ground in deciduous and coniferous forests, with fruiting in autumn. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing scattered or gregariously in forest litter under conifers in late summer and fall. Its illustrated collections are from high elevations in Colorado, and it says the North American distribution of the true L. clypeolaria is uncertain. Kuo reports finding it only in the Rocky Mountains. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
