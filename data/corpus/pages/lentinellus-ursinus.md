## Summary

Lentinellus ursinus (Fr.) Kühner 1926 is a species of fungus in the family Auriscalpiaceae (Russulales). MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a stemless, wood-growing, fan- to kidney-shaped species with saw-toothed gills and a white spore print, and notes that it is difficult to separate from other pleurotoid Lentinellus species, such as L. vulpinus and L. castoreus, without microscopy or a chemical test. GBIF descriptions list distribution records for Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com says the common name 'Bear Lentinellus' is a translation of the Latin name ursinus. Its author says he cannot tell what Fries was thinking when he chose the name, and that nothing about the mushroom strikes him as bear-like. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

MushroomExpert.Com says the species is sometimes called the 'Bear Lentinellus', a translation of its Latin name ursinus. It gives no further history of the common name. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the species was originally described from Sweden (Fries 1821) and the combination is credited to Kühner (1926). The same source says a form, Lentinellus ursinus robustus, was designated by Petersen (2004). *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes L. ursinus as saprobic, growing in groups or in shelf-like clusters on the wood of hardwoods. The form robustus usually grows on the deadwood of conifers instead. That source gives the season as summer and fall and says the species is widespread in Europe and North America and present in eastern Asia. Its illustrated collections are from Illinois, Michigan and Ohio. Wikipedia states that in North America it fruits from October to March on the West Coast and from July to October elsewhere. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
