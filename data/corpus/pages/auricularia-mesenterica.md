## Summary

Auricularia mesenterica (Dicks.) Pers., 1822, known as the tripe fungus, is a species in the family Auriculariaceae. Wikipedia describes gelatinous fruit bodies that form in coalescing tiers on stumps and logs. They are partly pileate, with hirsute, zoned caps, and partly resupinate, with smooth to wrinkled undersurfaces spreading over the wood. It gives the mature fruit bodies as typically 3 to 7 cm across, with a white spore print. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is a Latin adjective formed from the Ancient Greek word μεσεντέριον (mesentérion), "middle intestine", from μεσο- (meso-, "middle, center") and ἔντερον (énteron, "intestine"), referring to the fungus's shape. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was described from England in 1785 by James Dickson as Helvella mesenterica, and Christiaan Hendrik Persoon transferred it to Auricularia in 1822. It was long treated as cosmopolitan and applied to collections from America, Asia, Australia and Europe. Wikipedia reports that molecular (DNA sequence) research has since shown A. mesenterica as previously understood to be a complex of related species, with A. mesenterica sensu stricto confined to Europe and Central Asia. This account comes from one independent source, Wikipedia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes A. mesenterica as a saprotroph on dead deciduous trees and shrubs, with basidiocarps forming on various deciduous tree stumps and logs. It gives the range as throughout Europe and into Central Asia as far as Uzbekistan. A GBIF-held description from a Brazilian study, which uses the broader concept of the species, states that it grows on deciduous wood and has a worldwide distribution. That study lists Brazilian states including Amazonas, Goiás, Mato Grosso, Pará, Rio Grande do Sul, São Paulo and Tocantins. The two sources differ on geographic range because they use different taxonomic concepts. No supplied source gives a fruiting season. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
