## Summary

Ophiocordyceps robertsii (Hook.) G.H. Sung, J.M. Sung, Hywel-Jones & Spatafora, 2007, is an entomopathogenic fungus in the family Ophiocordycipitaceae (order Hypocreales). Wikipedia says it is known in New Zealand as vegetable caterpillar (Māori: āwhato or āwheto). It invades the caterpillars of leaf-litter dwelling moths and sends a fruiting spike up above the forest floor. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Europeans first thought the species was a worm or caterpillar that burrowed from the top of a tree to the roots, where it exited and grew a shoot of the plant out of its head. It also states that this was the first fungus from New Zealand to be given a binomial name. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

According to Wikipedia, Māori used the parasitised caterpillar to make an ink called ngārahu for traditional tā moko tattoos, by mixing the charred caterpillars with tree sap to produce an almost black ink. Wikipedia also states that in the early 20th century, mummified caterpillars were sold to tourists as a curio. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the fungus as invading the caterpillars of leaf-litter dwelling moths and sending a fruiting spike above the forest floor; the dried remains of the host caterpillar lie underneath the small brown stems that push through. It gives the common name as used in New Zealand. No source supplied here states host trees, substrate, season or a detailed range. *[wikipedia]*
