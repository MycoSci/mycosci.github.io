## Summary

Lentinus brumalis (Pers.) Zmitr., the winter polypore, is a species of Polyporaceae that causes white rot on dead hardwood. Wikipedia describes it as inedible and as distributed throughout temperate and boreal zones of the Northern Hemisphere. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet brumalis means "occurring in the winter", describing how the species tends to fruit during winter. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that it was first described as Boletus brumalis in 1794 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon in "Neuer Versuch einer systematischen Eintheilung der Schwämme", and was transferred to Lentinus in 2010 by Ivan V. Zmitrovich. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Per Wikipedia, saprotrophic on dead hardwoods, in particular birch, beech and mountain ash, and in rare cases on conifers such as hemlock and fir; in Uzbekistan it also grows on European nettle, willow and poplar. It grows solitary or in small groups. In North America it is said to be more common in the east, fruiting June through October; in Northern Europe it is said to fruit in late October and March. A GBIF materials-examined record describes white rot on an angiosperm trunk in a broad-leaved forest in Yunnan, China. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
