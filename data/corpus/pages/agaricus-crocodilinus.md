## Summary

Agaricus crocodilinus Murrill (1912) is a species in the family Agaricaceae, described from California and also called the crocodile agaricus or macro mushroom. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a robust species with a white cap, a ring with a cogwheeled underside, and gills that go from whitish to dark brown without a pink stage. The primary literature supplied for this page (2025–2026) consists of molecular studies of cadmium and zinc transport and binding in the species. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, wikispecies, lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Etymology

Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com say the epithet refers to the crocodile-skin look of the cap. MushroomExpert.Com quotes Murrill's 1912 description of a specimen 'conspicuously ornamented with large, gemmate warts originating from the cracking of the epidermus, causing it to resemble the skin of a crocodile.' It adds that especially dry conditions appear to have caused this, and that more typical specimens become only finely scaly or cracked. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

William Murrill described the species in 1912 in 'The Agaricaceae of the Pacific Coast—I' (Mycologia 4(6): 294–308), with the original description on p. 300. Wikispecies records the locality as Stanford University, California, on soil, and lists a lectotype (Murrill 1912: pl. 77, fig. 1) and an epitype, HSU 7280. MushroomExpert.Com says Murrill collected the specimen that gave the species its name, and lists Agaricus macrosporus, Agaricus excellens and Agaricus kuehnerianus as synonyms. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Sources give the following, and the wording differs between them. Wikipedia says it often grows in prairies and pastures with a lot of grass, and that it is saprophytic. MushroomExpert.Com (a single field-guide source) describes it as saprobic and gregarious in grassy areas or in woods, fruiting in fall or, in warm climates, over winter. It says the species is possibly widely distributed in North America, and that its illustrated collection came from Massachusetts. Wikispecies records the type collection as on soil at Stanford University, California. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, wikispecies]*
