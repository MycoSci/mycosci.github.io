## Summary

Rhizopogon roseolus (Corda) Th. Fr. (1909) is a species of Rhizopogonaceae in the order Boletales. Wikipedia describes it as an ectomycorrhizal fungus with roughly spherical to pear-shaped fruiting bodies up to 3 cm across in dry specimens. These are white at first, then turn pink to reddish-brown, and have root-like mycelial strands at the base. Wikipedia calls it a cosmopolitan species recorded in Europe, North America and northeastern Asia. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia (single-source claim; iNaturalist repeats it as a declared derivative) reports that techniques for commercial cultivation of this fungus in pine plantations have been developed and applied in Japan and New Zealand. It also states that the fungus is used as a soil inoculant in agriculture and horticulture. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fungus forms ectomycorrhizae with pine trees and often appears where typical pioneer plants settle in strongly disturbed areas. For Europe it reports growth under Pinus nigra on calcareous soil, with fruiting bodies forming from August to November. For Japan it reports occurrence under pines such as Pinus densiflora and Pinus thunbergii, with records from Honshu, Shikoku and Kyushu. *[wikipedia]*
