## Summary

Atheniella adonis (Bull.) Redhead, Moncalvo, Vilgalys, Desjardin & B.A. Perry (2012) is a species in the family Mycenaceae, known in English as Scarlet Bonnet. Wikipedia describes it as producing small orangish to reddish mushrooms with caps up to 1.2 cm in diameter and thin pinkish-white stems reaching 4 cm long. GBIF descriptions list it for Denmark and Norway, and the site catalog records it from many countries. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species epithet refers to the handsome youth Adonis in Greek mythology. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first named Agaricus adonis in 1792 by Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard and placed in Mycena by Samuel Frederick Gray in 1821. Gray called it the "Adonis high-stool" in his 1821 Natural Arrangement of British Plants, and Mordecai Cubitt Cooke later called it the "delicate Mycena". Rolf Singer moved it to Hemimycena (1943), then Marasmiellus (1951), and in 1986 considered it a Mycena again. Wikipedia reports that recent molecular (DNA sequence) research showed the species is not closely related to Mycena and belongs in the genus Atheniella. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow solitarily or in groups in conifer forests and heaths and appear in spring and autumn. It also states they grow in groups or scattered on needle beds under spruce and hemlock in wet coastal conifer forests, or in the higher mountains. Wikipedia reports the fungus from Europe and western North America, where Alexander H. Smith found it in Washington, Oregon and California, and from the Ussuri River valley in northeast China in 2007. In one instance, fruit bodies were found on the deciduous trees Spanish Maple (Acer granatense) and willow (Salix alba) near Amsterdam. GBIF descriptions list distribution as Denmark and Norway. All of the habitat detail other than the GBIF country list comes from a single independent source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
