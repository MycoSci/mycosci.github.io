## Summary

Helvella macropus (Pers.) P. Karst. is a cup fungus in the family Helvellaceae (order Pezizales), described by Persoon in 1796 as Peziza macropus and transferred to Helvella by Karsten in 1871. It has a cup-, saucer- or disc-shaped cap on a slender, finely hairy stem. Sources record it across the Northern Hemisphere, including North America, Europe, China and Japan, and, per a GBIF-held description, Colombia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was described in 1796 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon in the genus Peziza, with the type specimen collected in Sweden, and was transferred to Helvella by Petter Adolf Karsten in 1871. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) notes that Macroscyphus macropus is a synonym, and that Helvella macropus var. brevis, formerly separated on the basis of a shorter stem and shorter spores, was synonymized by Abbott & Currah (1997). Kuo also writes that this group of Helvella species is in need of contemporary study and revision. A description held at GBIF reports a phylogeny confirming at least four species within the H. macropus morphospecies complex. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruiting bodies are found in summer and autumn in woodland, usually (though not exclusively) with broad-leaved trees, on the ground or on rotting wood along with moss. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as probably mycorrhizal, growing alone or gregariously on the ground or in moss under hardwoods (especially oaks) or conifers, or on rotting wood, in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. A description held at GBIF gives the habitat as scattered or gregarious on the ground under Quercus or Pinus forests, and records a Colombian collection (Tolima) under Quercus humboldtii at 2540 to 2900 m a.s.l. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
