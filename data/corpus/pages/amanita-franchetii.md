## Summary

Amanita franchetii (Boud.) Fayod, 1889, is a fungus in the family Amanitaceae, also called Franchet's amanita. One source describes it as considered inedible and reported as toxic when raw or undercooked, and a case report links it to fatal poisonings. *[wikipedia, lit_1]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the current name was given in honor of French botanist Adrien René Franchet. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Swiss mycologist Victor Fayod gave the species its current name in 1889. Wikipedia also notes that a similar fungus in western North America was long referred to as A. franchetii but was formally described as A. augusta in 2013. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that A. franchetii occurs in Europe and North Africa with oaks (Quercus), chestnuts (Castanea) and pines (Pinus). A 2013 survey of the Ukrainian Carpathians lists it among four Amanita species that should be considered regionally rare there. *[wikipedia, lit_2]*
