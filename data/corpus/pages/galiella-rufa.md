## Summary

Galiella rufa (Schwein.) Nannf. & Korf is a cup fungus in the family Sarcosomataceae, known as the hairy rubber cup. Its cup-shaped fruit bodies have tough, gelatinous, rubbery flesh, a blackish-brown felt-like outer surface and a reddish-brown to orange-brown inner surface. Sources place it in eastern and Midwest North America and in Malaysia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet rufa means "rusty" or reddish-brown and refers to the color of the hymenium. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Lewis David de Schweinitz named it Bulgaria rufa in 1832, from material collected at Bethlehem, Pennsylvania. Saccardo transferred it in 1913 to Gloeocalyx, as defined by Massee, because of its hyaline spores. Korf made it the type species of the new genus Galiella in 1957. Wikipedia also says Peck described the variety magna in 1906 from North Elba, New York; it grew among fallen leaves under balsam fir or among mosses on the ground rather than on buried wood. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on decaying hardwood sticks and logs, growing alone, gregariously or most often in loose clusters, apparently widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains; its illustrated collections are from Illinois. Kuo gives the season as early summer and summer. Wikipedia gives decaying hardwood branches and logs, with fruiting in late summer and autumn, and a range from New York to Minnesota and from Missouri to North Carolina, plus Malaysia. Wikipedia also says it has been noted to fruit readily on logs used for cultivating shiitake. The two sources differ on season. The GBIF descriptions record only a distribution of Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
