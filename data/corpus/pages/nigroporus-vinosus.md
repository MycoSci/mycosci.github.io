## Summary

Nigroporus vinosus (Berk.) Murrill is a poroid fungus in the family Steccherinaceae, the type species of the genus Nigroporus. Wikipedia describes its fruit bodies as having brownish caps with tinges of purple or red and a pore surface of the same colour with minute pores, and MushroomExpert.Com likewise describes purplish young caps and pore surfaces that become brown with age. It is described as a wood-decay fungus causing a white rot, with a pantropical distribution. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Miles Joseph Berkeley first described the fungus as Polyporus vinosus in 1852, from a type collected in Saint-Domingue by Augustus Sallé. Berkeley called it "a very remarkable species, to which I can point out nothing closely allied." In 1905 William Alphonso Murrill made it the type species of his newly created genus Nigroporus, noting that it was "easily recognized by its wine-coloured context." Between Berkeley and Murrill the species was placed in Polystictus (Saccardo, 1888), Microporus (Kuntze, 1898) and Coriolus (Patouillard, 1900), and in 1952 Rokuya Imazeki proposed a transfer to Fomitopsis. This account comes from a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes N. vinosus as a white rot fungus that typically grows on the decaying wood of hardwood trees, although it has also been recorded on pine, with fruit bodies occurring singly, in groups, or in overlapping clusters. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on decaying hardwood wood (especially oaks and sweet gum) or, more rarely, conifers, growing alone or gregariously in late summer and fall; its illustrated collections came from Missouri, and it says the range is primarily the tropics, the Gulf Coast and the lower east coast of the United States. A GBIF descriptive record lists collections in Yunnan Province, China, from trunks of angiosperms and from a trunk of Pinus yunnanensis, described as white rot in broad-leaved and mixed coniferous broad-leaved forest, and notes a first record for Tolima. Wikipedia lists records from China, India, the Philippines, Peninsular Malaysia, Micronesia and Papua New Guinea, says it is widespread but not common in Africa, and says it is the only Nigroporus species found in North America; it also reports the fungus from Central America, South America and Cuba. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
