## Summary

Auricularia auricula-judae (Bull.) Quél. (1886) is a species in the family Auriculariaceae, known in English as wood ear, jelly ear, or historically Jew's ear. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as brown, gelatinous and noticeably ear-shaped, growing on wood, especially elder. Wikipedia also states that, following molecular work, the name is now restricted to the European species. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet as auricula, the Latin word for ear, plus Judae, meaning 'of Judas'. It links the name to the belief that Judas Iscariot hanged himself from an elder tree. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

According to Wikipedia, the medieval Latin name auricula Judae ('Judas's ear') matches the vernacular name in most European languages. The English name was originally 'Judas's ear', was shortened to 'Judas ear' and then 'Jew's ear', and the mistranslation 'Jew's Ear' appeared in English by 1544. Wikipedia adds that the British Mycological Society now recommends 'jelly ear' rather than 'Jew's ear'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Linnaeus first described the species as Tremella auricula in 1753. Bulliard redescribed it as Tremella auricula-judae in 1789, and Fries transferred it to Exidia as Exidia auricula-judae in 1822. Fries thereby sanctioned the name, so the epithet auricula-judae takes priority over Linnaeus's earlier auricula. Wikispecies lists Tremella auricula-judae Bull. (1789) as the basionym of the current combination, Auricularia auricula-judae (Bull.) Quél., published in 1886. Wikipedia says the species was long thought to be variable and cosmopolitan. It reports that DNA-based research showed the species as previously understood comprises at least seven species worldwide, and that the name is now restricted to the European species. According to the same source, the East Asian species often marketed and described under this name or as A. auricula is Auricularia heimuer. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports a folk belief that the ears are Judas's returned spirit, left to remind people of his suicide. It says the species is referred to in Christopher Marlowe's play The Jew of Malta, and that it was probably partly the inspiration for Emily Dickinson's poem beginning 'The Mushroom is the Elf of Plants'. Wikipedia presents these as single-source claims. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species grows on the wood of deciduous trees and shrubs, particularly Sambucus nigra (elder). It also reports the species on Acer pseudoplatanus (sycamore), Fagus sylvatica (beech), Fraxinus excelsior (ash) and Euonymus europaeus (spindle), and very rarely on conifers. It is said to favour older branches, living as a saprotroph on dead wood or a weak parasite on living wood, and to cause a white rot. Wikipedia describes it as growing solitary, gregarious or caespitose, found all year and most common in autumn. It describes the species as widespread throughout Europe and not known to occur elsewhere. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*
