## Summary

Agrocybe putaminum (Maire) Singer, the mulch fieldcap, is a species of agaric fungus in the family Strophariaceae, placed in the Agrocybe sororia complex by Wikipedia. It is described there as growing in woodchip mulch in parks, gardens and roadsides. Wikipedia, the only source that comments on edibility, describes the taste as bitter and states the mushrooms are inedible. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was first described as Naucoria putaminum by René Maire in 1913, from garden soil covered in plum stones, and was transferred to Agrocybe by Rolf Singer in 1936. A 1958 Dutch paper (Notes on Agaricales I) gave full descriptions of the species. *[wikipedia, lit_3]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes A. putaminum as saprobic, growing on the ground in clusters or close groups, usually in woodchips, in gardens, parks and other areas that use woodchip mulch. It says the species was once considered rare but has become more common as woodchip mulch has come into wider use in ornamental flower beds, and it gives records from western North America, Europe and Southwest Australia. A 1958 paper (Notes on Agaricales I) states that the habitat of A. putaminum is not confined to fruit-stones. *[wikipedia, lit_3]*
