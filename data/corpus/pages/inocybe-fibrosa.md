## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com's Michael Kuo reports that Charles Kauffman (1918, 1924) applied the European name Inocybe fibrosa to New York and Michigan collections of a large, white Inocybe. Kuo identified his own collection using Kauffman's descriptions, which it matched fairly well. He notes that the European species, as described by Saccardo (1887) and Moser (1978), is recorded from different habitats. He therefore suggests that Kauffman's concept might be distinct from the European species. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

This is a single-source account, and it concerns the concept of the name sensu Kauffman, not necessarily the European species. MushroomExpert.Com describes that concept as presumably mycorrhizal, growing gregariously in hardwood lowlands in summer and fall in eastern North America. Kauffman is quoted as reporting it from "low, moist, frondose woods". For the European species, the same source quotes Saccardo (1887) as giving "silvis arenosis" ("sandy woods") and Moser (1978) as giving "[m]ountain coniferous woods on chalk" and "meadow woods (with Allium ursinum) etc." *[mushroomexpert]*
