## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, citing Noordeloos (1982), confusion over Persoon's original concept of Entoloma dichroum (1801) began as early as Fries (1849), when authors began substituting a brown-capped, rough-stemmed species for what Persoon had described as a purplish-capped, smooth-stemmed mushroom. Noordeloos resuscitated Persoon's concept for Entoloma dichroum and described the misnamed mushroom as a new species, Entoloma tjallingiorum. The same page notes that Entoloma dichroum in the sense of some authors is the same species. This account comes from a single source. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (a single source) describes the species as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously on the well-rotted, mossy deadwood of oaks in fall. The illustrated and described collection was made in Illinois, and the page says the North American distribution is uncertain. It characterizes Noordeloos's original concept as European, low-altitude and primarily hardwood-inhabiting. The same page reports that Largent (1994) recorded the name from high-elevation conifer logs in California, Colorado and New Mexico. It says Largent's species differs in substantial ways from Noordeloos's original concept. *[mushroomexpert]*
