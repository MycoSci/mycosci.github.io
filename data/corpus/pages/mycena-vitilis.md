## Summary

Mycena vitilis (Fr.) Quél. 1872, the snapping bonnet, is a small mushroom in the family Mycenaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a small, pale gray to whitish species with a grooved cap up to 2.2 cm across and a long, slender stem that roots into the ground. It is recorded in Europe and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet vitilis is derived from the Latin word for "good for tying or binding with", or "plaited". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus vitilis by Elias Magnus Fries in 1838 and was assigned to Mycena by Lucien Quélet in 1872. Two varieties are mentioned: var. corsica, described from Italy with white fruit bodies, and var. amsegetes, named by Carleton Rea, with an "obsoletely umbonate" cap, a shorter and thicker stem, and a typical habitat of meadows and roadsides. The same source notes that the name "Mycena filopes" has been confusingly applied to this species by some authors, although M. filopes (Bull.) P. Kumm. is recognized as a distinct species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow scattered or in groups on debris in hardwood or mixed conifer and hardwood forests, and that the species is usually attached to small sticks buried in leaves and detritus, in damp places and especially under alder. Along the Pacific Coast it is sometimes abundant in red alder slashes. In eastern North America it is commonly found in the autumn months of October and November, growing with M. semivestipes and M. pullata. It is widely distributed in Europe (Britain, Germany, Italy, Norway, Poland and Portugal are named). The GBIF description record lists a distribution of Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Madeira Island and Sweden. These are single-cluster statements from Wikipedia and GBIF. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
