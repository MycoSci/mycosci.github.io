## Summary

Cantharellus californicus D. Arora & Dunham (2008) is a chanterelle in the family Hydnaceae, recorded from California. Wikipedia reports it as the largest-known species of chanterelle, with caps 5–30 cm wide, and MushroomExpert.Com describes an egg-yolk yellow cap that is sticky to nearly slimy when fresh. Both sources describe it as mycorrhizal with oaks. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

MushroomExpert.Com says chanterelle collectors in coastal California called it the "mud puppy" because it is a messy, egg-yolk yellow mushroom that pushes up through muddy debris, often creating "mushrumps" on the forest floor. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the mushroom was long familiar to coastal California chanterelle collectors and passed for many years as "Cantharellus cibarius". A 2008 paper by Arora & Dunham provided the name Cantharellus californicus. That site had previously shown it (August 2007 to February 2009) as "Cantharellus species 01: The Mud Puppy". *[mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that it became the official state mushroom of California in 2024. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it forms a mycorrhizal association with oaks, particularly coast live oak, in the woodlands of coastal California. It adds that the species has also been found with interior live oak, California black oak, canyon live oak and tanoak, and possibly Pacific madrone and manzanita. It gives the most common season as November to April. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with California live oak and occasionally other oaks or tanoak. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously in fall and winter, often pushing up through mud and forest debris after heavy rains and remaining partially covered. That source gives the range as California, from the Bay Area to Los Angeles, with the described collections from San Mateo and Santa Cruz counties. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
