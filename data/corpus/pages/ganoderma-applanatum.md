## Summary

Ganoderma applanatum (Pers.) Pat., described in 1887 and placed in the Polyporaceae in the site catalog, is a perennial bracket fungus known as the artist's bracket, artist's conk or artist's fungus. Sources describe it as a woody bracket that adds a new tube layer each year, with a white to grayish pore surface that bruises brown. It is recorded from many countries, and Wikipedia describes its distribution as cosmopolitan. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Two sources say the name "artist's fungus" or "artist's conk" comes from the white pore surface, which bruises or scratches dark brown. The marks become permanent when the fungus is dried, so it can be used as a drawing surface. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that it has been used to make amadou, a flammable leathery material, although Fomes fomentarius is more commonly associated with amadou. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) mentions etchings made on the fungus that he saw in craft stores and yard sales. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a wood-decay fungus that rots heartwood, and says it can also act as a pathogen of live sapwood, particularly on older, sufficiently wet trees. It lists beech and poplar as common hosts. It names several other hosts, including alder, apple, elm, maple, oak, walnut, willow, Douglas fir and spruce, and says it grows more often on dead trees than living ones. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and sometimes parasitic, on decaying hardwood logs and stumps or from wounds of living trees, producing a white to straw-colored rot. It calls the species common and widely distributed in North America and says it is typically seen at the bases of hardwood stumps or on hardwood logs. A GBIF-hosted taxonomic treatment reports it from subtropical forest in Costa Rica, at 1100 m in the record fields and up to 2300 m in the specimens examined. That treatment notes that the identification of the Costa Rican material as G. applanatum, or a closely related species, needs further collections and molecular data. Another GBIF-hosted text says that in Europe it was recorded on 35 genera of trees and grows mostly in forests. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
