## Summary

Pseudohydnum gelatinosum (Scop.) P. Karst., 1868, is a fungus in the order Auriculariales, known in English as cat's tongue, jelly tongue and jelly tooth. MushroomExpert.com describes it as both a jelly fungus and a toothed mushroom, found on conifer wood or on woody debris in conifer forests. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the common names refer to the fungus's gelatinous consistency and its hydnoid (toothed) undersurface. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Hydnum gelatinosum Scop., 1772. MushroomExpert.com cites the current combination as (Scopoli, 1772) P. Karsten, 1868, and says the species was originally described from Slovenia. No supplied source says why the transfer was made. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.com describes it as saprobic on the wood or woody debris of conifers, causing a white rot, and sometimes growing from standing trees. It says the fungus grows alone, scattered or gregariously, in late summer and fall, or in fall and winter in warmer climates. Wikipedia likewise says it grows on dead conifer wood. A GBIF description record lists the habit as lignicolous (wood-dwelling) on decaying trunks. The sources disagree on range. MushroomExpert.com describes a very wide distribution across most continents but wonders whether more than one phylogenetic species is involved. Wikipedia says recent DNA evidence suggests the species is confined to Europe and northern Asia, with superficially similar but distinct taxa elsewhere. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
