## Summary

Aspropaxillus giganteus (Sowerby) Kühner & Maire 1934 is a species in the family Tricholomataceae, known in English as the giant funnel. Wikipedia describes it as a saprobic fungus with a white or pale cream cap that can reach up to 50 cm across. The cap is funnel-shaped when mature, and the gills run down the stem. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus giganteus by the English naturalist James Sowerby in 1809, who illustrated it in Coloured Figures of English Fungi. In 1934 Robert Kühner and René Maire created the genus Aspropaxillus for smooth-spored species then placed in Leucopaxillus, and made this species its type. Rolf Singer transferred it to Leucopaxillus in 1938. He kept the smooth-spored species distinct by making it the type of section Aspropaxilli. Wikipedia reports that in 2012 Vizzini et al. showed Leucopaxillus in its then-current sense to be polyphyletic and that this species does not belong to the core Leucopaxillus clade. It was therefore moved back to Aspropaxillus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only source with descriptive text, says the species is saprobic and is typically found growing in groups or rings in grassy pastures, roadside hedges or woodland clearings. It says the species can form fairy rings in grassy areas such as pastures, and that it fruits in summer and autumn. Wikipedia gives the range as the temperate zone of the northern hemisphere, including North America, Britain and Europe. It attributes to David Arora the statement that in North America the species is most common in the Pacific Northwest and the Rocky Mountains. These habitat statements come from a single source. *[wikipedia]*
