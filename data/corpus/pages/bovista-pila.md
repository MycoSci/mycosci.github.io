## Summary

Bovista pila Berk. & M.A. Curtis (1873) is a puffball in the family Lycoperdaceae, known as the tumbling puffball, with its type locality in Wisconsin. Wikipedia describes the fruit body as egg-shaped to roughly spherical, up to 8 cm across, with a white outer skin that flakes off to reveal a shiny, bronze-coloured inner skin. It is described as widely distributed in North America, with few well-documented occurrences elsewhere. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet pila is Latin for "ball". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the name "tumbling puffball" refers to the propensity of detached puffballs to be blown about by the wind. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was described as new to science in 1873 by Miles Joseph Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis from specimens collected in Wisconsin. In their short description they emphasized the short pedicels (tube-like extensions) on the spores, which they noted are initially about as long as the spore is wide and soon break off. Wikipedia also quotes Coker and Couch as calling B. pila "the American representative of B. nigrescens in Europe", referring to the close resemblance of the two. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia (a single independent source) reports that the Chippewa people of North America used the puffball as a charm and medicinally as a hemostat. It also reports that livestock farmers in British Columbia, Canada, working under certified organic programs, apply the spore mass to bleeding hoof-trimming nicks, disbudding sites and sternal abscess wounds, wrapped with breathable first-aid tape. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that Bovista pila grows on the ground in corrals, stables, roadsides, pastures, lawns, parks and open woods. The puffballs fruit singly, scattered or in groups. The spore cases are persistent and may overwinter, and fruiting is said to occur throughout the mushroom season. It is described as widely distributed in North America, including Hawaii. Records from elsewhere are few: Russia (Sakha Republic), tentatively the Galápagos Islands, and Pernambuco and São Paulo in Brazil. The Brazilian material is described as a tentative identification, and reports from Turkey lacking supporting microscopic or macroscopic information are viewed with skepticism. *[wikipedia]*
