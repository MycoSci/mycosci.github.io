## Summary

Omphalotus illudens (Schwein.) Bresinsky & Besl, 1979, in the family Omphalotaceae, is commonly called the eastern jack-o'lantern mushroom. Sources describe it as a large orange mushroom with decurrent gills that grows in clusters on wood in eastern North America. It is poisonous to humans when eaten, whether raw or cooked. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was originally described from North Carolina by L. D. von Schweinitz in 1822. The combination in Omphalotus is credited to Bresinsky & Besl, 1979. Wikipedia notes that some older literature treats the name as synonymous with Omphalotus olearius, but that phylogenetic analysis confirms the two as distinct species. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports it is often found in clumps on decaying stumps, buried roots, or at the base of hardwood trees in eastern North America, from June to November. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing in large clusters on the stumps or buried roots of hardwoods, especially oaks, in late summer and fall. It says the species is widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains and in Mexico, and also occurs in northern and north-temperate Europe. Both descriptions are single-source statements. Wikipedia says O. subilludens replaces it on the Gulf Coast and O. olivascens is found in California. MushroomExpert.Com likewise says O. olivascens replaces it in California. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
