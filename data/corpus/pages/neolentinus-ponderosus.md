## Summary

Neolentinus ponderosus, known as the giant sawgill, is a species in the family Gloeophyllaceae, transferred to Neolentinus by Redhead & Ginns in 1985. The sources describe an enormous, tough gilled fungus with a scaly brownish cap and serrated gill edges. It grows on decayed conifer wood in western North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet derives from the Latin word for "heavy". No other source discusses the derivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the gill edges are serrated, "notched like a saw", and gives this as the reason for the common name "sawgill". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Orson K. Miller described the fungus in 1965 as a species of Lentinus. MushroomExpert.Com says it was originally described from Idaho (Miller 1965) and lists Lentinus ponderosus as a synonym. Wikipedia says that in 1985 it was transferred to Neolentinus, a segregate genus created for Lentinus-type fungi that cause a brown rot in wood. MushroomExpert.Com adds that its hyphal structure and brown rot led to suspicions that it was closer to polypores than to gilled mushrooms. It says a 2005 DNA study (Binder et al.) found Neolentinus closely related to Gloeophyllum sepiarium, which is placed in the Gloeophyllales. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on well-decayed, bark-less logs and stumps of ponderosa pine and other conifers, growing alone or in small troops. It notes that the fungus tends to appear in the hotter, drier summer months. Wikipedia likewise describes a saprophyte on conifer stumps and logs, particularly ponderosa pine, causing a brown rot. It adds that fruit bodies grow singly or in small clusters and usually prefer open, sunny spots. Wikipedia gives fruiting as late spring, or summer in mountainous locales. Both sources place it in western North America. Wikipedia restricts the range to the Pacific Northwest, while MushroomExpert.Com says the illustrated and described collections are from Colorado and Oregon. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
