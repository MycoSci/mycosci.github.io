## Summary

Phellinus gilvus (Schwein.) Pat. (1900) is a species in the Hymenochaetaceae; Index Fungorum treats Fuscoporia gilva as the current name, and Wikipedia uses that name and calls it the oak conk. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a polypore forming stalkless, semicircular to kidney-shaped brackets on hardwoods in North America. It is recorded from many countries, per GBIF. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the dead wood of hardwoods, especially oaks, causing a white rot of the sapwood, and apparently occasionally parasitic on the heartwood of living hardwoods. It grows alone or in overlapping clusters, is usually annual but occasionally perennial, and is found year-round, especially in warmer climates, though usually appearing in late spring, summer and fall. The same source calls it widely distributed in North America but absent or rare in the Rocky Mountains. Wikipedia describes it as a plant pathogen infecting several hosts. A GBIF description record, with records from Brazil, gives the substrate as dead wood. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
