## Summary

Gyroporus castaneus (Bull.) Quél. 1886, the chestnut bolete, is a bolete in the family Gyroporaceae (order Boletales). Wikipedia describes it as small, with white pores, a brown cap and a pale straw-coloured spore print. MushroomExpert.Com adds a brittle, hollowing stem and a cap margin that often splits, and treats the species as variable in colour. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives Gyroporus as meaning 'having round pores' and castaneus as a reference to the chestnut colouration. MushroomExpert.Com likewise links the epithet to the 'chestnut brown' of the species' colour range. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia credits the initial description to the French mycologist Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard, and MushroomExpert.Com cites Bulliard 1788 and says the species was originally described from France. Wikipedia says the species was formerly placed in the family Paxillaceae and that research now places it in Gyroporaceae. MushroomExpert.Com's author, Michael Kuo, states that he does not consider Gyroporus borealis, G. purpurinus, G. smithii or an unnamed California form to be supported as separate species; this is a single-source opinion. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes an ectomycorrhizal association with oaks (Quercus), with fruiting in small groups or singly from summer to autumn, and a preference for acid, sandy soils. It reports the species as occasional in Britain and throughout continental Europe and in eastern North America, rare in western North America, associated with Leptospermum in New Zealand, and recorded from Taiwan. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and other hardwoods (sometimes reported under conifers), growing alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall, widespread in Europe and Asia, fairly widely distributed in North America but rare to absent in most western areas. Its illustrated and described collections are from Illinois, Indiana, Minnesota, Ohio and Pennsylvania. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
