## Summary

Agaricus campestris L. (1753) is a member of the Agaricaceae, known in English as the field mushroom or meadow mushroom. Sources describe a white cap, gills that pass from pink to dark brown, and a dark brown spore print. GBIF's description records it as the generitype of Agaricus, and it is recorded from meadows and other grassy places. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the Latin specific epithet campestris means "of the fields". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was named in 1753 by Carl Linnaeus as Agaricus campestris, and that Lucien Quélet placed it in the genus Psalliota in 1872. It adds that several variants once treated as part of A. campestris have since been given species status. GBIF's description records the original material as an illustration in Bauhin & Cherler, Hist. Pl. Univ. 3 (2): 824 (1651), notes that no type was designated, and lists the species as generitype of Agaricus. MushroomExpert.Com reproduces Bulliard's 1780 illustration of the species in France. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia, the only source to mention it, says that in the past slices of A. campestris were applied to scalds and burns in parts of Scotland. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes the species as saprobic, growing alone, gregariously, or in arcs and fairy rings in meadows, fields, lawns, and grassy areas. GBIF's description carries the historical note "Habitat in Pratis" (in meadows). Wikipedia states that it is found in fields and grassy areas after rain from late summer onwards, often on suburban lawns, and that it is rarely found in woodland. Mushroom World also describes it as growing on grassy soil in lawns, pastures or cultivated fields. On range, MushroomExpert.Com reports that, following Kerrigan (2016), its North American distribution is uncertain: sequenced collections east of the Rocky Mountains belong to other species, and use of the name in the far west is provisional. Wikipedia and Mushroom World instead list it as occurring worldwide, including North America. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
