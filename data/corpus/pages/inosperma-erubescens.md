## Summary

Inosperma erubescens (A. Blytt) Matheny & Esteve-Rav. is a member of the Inocybaceae, commonly called the deadly fibrecap. Wikipedia describes it as a poisonous fungus, one of the few in the former genus Inocybe known to have caused death, and says guidebooks advise avoiding the entire Inocybaceae for consumption. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Norwegian naturalist Axel Gudbrand Blytt first described it in 1904 as Inocybe erubescens. It was widely known for many years as I. patouillardii, the name Giacomo Bresadola published in 1905, but Blytt's older name takes priority. A 2019 multigene phylogenetic study by Matheny and colleagues found that I. erubescens and its relatives in subgenus Inosperma were only distantly related to the rest of Inocybe. Inosperma was raised to genus rank and the species was transferred to it. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows in small groups on leaf litter in association with beech. It is described as commonest in beech woods and on chalky soils, but also grows in other broad-leaved woodland. Fruit bodies appear in spring and summer. The same source says it is found in southern Europe and has been recorded from eastern Anatolia in Turkey. In Israel it is reported under Palestine oak (Quercus calliprinos) and pines, and it is described as mycorrhizal. This is a single-source account (Wikipedia). GBIF's description record lists only the distribution DK, NO, SE. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
