## Summary

Hapalopilus rutilans (Pers.) Murrill (1904) is a polypore in the family Phanerochaetaceae, known in English as the Cinnamon Bracket or Tender Nesting Polypore. It is recorded across many countries in Europe, North America and elsewhere. Its fruit bodies are reported to be neurotoxic if eaten, and poisonings with kidney and liver involvement have been documented. *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_3]*

## Etymology

Per Wikipedia, the specific epithet rutilans is Latin for "orange-red", whereas the epithet nidulans (used in the synonym Hapalopilus nidulans) means "nesting". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Bulliard first described the species in 1791 as Boletus suberosus, an illegitimate homonym. Persoon published Boletus rutilans in 1798, and Fries published Polyporus nidulans in 1821. Karsten transferred the species to Hapalopilus in 1881, and Murrill transferred Persoon's name to Hapalopilus in 1904. A GBIF-hosted taxonomic treatment states that both names were sanctioned by Fries, so the older name H. rutilans has priority. It also states that neither name had been typified, so neotypes were designated: one for H. rutilans based on a French specimen from oak, and one for H. nidulans based on a Finnish specimen. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states the fungus is used in mushroom dyeing and that, with alkaline fixatives, the fruit bodies can produce striking violet colors. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a saprobic species that causes a white rot and fruits singly, in groups, or in fused and overlapping clusters on dead and decaying wood of deciduous trees. Preferred hosts include Quercus (oak), Fagus (beech) and Betula (birch), and it has rarely been recorded on conifer wood. In central Europe oak is the preferred host, while in northern Europe it is most commonly found on Corylus and Sorbus. Fruiting typically occurs from early summer to autumn, but the tough fruit bodies persist and may be found out of season. These statements come from a single cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
