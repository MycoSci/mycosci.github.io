## Summary

Pholiotina cyanopus (G.F. Atk.) Singer, 1950, is a species in the family Bolbitiaceae, originally described as Galerula cyanopus. English Wikipedia treats it under the current name Conocybula cyanopus and describes it as a small saprotrophic mushroom containing psilocybin and the uncommon aeruginascin. It is recorded from North America, Europe and Asia. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was originally described as Galerula cyanopus by the American mycologist George Francis Atkinson in 1918. Robert Kühner transferred it to Conocybe in 1935, Rolf Singer transferred it to Pholiotina in 1950, and T. Bau & H. B. Song transferred it to Conocybula in 2024. It is recognized as the type species of Conocybula sect. Cyanopodae. Wikipedia also notes that Conocybula smithii has sometimes been treated as a junior synonym, but describes it as a much more common species that differs in distribution, DNA barcode, and the length of its cheilocystidia and pileocystidia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows in lawns, fields and grassy areas in temperate areas of North America, Europe and Asia, and that it is rare where it occurs. It also says reports from the United Kingdom are doubtful. A Polish study (Open Life Sciences, 2014) reports basidiomata collected in June from wood chips and other woody remnants of undetermined tree species, within the area of a closed sawmill in Żywiec, SW Poland. *[wikipedia, lit_2]*
