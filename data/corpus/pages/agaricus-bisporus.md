## Summary

Agaricus bisporus (J.E. Lange) Imbach, 1946, is a member of the Agaricaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a basidiomycete native to grasslands in Eurasia and North America, and as cultivated in more than 70 countries. MushroomExpert.Com says its basidia mostly bear two spores each, unlike most other species of Agaricus. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet bispora distinguishes the two-spored basidia from four-spored varieties. MushroomExpert.Com likewise says the epithet points to the microscopic feature of two spores per basidium. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia lists different common names for the white and brown immature forms and for the mature form, and gives 'champignon' as French for 'mushroom'. It says the etymology of 'portobello' is disputed. MushroomExpert.Com says the industry markets brown strains as 'crimini' and 'portobello', with portobellos being mushrooms allowed to mature past the button stage. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says Mordecai Cubitt Cooke first described the taxon in his 1871 Handbook of British Fungi, as a variety (var. hortensis) of Agaricus campestris. Jakob Emanuel Lange later reviewed a cultivar specimen and named it Psalliota hortensis var. bispora in 1926. It was promoted to species rank as Psalliota bispora in 1938. Emil Imbach gave the current name, Agaricus bisporus, in 1946, after the genus Psalliota was renamed Agaricus. Wikispecies cites Imbach 1946 (Pilzflora des Kantons Luzern und der angrenzenden Innerschweiz) for the name. MushroomExpert.Com lists Agaricus brunnescens as a synonym. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia gives Joseph Pitton de Tournefort's 1707 account as the earliest scientific description of commercial cultivation. Early cultivation was unreliable because growers dug up wild mycelium and replanted it in beds of composted manure or in inoculated 'bricks'. That spawn carried pathogens. Wikipedia says sterilized pure-culture spawn was produced by the Pasteur Institute in Paris in 1893, for cultivation on composted horse manure. It says the white form was found in 1925 among brown mushrooms at the Keystone Mushroom Farm in Coatesville, Pennsylvania, and that most cream-colored store mushrooms descend from that chance mutation. MushroomExpert.Com says the mushroom industry has developed brown strains of the species in recent years. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the species is native to grasslands in Eurasia and North America, often grows in rich soil and compost, and is widespread worldwide, fruiting from autumn to winter, especially in association with cypress, rich soil, compost and manure. MushroomExpert.Com cites a 1995 study (Kerrigan and collaborators) of native North American populations from coastal California (under Monterey cypress), montane New Mexico and montane Alberta (under spruce). It says other studied North American populations are escapees from cultivation, occurring on manured soil, compost piles and lawns. GBIF description records for the species include an entry of oak woodland habitat and a solitary growth habit, without further provenance. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
