## Summary

Cortinarius smithii Ammirati, Niskanen & Liimat. (2013) is an agaric in the family Cortinariaceae, also called the western red dye mushroom. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a deep red to maroon, dry, silky cap, dark red gills and a contrasting yellowish stem. It is recorded from North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species was renamed to honor the American mycologist Alexander H. Smith, who had originally described it as a variety of Cortinarius phoeniceus. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Alexander H. Smith originally named it Cortinarius phoeniceus var. occidentalis in 1939. It was later given its own name, C. smithii, after molecular analysis showed it to be genetically different from Cortinarius phoeniceus (Wikipedia). MushroomExpert.Com lists C. phoeniceus occidentalis as a synonym and says the species was originally described from Oregon. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia, citing Siegel & Schwarz's Mushrooms of the Redwood Coast (2016), says the mushroom is prized as a source of natural dye and quotes that book calling it the "holy grail of California dye mushrooms". This is a single-source claim. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, growing scattered or gregariously in fall and winter. At higher elevations it is sometimes found in late summer. It is said to be distributed from northern California to Alaska, and the illustrated and described collections are from California. Wikipedia says only that it is found in North America. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
