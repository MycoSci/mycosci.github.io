## Summary

Ceratobasidium cornigerum (Bourdot) D.P. Rogers, 1935, is a species in the family Ceratobasidiaceae, order Cantharellales. Wikipedia describes it as forming thin, whitish, web-like effused fruit bodies, and as a saprotroph that is also a facultative plant pathogen and an orchid endomycorrhizal associate. The name is genetically diverse and is sometimes treated as a complex of closely related taxa. DNA-based research places it within the genus Rhizoctonia, a placement Wikipedia says has not yet been resolved. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described in 1922 by Hubert Bourdot as Corticium cornigerum, from material found in France on dead stems of Jerusalem artichoke. Donald P. Rogers transferred it to Ceratobasidium in 1935. Wikispecies independently gives the basionym as Corticium cornigerum Bourdot, Rev. Sci. Bourb. Centr. Fr. 35(1): 4 (1922), and the combination as Rogers, Univ. Iowa Stud. nat. Hist. 17(1): 5 (1935). Wikipedia states that molecular work places the species in Rhizoctonia but that this taxonomic problem is unresolved. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (a single source for these statements) describes the species as a soil saprotroph that produces fruit bodies on dead stems and fallen litter. It says the species is also a facultative pathogen of crops and turf grass, and can grow as a 'web blight' pathogen on living leaves of trees and shrubs, particularly in the tropics and subtropics. It also says the species is an endomycorrhizal associate of terrestrial orchids. A 1997 Canadian Journal of Botany study grew C. cornigerum in vitro as a symbiont with germinating seeds of the terrestrial orchid Spiranthes sinensis. Wikipedia says that if treated as a single species it is cosmopolitan, reported from Asia, Australia, Europe, and North and South America. The GBIF description record lists only Belgium, the Flemish Region, Norway (NO) and Terceira Island as distribution entries. *[wikipedia, lit_2, gbif_desc]*
