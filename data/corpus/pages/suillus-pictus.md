## Summary

Suillus pictus (Peck) Kuntze, 1898, is a name in the family Suillaceae (order Boletales), and Index Fungorum treats Suillus spraguei as the current name. The source describes the fruit body as having a dry, dark red cap covered in mats of hairs and scales separated by yellow cracks, small yellow angular pores that turn brownish with age, and a grayish cottony ring on the stalk. Wikipedia is the only source that could be retrieved for this page. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet spraguei honors the collector Sprague, and that pictus means "painted" or "colored". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the name "eastern painted Suillus" is used to contrast with the "western painted Suillus" (Suillus lakei). It does not explain how the other common names (painted slipperycap, painted suillus, red and yellow suillus) arose. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the first specimen was collected in New England in 1856 by Charles James Sprague. Berkeley and Curtis published the formal description in 1872 as Boletus spraguei. Charles Horton Peck named the same species Boletus pictus in a publication that appeared in 1873, but the date stamp on that publication showed he had sent his documents to the printer before the 1872 Berkeley and Curtis publication appeared, which gave his name nomenclatural priority. In 1945 Rolf Singer reported that Boletus pictus was illegitimate because it was a homonym of a polypore name published by Carl Friedrich Schultz in 1806. Otto Kuntze had transferred the taxon to Suillus in 1898, and the name was officially switched to Suillus spraguei in 1986. Wikipedia also says Berkeley and Curtis's Boletus murraii was later considered by Singer to be a younger stage of Boletus spraguei. Wikipedia notes that the taxon is frequently still called Suillus pictus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes Suillus spraguei as ectomycorrhizal with five-needled pines, particularly eastern white pine, with fruit bodies growing on the ground. It says the fungus is host-specific in natural soils and associates only with white pines (subgenus Strobus of Pinus). In laboratory pure culture it also formed associations with red, pitch and loblolly pine. Asian populations are associated with Korean pine, Chinese white pine, Siberian dwarf pine and Japanese white pine. In North America, fruit bodies appear from as early as June to as late as October. In a Japanese study of a Korean pine stand, they appeared mostly from August to November and tended to grow in clumps. The range is disjunct: China, Japan, Korea and Taiwan; in North America from Nova Scotia south to the Carolinas and west to Minnesota; Mexico (Coahuila and Durango). The source also says it has been introduced to Europe (Germany, Lower Saxony; Netherlands). *[wikipedia]*
