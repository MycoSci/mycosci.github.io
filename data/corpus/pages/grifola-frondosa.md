## Summary

Grifola frondosa (Dicks.) Gray, 1821 is a polypore in the family Grifolaceae (Polyporales), with GBIF occurrence records across Europe, North America and East Asia. It is known in English as hen of the woods, ram's head, sheep's head and maitake, the Japanese name 舞茸, which Wikipedia glosses as "dancing mushroom". MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a soft-fleshed polypore with smoky brown, wavy caps in large rosette clusters arising from a single branched stem structure, usually found near the bases of oaks. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it grows at the base of trees, particularly old-growth oaks or maples, and is a perennial fungus that often grows in the same place for several years in succession. It gives the native range as China, Europe (August to October) and eastern North America (September to October), and says it occurs most prolifically in eastern North America but has been found as far west as Idaho. MushroomExpert.Com (a separate source) describes it as weakly parasitic on living oaks and other hardwoods and also saprobic on decaying wood, causing a white butt rot. It fruits near the bases of trees, often reappearing in the same place in later years, in summer and fall. MushroomExpert.Com calls it widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains and rare in the west, and its described collections are from Illinois. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
