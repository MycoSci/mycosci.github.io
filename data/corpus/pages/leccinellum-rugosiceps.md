## Summary

Leccinellum rugosiceps (Peck) C. Hahn is a bolete in the family Boletaceae. Wikipedia describes it as an ectomycorrhizal associate of oak, reported from Asia, North America, Central America and South America, with a convex yellowish cap up to 15 cm across that becomes wrinkled and often cracked with age. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet rugosiceps is derived from the Latin roots for "rough" and "head" and refers to the wrinkled cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1904 by Charles Horton Peck as Boletus rugosiceps, and the type collection was made in the woods of Port Jefferson, New York. Rolf Singer transferred it to Leccinum in 1945. Wikipedia also lists Krombholzia rugosiceps (Singer, 1942) and Krombholziella rugosiceps (Šutara, 1982) as synonyms. Wikipedia is the only source for this account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that L. rugosiceps is ectomycorrhizal with oak, and that pin oak (Quercus palustris) is a frequent host in eastern North America. It fruits singly or in groups in forests, shaded lawns, and areas disturbed by human activity such as pathsides and picnic areas, typically from July to September. Wikipedia gives the range as eastern Canada south to Florida and Mississippi and west to Michigan, extending south to Mexico, Costa Rica and Colombia. In Asia it has been reported from India, Korea, China and Taiwan. A GBIF record documents a collection from Tolima, Colombia, dated 11 May 2006.
