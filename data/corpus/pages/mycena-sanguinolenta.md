## Summary

Mycena sanguinolenta (Alb. & Schwein.) P. Kumm. is a species in the family Mycenaceae, commonly called the bleeding bonnet. Wikipedia describes small reddish-brown to reddish-purple fruit bodies with conic to bell-shaped caps up to 1.5 cm wide on slender stipes up to 6 cm high, which exude a dark reddish-purple sap when fresh. The same source reports it as common and widely distributed across North America, Australia and Eurasia, and as bioluminescent. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin word sanguinolentus and means "bloody". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first called Agaricus sanguinolentus by Johannes Baptista von Albertini. It was transferred to Mycena in 1871 by the German Paul Kummer, when he raised many of Fries' "tribes" to the rank of genus. This account comes from a single independent source cluster (Wikipedia, which iNaturalist derives from). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow in groups on leaf mold, moss beds, or needle carpets during spring and fall. The same source says the species is common in forests of fir and beech and prefers soil of high acidity. It reports collections in North America (Maine to Washington and south to North Carolina and California; Nova Scotia to British Columbia), Jamaica at 1,800 m, Europe (Britain, Germany, the Netherlands, Norway, Romania, Sweden), Australia, and parts of China and Japan. A GBIF description record lists the substrate as "Hojarasca" (leaf litter) and gives distribution entries for Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont, but no further ecological text. The season, host and soil statements rest on Wikipedia alone. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
