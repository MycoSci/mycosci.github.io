## Summary

Inocybe godeyi Gillet is a species in the family Inocybaceae, recorded mainly in Europe. Wikipedia describes it as a poisonous mushroom containing muscarine compounds, with cream to tan conical caps that bruise red and a bulbous stem base. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific name honours the French mycologist Louis-Luc Godey. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the species was first described under its current name by Claude Casimir Gillet in his work Les Hyménomycètes. Mordecai Cubitt Cooke later described Inocybe godeyi var. rufescens (1909), now treated as a synonym. Other names treated as synonyms include Gillet's own Inocybe rubescens, Patouillard's Agaricus trinii var. rubescens, and Inocybe rickenii and Inocybe boltonii. Wikipedia also reports that the species has been placed in different sections of Inocybe by different authors (Kuyper in Marginatae, Singer in Geophyllinae), and that a 2002 phylogenetic study suggested it forms a clade with I. abietis, I. corydalina, I. agglutinata and I. pudica. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to Wikipedia, it grows on the ground in deciduous woodland, particularly on chalky soil. It is ectomycorrhizal and favours beech. Fruiting is reported from August to November, solitary or in trooping groups. Wikipedia says it can be locally common but is typically uncommon. This is a single-cluster account. *[wikipedia]*
