## Summary

Ramaria gracilis (Pers.) Quél. (1888) is a coral fungus in the family Gomphaceae, order Gomphales. Wikipedia places it in Ramaria subgenus Lentoramaria and describes the fruit bodies as dense clusters of forked branches, up to 8 cm tall and 4 cm wide, ranging from pale brown to white to pink-beige. Wikipedia describes it as growing in European coniferous woodland. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon in his 1797 Commentatio de Fungis Clavaeformibus as Clavaria gracilis. It was later transferred to Merisma (Sprengel), to Ramalina (Nylander, 1860) and to Clavariella (Karsten, 1881). It was then described as Ramaria gracilis in Lucien Quélet's 1888 Flore mycologique de la France et des pays limitrophes. Clavaria fragrantissima (G.F. Atk., 1908) is now considered a synonym. Wikispecies lists the same basionym and synonyms with their places of publication. No supplied source explains why the successive transfers were made. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that R. gracilis is found in European coniferous woodland, where it grows on leaf litter, and that it has an uneven distribution and is very rare. It says basidiocarps are most often encountered between August and December. It also says reports from Australia are likely misidentifications of R. filicicola, citing a 2014 study.
