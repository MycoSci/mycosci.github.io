## Summary

Collybiopsis confluens (Pers.) R.H. Petersen, the clustered toughshank, is a mushroom in the family Omphalotaceae. Wikipedia describes it as fruiting from summer until autumn in deciduous and coniferous forests, and states that it is not an edible mushroom. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the Latin epithet confluens as meaning "to flow together" or "to flow into each other". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Christian Hendrik Persoon first described the species as Agaricus confluens in 1796. It was later placed in Marasmius (as Marasmius archyropus by Fries, then Marasmius confluens by Karsten in 1898), in Collybia (Collybia confluens, Kummer, 1871) and in Chamaeceras (Kuntze, 1898). It was moved to Gymnopus in 1997 by Antonín, Halling and Noordeloos. Genetic studies later showed it to be more closely related to Marasmiellus ramealis than to the type species of Gymnopus, so it was placed in Marasmiellus. In 2021 the Marasmiellus species, including this one, were transferred to the genus Collybiopsis. This account comes from a single source, Wikipedia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruiting body appears from July to November, mostly in rows of clusters or in fairy circles 1 to 5 metres in diameter. It grows on deciduous and coniferous wood, most frequently on copper beech wood, from lowland to high mountain land. Wikipedia also says it appears in almost every native forest. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*
