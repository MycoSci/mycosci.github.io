## Summary

Coltricia cinnamomea (Jacq.) Murrill, 1904 is a polypore in the family Hymenochaetaceae, with GBIF occurrence records in many countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small, vase-shaped polypore with a silky, cinnamon-brown cap (1-5 cm) that is often banded with concentric zones. The same source describes the flesh as thin, tough, and rusty brown to orange, and reports that it turns instantly black with KOH. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as growing alone or in small groups under hardwoods in packed soil, often along paths and roadways, and infrequently on deadwood. It is described as frequently found in moss along trails, and as found east of the Rocky Mountains and on the West Coast. The same source calls it apparently mycorrhizal, at least facultatively, and possibly also saprobic. All of this comes from a single source. A GBIF specimen record also places a collection in Colombia (Tolima, Municipality of Murillo, Bosque El Roble, 2600 m a.s.l., 9 May 2006). *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
