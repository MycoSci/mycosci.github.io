## Summary

Armillaria mellea (Vahl) P. Kumm. 1871 is placed in the family Physalacriaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a plant pathogen that belongs to a cryptic species complex of closely related, morphologically similar species. It causes Armillaria root rot and produces mushrooms around the base of trees it has infected. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first named Agaricus melleus by the Danish-Norwegian botanist Martin Vahl in 1790, and was transferred to Armillaria by Paul Kummer in 1871. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says it was first named from Europe in the 18th century. It also recounts that Armillaria once held many mushrooms and was later cut down to a small genus, and that mating studies then defined about nine or ten North American species, with DNA studies largely upholding them. A 1979 Mycologia paper reported that A. mellea in the broad sense consisted of at least ten reproductively isolated groups ("biological species"), based on monosporous testers from 97 North American fruiting bodies. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_1]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia is the only source that describes cultivation. It says cultivation of A. mellea began with growing its mycelium on inoculated pieces of wood to support cultivation of the orchid Gastrodia elata, and that this has been done since 1994. It also says farmers in Heilongjiang produced fruiting bodies by cultivation in 2017, using either wood pieces or bagged media. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes A. mellea as widespread in northern temperate zones: North America, Europe and northern Asia, and introduced to South Africa. It says the fungus grows parasitically on a large number of broadleaf trees and fruits in dense clusters at the base of trunks or stumps, in late summer and autumn. MushroomExpert.Com describes tightly packed clusters, usually on the wood of hardwoods and occasionally on conifer wood, typically appearing in the fall after rains and found nearly year-round in warmer climates. It gives the illustrated and described collections as coming from Illinois and Tennessee. Mushroom World describes it as growing midsummer to late fall, densely clustered or in groups, around the bases of living or dead trees or stumps of conifers or hardwoods. The sources differ on North American range. MushroomExpert.Com limits it to roughly the eastern half of the continent, from about the Great Lakes to the Gulf Coast and East Coast, with reports from northern California. Wikipedia says it has been found throughout North America. The 1979 Mycologia paper states that the biological species within the complex are not clearly distinguishable by geographic range or substrate. Wikipedia also notes that older records may not refer to A. mellea as now narrowly defined. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, lit_1]*
