## Summary

Rubroboletus pulcherrimus (Thiers & Halling) D. Arora, N. Siegel & J.L. Frank, known as Boletus pulcherrimus until 2015, is a large bolete in the family Boletaceae, recorded from western North America. It is described as having a netted stem, a red to brown cap and stem, and red pores that stain blue on injury. Wikipedia describes the species as poisonous and potentially fatal, implicated in at least one death. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states the epithet comes from the Latin pulcherrimus, meaning "very pretty". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Harry D. Thiers and Roy E. Halling were aware of confusion over red-pored boletes on the west coast of North America, where Boletus satanas and B. eastwoodiae were traditionally recognised. They suspected the type specimen of B. eastwoodiae was in fact B. satanas. Thiers had written about the taxon in local guidebooks as B. eastwoodiae, and they felt that name was invalid. They formally described Boletus pulcherrimus in 1976. It was transferred to Rubroboletus in 2015 along with several other reddish, blue-staining boletes, such as Rubroboletus eastwoodiae and Rubroboletus satanas. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Per Wikipedia, found in western North America from New Mexico and California to Washington. It may feasibly occur in British Columbia. Wikipedia notes that sources disagree on elevation: one reports low altitudes in the Cascade Range and Olympic Mountains, another reports high elevations over 1,500 m. It is reported to fruit in autumn, singly or in groups (another source claims "never in groups"), in humus in mixed woodlands. The original publication is reported to associate it with forests containing tanbark oak (Lithocarpus densiflora), Douglas-fir (Pseudotsuga menziesii) and grand fir (Abies grandis). Smith and Weber are cited as mentioning increased fruiting after warm heavy fall rains following a humid summer. *[wikipedia]*
