## Summary

Gymnopilus sapineus (Fr.) Murrill, the scaly rustgill, is a small rusty-spored mushroom in the family Hymenogastraceae. Sources describe it as growing on dead conifer wood, with a rusty orange to rusty brown spore print and a cap 2–8 cm across. Its separation from Gymnopilus penetrans is disputed in the literature. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Michael Kuo (MushroomExpert.Com), Elias Fries named Agaricus penetrans in 1815. In 1821 Fries described Agaricus sapineus as new and reduced penetrans to a form of it. Kuo writes that later authors have confused the two names for about 200 years, and he treats them as combined, following Fries's 1821 account. Wikipedia likewise describes the distinction between the two as arbitrary. Kuo quotes a DNA study (Guzmán-Dávalos et al., 2003) that grouped G. penetrans and G. sapineus together and said further studies are needed to confirm whether they are conspecific. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the dead wood of conifers (especially pines). It grows alone, gregariously, or in small clusters, in summer and fall, and is described as widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia gives rotting wood under conifer trees throughout North America, generally June to September, or September to February on the West Coast. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
