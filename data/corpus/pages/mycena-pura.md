## Summary

Mycena pura (Pers.) P. Kumm. 1871 is a small to medium-sized gilled mushroom in the family Mycenaceae, known in English as the lilac bonnet. Its cap is 2-6 cm across, typically lilac to purple when young but variable in colour, and it has a radish-like odour. Wikipedia states that it contains muscarine and should not be consumed. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com gives the citation as Persoon, 1794, with the current combination by Kummer, 1871. It also notes that Alexander Smith (1947) declined to distinguish subdivisions of the species, and that pure pink forms are sometimes called Mycena rosea. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on forest debris under conifers and occasionally hardwoods, growing alone, scattered or gregariously, and terrestrial. It is reported as widely distributed across North America, fruiting in spring, summer and fall (also winter in California). Wikipedia gives a similar account, describing it as found beneath conifers or occasionally hardwoods on decomposing forest litter. Mushroom World, an aggregator, describes wooded and open habitats in humus-rich soil, which differs slightly from the other two accounts. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
