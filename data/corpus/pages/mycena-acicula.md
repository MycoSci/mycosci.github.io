## Summary

Mycena acicula, known in English as the orange bonnet, is a species of Mycenaceae first described as Agaricus acicula by Schäffer and combined into Mycena by P. Kummer in 1871. It has small orange-red caps up to 1 cm across on slender yellowish stems up to 6 cm long. GBIF records it from many countries, mainly in Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet acicula is derived from the Latin word meaning "small needle". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first named Agaricus acicula by Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1774. August Batsch also referred to it as Agaricus miniatus. Paul Kummer gave it its current name in 1871. Rolf Singer transferred it to Hemimycena and Marasmiellus, but the resulting binomials are now considered synonyms. It is classified in section Aciculae of Mycena. This account comes from a single encyclopedia source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fungus grows on dead twigs and other woody debris of forest floors, especially along streams and other wet places. Fruit bodies grow singly, in groups, or somewhat clustered on debris in wet places, especially along streams or the borders of swamps. The same source describes it as widely distributed in the eastern United States and Canada, and as occurring in Washington, Oregon and California. It also reports the species from Trinidad, Britain, Norway, Spain, Korea and the Ussuri River Valley in northeast China. GBIF's distribution record lists Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Madeira, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
