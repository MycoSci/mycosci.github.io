## Summary

Tremellodendropsis tuberosa (Grev.) D.A. Crawford, the ashen coral, is a coral fungus in the family Tremellodendropsidaceae. Its fruit body is small and whitish, with sparsely branched arms, a tough stem and tough flesh, and is described as 3 to 6 cm tall. It has been recorded in Europe and North America, and Wikipedia also lists South Brazil and Borneo. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described as Merisma tuberosum by Robert Kaye Greville in 1825. D.A. Crawford transferred it to Tremellodendropsis in 1954 and made it the type species. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says the species has caused taxonomic problems since its discovery because its basidia are partially divided. According to that page, the genus and a separate family were created to hold it before DNA studies, and Berbee and collaborators (2016) support giving the genus its own order, Tremellodendropsidales. These points about placement come from a single source. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow on the ground in woods or clearings, singly or in groups, and that the fungus occurs in north temperate regions including Europe and North America, as well as South Brazil and Borneo. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as growing scattered to gregariously on the ground in woods, sometimes from well-decayed woody debris, and calls its ecological role uncertain. That page says it is widely distributed in North America but appears most common on the West Coast and in the southern Appalachians, based on herbarium records, and that its illustrated collection is from Maryland. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
