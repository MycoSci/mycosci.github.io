## Summary

Russula betularum Hora (1960) is a member of the Russulaceae, known in English as the birch brittlegill. The English Wikipedia article describes it as a small, very pale Russula that grows with birch trees. Its type locality is Great Britain, and GBIF records it across much of Europe and in some North American and Asian countries. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet betularum means "of the birches" and refers to the species' association with birch (Betula species). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies lists the name as Russula betularum Hora, 1960, with the synonym Russula emetica var. betularum (Hora) Romagn. 1967. Wikipedia notes that some sources regard it as a variety of the sickener (R. emetica). Neither source gives the history behind the original description or the reasons for the reclassification. *[wikispecies, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (a single-cluster source) says it appears in summer and early autumn, is widespread in Britain, Europe and Scandinavia, is probably mycorrhizal with birch trees, and often grows in damp places in woodlands. *[wikipedia]*
