## Summary

Anthracophyllum archeri (Berk.) Pegler, known as the orange fan, is a species in the family Omphalotaceae, placed in Anthracophyllum in 1965. Wikipedia describes it as a small, rust-coloured saprobic fungus that grows in groups on rotting wood. It is recorded in Australia and New Zealand, among other countries. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Miles Joseph Berkeley first described the species in 1860 as Xerotus archeri, and David Pegler moved it to Anthracophyllum in 1965. The holotype was collected by Samuel N. Archer in Tasmania. Wikipedia also reports that Mordecai Cubitt Cooke described Xerotus drummondii in the Handbook of Australian Fungi in 1892, and that Pegler later stated it was synonymous with A. archeri in 1965 and reconfirmed this in 1989. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only independent source available, says the species grows on dead wood such as fallen twigs, branches and stumps, mostly of angiosperms. It describes the species as common in rainforests in Australia and New Zealand, and in wet and dry sclerophyll forests. It is reported from native forests throughout New Zealand, and in Australia on the East Coast, in Tasmania and at the south-west point of Western Australia. It has also been observed on Lord Howe Island, Raoul Island and Te One. *[wikipedia]*
