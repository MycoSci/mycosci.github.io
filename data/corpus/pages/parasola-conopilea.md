## Summary

Parasola conopilea (Fr.) Örstadius & E. Larss. (2008) is a member of the family Psathyrellaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a dark-spored mushroom with a fragile stem and a hygrophanous brown cap. It says the species is distinctive under the microscope, with long setae on the cap surface, abundant cheilocystidia and large spores, but not to the naked eye. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the species was until recently known as Psathyrella conopilea. Three DNA studies published within a two-year period (Padamsee and collaborators, 2008; Larsson & Örstadius, 2008; Nagy and collaborators, 2009) grouped specimens labeled Psathyrella conopilea with the genus Parasola, usually in a basal position. The same studies found Psathyrella as traditionally defined to be incoherent. The three papers proposed different treatments. Padamsee and collaborators suggested the species could represent a unique genus, because it has pileocystidia and no other known Parasola species does. Larsson and Örstadius transferred it to Parasola as a new combination. Nagy and collaborators agreed with the transfer, citing the aim of keeping the number of new genera low. MushroomExpert.Com also notes that "Parasola conopilus" is an error in Latin agreement. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as saprobic, growing scattered to gregariously, or in loose clusters of 2–4 mushrooms (not densely clustered). It grows from the deadwood or litter of hardwoods, or from dung, in hardwood forests or cultivated areas. That source gives the season as spring and fall and says the species is widely distributed in North America and also found in Europe and Australasia. The collections it illustrates and describes are from Illinois and Michigan. This habitat account rests on that single source. *[mushroomexpert]*
