## Summary

Lactifluus volemus (Fr.) Kuntze, 1891, is a species of fungus in the family Russulaceae, formerly placed in Lactarius and known by the common names weeping milk cap and bradley. Wikipedia reports that phylogenetic analysis suggests L. volemus represents several species or subspecies rather than a single taxon. The GBIF description record lists only a distribution entry (Global; Belgium, including the Flemish and Walloon Regions; Denmark; Norway; Sweden; Vermont). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet "volemus" is derived from the Latin vola, meaning "the hollow of the hand", suggestive of Fries's reference to the large amount of latex "flowing enough to fill the hand". This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that in the West Virginian mountains of the United States the mushroom is called a "leatherback" or a "bradley", and that the latter name may originate from its German name Brätling. This is a single-source, hedged account. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the first mention in the scientific literature was in Linnaeus's 1753 Species Plantarum, under the name Agaricus lactifluus. In 1821 Elias Magnus Fries called it Agaricus volemus in his Systema Mycologicum, where he grouped related species in a tribe he named Galorrheus. Fries later recognised Lactarius as a distinct genus in his 1838 Epicrisis Systematis Mycologici. Wikipedia says Fries's name is sanctioned and so has nomenclatural priority over Linnaeus's earlier name. In 1871 Paul Kummer raised most of Fries's tribes to generic rank, renaming the species Galorrheus volemus. In 1891 Otto Kuntze moved it into Lactifluus, a genus that was long considered a synonym of Lactarius and was confirmed as separate through molecular phylogenetics in 2008. Wikipedia also records Lactarius lactifluus as a historical synonym used by Lucien Quélet in 1886, and says Lactarius wangii, reported as a new species from China in 2005, was synonymised with L. volemus two years later. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes L. volemus as a mycorrhizal (ectomycorrhizal) fungus whose fruit bodies grow on the ground at the base of various tree species, both coniferous and broad-leaved, and states it is more common in deciduous woods. It says the fungus may also sometimes be found in peat moss beds. Fruit bodies are said to appear from summer to autumn, singly or in groups, and to be more abundant in warm, humid weather. For distribution, Wikipedia gives temperate Eurasia and North America plus some subtropical and tropical regions of Asia. It lists Europe (in decline in some countries and considered locally extinct in the Netherlands and Flanders), Asia (including China, Japan, India, Korea, Nepal and Vietnam), the Middle East (Iran and Turkey), and North America (southeast Canada, the East Coast of the United States, Mexico, and Guatemala). This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*
