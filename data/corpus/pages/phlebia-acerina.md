## Summary

Phlebia acerina Peck, described in 1889 with a type locality in New York, is a species in the family Meruliaceae (order Polyporales). A 1993 Mycologia study describes it as common and widely distributed in North America and closely related to P. rufa and P. radiata. A 2023 study examined a strain of it as a white-rot fungus able to degrade benzo[a]pyrene under laboratory ligninolytic conditions. *[lit_1, lit_2]*

## Discovery and naming

A 1993 biosystematic study (Mycologia) notes that P. acerina and P. rufa were often considered conspecific. It found them intersterile and reported differences in basidioma and cultural traits and slight differences in ribosomal DNA sequence, which support treating them as separate but closely related species. *[lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

Reported as common and widely distributed in North America, whereas P. rufa is restricted to Washington, Oregon and British Columbia. It is described as a white-rot fungus in a 2023 laboratory study. *[lit_1, lit_2]*
