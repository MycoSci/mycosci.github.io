## Summary

Amanita vaginata (Bull.) Lam., the grisette, is a member of the Amanitaceae described in 1783. Sources describe a gray to grayish-brown cap with a prominently lined or grooved margin, white gills, no ring on the stem, and a loose sack-like volva at the base. Michael Kuo (MushroomExpert.Com) argues that many North American species may be going under this European name. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the tawny grisette (Amanita fulva) was once thought to be a variety of A. vaginata, and that in North America A. vaginata is considered part of a species complex that includes A. constricta, A. pachycolea and A. protecta. MushroomExpert.Com notes that Rod Tulloss treats dozens of unnamed, vaginata-like numbered taxa in his North American keys, and says a comprehensive DNA and morphological study will be needed before these have good names. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Described as mycorrhizal. MushroomExpert.Com gives hosts as oaks and other hardwoods and conifers, and Wikipedia says it grows singly or in numbers in both coniferous and hardwood forests. Both describe it in disturbed ground settings: grassy areas at the edges of woods, unkempt lawns, urban areas and parks. Fruiting is given as late spring to fall (MushroomExpert.Com) or spring to fall (Wikipedia). Mushroom World gives solitary or scattered growth on soil in broad-leaf woods and on heaths, fruiting in summer and autumn. Wikipedia says it is widely distributed in Europe and North America, and MushroomExpert.Com says it is widely distributed in North America. In one laboratory study, A. vaginata formed mycorrhiza with Pinus tabulaeformis seedlings. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld, lit_1]*
