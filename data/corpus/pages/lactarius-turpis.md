## Summary

Lactarius turpis (Weinm.) Fr., the ugly milk-cap, is a member of the Russulaceae that Fries treated in 1838. Wikipedia describes it as a Eurasian species with a messy, dirty-looking cap, found mainly with birch, and says it cannot be recommended for eating. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet turpis derives from the Latin turpis, 'ugly', and refers to the mushroom's messy, dirty appearance. It notes that the synonym epithet plumbeus derives from the Latin for 'lead-coloured'. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the messy, dirty appearance of the species gives it its English name, ugly milk-cap, as well as its Latin epithet. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the name turpis was originated by Johann Anton Weinmann and taken over by Elias Magnus Fries in 1838. Wikipedia also says the species has a contested naming history. The epithets necator and plumbeus were coined by Pierre Bulliard as Agaricus necator (1791) and Agaricus plumbeus (1793), and there is confusion over which mushrooms he meant. The three names are usually, but not always, treated as synonyms. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that, despite the acrid taste, the mushroom has popularly been used after boiling as a spice in mushroom dishes in northern and eastern Europe and Siberia. It says the mushroom is commercially available preserved in salt, and that in Russia it is considered a choice mushroom, one of the best for pickling (salt only, no vinegar). This is a single-cluster account, and it conflicts with the assessment of most western European authorities (see safety). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the species is found naturally in Europe and Siberia and has been introduced to Australia and New Zealand. It also occurs in North America, in the northeast (July–September) and near the West Coast (September–November). It is especially associated with birch, but is also found with spruce, pine and other trees in mixed woodland. Mushroom World, which shares a cluster with Wikipedia, says it grows solitary or in scattered groups on soil in damp places under birch. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
