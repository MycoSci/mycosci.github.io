## Summary

Chroogomphus ochraceus (Kauffman) O.K. Mill., 1964, is a species in the family Gomphidiaceae (order Boletales), based on the earlier name Gomphidius ochraceus. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com, which are independent of each other, both treat it as the widespread North American Chroogomphus, genetically distinct from the European C. rutilus. It is listed under the common name Olive Pine Spike. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that C. ochraceus was originally described from the Pacific Northwest on the basis of its yellowish colors (Kauffman, 1925), but is apparently also capable of reddish, purplish, and brown colors. North American collections long called C. rutilus proved genetically distinct from European C. rutilus and genetically inseparable from C. ochraceus, so C. rutilus in the North American sense is treated as a synonym. Wikipedia gives the same account and describes it as now the accepted view that C. rutilus is the European species and C. ochraceus the American one. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially pines, growing alone, scattered, or loosely gregariously, in summer and fall (over winter in coastal California), and widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia describes it as closely associated with conifers, especially pines, found across North America from Canada to Mexico and in Hispaniola, fruiting in summer and early fall in cool weather (through winter in coastal California), in small clusters. The two sources differ on the nature of the association: Wikipedia says it was once assumed to be mycorrhizal with conifers but is now understood to be likely a parasite of Suillus species that are themselves associated with conifers, whereas MushroomExpert.Com calls it mycorrhizal. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
