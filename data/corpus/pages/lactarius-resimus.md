## Summary

Lactarius resimus (Fr.) Fr. 1838 is a milkcap in the family Russulaceae, recorded in the catalog as Rollrim Milkcap. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as associated with birches and pines, with a whitish, non-zoned cap with a finely hairy margin. Its milk is white as exuded but turns yellow within minutes of exposure to air, and the taste is acrid to bitter. Wikipedia describes it as a salt-pickled delicacy in Russia and parts of Eastern Europe. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states the species was originally described from Sweden. Wikispecies lists the basionym as Agaricus resimus Fr. (1821), with later transfers to Galorrheus (Kummer, 1871) and Lactifluus (Kuntze, 1891). The supplied sources do not say why these transfers were made. *[mushroomexpert, wikispecies]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states it is considered a delicacy in Russia and some other countries of Eastern Europe when pickled in salt, and that it is ranked there among the three tastiest edible mushrooms alongside Boletus edulis and Lactarius deliciosus. This comes from a single source cluster (Wikipedia and its derivative iNaturalist entry). The iNaturalist text adds that it is not held in high esteem elsewhere. *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com states it is mycorrhizal with birches and pines, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall. It is widespread in Europe, occasionally reported in western Asia, and widely reported in North America, especially in northern and montane regions. MushroomExpert.Com cautions that North American material may represent several versions and needs critical study. Wikipedia separately states it typically grows on acidic soil and appears in late summer to autumn, and is commonly found in northern and eastern Europe including Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, the Baltic states and parts of Scandinavia. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
