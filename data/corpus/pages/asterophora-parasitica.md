## Summary

Asterophora parasitica (Bull.) Singer is a species in the family Lyophyllaceae, published in 1951 as a transfer from Bulliard's earlier Agaricus parasiticus. Wikipedia describes it as a small mushroom that grows as a parasite on other mushrooms, fruiting in clusters on the decaying remains of Lactarius and Russula species. Most of the descriptive detail available for it comes from a single encyclopedia entry and one morphological description of Panamanian and German material. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard first described the species as Agaricus parasiticus in 1791, and Elias Magnus Fries sanctioned that name in his 1822 Systema Mycologicum. Rolf Singer transferred it to Asterophora in 1951. Wikipedia lists the synonyms Gymnopus parasiticus (Gray, 1821), Merulius parasiticus (Purton, 1821) and Nyctalis parasitica (Fries, 1838). It also notes that MycoBank lists Agaricus umbratus (Withering, 1796), Agaricus pilipes (Sowerby, 1803) and Agaricus microphyllus (Corda, 1840) as facultative synonyms based on a different type. No supplied source explains why the transfers were made. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow in groups or clusters on the decomposing mushrooms of Lactarius and Russula species, particularly those in the Russula nigricans group. A separate GBIF-hosted description (Panama and Germany material) independently reports it as parasitic on basidiocarps of Russulaceae, and its German specimen was collected from rotting basidiocarps of Russula sect. Compactae in mixed coniferous and deciduous forest. The same description gives the Panamanian collections as coming from montane forest with Alnus acuminata and Quercus at 1800–2500 m, and the authors note that, because the host fungi are ectomycorrhizal, it is restricted to habitats with ectomycorrhizal host plants. Wikipedia reports it from Europe and North America, and describes it as not uncommon in southern and central Europe but rare in Scandinavia, where it does not grow outside the northern limit of oak (Quercus). Wikipedia also states that development on a host mushroom takes about three weeks. No supplied source gives a fruiting season for the species. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
