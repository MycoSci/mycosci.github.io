## Summary

Inocybe lacera (Fr.) P. Kumm., the torn fibrecap, is a small brown species in the family Inocybaceae. Wikipedia describes it as poisonous because it contains muscarine. It is distinguished from similar little brown mushrooms mainly by its long, smooth, cylindrical spores seen under the microscope. *[wikipedia, inaturalist, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described by Elias Magnus Fries and was placed in the genus Inocybe by Paul Kummer in his 1871 work Der Führer in die Pilzkunde. It also notes that several forms are recognised, so the main variety is sometimes called Inocybe lacera var. lacera. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that it fruits in autumn on sandy soil, especially with pine, though it is typically found in mixed woods. It is mycorrhizal with both conifers and hardwoods, and grows alone, scattered or gregariously. Wikipedia also lists path edges in woodland, old moss-covered fire sites, heathland and coastal dunes, and says that in some areas of Montana it fruits in spring under Populus tremuloides. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers or hardwoods, fruiting in summer and fall (and spring at high elevations in Montana, under Quaking Aspen), and widely distributed in North America. Mushroom World mentions sandy soil with coniferous trees and old moss-covered fire sites, but its description closely tracks the Wikipedia wording. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
