## Summary

Lactarius vinaceorufescens A.H. Sm. (1960) is a milkcap in the family Russulaceae, with type locality Michigan. Wikipedia describes it as having a pinkish-cinnamon cap and a white latex that rapidly turns sulfur-yellow when the mushroom is cut, and as poisonous. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet vinaceorufescens derives from a Latin word meaning "becoming wine reddish", and gives the common names "yellow-latex milky" and "yellow-staining milkcap". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was described from specimens collected in Muskegon, Michigan, in 1936. A variety, var. fallax, was named in the same publication for individuals with prominently projecting pleurocystidia, and was reduced to synonymy with the main species in the 1979 monograph of North American Lactarius. MushroomExpert.Com notes that the West Coast species Lactarius xanthogalactus appears in older western field guides as L. vinaceorufescens, but that Methven (1997) argues it is distinct because it lacks the red staining and associates with coast live oak and tanoak rather than pines. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that fruit bodies grow scattered or in groups on the ground under pine between August and October, that the species forms mycorrhizal associations with Douglas fir (Pseudotsuga menziesii), and that it is fairly common and widely distributed in North America, including boreal and high-elevation forests of the Southern Appalachians with Picea, Abies and Pinus. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with pines, especially eastern white pine, growing scattered or gregariously in summer and fall, fairly widely distributed in northeastern North America, and as a prolific late summer and fall species in the conifer forests of northern Michigan. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
