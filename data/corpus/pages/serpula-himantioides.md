## Summary

Serpula himantioides (Fr.) P. Karst. (1884) is a species in the family Serpulaceae, order Boletales. Wikipedia describes it as a fungus that damages timber, causing what it calls dry rot. It also states that recent molecular work indicates S. himantioides is a species complex containing multiple cryptic lineages. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the fungus was first described in 1818 by Elias Magnus Fries as Merulius himantioides, and that Petter Karsten transferred it to the genus Serpula in 1884. No supplied source gives the type material or the reasons for the transfer. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that S. himantioides prefers the moist wood of coniferous hosts such as fir, larch, spruce and pine, and that it is commonly found in the wild. It contrasts this with S. lacrymans, which it says is rarely found in the wild and is generally found on lumber in buildings and construction. Wikipedia also says S. himantioides is a common cause of timber rotting in buildings. The GBIF description record lists distribution in Belgium (Flemish and Walloon regions), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
