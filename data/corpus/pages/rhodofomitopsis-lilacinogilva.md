## Summary

Rhodofomitopsis lilacinogilva (Berk.) B.K. Cui, M.L. Han & Y.C. Dai (2016) is a bracket fungus in the family Fomitopsidaceae. Wikipedia describes it as known primarily from Australia, with its main identifying feature being the lilac colour of the pore surface on the underside of the fruit body. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was first described by Miles Joseph Berkeley in 1839 as Polyporus lilacino-gilvus. The type specimen was found growing on charred wood in Van Diemen's Land (now Tasmania). It was sent to Berkeley through Sir William Jackson Hooker, who had received specimens from collections made by Ronald Campbell Gunn. Berkeley considered it allied to Polyporus gilvus. The same source lists later transfers: to Polystictus by Cooke in 1886, to Microporus by Kuntze in 1898, to Trametes by Lloyd in 1915, and to Fomitopsis by Wright and Deschamps in 1975. Chinese mycologists moved it to the newly created genus Rhodofomitopsis in 2016. This account comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only source with habitat text, describes it as a white-rot fungus growing on rotting eucalyptus wood. The type specimen was found on charred wood in Tasmania. The same source says it is widely distributed in Australia, with reports from Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria, Tasmania, South Australia, Western Australia and Northern Territory. It was reported from India for the first time in 2009 and from Brazil in 2015. It is described as not native to Europe, but was reported there as an exotic species on imported eucalyptus wood sheet piling in a new housing estate in Groningen. These habitat statements rest on a single source. *[wikipedia]*
