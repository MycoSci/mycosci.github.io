## Summary

Armillaria novae-zelandiae (G. Stev.) Boesew. (1977) is a species in the family Physalacriaceae, recorded in New Zealand, Australia, Chile, Argentina, Papua New Guinea and several Asian countries. One supplied source describes it as a white rot wood decay fungus and root disease pathogen that has caused substantial losses in Pinus radiata plantations and kiwifruit orchards in New Zealand. The same source says it also recycles carbon and nutrients as a wood decomposer. *[lit_2, wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia (a single source) reports that in Mātauranga Māori the presence of harore (the Te Reo name) in autumn forests is a signifier of the future. Depending on regional tradition, it indicates either positive changes, such as a good fishing season, or a time of famine. Because the mushroom disappears soon after it arrives, it gave rise to the whakataukī (proverb) "he harore rangitahi", or "one-day mushroom". The phrase describes transient aspects of life, or is used as an insult for people who only briefly contribute to work. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that A. novae-zelandiae occurs naturally in southern beech and broad-leaf podocarp forests in New Zealand. It survives in the soil as a wood-decay fungus after native forest is cleared, and it attacked planted Pinus radiata around the stumps of felled native trees. It has also been described infecting kiwifruit vines (Actinidia). A CABI-type datasheet (lit_2) says its toadstool fruit bodies appear on wood during the winter months. Wikipedia's section on Māori tradition instead places harore in autumn forests, so the fruiting season differs between the two sources. The datasheet also says it is presumed indigenous to the Southern Hemisphere countries and parts of tropical and subtropical Asia where it occurs. Wikipedia cites a study of Pinus radiata plantations in which the fungus preferred previously forested sites with a mean annual temperature below 13 °C and rainfall of 1000–1600 mm per year. These site preferences come from that one plantation study and should not be read as a general habitat statement. *[wikipedia, lit_2]*
