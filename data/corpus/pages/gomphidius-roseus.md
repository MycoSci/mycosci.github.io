## Summary

Gomphidius roseus (Fr.) Oudem., family Gomphidiaceae, order Boletales, is known in English as the rosy spike-cap, pink gomphidius or Rosy Spike. It is a coral-pink-capped gilled mushroom described as found in Europe and as growing in association with Suillus bovinus. One source describes it as appearing to be parasitic on that species. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name derives from the Greek 'gomphos', meaning "plug" or "large wedge-shaped nail", and that the specific epithet roseus is the Latin adjective for "pink". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries first described this fungus as Agaricus glutinosus β roseus in 1821 and later raised it to species rank. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as an uncommon fungus found in Europe and not in North America, where the similar pinkish G. subroseus occurs. It is reported from pine woods, particularly Pinus sylvestris, always with Suillus bovinus, and is often hidden in undergrowth. Fruiting bodies appear in autumn. Mushroom World says it occurs in coniferous forests, particularly under pine, fruiting in grass or needle litter. Wikipedia adds that, although the family has been thought ectomycorrhizal, this species is now thought to be parasitic on the mycelium of S. bovinus, and it is never found growing without it. That ecology claim rests on Wikipedia alone. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
