## Summary

Ischnoderma resinosum (Schrad.) P. Karst. 1879 is a polypore in the family Ischnodermataceae (order Polyporales), known in English as the Late Fall Polypore or Resinous Polypore. Wikipedia describes it as a shelf mushroom with a velvety, dark red-brown cap that darkens and forms zones with age. It is reported from Africa, Asia, Europe and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Boletus resinosus in 1794 by the German botanist Heinrich Schrader. It was later moved between several genera and acquired an extensive synonymy. Petter Karsten transferred it to Ischnoderma in 1879, giving the name in use today. MushroomExpert.Com's reference list likewise credits Schrader 1794 and Karsten 1879. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fungus fruits on hardwood logs and stumps in autumn. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods and annual, causing a white rot. It appears on recently fallen wood and on wood that has been down for several years, but not typically on well-rotted wood. It grows alone, gregariously, or in overlapping clusters, usually appearing in fall. Kuo describes it as widely distributed in North America but more common in the Midwest and eastern United States. His described collections are from Illinois and Wisconsin. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
