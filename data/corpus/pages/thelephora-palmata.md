## Summary

Thelephora palmata (Scop.) Fr. is a clavarioid fungus in the family Thelephoraceae, known in English as the stinking earthfan or fetid false coral, and recorded from Europe, North America, Asia and elsewhere. Wikipedia describes leathery, coral-like fruit bodies whose branches flatten into fan-like tips that darken as the fungus matures. MushroomExpert.Com likewise singles out a strong, foul odor, reminiscent of fetid garlic or rotting cabbage, as a key recognition feature. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet palmata is derived from Latin and means "having the shape of a hand". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

According to Wikipedia, the common names "stinking earthfan" and "fetid false coral" refer to the fungus's pungent odor, which is likened to fetid garlic. The same source notes that Samuel Frederick Gray called it the "stinking branch-ear" in his 1821 work A Natural Arrangement of British Plants. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described in 1772 by the Italian naturalist Giovanni Antonio Scopoli as Clavaria palmata, and that Elias Fries transferred it to Thelephora in 1821. Later generic transfers produced synonyms in Ramaria (Holmskjold, 1790), Merisma (Persoon, 1822) and Phylacteria (Patouillard, 1887). Wikipedia also notes that Persoon published a different species under the name Thelephora palmata in 1822. That name was an illegitimate homonym, and the species is now known as Thelephora anthocephala. MushroomExpert.Com adds that the species was originally described from Carniola, in present-day Slovenia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies can be used for mushroom dyeing. Depending on the mordant, colors from blackish brown through dark grayish green to greenish brown are obtained, and without a mordant the dye is light brown. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes T. palmata as an ectomycorrhizal species that forms associations with conifers. It reports the fruit bodies growing singly, scattered or in groups on the ground in coniferous and mixed forests and in grassy fields, with a noted preference for moist ground and woodland paths. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, growing alone or gregariously on the ground in summer and fall, and reports it as widespread in Europe and North America; its illustrated collection is from Colorado. A GBIF description record gives the substrate as "Sobre mantillo" (Spanish). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
