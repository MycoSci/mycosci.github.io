## Summary

Tricholomopsis rutilans (Schaeff.) Singer, 1939, is a gilled mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae, commonly called plums and custard. Wikipedia describes it as found across Europe and North America. It has a cap covered in red to purplish-red fibrils or scales over yellow, yellow gills, and a white to creamy-white spore print. Wikipedia says older texts list it as apparently edible after boiling though not recommended, and that some more recent books rate it poor quality. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the common name 'plums and custard' stems from the plum-red scaled cap and custard-yellow gills. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com reports that a DNA study (Olariaga and collaborators 2015) found five phylogenetically separate species among collections labelled Tricholomopsis rutilans from Europe and North America, so the North American form may be a separate, unnamed species. The authors named one of these, Tricholomopsis pteridicola, and designated a Swedish type collection for the 'true' Tricholomopsis decora, but did not name the others. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports it on tree stumps and logs, especially spruce, in coniferous woodlands across the northern hemisphere, in late summer and autumn (June until November). Wikipedia also reports it in Australia, New Zealand and Costa Rica on introduced pine, probably accidentally introduced. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the well-decayed wood of conifers, occasionally reported in woodchips, sawdust and lignin-rich soil, growing alone, scattered or gregariously, in summer and fall (also winter and spring in warm climates), and widely distributed in North America. The GBIF description gives solitary or caespitose on freshly cut or decayed conifer wood, causing white rot. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
