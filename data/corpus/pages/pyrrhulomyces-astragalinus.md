## Summary

Pyrrhulomyces astragalinus (Fr.) E.J. Tian & Matheny is a species of fungus in the family Strophariaceae, placed in its current genus in 2020. Wikipedia gives its common name as the pinkish-orange pholiota and describes a pink-orange cap and stem that blacken when bruised, with a bitter taste. It grows on decaying conifer wood and is regarded as inedible. *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*

## Discovery and naming

Elias Fries described it in 1821 as Agaricus astragalinus. Rolf Singer transferred it to Pholiota (1951, per the iNaturalist text, which is a declared derivative of Wikipedia). Tian and Matheny (2020) then moved it to the new genus Pyrrhulomyces. Their multigene analysis (ITS, 28S, rpb2) showed that P. astragalinus groups with a new species, P. amariceps, in a lineage separate from Pholiota. All of this comes from a single independent source cluster (Wikipedia and its derivative). *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as saprotrophic on decaying wood, primarily of conifers, on logs and stumps in late stages of decay. It fruits solitary or in small clusters, from late summer through autumn and, in mild coastal climates, into winter. The same source gives a temperate Northern Hemisphere range: Europe (e.g. Italy, Sweden, Switzerland), East Asia (Jilin and Jiangxi provinces, China), and North America from Alaska, British Columbia, Washington and Idaho eastward to Newfoundland and Maine, southward along the Pacific coast and in high-elevation conifer forests of the southern Appalachians (North Carolina, Tennessee). GBIF's description record lists distribution for DK, NO and SE. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
