## Summary

Sarcoscypha austriaca (Beck ex Sacc.) Boud., 1907, the scarlet elfcup, is a saprobic cup fungus in the family Sarcoscyphaceae (Pezizales, Ascomycota). Sources describe a bright red, cup- to saucer-shaped fruiting body, 2 to 7 cm across, with a whitish to pinkish downy underside. It is recorded in Europe and in eastern North America. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name means "from Austria". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Lachnea austriaca Beck ex Sacc. (Syll. Fung. 8: 169, 1889), with the type recorded as on branches in Austria. The combination Sarcoscypha austriaca was made by Boudier in Hist. Class. Discom. Eur. (1907). Wikispecies also lists Peziza imperialis Beck (1884, illegitimate), Scutellinia imperialis Kuntze (1891) and Sarcoscypha austriaca var. lutea Ruini & Ruedl (1999) as heterotypic synonyms. MushroomExpert.Com notes that, to its author's knowledge, no DNA studies have been published to show that the North American version is the same as the original Austrian species. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it growing on fallen pieces of dead hardwood among mosses and leaf litter in damp habitats in winter and early spring, and lists reported hosts including Alnus incana, Salix, Acer and Robinia. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on decaying hardwood sticks and logs, sometimes with buried wood so that the fruiting bodies appear terrestrial, fruiting in spring and widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains. Mushroom World gives late winter to early spring, on decaying hardwood branches, especially willow or alder. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
