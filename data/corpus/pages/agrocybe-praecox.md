## Summary

Agrocybe praecox (Pers.) Fayod, 1889, the spring fieldcap, is a species in the family Strophariaceae. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as appearing early in the year, and Wikipedia treats it as one of a cluster of closely similar species known as the A. praecox complex. MushroomExpert.Com says physical features do not always separate it from its relatives. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the Latin epithet praecox ('early') is related to the word "precocious" and refers to the species often appearing early in the year. MushroomExpert.Com likewise ties the epithet to the species being most frequently found in spring and early summer. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Christiaan Hendrik Persoon first described the mushroom in 1800 as Agaricus praecox. In 1889 Victor Fayod created the genus Agrocybe and made A. praecox its type species. Wikipedia also reports a 1990 study by Timothy Flynn and O. K. Miller that found A. praecox to be one of four species indistinguishable by superficial characters but separable by habitat and location. If that proposal is accepted, A. praecox belongs to a cluster with A. molesta and three species that do not yet have proper names. MushroomExpert.Com states that it was originally described from Germany. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows alone or gregariously in grass, compost, or on wood debris in woods and gardens, and that it is a saprophyte. It gives the North American season as spring to summer. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on woody debris such as woodchips and garden compost, growing gregariously in urban settings and disturbed ground. It reports the mushroom mostly in late spring and early summer, but also in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. MushroomExpert.Com says it is widespread in Europe and widely distributed in North America, and also well documented in Japan and Oceania. Mushroom World describes it growing tufted or scattered on soil among grass, occasionally on rotting straw, and in thickets and woodland edges. These accounts differ somewhat in emphasis (urban woodchips versus grass and woodland edges), and the sources also note that the species is hard to separate from close relatives, so habitat may not be a fully reliable guide to identity. MushroomExpert.Com says distribution and ecology are the main way to tell the related species apart. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
