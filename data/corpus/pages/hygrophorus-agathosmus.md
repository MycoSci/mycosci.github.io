## Summary

Hygrophorus agathosmus (Fr.) Fr. 1838, known in English as the almond woodwax, is a waxy cap in the family Hygrophoraceae. MushroomExpert.Com sets it apart from other gray to brownish gray Hygrophorus species by an almond odor, a preference for conifers, and a dry, whitish, unornamented stem. Wikipedia describes a light grayish cap up to 8 cm across with waxy gills and a distinct odor of bitter almonds. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet agathosmus is derived from the Greek agathos, meaning "good", and osme, meaning "scent". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries first described the species as Agaricus agathosmus in 1815 and moved it into Hygrophorus in 1838. In the interim, Miles Joseph Berkeley named the same species Agaricus cerasinus in 1836 and transferred it to Hygrophorus in 1860. In 1948 Richard Dennis examined the type material and concluded that the two names referred to the same species. Wikipedia also lists Limacium pustulatum var. agathosmum (Kummer, 1871) and Limacium agathosmum (Wünsch, 1877) as historical synonyms, and reports that Hesler and Smith (1963) placed the species in subsection Camarophylli. All of this comes from Wikipedia alone. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia calls it an ectomycorrhizal fungus and says fruiting bodies are found scattered under spruce and pine and in mixed woods in the United States. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, growing scattered or gregariously in late summer and fall, or over winter in warmer climates. It says the species is apparently widely distributed in North America, with the described collections from Colorado and California. Wikipedia also lists Europe (Czech Republic, Poland, Russia, Turkey, United Kingdom), Africa and India; this is a single-source statement. The GBIF description record lists only distribution entries: Belgium, Walloon Region, Flanders, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
