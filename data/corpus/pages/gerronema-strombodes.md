## Summary

Gerronema strombodes (Berk. & Mont.) Singer, 1962, is placed in the Marasmiaceae (Agaricales) and has GBIF occurrence records from many countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a saprobic mushroom of hardwood deadwood. It has a cap that is brown-fibrillose when young and shows yellow as it expands, yellow gills that run down the stem, and a white spore print. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of oaks and other hardwoods, growing gregariously or more often in clusters, from late spring through fall. Kuo calls it well documented on hardwood logs but reports two apparently aberrant collections of his own: one on the deadwood of eastern white pine, and one fruiting from the ground under hardwoods with no woody debris detected nearby. He says the macro- and microscopic features of both match the species but that neither collection has been DNA-sequenced. For North America he describes it as apparently limited to the southeastern United States, the lower Midwest and Mexico, and primarily southeastern and southern Midwestern in herbarium records, with a range extending north to New York. He adds that it has been reported from Central America. The GBIF species description record lists the ecosystem as disturbed secondary forest ("Bosque secundario perturbado"). It gives no host, substrate or season. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
