## Summary

Phellodon confluens (Pers.) Pouzar, 1956, is a tooth fungus placed in the family Thelephoraceae and known in English as the Fused Tooth. It is described as having a northern hemisphere distribution across Asia, Europe and North America, and Wikipedia reports it as considered vulnerable in Switzerland. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the fungus was originally described in 1825 as Hydnum confluens by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon, and that the Czech mycologist Zdenek Pouzar transferred it to Phellodon in 1956. MushroomExpert.Com lists Phellodon amicus and Phellodon vellereus as synonyms. Neither source gives the type material or the reasons for the transfer. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia gives a northern hemisphere distribution in Asia, Europe and North America. It states that in northern Europe the fungus follows the distribution of its host tree (Quercus), extending to central Sweden and southern Finland. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo), writing about eastern North America, describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers, growing alone or gregariously in summer and fall.
