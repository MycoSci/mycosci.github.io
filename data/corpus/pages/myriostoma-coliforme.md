## Summary

Myriostoma coliforme (Dicks.) Corda, 1842, is an earthstar-like fungus in the family Geastraceae, known in English as the pepper pot or saltshaker earthstar. Unlike other earthstars, its spore sac is supported by multiple columns and opens through multiple ostioles rather than a single apical one. It is recorded across the north temperate zone, is uncommon, and is on the Red Lists of 12 European countries. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the generic name Myriostoma derives from Ancient Greek 'myrios' (ten thousand) and 'stoma' (mouth), meaning 'thousands of mouths'. The epithet coliforme derives from Latin 'colum' (strainer) and 'forma' (shape). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Samuel Doody first mentioned the fungus in 1696 in the second edition of John Ray's Synopsis methodica stirpium Britannicarum, describing it as a powdery fungus perforated like a colander with a star-shaped volva, found in Kent in 1695. James Dickson described it scientifically in 1785 from English collections as Lycoperdon coliforme, growing on roadside banks and hedgerows among nettles in Suffolk and Norfolk. Persoon placed it in Geastrum in 1801, and Desvaux erected Myriostoma in 1809 with the illegitimate name Myriostoma anglicum. Gray described the genus Polystoma for it in 1821. Corda moved Dickson's name to Myriostoma in 1842, replacing Desvaux's illegitimate name. Berkeley gave it the English name 'Cullenden puff-ball' and Gray called it the 'sievelike pill-box'. Wikipedia also reports that until 2017 the species was thought to be the only one in the genus, with a worldwide distribution. Molecular work has since revealed at least five additional species in Central and South America, South Africa and Australia, so the range of M. coliforme in the strict sense is uncertain. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as saprotrophic, with fruit bodies growing in groups in well-drained or sandy soil, often in partial shade of trees. It lists deciduous and mixed forests, gardens, hedges, grassy road banks and grazed grasslands, and says the species tends to grow on well-drained south-facing slopes. In Europe its major habitat is given as riparian mixed forest dominated by Salix alba and Populus alba along the great rivers. In Hawaii it has been collected above 2,000 m, apparently favouring mamane (Sophora chrysophylla) forest. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently describes it as saprobic and gregarious in sandy soil in open places but often near trees, fruiting spring through fall. It adds that dried-out specimens are sometimes found over winter, especially in warm climates, and that the illustrated collection is from Michigan. Kuo notes that records from outside Europe, North America and Hawaii probably represent other Myriostoma species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
