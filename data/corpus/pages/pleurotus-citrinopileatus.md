## Summary

Pleurotus citrinopileatus Singer (1943), the golden oyster mushroom, is a gilled fungus in the family Pleurotaceae. Wikipedia describes it as native to eastern Russia, northern China, and Japan. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as apparently escaped from cultivation and naturalizing in eastern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states the species was originally described from southeastern Russia (Singer 1943) and lists Pleurotus cornucopioides var. citrinopileatus as a synonym. Wikipedia states it is very closely related to P. cornucopiae of Europe, with some authors considering them to be at the rank of subspecies. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states that golden oyster mushrooms are cultivated commercially, usually on a medium of grain, straw, or sawdust, with pomace from grapes and olives as alternative substrates. It also reports substrate trials in India using several grass species. MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as a cultivated yellow oyster mushroom that has apparently escaped from cultivation in eastern North America. This is documented in these two sources only; no cultivation protocol was supplied. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing in clusters on the deadwood of hardwoods, especially elms, in summer and fall. It reports the species as originally described from southeastern Russia, also found in China and western Europe, and in North America distributed from Iowa to New York through the Great Lakes states. Wikipedia describes it as a wood-decay fungus that in the wild most commonly decays hardwoods such as elm. Wikipedia also reports naturalized populations in the United States on oak, elm, beech, and other hardwoods. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
