## Summary

Suillus cothurnatus is a species of Suillus in the family Suillaceae, described by Rolf Singer in 1945 and originally described from Florida. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with pines and notes that it is very difficult to separate morphologically from Suillus salmonicolor. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described scientifically by Rolf Singer in 1945, and MushroomExpert.Com gives Florida as the place of original description (Singer 1945). MushroomExpert.Com's author, Michael Kuo, writes that the morphological differences between S. cothurnatus and S. salmonicolor are few and unreliable. Kuo cites a 2016 DNA study by Nguyen and collaborators in which specimens identified as either species formed one group without separating into two clearly defined sub-groups, which suggests the two may be the same species. According to Kuo, the authors could not study the type collections, so support for synonymy remained inconclusive. This is a single-source account from MushroomExpert.Com. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes S. cothurnatus as mycorrhizal with loblolly pine and longleaf pine (possibly also slash pine). It grows alone, scattered, or gregariously in late summer and fall, or over winter in warmer climates. That source describes it as widely distributed where the host trees occur naturally, from Mexico through Texas to Florida and New Jersey, and in plantations outside that range. It also reports the species from the Caribbean, Central America, South America, South Africa and Australia. Wikipedia separately states that it grows scattered or in groups on the ground in sandy soil near pine, especially two-needled pines such as red pine and jack pine. It gives the US range as Texas to Florida and north to North Carolina, and says the species is also present in Mexico, has been introduced to Brazil, and has been recorded from the Cameron Highlands in Malaysia. The two sources name different host pines and different northern range limits. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
