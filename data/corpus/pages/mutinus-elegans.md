## Summary

Mutinus elegans (Mont.) E. Fisch. (1888) is a stinkhorn in the family Phallaceae. Sources describe a headless, hollow orange stalk that rises from a whitish "egg" and is topped with foul-smelling, olive-brown to brown spore slime. It is recorded mainly from eastern North America, with reports from Europe and Japan. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name Mutinus refers to the Roman phallic deity Mutunus Tutunus, and that the specific epithet elegans is derived from the Latin word meaning "graceful" or "elegant". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described in 1679 by John Banister, a British missionary who chronicled the natural history of Virginia, and that this is thought to be the first account of a fungus in North America. It was first characterized scientifically by Jean Pierre François Camille Montagne in 1856 as Corynites elegans. Wikispecies lists Corynites elegans Mont. (1856) as the basionym and gives the combination in Mutinus as E. Fisch., Syll. Fung. 7: 13, 1888. MushroomExpert.Com notes that Montagne originally described it as "indigenous to North America" and lists Mutinus curtisii as a synonym. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously in gardens and cultivated areas or in woods, in summer and fall. It reports the species as widely distributed in eastern North America and the Midwest, from the northeastern United States to Ontario, Minnesota, Texas and Florida, and also present in Hawaii and temperate Europe. Wikipedia describes it as commonly found in gardens and farm areas enriched with manure, near well-decayed stumps and logs, and in wood chips. It also cites a Japanese publication recording fruiting in November and December on the ground along paths or in open spaces, under or near bamboo (Phyllostachys bambusoides) and hardwoods such as sawtooth oak, Japanese Zelkova and Camphor tree. Wikipedia gives the North American range as Quebec to Florida and west to the Great Lakes, Iowa, Colorado and Texas. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
