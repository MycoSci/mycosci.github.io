## Summary

Strobilurus tenacellus (Pers.) Singer, 1962, is an agaric in the family Physalacriaceae, known in English as the pinecone cap. Wikipedia describes it as a small mushroom found in Europe and Asia on fallen pine and spruce cones. GBIF's description record lists a distribution in Belgium, Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet tenacellus is a diminutive form of the Latin word tenax, meaning "tough". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus tenacellus by Christian Hendrik Persoon in his 1796 Observationes Mycologicae. It was later moved to Collybia, then to Marasmius by Jules Favre in 1939, and then to Pseudohiatula by Georges Métrod in 1952. Rolf Singer transferred it to the newly circumscribed genus Strobilurus in 1962, giving the name in current use. This account comes from one source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a saprobic wood-rotting fungus that fruits singly or in small groups on fallen and often partially buried cones of Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris), European black pine (Pinus nigra) and sometimes spruce (Picea), in coniferous and mixed forests. The same source says it has been recorded in Europe and Asia, including Japan and Jordan. In Europe it usually fruits from March to June, and its occurrence is described as occasional. All of this comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*
