## Summary

Auricularia nigricans (Sw.) Birkebak, Looney & Sánchez-García (2013) is a jelly fungus in the family Auriculariaceae. Wikipedia describes its fruitbodies as gelatinous and ear-like, growing on dead wood of broadleaf trees. Wikipedia also notes that the name Auricularia polytricha is a synonym of A. nigricans when used by authors from the Americas, but when used by Asian authors it likely refers instead to Auricularia cornea. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1788 as Peziza nigrescens by the Swedish botanist Olof Swartz, from a collection he made in Jamaica. Swartz later changed the name to Peziza nigricans, which the mycologist Elias Magnus Fries accepted, and Fries's sanctioned name takes priority over Swartz's original. Bernard Lowy rejected the name as "doubtful" in his monograph of Auricularia because the habitat description read "ad terra", and he preferred Auricularia polytricha. Swartz's original collection still exists, which allows the identity of his species to be confirmed. Molecular research based on DNA sequences has shown that A. nigricans is distinct. This account comes from a single source cluster, the Wikipedia article. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes A. nigricans as a wood-rotting species, typically found on dead attached or fallen wood of broadleaf trees. A GBIF description text gives the substrate as deciduous wood. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
