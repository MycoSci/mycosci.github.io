## Summary

Lentinula edodes (Berk.) Pegler 1976, published in Index of Fungi 4: 411, is a member of the Omphalotaceae known in English as shiitake. Wikipedia describes it as native to East Asia and mainland Southeast Asia and cultivated around the globe, with the earliest written record of cultivation dating to 1209 in Song dynasty China. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia gives the Japanese name shiitake (椎茸) as a compound of shii (椎, Castanopsis), for the tree Castanopsis cuspidata that provides the dead logs on which it is typically cultivated, and take (茸, 'mushroom'). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was first described scientifically as Agaricus edodes by Miles Joseph Berkeley, and David Pegler placed it in the genus Lentinula in 1976. Wikipedia's text gives 1877 for Berkeley's description, while its synonym list dates Agaricus edodes Berk. to 1878; the source does not resolve this. It lists an extensive synonymy, including Armillaria edodes (Sacc., 1887), Cortinellus edodes (S.Ito & S.Imai, 1938) and Lentinus edodes (Singer, 1941), as well as names based on Collybia shiitake J.Schröt. (1886). It does not say why the transfers were made. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states that the earliest written record of shiitake cultivation is in the Records of Longquan County (龍泉縣志), compiled by He Zhan (何澹) in 1209 during the Song dynasty. It says this 185-word description was later cross-referenced many times and adapted in 1796 by the Japanese horticulturist Satō Chūryō (佐藤中陵) in the first book on shiitake cultivation in Japan. In the traditional Japanese method, shii trees were cut with axes and the logs placed by trees already growing shiitake or containing shiitake spores. Wikipedia reports that shiitake are widely cultivated worldwide, contributing about 25% of total yearly mushroom production. It adds that commercial cultivation is typically on artificial substrate or hardwood logs such as oak, in conditions similar to the natural environment. These are single-source claims (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that shiitake grow in groups on the decaying wood of deciduous trees, particularly shii and other chinquapins, chestnut, oak, maple, beech, sweetgum, poplar, hornbeam, ironwood and mulberry. It gives the natural distribution as warm and moist climates in Southeast Asia and describes the species as native to East Asia and mainland Southeast Asia. GBIF's description records give the native range as East Asia (Eastern Asia). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
