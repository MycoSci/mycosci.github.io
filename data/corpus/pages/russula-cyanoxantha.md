## Summary

Russula cyanoxantha (Schaeff.) Fr. (1863) is a species in the family Russulaceae, known in English as the charcoal burner or variegated russula. Sources describe a cap whose colour varies considerably and is often mottled, with a white spore print and gills that are flexible and greasy rather than brittle. MushroomExpert.Com cautions that several distinct North American mushrooms may currently be labelled with this name and may not be the same as the European species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the German Association of Mycology designated it "Mushroom of the Year" in 1997. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a mycorrhizal fungus growing in slightly acidic, nutrient-rich soil, found most commonly in beech forests and often in deciduous or mixed forests. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall, and widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia reports it as described from July to October in eastern North America and the Mountain states, and from October to January further west. Both sources say that North American material may not be the European species. A GBIF description record lists Colombia (Tolima). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
