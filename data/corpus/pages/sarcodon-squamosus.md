## Summary

Sarcodon squamosus (Schaeff.) Quél. 1886, known in English as Scaly Tooth, is a member of the Bankeraceae (Thelephorales). Mushroom World describes it as a large mushroom with a coarsely scaly, pale brown cap 10–25 cm across and a greyish, spiny under-surface, and gives its location as Europe. *[mushroomworld]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was previously regarded as a subspecies of Sarcodon imbricatus. It reports that molecular analysis of DNA showed the two forms to be genetically distinct, so populations that had been described as S. imbricatus were reassigned to S. squamosus, including collections from the British Isles and the Netherlands. This account comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia notes the species is significant in mushroom dyeing as one of the only mushroom species said to dye in shades of blue. It reports that young mushrooms give brown shades, while older, almost mature ones give rarer blues, turquoise and greens. This is a single-source claim. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Mushroom World states that it grows particularly with pines, solitary or in scattered groups, on the soil in coniferous woods, and gives its location as Europe. Wikipedia states that it often grows in large fairy rings near pine heaths and in rocky flat soil. Wikipedia also says it belongs to growing areas with old trees where forest type and growth type have long continuity, and that it does not withstand complete felling. *[mushroomworld, wikipedia]*
