## Summary

Lentinellus micheneri (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Pegler is a wood-inhabiting fungus in the family Auriscalpiaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small, brown, "omphalinoid" mushroom with a 1–5 cm cap that develops a deep central navel, white spore print, and saw-toothed gill edges. MushroomExpert.Com treats the name as covering a group of cryptic species that are virtually inseparable without mating studies or ITS sequences. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states it was first described in 1853 by Miles Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis as Lentinus micheneri, and that David Pegler transferred it to Lentinellus in 1983. MushroomExpert.Com gives the original collection locality as Pennsylvania, and notes that mating studies (Petersen & Hughes 2004) led to the separation of L. micheneri, L. subaustralis and L. flabelliformis, with the author of that page choosing to keep them together as a species group. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (a single source) describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, or most commonly gregariously in small clusters on deadwood of hardwoods (North America) or conifers (Europe), or appearing terrestrial amid woody debris, from spring through fall. That page describes the distribution as that of a species group, throughout Europe and North America, and its illustrated collections are from Illinois. *[mushroomexpert]*
