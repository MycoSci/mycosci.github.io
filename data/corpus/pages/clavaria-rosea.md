## Summary

Clavaria rosea Fr. (1821) is a coral fungus in the family Clavariaceae, recorded in many countries including Great Britain, the Netherlands, Canada and the United States. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as pink, smooth and unbranched with rounded tips, up to 4 cm high and 3 mm thick, on a black stem up to 1.1 cm long. The same source gives the spores as smooth, translucent, inamyloid and pip-shaped, measuring 7–10 by 2–3 μm; this description comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows singly on the ground in mixed forests, and that it is found in Asia, Europe and North America. A 2009 survey of clavarioid fungi in Estonia (Folia Cryptogamica Estonica) lists it among rare and threatened species of old-growth habitats. A 2014 paper in Acta Mycologica lists it among 19 rare or infrequently collected aphyllophoroid fungi in Finland. GBIF distribution data list Denmark, Norway and Vermont. *[wikipedia, lit_3, lit_2, gbif_desc]*
