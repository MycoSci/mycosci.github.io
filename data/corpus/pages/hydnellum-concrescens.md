## Summary

Hydnellum concrescens (Pers.) Banker 1906, in the family Bankeraceae, is a tooth fungus commonly called the zoned tooth. Its spores are produced on spines under the cap rather than on gills. The cap usually shows concentric zones of colour or texture and may develop radial ridges or outgrowths. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com says the Latin name refers to the species' tendency to develop 'concrescent' fruiting bodies that have grown together and become fused. It adds that many Hydnellum species show this tendency to varying degrees. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, especially oaks. It grows alone or gregariously in summer and fall and is widely distributed in eastern North America. The same source notes that a western form has been recorded under conifers, and that it may not be the same species. This is a single-source description, and the author also says the North American version may not be the same as the classical European species. *[mushroomexpert]*
