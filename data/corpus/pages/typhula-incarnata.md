## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only descriptive source, presents this as a turfgrass pathogen of areas with extended snow cover. It lists hosts including annual bluegrass, colonial bentgrass, creeping bentgrass, fine-leaf fescues, Kentucky bluegrass, perennial ryegrass and tall fescue. It states that the fungus begins producing sclerotia in the cool, damp conditions of fall and develops when prolonged snow cover (around 60 days) lies on unfrozen ground. Signs are described as circular grayish-brown patches of mycelium, about 15 cm across, visible in spring after snowmelt. Wikipedia also calls it common in Wisconsin and other areas of the Midwest. GBIF's distribution note lists Belgium (Flemish Region), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
