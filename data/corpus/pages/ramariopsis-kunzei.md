## Summary

Ramariopsis kunzei (Fr.) Corner, 1950, is a coral fungus in the family Clavariaceae and the type species of the genus Ramariopsis. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a white, repeatedly branched fungus with blunt branch tips and a white spore print. Wikipedia reports it from North America, Eurasia and Australia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the name "white coral" comes from the branched structure of the fruit bodies, which resemble marine coral. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described as Clavaria kunzei by Elias Magnus Fries in 1821, and that E. J. H. Corner transferred it to Ramariopsis in 1950 and made it the type species. Wikipedia also says it was placed in Ramaria by Lucien Quélet in 1888 and in Clavulinopsis by Walter Jülich in 1985. MushroomExpert.Com's reference line gives the same authority and dates (Fries, 1821; Corner, 1950). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the species is thought to be saprobic. It grows on the ground, in duff, or less frequently on well-decayed wood, either singly, in groups or clustered. Wikipedia notes that David Arora has recorded a preference for growing under conifers and a prevalence in North American redwood forests, whereas an earlier author claimed it grows "rarely in coniferous woods." MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently describes it as probably saprobic, growing terrestrially or rarely from well-decayed wood, scattered or gregarious, in woods, in summer and fall (or winter in warm climates). Kuo calls it widely distributed in North America but apparently rare in the Rocky Mountains. His own collections came from under mixed conifers and hardwoods in northern Michigan and from under redwood in California. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
