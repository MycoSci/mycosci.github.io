## Summary

Squamanita paradoxa (A.H. Sm. & Singer) Bas, 1965, has occurrence records across Europe and North America. Wikipedia describes it as a parasite on the fruit bodies of Cystoderma amianthinum: it replaces the host's cap and gills with its own but keeps the host's stipe. Wikipedia also reports that recent DNA-based work places it in the genus Dissoderma, not in Squamanita sensu stricto. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Cystoderma paradoxum by Alexander H. Smith and Rolf Singer in 1948, from specimens collected in Mount Hood National Forest, Oregon. Wikispecies lists the holotype as MICH, Smith 27987, and gives the original publication as Mycologia 40(4): 454 (1948). Cornelis Bas transferred it to Squamanita in 1965. Wikispecies records a further combination, Dissoderma paradoxum (A.H.Sm. & Singer) Singer, from 1973. Wikipedia says that molecular phylogenetic analysis has since shown the species belongs in Dissoderma. Wikispecies also lists Squamanita basii and Squamanita umbilicata, both described by Harmaja in 1988, as heterotypic synonyms. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it is a parasitic fungus growing on the fruit bodies of another fungus, Cystoderma amianthinum, and that it occurs in both North America and Europe. *[wikipedia]*
