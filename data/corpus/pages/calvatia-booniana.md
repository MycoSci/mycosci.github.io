## Summary

Calvatia booniana A.H. Sm. (1964), the western giant puffball, is a puffball species whose type locality is Oregon. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a very large, stemless puffball that can exceed 60 cm across. Its surface is sculpted into large polygonal warts, and it is described as common in the Rocky Mountains. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that Alexander Smith named the species in honor of William Judson Boone, the first president of the College of Idaho, who was a botanist and a Presbyterian minister. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the original publication as A.H. Sm., in Zeller & Smith, Lloydia 27: 164 (1964). MushroomExpert.Com adds an anecdote, introduced with "Legend has it", that the naming is linked to Boone officiating at the wedding of Alexander and Helen Smith. In that account the prenuptial class turned into a discussion of local mushrooms and a field trip to see some large puffballs Boone had been watching. This anecdote comes from a single source and is presented there as legend. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and terrestrial, growing alone, gregariously, or in fairy rings in grassy areas or under sagebrush. It is found at low elevation in fields and desert-like meadows and at higher elevation in subalpine meadows, in summer and fall. That source says it is distributed throughout the western mountains but is more common in the Rockies, and the collections it illustrates and describes are from Colorado. Wikipedia says it grows on composted soil in meadows, fields and forests, and on roadsides, sagebrush flats and pastures. Wikipedia also says it occurs on the west side of the Rocky Mountains. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
