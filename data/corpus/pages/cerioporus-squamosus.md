## Summary

Cerioporus squamosus (Huds.) Quél. 1886 is a bracket fungus in the family Polyporaceae, known in English as dryad's saddle and pheasant's back mushroom. Wikipedia describes it as a white-rot fungus of hardwood trees, widespread in Eurasia and North America. It is still widely known under the name Polyporus squamosus. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that squamosus comes from the Latin for covered with scales or scaly, referring to the dark brown scales on the cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says "dryad's saddle" refers to the dryads of Greek mythology, who could conceivably sit and rest on the mushroom, and that "pheasant's back" derives from the colour pattern of the bracket matching a pheasant's back. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that William Hudson first described the species in 1778 as Boletus squamosus. Lucien Quélet gave it its current name in 1886, although it is still widely known by Fries's name Polyporus squamosus. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia notes that some people value the thick, stiff paper that can be made from this and many other mushrooms of the genus Cerioporus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as an annual that grows on dead logs and stumps or on living hardwood trees, causing a white rot in the heartwood. It is particularly common on dead elm and is also found on living maple. Other hosts listed are ash, beech, horse-chestnut, Persian walnut, lime, plane, poplar, magnolia and willow. It is occasionally a parasite on living trees. It is reported over much of Europe (July–November) and east of the Rocky Mountains in the United States (April–October), and also in Asia and Australia. It commonly fruits in spring, occasionally in autumn, and rarely in other seasons. This is a single-cluster account (Wikipedia, with iNaturalist as a derivative). *[wikipedia]*
