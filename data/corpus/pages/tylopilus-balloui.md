## Summary

Tylopilus balloui (Peck) Singer, 1947, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, listed under the vernacular name Burnt-orange Bolete. MushroomExpert.Com describes the cap as bright reddish orange when young, fading to reddish brown, cinnamon brown or tan, with a creamy whitish pore surface that bruises brown. The same source, quoting Halling et al. (2008), says the species as currently circumscribed represents a species complex rather than a single species. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com gives the reference authority as (Peck, 1911) Singer, 1947. It reports that the species was combined into Rubinoboletus by Heinemann & Rammeloo (1993), but that later studies (Halling et al. 2008; Osmundson & Halling 2010) confirm its position in Tylopilus. The same source notes that Tylopilus oradivensis, a balloui-like species, has been named from Costa Rica and that others may be separated in the future. It lists Tylopilus ballouii as an alternate spelling. This account comes from a single field-guide source. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the only supplied source with ecological text, T. balloui is mycorrhizal with hardwoods (especially oaks and beech) but occasionally reported with pines. It grows alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall. It is primarily southeastern in distribution but reported as far north as New York and Massachusetts. The illustrated and described collections are from Maryland, Mississippi, Missouri, New Jersey, and Virginia. *[mushroomexpert]*
