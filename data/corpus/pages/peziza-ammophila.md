## Summary

Peziza ammophila Durieu & Lév. (1848) is a cup fungus in the family Pezizaceae, with a catalog type locality of Algeria. Sources describe it as a cup-shaped fungus of sand dunes and beaches that emerges from the sand and splits as it ages. A Dutch common name, Zandtulpjes ("sand tulips"), is reported by Wikipedia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that synonymy for this species is confusing and poorly documented, and that there may be several genetically distinct species "masquerading" as Peziza ammophila. Its author suspects that Sepultaria arenicola and Sepultaria arenosa, both described as sand-loving species, should be regarded as synonyms, but says this synonymy has not been officially published to his knowledge. This is a single-source opinion. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both place it on sand dunes and beaches. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or in clusters in sand on or near beaches and dunes, buried when young and emerging somewhat and splitting at the edges as it matures. The two sources differ on season: Wikipedia says winter and spring, while MushroomExpert.Com says spring through winter. Wikipedia lists records from North America, Argentina, Chile, Uruguay, the UK, Europe, Australia and South Africa. MushroomExpert.Com says it is probably widely distributed in North America. The GBIF descriptions record lists distribution entries for Belgium (including the Flemish Region), Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
