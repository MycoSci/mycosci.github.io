## Summary

Hemileccinum impolitum (Fr.) Šutara (2008) is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the Iodine Bolete. Wikipedia describes it as native to Europe and widely distributed in temperate and southern Europe, where it grows in mycorrhizal association with broad-leaved trees, particularly oak (Quercus). It has tubes and pores rather than gills. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the epithet impolitum as meaning "rough" and says it likely refers to the cap, which is initially felty and covered in a finely filamentous coating. This is the only source that discusses the derivation, and it hedges with "likely". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the name iodine bolete is used because the fruit bodies tend to emit an iodine-like odour when cut. It adds that the odour is more detectable in the stem base or in overripe specimens. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by Elias Magnus Fries in the genus Boletus, and Wikispecies lists the basionym as Boletus impolitus Fr. (1838). Wikipedia says its placement long remained uncertain, with various authors using other genera including Tubiporus and Versipellis. It says Manfred Binder and Halmut Besl placed the species in Xerocomus in 2000, based on preliminary analysis of the 28S ribosomal RNA locus. In 2008 Josef Šutara transferred it to the new genus Hemileccinum, based on its distinctive morphology. Wikipedia reports that phylogenetic work by Wu and colleagues in 2014 found collections identified as this species in a distinct lineage within the subfamily Xerocomoideae, closely related to Corneroboletus, and so not in Boletus, Xerocomus or Leccinum. It adds that later studies have confirmed the monophyly of the genus, which presently includes two European species, H. impolitum and H. depilatum. Wikispecies lists further synonyms, including Boletus suspectus Krombh. (1836), Leccinum impolitum (Fr.) Bertault (1980), Tubiporus impolitus (Fr.) P. Karst. (1882), Versipellis impolita (Fr.) Quél. (1886) and Xerocomus impolitus (Fr.) Quél. (1888). *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as ecologically versatile. It forms ectomycorrhizal associations with several species of oak (Quercus), and occasionally with beech (Fagus) and chestnut (Castanea). The same source says it does not appear to be substrate-specific and has been reported from both calcareous and acidic soil. It says that in the United Kingdom the species is occasionally found in southern England, with finds elsewhere in the country also reported. Wikipedia states that molecular phylogenetic testing has verified its presence in Estonia, France, Germany, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, and the Mediterranean islands of Cyprus and Sardinia. These habitat statements come from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*
