## Summary

Coprinopsis nivea, the snowy inkcap, is a species of Psathyrellaceae. It was transferred to Coprinopsis by Redhead, Vilgalys & Moncalvo in 2001. Wikipedia describes it as a small inkcap with a white, powdery-veiled cap when young, and reports it growing on old dung. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet nivea (originally niveus) as Latin for 'snowy' or 'snow-covered', and says it refers to the powdery white appearance of the mushroom. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Christiaan Hendrik Persoon first described the species in 1801 as Agaricus niveus. Elias Magnus Fries reclassified it as Coprinus niveus in 1838. In 2001, phylogenetic analysis restructured the genus Coprinus, and Scott Alan Redhead, Rytas J. Vilgalys and Jean-Marc Moncalvo reclassified it as Coprinopsis nivea. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, a single-source claim, reports that it grows in small trooping or tufting groups on old dung, especially that of cows and horses, from summer through late autumn. *[wikipedia]*
