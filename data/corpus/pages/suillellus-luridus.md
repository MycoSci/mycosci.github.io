## Summary

Suillellus luridus (Schaeff.) Murrill, the lurid bolete, is a member of the Boletaceae, described by Schaeffer in 1774 as Boletus luridus and transferred to Suillellus by Murrill in 1909. Wikipedia describes it as a firm bolete with an olive-brown cap up to 20 cm across, small orange or red pores (yellow when young), and a stout ochre stem patterned with a red network. Like several other red-pored boletes, it stains blue when bruised or cut. GBIF holds occurrence records from many European countries. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is the Latin adjective luridus, meaning 'sallow'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the German botanist Jacob Christian Schäffer described Boletus luridus in 1774 in his series on the fungi of Bavaria and the Palatinate, Fungorum qui in Bavaria et Palatinatu circa Ratisbonam nascuntur icones. It was later moved between Boletaceae genera: Leccinum by Gray in 1821, Tubiporus by Karsten in 1881, Dictyopus by Quélet in 1888 and Suillellus by Murrill in 1909. According to the same source, phylogenetic studies showed Boletus to be strongly paraphyletic in its traditional sense. Boletus was then fragmented, and the fungus has been placed in Suillellus since 2014. Several varieties, a subspecies and a form were described by European mycologists. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fungus grows in a mycorrhizal association with broad-leaved trees such as oak (Quercus), birch (Betula), chestnut (Castanea) and beech (Fagus), on chalky (calcareous) soils. Fruit bodies grow singly or scattered on the ground, from June to November after summer rains. The same source says it is very rarely found in acidic soils and may occur in parks near a single tree. From phylogenetically verified collections and DNA studies, Wikipedia concludes the fungus appears to be native to Europe. It lists Austria, Cyprus, Denmark, Estonia, France, Italy, Montenegro and Sweden as documented. It describes reports from outside Europe that predate DNA studies as unconfirmed and more likely to be misidentified similar taxa. *[wikipedia]*
