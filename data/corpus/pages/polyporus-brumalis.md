## Summary

Polyporus brumalis (Pers.) Fr. (1818) is a polypore in the family Polyporaceae, recorded in GBIF from many countries in Europe, North America and Asia. Index Fungorum currently treats it as Lentinus brumalis. Sources describe a small, stalked, dark yellow-brown to dark brown polypore with a white spore print and small white pores, growing on dead hardwood. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet brumalis means "occurring in the winter" and describes the species' tendency to fruit in winter. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Boletus brumalis in 1794 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon, in his "Neuer Versuch einer systematischen Eintheilung der Schwämme". Wikipedia says it was transferred to Lentinus in 2010 by Ivan V. Zmitrovich. MushroomExpert.Com also gives the reference as Persoon 1794, Fries 1818. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com and Wikipedia both describe it as saprobic on dead or decaying hardwood, especially birch. Wikipedia also names beech and mountain ash, and says that in rare cases it grows on conifers such as hemlock and fir. Wikipedia describes it as growing solitary or in small groups. Mushroom World gives fallen branches and logs lying on soil. The sources differ on season. MushroomExpert.Com says fall and spring, but found nearly year-round in North America. Wikipedia says June through October in eastern North America and late October and March in Northern Europe. Mushroom World says late autumn and throughout winter. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
