## Summary

Volvopluteus gloiocephalus (DC.) Vizzini, Contu & Justo (2011) is a species in the family Pluteaceae and the type species of the genus Volvopluteus. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe a large mushroom with a cap that is sticky when fresh and white to grey, gills that start white and turn pink, and a cup- or sack-like volva at the base of the stem. For most of the 20th century it was known as Volvariella gloiocephala or V. speciosa. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet gloiocephalus comes from the Greek gloia (glue or glutinous substance) and kephalē (head), meaning "with a sticky head", in reference to the viscid cap surface. This derivation is given by a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described as Agaricus gloiocephalus by Augustin Pyramus de Candolle in 1815 and sanctioned under that name by Fries in 1821. Claude Gillet transferred it to Volvaria in 1878. The genus name Volvariella was adopted for this group in 1953, but the combination Volvariella gloiocephala was not formally proposed until 1986, by Teun Boekhout and Manfred Enderle. Most 20th-century authors had treated the dark-capped form as a variety, Volvariella speciosa var. gloiocephala, of the white-capped V. speciosa. Boekhout & Enderle showed that white and dark basidiocarps can arise from the same mycelium, and treated the two epithets as one species with gloiocephalus having priority. They designated a neotype in 1996. A phylogenetic study by Justo and colleagues found the species falls outside the majority of Volvariella, which led to the genus Volvopluteus, created in 2011 with this species as its type. MushroomExpert.Com's author, Michael Kuo, says he is not sure the colour forms are one species, based on differences between his California and Midwestern collections. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a saprotrophic mushroom that grows on the ground in gardens and grassy fields, both in and outside forest areas, and on accumulations of vegetable matter such as compost or woodchip piles. It has also been reported fruiting in greenhouses, and in bamboo thickets in China. It usually fruits in groups but can also be solitary, and a season of spectacular fruiting may be followed by several years with no appearance. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and terrestrial, growing alone or gregariously in urban settings and disturbed ground (landscaping, ditches, beaches, lawns, gardens) and generally not from logs or trees. It adds that east of the Rocky Mountains it sometimes grows in forests, and that it is found year-round depending on climate. Wikipedia reports the species from all continents except Antarctica, usually under the names Volvariella gloiocephala or V. speciosa. It says molecular data have so far confirmed its occurrence only in Europe and North America. MushroomExpert.Com notes that some records from east of the Rocky Mountains may represent other similar species, including V. michiganensis. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
