## Summary

Cyanoboletus pulverulentus (Opat.) Gelardi, Vizzini & Simonini, 2014, is a species of Boletaceae commonly called the ink stain bolete; it is the type species of Cyanoboletus. Wikipedia describes the flesh as turning blackish-blue when handled. GBIF holds occurrence records from dozens of countries, but the supplied sources contain little descriptive text beyond a single encyclopedia entry. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet pulverulentus means "covered with powder" and refers to the somewhat dry powdery surface of the young cap and stalk. This derivation comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Boletus pulverulentus by the German mycologist Wilhelm Opatowski in 1836. It was transferred in 2014 to the newly created genus Cyanoboletus, where it is the type species. Wikipedia also notes that a North American collection reported in GenBank (accession KF030313) does not match European material on 28S rDNA. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes C. pulverulentus as an ectomycorrhizal species that associates with coniferous and deciduous trees, particularly oak. It says fruit bodies appear on the ground, usually singly, in woodland. It also gives deciduous and mixed forests, particularly on moist soil on slopes and under beech and oak, as habitat. Wikipedia reports the species from northern Asia, Europe, North Africa, Central and northern South America, and eastern North America, where it gives July to September as the fruiting period. It adds that reports from western North America could refer to the similar Cyanoboletus rainsii. All of this habitat information comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*
