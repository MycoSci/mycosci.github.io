## Summary

Mrakia frigida (Fell, Statzell, I.L. Hunter & Phaff) Y. Yamada & Komag. 1987 is a species in the family Mrakiaceae, order Cystofilobasidiales. It occurs in both a yeast and a hyphal state, the latter producing teliospores from which basidia emerge. It was originally isolated in its yeast state from Antarctic snow. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Leucosporidium frigidum Fell, Statzell, I.L. Hunter & Phaff, published in Antonie van Leeuwenhoek 35(4): 450 (1969), with the locality recorded as isolated from snow in Antarctica. The combination Mrakia frigida was made by Y. Yamada & Komagata in the Journal of General and Applied Microbiology 33(5): 457 (1987). Wikispecies lists a paper by those authors that establishes the genus Mrakia, titled as a genus for Q8-equipped, self-sporulating organisms that produce a unicellular metabasidium, formerly classified in Leucosporidium. Wikispecies also lists Candida curiosa Komag. & Nakase (1965), isolated from frozen Sillago japonica in Japan, as a heterotypic synonym. *[wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species was originally isolated in its yeast state from Antarctic snow. Wikispecies records the heterotypic synonym Candida curiosa as isolated from frozen Sillago japonica in Japan. Wikipedia classifies M. frigida as an obligate psychrophile that grows at −2 to 20 °C and cannot grow above about 18–20 °C. The GBIF description record lists a distribution of Norway and Sweden. No source states a host, substrate type or season beyond these isolation records. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, gbif_desc]*
