## Summary

Gymnopilus junonius (Fr.) P.D. Orton, 1960, is a member of the Hymenogastraceae. Sources describe it as a large orange mushroom that grows in dense clusters on stumps and logs and has a bitter taste. Wikipedia describes it as inedible. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that Fries (1821) originally described the species from Europe as Agaricus junonius, and that Gymnopilus spectabilis is a synonym. The same source says that, to its author's knowledge, no contemporary DNA-based study has determined whether North American versions of this fungus, which vary substantially in ecology and morphology, deserve new names. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the rotting wood of hardwoods and conifers, usually growing in clusters, in summer and fall (fall through spring on the West Coast). It describes the species as widely distributed in North America, with the illustrated collections from California, Oregon and Québec. Wikipedia says it grows in dense clusters on stumps and logs of hardwoods and conifers, is most common in moist, lowland wooded areas near rivers, and is found in Europe, Australasia and South America. The two sources disagree on North American occurrence (see disagreements). *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
