## Summary

Hericium erinaceus (Bull.) Pers. 1797, commonly called lion's mane, is a tooth fungus in the family Hericiaceae. Sources describe it as native to North America, Europe and Asia. Its fruiting body is a single unbranched clump of dangling spines, mostly longer than 1 cm. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, inaturalist]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that both the genus name Hericium and the species epithet erinaceus mean 'hedgehog' in Latin. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia reports that the Japanese name yamabushitake refers to the yamabushi, the mountain ascetics of the syncretistic religion Shugendo. The Chinese name hóutóugū means 'monkey-head mushroom', and the Korean name noru gungdeng-i beoseot means 'deer butt mushroom'. It also gives the German name Igel-Stachelbart (literally 'hedgehog-goatee') and notes that English names such as bearded hedgehog reflect the same 'hedgehog' meaning. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia describes the fruiting bodies as common in gourmet cooking, with young specimens considered best, and used as a specialty mushroom alongside shiitake and oyster mushrooms. A 2013 review (lit_1) describes a long history of use in traditional Chinese medicine, and a 2015 review (lit_2) describes it as widely consumed in Asian countries. Wikipedia says it is marketed as a dietary supplement for purported memory benefits, but that evidence for the supplements' safety and effectiveness is insufficient. *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_2]*

## Cultivation

According to Wikipedia, production is widespread in Asia, mostly by extensive methods on wood logs or stumps. Production in North America is small-scale and mostly intensive indoor work, with a few outdoor log-cultivation sites. Substrates are typically hardwood or conifer sawdust supplemented with grains or brans. Wikipedia reports a first artificial cultivation in China in 1988, using artificial logs, bottles and polypropylene bags. It also describes submerged liquid culture for producing mycelial biomass and metabolites. The same article reports that optimum mycelial growth was found at about 25 °C and 85–90% relative humidity. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as common in late summer and autumn on hardwoods, particularly American beech and maple, mostly as a saprophyte on dead trees. It can also occur on living trees, usually at a wound, where it causes a white pocket rot. Wikipedia gives fruiting times of August to November in Europe, and October to February in the west, July to October in the Mountain states and September to February in the east of North America. It lists North American hosts including maple, ash, oaks and eucalyptus. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic and parasitic, usually growing alone or in pairs from wounds of living hardwoods, especially oaks. It gives fruiting as late summer and fall, or over winter and spring in warmer climates. The guide notes the species was originally described from France and is widespread in Europe and across North America from Canada to Mexico. Its illustrated collections come from Illinois and Missouri. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
