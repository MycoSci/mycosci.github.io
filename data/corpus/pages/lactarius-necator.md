## Summary

Lactarius necator (Bull.) Pers., 1800, is a member of the Russulaceae with occurrence records across Europe and in North America, Australia, New Zealand and Japan. Wikipedia describes the mushroom under the name Lactarius turpis, known in English as the ugly milk-cap or green birch milk cap, and says necator, turpis and plumbeus are usually, but not always, considered the same species. It describes a cap 4–20 cm across that is olive-brown to yellow-green, often sticky or slimy in the middle and darkening to blackish with age, with crowded, somewhat decurrent gills and a cream spore print. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the species' messy, dirty appearance gives it its English name (ugly milk-cap) and the Latin epithet turpis. This is a single-source account and concerns the name as applied under L. turpis. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the species is very variable and that there is much disagreement over its naming. It lists Lactarius turpis, L. necator and L. plumbeus as the three main designations, usually but not always treated as synonyms. It reports that the epithets necator and plumbeus were both coined by Pierre Bulliard, as Agaricus necator (1791) and Agaricus plumbeus (1793), and that there is and was confusion over which mushrooms he meant. It attributes the name turpis to Johann Anton Weinmann, taken over by Elias Magnus Fries in 1838. This comes from a single encyclopedia source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, writing under the name Lactarius turpis, says it is found naturally in Europe and Siberia and has been introduced to Australia and New Zealand. It also reports the species in North America, in the northeast (July–September) and near the West Coast (September–November). It is described as especially associated with birch, but also found with spruce, pine and other trees in mixed woodland. GBIF's distribution entry lists Belgium (including the Brussels-Capital, Flemish and Walloon regions), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
