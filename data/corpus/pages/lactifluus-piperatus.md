## Summary

Lactifluus piperatus (L.) Roussel, 1806, is a milkcap in the family Russulaceae, known in English as the Peppery Milkcap. Sources describe a white, funnel-shaped mushroom with very crowded gills that bleeds a peppery-tasting whitish milk when cut. It is reported from Europe and North America. Sources disagree on whether it is safe to eat; see the disagreements section. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet derives from the Latin adjective piperatus, meaning "peppery". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Linnaeus described the species in Species Plantarum (1753) as Agaricus piperatus. Giovanni Antonio Scopoli was long considered the author of the first description, until a 1987 revision of the botanical code began accepting works on fungi from as far back as 1753. It was the original type species of Lactarius. After Lactarius was found to represent more than one genus, L. torminosus was conserved as its type, and L. piperatus became the type species of Lactifluus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports it on the floor of deciduous woodland, particularly under beech (Fagus), as a mycorrhizal associate of deciduous trees, solitary or in scattered groups, from summer through autumn and into early winter. It gives the range as Europe, the Black Sea region of northeastern Turkey, and eastern and central North America east of Minnesota. It has been accidentally introduced to Australasia. Mushroom World likewise describes it growing scattered or grouped on soil in deciduous woods from summer into early winter. Wikipedia notes that "L. piperatus in the wide sense" probably includes several species. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
