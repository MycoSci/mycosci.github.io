## Summary

Geastrum pectinatum Pers. (1801), the beaked earthstar, is an earthstar in the family Geastraceae. The outer wall splits into 7–10 rays that bend back and lift a small spore sac topped by a conical, comb-like opening (peristome). Wikipedia describes it as inedible. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin pectinatum, "like a comb", referring to the comb-tooth-like peristome. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia gives the common names "beaked earthstar" and "beret earthstar" in reference to the shape of the spore sac and its prominent, protruding peristome. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Christian Hendrik Persoon published the first description in 1801. Wikipedia reports that in 1959 J. T. Palmer compared Persoon's original specimen with fresh samples of what were then thought to be the distinct species G. plicatum and G. tenuipes and concluded the three were synonymous; Persoon's specimen was then designated the neotype. Wikipedia also notes that Geastrum biplicatum, described by Berkeley and Curtis in 1860 from Bonin Islands material, was considered identical to G. pectinatum by Sanshi Imai in 1936. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that the species grows on the ground in open woods, solitary or in groups on sandy or rich composted soil in mixed and coniferous forests, often beneath cedars. In Hawaii it is usually found in duff under coastal Casuarina and groves of Cupressus. In Britain and Europe it has been noted in late summer and autumn, though fruit bodies may dry and persist for some time.
