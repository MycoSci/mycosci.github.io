## Summary

Cantharellus cibarius Fr. (1821) is the golden chanterelle, in the family Hydnaceae, and is recorded across Europe and in many other countries. Wikipedia describes it as a commonly consumed edible species, and a 2015 paper on its extracts calls it a wild edible mushroom. Recent work, reported by several sources, restricts C. cibarius in the strict sense to Europe. Many yellow chanterelles elsewhere, including in North America, belong to separate species in the C. cibarius group. *[wikipedia, lit_1, mushroomexpert, lit_3]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the meaning of the Latin name: cantharellus is "chanterelle" and cibarius is "culinary". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

The GBIF descriptions record that the original material cited was a figure in Vaillant, Bot. Paris (1727), and that no type was designated. A neotype was later chosen from England (Devon, Bovey Tracey, on the ground near Betula, 1992, N. W. Legon), attributed to Olariaga et al. 2016. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both state that yellow chanterelles in North America were once all treated as C. cibarius. DNA work has since shown them to be a group of related species, with C. cibarius sensu stricto restricted to Europe. A 2011 Fungal Diversity study of the complex in the southeastern USA described C. tenuithrix as the American sister taxon of the European C. cibarius s.s. *[gbif_desc, wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_3]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia says the chanterelle is used in many culinary dishes and is typically harvested from late summer to late fall in its European range. A 2015 study of its methanolic extract refers to its traditional use as a popular delicacy food. *[wikipedia, lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species grows in Europe from Scandinavia to the Mediterranean Basin, mainly in deciduous and coniferous forests, typically from June to December. Mushroom World (a single-source claim) says it occurs in mixed woodland, notably under birch trees and often beside paths. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
