## Summary

Pluteus thomsonii (Berk. & Broome) Dennis, 1948 is a species in the family Pluteaceae (order Agaricales). MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small, brown-capped Pluteus whose cap surface often becomes elaborately veined, with rostrate cheilocystidia visible under the microscope. GBIF's description record lists its distribution as Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Madeira Island. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com notes that the spelling "Pluteus thompsonii" (with a P) is a misspelling propagated by a typo in Smith, Smith & Weber (1979). It also quotes Minnis & Sundberg (2010): "[a] critical re-evaluation of Pluteus thomsonii is needed on a worldwide basis to determine if cryptic species are present," and says several species may be passing under this name. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to MushroomExpert.Com (a single source, describing North American collections, with the illustrated collections from Illinois), it is saprobic on decaying hardwood logs and debris, growing alone or gregariously from early summer through fall. It is described there as widely distributed in eastern North America and documented in California. *[mushroomexpert]*
