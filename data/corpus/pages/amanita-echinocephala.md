## Summary

Amanita echinocephala (Vittad.) Quél. is a member of the Amanitaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a large, whitish or ivory-coloured mushroom with a spiny, warty-looking cap. It is often found singly or in small groups, which is why it is called the solitary amanita. All descriptive detail here comes from one independent source cluster, Wikipedia and its iNaturalist derivative. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet echinocephala derives from the Greek echino- 'hedgehog' and kephale 'head'. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the mushroom frequently occurs singly or in small groups, and that this is why it is referred to as the solitary amanita or, more specifically, the European solitary lepidella. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described as Agaricus echinocephalus in 1835 by the Italian mycologist Carlo Vittadini. Lucien Quélet later placed it in Amanita, giving the current binomial. Some authors place it in section Lepidella of the Amanitaceae because it lacks a well-formed volva. Wikipedia also says Amanita solitaria is treated there as a synonym, but that many modern Amanita specialists (it names C. Bas and R.E. Tulloss) regard A. solitaria as the valid name for this species. It adds that opinions are divided over which name takes precedence. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species occurs in Britain, Europe, Western Asia and North Africa. In Britain it appears in summer and early autumn, and earlier than most mushrooms of similar size in southern England. It is reported on chalky or light, dry calcareous soils, with broadleaved trees (usually beech, Fagus) and with coniferous trees. In the Czech Republic it is reported with hardwoods on calcareous soils, often associated with limes (Tilia), sometimes alongside A. strobiliformis. The same source calls it very drought-tolerant, and says it is rare, protected and on the United Kingdom Red Data List. This is single-source information from the Wikipedia/iNaturalist cluster. *[wikipedia]*
