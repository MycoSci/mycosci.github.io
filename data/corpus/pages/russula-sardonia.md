## Summary

Russula sardonia Fr. (1838) is a mushroom in the family Russulaceae, commonly called the primrose brittlegill. Wikipedia describes it as having a reddish-purple fruiting body and growing in coniferous woodland in summer and autumn. The same source calls it inedible, with a hot, peppery taste. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet sardonia is from the Greek, meaning bitter or acrid, and refers to the mushroom's taste. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Elias Magnus Fries gave the species its present binomial name in 1838. It lists Russula drimeia (Cooke, 1881), R. chrysodacryon (Singer, 1923) and R. emeticiformis (Murrill, 1938) as synonyms, and notes that the name has also been applied to what is now considered R. queletii. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species appears in late summer and autumn, growing with Pinus (pine) in coniferous woodland on sandy soils, and that it is found across Britain and Northern Europe.
