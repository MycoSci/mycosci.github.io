## Summary

Mycena filopes (Bull.) P. Kumm., 1871 is a species in the family Mycenaceae (Agaricales), with records from many countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small Mycena with a proportionally very long stem, a hoary brownish cap, and an iodine odor that may be strong, weak, or absent. The same source notes that the name filopes is European and is questionably applied to North American mushrooms. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com cites the name to Bulliard (1788) and Kummer (1871). It says a number of additional names have been applied over more than 100 years to species roughly matching the description on both continents. In North America, Smith (1947) separated Mycena filopes from Mycena iodiolens mainly on whether an iodine odor was present, and he synonymized his own Mycena graveolens to M. iodiolens. The author of that page treats Mycena amygdalina, Mycena graveolens and Mycena iodiolens (at a minimum) as synonyms of M. filopes. He says these names will probably have to be sorted out when DNA studies of filopes-like collections from both continents are done. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com, describing North American material, gives it as saprobic on terrestrial forest debris in both hardwood and conifer forests. It usually grows gregariously but sometimes scattered or solitary, and fruits in fall (or over winter in warmer climates). The source says it is widely distributed, at least as a species group, in North America. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*
