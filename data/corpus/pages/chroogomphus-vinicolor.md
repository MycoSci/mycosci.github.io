## Summary

Chroogomphus vinicolor (Peck) O.K. Mill., in the family Gomphidiaceae, is described by Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com as having a shiny cap that ranges from wine-red to reddish-brown, orange-brown or yellow-brown, and thick, decurrent gills that darken from pale orange to blackish as the spores mature. Both sources say cap color is too variable to separate it from the similar C. rutilus and C. ochraceus, and that thick-walled cystidia (walls up to 5–7.5 µm) are what distinguish it. Both sources describe it as widely distributed in North America; Wikipedia also records it from the Dominican Republic. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet vinicolor means "wine-colored". MushroomExpert.Com likewise remarks that the cap is usually wine-colored, "as its Latin name suggests". *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Gomphidius vinicolor by Charles Horton Peck in 1898, from specimens collected near Lake Mohonk in Ulster County, New York. Peck noted a resemblance to Gomphidius roseus, which he thought was closely related. Orson K. Miller, Jr. transferred it to the newly created genus Chroogomphus in 1964. This account comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes C. vinicolor as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially pines. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, occasionally in small clusters, and fruits in fall, or over winter in coastal California. Wikipedia likewise describes it as mycorrhizal, fruiting on the ground under pines and other conifers, singly or more often scattered or in groups, usually in the cooler weather of later summer and autumn. Wikipedia alone adds that it is often found near Suillus luteus and S. brevipes and is known to parasitize the mycelium of those species and of truffle-like Rhizopogon species. Both sources give a widespread North American range; Wikipedia says it extends south to Mexico and has also been recorded from the Dominican Republic. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
