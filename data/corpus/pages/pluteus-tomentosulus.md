## Summary

Pluteus tomentosulus Peck (1885) is a member of the Pluteaceae, described from New York and recorded on decaying wood. MushroomExpert.Com describes the young cap, gills and stem as white and finely granular-tomentose, with gills that turn pink as the spores mature. GBIF's description record lists a distribution in Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (a single-source account) describes P. tomentosulus as saprobic, growing alone, scattered or gregariously on decaying logs of spruces, eastern hemlock and other conifers. It is occasionally reported on hardwood, especially in the lower Appalachians, and the author also found it on landscaping woodchips in the Midwest. It is often found in low, wet areas and appears in summer and fall. The same source says it appears most common in northern and montane North America, and that it is reported from Florida, the Southwest and the Pacific Northwest. GBIF's description record gives Vermont as a distribution. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
