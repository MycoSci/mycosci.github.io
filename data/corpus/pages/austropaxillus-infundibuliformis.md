## Summary

Austropaxillus infundibuliformis (Cleland) Bresinsky & Jarosch 1999 is a species of fungus in the family Serpulaceae, order Boletales. It was first described as Paxillus infundibuliformis. Wikipedia describes it as a mycorrhizal species of eucalypt forests in southeastern Australia. It says the species is readily recognised by its tawny yellow colour, its large size relative to other Australian mushrooms, and its forked decurrent gills. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia (the only independent source supplied), John Burton Cleland first described the species in 1927 as Paxillus infundibuliformis. The initial specimens came from Kuitpo Forest, Mount Lofty, Mount Sedgwick, and near Bendigo. Rolf Singer's 1945 Phylloporus infundibuliformis is listed as a synonym. The current name dates from 1999. In that year Andreas Bresinsky and colleagues, studying the genus Paxillus, determined that several Southern Hemisphere species belong to a lineage most closely related to the brown rot genus Serpula. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (single source) describes the species as mycorrhizal, fruiting on the ground in eucalypt forests, such as those of Eucalyptus obliqua. It gives records across southern Australia, from Western Australia to Victoria and New South Wales. *[wikipedia]*
