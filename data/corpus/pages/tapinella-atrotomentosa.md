## Summary

Tapinella atrotomentosa (Batsch) Šutara, 1992, the velvet roll-rim or velvet-footed pax, is a gilled member of the Boletales in the family Tapinellaceae. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a saprobic fungus of conifer stumps and decaying conifer wood, with a dark brown, velvety stem. It is recorded from Eurasia and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name derives from the Latin atrotomentosus, meaning "black-haired". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that August Batsch described the species as Agaricus atrotomentosus in his 1783 Elenchus Fungorum. Josef Šutara gave it its current name in 1992. Other names it has carried include Paxillus atrotomentosus, Rhymovis atrotomentosa (Rabenhorst, 1844) and Sarcopaxillus atrotomentosus (Zmitrovich, 2004). According to Wikipedia, it and T. panuoides were separated into Tapinella because they grow on and rot wood, have much smaller spores, lack cystidia and have different basidia. Their off-centre stipe also set them apart from Paxillus, and genetic analysis confirmed them as only distantly related. MushroomExpert.Com makes the same ecological point: Paxillus species such as P. involutus are terrestrial and mycorrhizal, while Tapinella species are wood-decomposing saprobes. Both sources note that older field guides still use the name Paxillus atrotomentosus. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, in groups or sometimes in clusters on conifer stumps, decaying conifer wood or living conifers. It also reports the species on madrone wood and says it causes a brown rot. It fruits in summer and fall and is widely distributed in northern North America and the Appalachian Mountains. Wikipedia likewise describes a saprobe on conifer stumps, recorded from North America, Europe, Central America (Costa Rica), Pakistan and China, with fruit bodies in summer and autumn. Mushroom World describes it as growing singly or in groups or clumps on decaying conifer logs, stumps, roots or partially buried wood. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
