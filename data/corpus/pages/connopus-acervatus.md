## Summary

Connopus acervatus (Fr.) K.W. Hughes, Mather & R.H. Petersen, 2010, is a member of the family Omphalotaceae. Wikipedia describes Connopus as a monotypic genus circumscribed in 2010 to hold this species, formerly placed in Gymnopus. It is described as growing in dense clusters on decaying wood in North America and Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) reports that a 2010 study by Hughes and collaborators used two genes (ITS and LSU) to examine many collybioid mushrooms. Collections of "Gymnopus acervatus" grouped separately from other Gymnopus species and from Rhodocollybia species, so the authors proposed the new genus Connopus for it. The same source notes that the authors' data found potential support for the idea that the western North American version "might be a distinct species". It lists Collybia acervata and Gymnopus acervatus as synonyms. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing in dense clusters on conifer stumps and sometimes from buried conifer wood, which can make it appear terrestrial. It fruits in summer and fall, or in winter in Texas and California. The same source calls it fairly widely distributed in North America but apparently absent or rare in southwestern and southeastern areas. Mushroom World (a single-source claim) says the clusters occur on decaying conifer wood, especially spruce and fir. Wikipedia gives the range as North America and Europe, on decaying wood. *[mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, wikipedia]*
