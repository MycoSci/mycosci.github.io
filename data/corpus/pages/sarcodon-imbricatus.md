## Summary

Sarcodon imbricatus (L.) P. Karst. (1881) is a tooth fungus in the family Bankeraceae, order Thelephorales, known in English as the shingled hedgehog, scaly hedgehog or scaly tooth. It is described as a large mushroom with a brown cap covered in coarse, dark brown scales, and with spines ("teeth") on the underside instead of gills. Its spore print is brown. The species is recorded across Europe and North America, but the source texts disagree on how far the name applies to European material. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is the Latin imbricatus, meaning "tiled" or "with overlapping tiles". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the Swedish botanist Olof Celsius reported the species near Uppsala in 1732, and Linnaeus wrote of it in Flora lapponica (1737). Linnaeus formally described it as Hydnum imbricatum in Species Plantarum (1753), and Petter Adolf Karsten placed it in Sarcodon in 1881. Wikipedia adds that European material long treated as S. imbricatus was later split on DNA evidence, with collections from the British Isles and the Netherlands now assigned to Sarcodon squamosus. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) likewise cites DNA work by Johannesson and collaborators (1999) and says the two taxa had been combined and confused in European literature for many years. Kuo notes that a similar study of North American collections would be interesting. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that old mushrooms of S. imbricatus and related species contain blue-green pigments used for dyeing wool in Norway. It also says that fresh specimens collected under pine yielded pigment, while under spruce only old ones did. It attributes this difference to the two genetically distinct forms that were later separated. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia gives fruiting seasons of August to October in Europe and July to September in North America, except on the West Coast, where it gives September to December. Wikipedia says the species can appear in fairy rings on sandy or chalk soils. It states an association with spruce (Picea) in its lead section, and also with firs (Abies), especially in hilly or mountainous areas, in its habitat section. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers and, reportedly, hardwoods, growing alone or gregariously. Kuo calls it especially common in the Rocky Mountains, where it grows under Engelmann spruce and subalpine fir during monsoon season. Kuo cites DNA work in Europe (Johannesson et al., 1999) supporting an obligate mycorrhizal partnership with spruces, and says S. squamosus associates with pines. He notes that North American collections under this name are reported under spruces, pines and hardwoods. Both sources therefore treat the host and range of the name as unsettled. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
