## Summary

Tricholoma ustale, the burnt knight, is a mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae, combined as (Fr.) P. Kumm. in 1871. Sources describe it as poisonous, and it is one of the species most commonly implicated in mushroom poisoning in Japan. Material from North America under this name may represent one or more different species. *[wikipedia, lit_1]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the species name as from the Latin ustalis, 'burnt', and relates it to the colour of the mushroom. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries described the fungus in 1818 as Agaricus ustalis, and Paul Kummer gave it its current name in 1871 when he transferred it to Tricholoma. Marcel Bon described the variety rufoaurantiacum from France in 1984. Within Tricholoma it is classified in section Albobrunnea of subgenus Tricholoma (Wikipedia). A 2021 molecular study of Japanese 'Kakishimeji' found it to be a complex of four phylogenetic clades, two of which were described as new species, T. kakishimeji and T. kakishimejioides. Two other clades included the type specimens of European T. stans and T. albobrunneum (Mycoscience 2021). *[wikipedia, lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes T. ustale as an ectomycorrhizal species that grows in association with beech, and says it can be locally common in the southern counties of England. The same source says it is found in Asia, Europe and North America, though North American material may represent one or more different species. *[wikipedia]*
