## Summary

Gyroporus cyanescens (Bull.) Quél. is a bolete in the family Gyroporaceae (order Boletales), known in English as the cornflower bolete. Wikipedia reports that it was first described from France in 1788 and is found in Eurasia, Australia and eastern North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as an easily recognized mushroom with straw-yellow colors, a brittle consistency, strong bluing reactions and a yellow spore print. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Ancient Greek κύανoς, meaning "dark blue", and that the varietal epithet violaceotinctus means "having a violet tinge". This derivation appears in that one source only. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the name as Gyroporus cyanescens (Bull.) Quél., Enchiridion Fungorum (1886), with the basionym Boletus cyanescens Bull., Herbier de la France 8: pl. 369 (1788). Wikipedia says Bulliard described the species in Herbier de la France (1788). It lists later synonyms: Boletus constrictus (Persoon, 1801), Leccinum constrictum (Gray, 1821), Suillus cyanescens (Karsten, 1882) and Leucoconius cyanescens (Beck, 1923). Wikipedia also says Roy Watling described the variety violaceotinctus in 1969 from Michigan collections. MushroomExpert.Com adds that Watling based that variety on two northern Michigan collections and one southern Michigan collection, in which the flesh stained lilac, then purple, then indigo. MushroomExpert.Com states that an epitype for the species was designated from Italy (Vizzini et al. 2015). Its author suggests that Gyroporus violaceotinctus may be the best name for the North American collections he illustrates, and that a multi-locus study of well documented North American collections compared with the epitype is needed. This last point is his opinion. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the variety violaceotinctus is used in mushroom dyeing, giving light yellow, beige, gold or brownish-orange colors depending on the mordant. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes G. cyanescens as ectomycorrhizal with a broad host range. It says fruit bodies grow singly or scattered on the ground in deciduous and mixed forests, often in association with birch and poplar. It says the fungus tends to prefer sandy soil, frequents road banks and woodland edges, and fruits in summer and early autumn. Wikipedia's lead text separately says coniferous and mixed forests. It reports the species from Europe, Asia, Australia (in eucalypt woodland) and North America, where it is widespread east of the Rocky Mountains. MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as mycorrhizal, probably primarily with oaks and other hardwoods but sometimes recorded under conifers. It says fruit bodies grow alone, scattered or gregariously, often in sandy soil and especially in disturbed ground such as roadbeds and path sides, in summer and fall. It calls the species widely distributed but fairly rare in Europe and widely distributed east of the Great Plains in North America. Its illustrated collections are from Michigan. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
