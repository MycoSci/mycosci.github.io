## Summary

Amanita battarrae (Boud.) Bon (1985) is a species in the family Amanitaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a rare species, originally named Amanitopsis battarrae by Boudier in 1902. Mushroom World describes a medium-sized, grey-brown, ringless agaric with a volval bag and a zoned, marginally grooved cap. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was described in 1902 by the French mycologist Jean Louis Émile Boudier as Amanitopsis battarrae, and that Marcel Bon gave it its currently accepted name in 1985. This is a single-source account. No source supplied the type material or the reasons for the transfer. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Sources differ in detail and each is a single-source statement. Wikipedia says the species is found in Italy in the fall and in eastern Europe. Mushroom World gives its location as Europe and says it grows solitary or scattered on soil in broad-leaf woods (particularly oaks, but also ash and hazel) and on heaths, fruiting in summer and autumn. A 2017 macrofungi survey of the Apuseni Mountains, Romania, reports it living in acidic deciduous and coniferous forests in the high mountains. A report on mycological trips to the Krkonoše Mts., Bohemia (1986-1989), lists it among noteworthy finds. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, lit_1, lit_2]*
