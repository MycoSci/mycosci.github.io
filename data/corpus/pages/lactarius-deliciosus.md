## Summary

Lactarius deliciosus (L.) Gray, 1821, is a member of the Russulaceae, commonly called saffron milk cap or red pine mushroom. Wikipedia describes it as a European species that has been accidentally introduced elsewhere along with pines. MushroomExpert.Com notes that it does not occur in North America, and that North American mushrooms long called by this name are genetically distinct. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet derives from Latin deliciosus, meaning "tasty". It adds that Linnaeus allegedly chose it after smelling the mushroom and presuming it tasted good, perhaps confusing it with a Mediterranean milk cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia reports that in the Girona area of Catalonia it is called pinetell (in Catalan) because it is collected near wild pine trees. No source narrates the origin of the other common names. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Linnaeus described it in Species Plantarum in 1753 as Agaricus deliciosus. Persoon added the varietal epithet lactifluus in 1801, and Samuel Frederick Gray placed it in Lactarius in 1821 in The Natural Arrangement of British Plants. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both report that DNA work by Nuytinck, Verbeken and colleagues found the European L. deliciosus to be distinct from deliciosus-like mushrooms in North America. MushroomExpert.Com adds that the North American taxa remain unresolved, and the GBIF descriptive text says the group needs revision. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that a fresco in the Roman town of Herculaneum appears to depict L. deliciosus, one of the earliest pieces of art to illustrate a fungus. It also reports that the mushroom is widely collected in the Iberian Peninsula and is traditionally preserved by salting in Russia. These are single-source statements from an encyclopedia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that L. deliciosus grows under conifers on acidic soils and forms a mycorrhizal relationship with its host tree. It gives the native range as the southern Pyrenees, where it grows under Mediterranean pines, and the wider Mediterranean basin. Wikipedia says it has been introduced to Chile, Australia and New Zealand, where it grows in Pinus radiata plantations, and also to South Africa. It gives collection seasons of August to early October in the Northern Hemisphere and April to June in the Southern Hemisphere. MushroomExpert.Com characterises the true European species by its mycorrhizal association with pines (Pinus). Mushroom World describes it as growing solitary or in scattered groups on soil under conifers, favouring pine. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
