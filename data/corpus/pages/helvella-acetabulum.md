## Summary

Helvella acetabulum (L.) Quél. is a cup fungus in the family Helvellaceae, order Pezizales, also known as the vinegar cup. Wikipedia describes it as a relatively large, deeply cup-shaped fungus with prominent branching ribs. MushroomExpert.Com describes a yellow-brown to brown cup held on a ribbed and pocketed stem, with the ribs extending onto the undersurface of the cup. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet acetabulum means "little vinegar cup" and was the Latin word for a small vessel used for storing vinegar. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the name "cabbage leaf Helvella" is used because the prominent branching ribs resemble a cabbage leaf. None of the supplied sources narrates the origin of the other common names. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was first named Peziza acetabulum by Carl Linnaeus in his 1753 Species Plantarum. It was later placed in several other genera: Octospora by Timm (1788), Macroscyphus by Gray (1821), Acetabula by Fuckel (1870), Aleuria by Gillet (1879) and Paxina by Kuntze (1891). Wikipedia also notes that Persoon independently described it as Peziza sulcata in 1801. The GBIF description record states that no type was designated. It lists the original material as illustrations in works by Buxbaum (1729), Vaillant (1727) and Micheli (1729). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow solitary, scattered or clustered on soil in coniferous and deciduous woods, typically in spring and summer. It also mentions sandy soils, and a noted association with coast live oak (Quercus agrifolia) in Californian populations. MushroomExpert.Com (single source for this description) calls it probably mycorrhizal, growing alone or gregariously under hardwoods or conifers, often near stumps or decaying wood. It gives the season in North America as late spring and early summer, late summer in the Rocky Mountains, and winter and spring in coastal California. The GBIF description record quotes the original protologue's habitat statement as "Habitat in Europa australi." *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
