## Summary

Neoboletus luridiformis (Rostk.) Gelardi, Simonini & Vizzini (2014) is a bolete in the family Boletaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a large, solid bolete with a bay-brown cap, red-dotted yellow stem and red pores, known as the scarletina bolete, and reports it from Europe and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet "luridiformis" signifies similarity to the previously known fungus Boletus luridus (now Suillellus luridus). *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the name 'scarletina bolete' refers to the species' red pores, which are yellow when young. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Rostkovius defined the red-pored species as Boletus luridiformis in 1844, which is the basis of the current name. The name Boletus erythropus (Persoon, 1796) was used for this species for about 200 years, but Persoon's mushroom had orange pores and is thought to be a different species, so that usage was invalid. A 2013 genetic analysis placed it in the dupainii clade, well away from the core Boletus edulis group, and it became the type species of the new genus Neoboletus in 2014. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states the fungus is common in Europe, growing in deciduous or coniferous woodland in summer and autumn, and is often found in the same places as Boletus edulis. It is also widely distributed in North America, where it is especially common under spruce from Northern California to Alaska. In Eastern North America it grows with both softwood and hardwood trees. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*
