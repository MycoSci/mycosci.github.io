## Summary

Xeromphalina cauticinalis (Fr.) Kühner & Maire 1934 is an agaric in the family Mycenaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a small species with convex yellowish caps and decurrent pale yellow gills, growing on conifer debris in North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small orangish-brown-capped mushroom with a tough stem and a fuzzy orangish base, and says a drop of KOH on the cap gives a bright red reaction. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was originally described in 1838 by Elias Fries as Marasmius cauticinalis, and was transferred to Xeromphalina by Robert Kühner and René Maire in 1934. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states it is found in North America, fruiting from summer to autumn (later on the West Coast) in conifer debris and sometimes on aspen leaves. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on conifer debris (including needle duff and well decayed wood), growing in troops or occasionally scattered or alone, usually on the ground, in summer and fall. It says the species is widely distributed and common in western North America and occasional in northeastern North America, and that the illustrated collections are from Colorado. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
