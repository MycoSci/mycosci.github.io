## Summary

Mycetinis scorodonius (Fr.) A.W. Wilson & Desjardin (2005) is a species in the family Omphalotaceae, recorded in GBIF occurrence data from Europe, North America and Asia. Wikipedia describes it as one of the garlic-scented mushrooms formerly placed in the genus Marasmius, with a beige cap of up to 3 cm and a tough slender stipe. MushroomExpert.Com likewise stresses its strong garlic-and-onion odor. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species epithet is a Latin adjectival form of the Greek word for garlic, scorodon (σκόροδον). This is a single-cluster claim. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first documented as Agaricus scorodonius by Fries in 1815, and that Fries established the combination Marasmius scorodonius in 1836. It then says a 2005 paper separated a group of garlic-smelling species, including this one, into the genus Mycetinis. MushroomExpert.Com independently says the 2005 study (Wilson & Desjardin) found from DNA evidence that several traditional generic concepts for marasmioid and collybioid mushrooms were not supported, and that the garlicky species of Marasmius belonged in Mycetinis. MushroomExpert.Com also reports a 1998 study (Gordon & Petersen) finding that the North American and European versions were able to mate, so a single biological species occurs on both sides of the Atlantic. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the mushroom is found from summer to late autumn, especially on conifer needles but also on sticks and other debris, on the bark of conifers and deciduous trees, and on grasses and other plants. It says the species is usually a saprophyte but has been reported as a parasite of grass. It describes the species as fairly common in northern and central Europe and reported from eastern North America, North Africa and Asia. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, primarily on fallen conifer needles, occasionally on the mossy bark of living hardwoods or conifers or arising from grass stems or twigs in woodland, growing scattered or gregariously in summer and fall. It calls the species widely distributed east of the Great Plains and absent or very rare in the west. The GBIF description text gives the distribution as Europe and North America, rare on the North American west coast. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
