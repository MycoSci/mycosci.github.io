## Summary

Floccularia albolanaripes (G.F. Atk.) Redhead, 1987, is a species in the family Agaricaceae, originally described as Armillaria albolanaripes. Wikipedia characterizes the mushrooms by yellow caps with a brownish center and scales over the margin, and by conspicuous partial-veil remains on the stipe. GBIF occurrence records come from the US, Mexico, Canada, China, Pakistan and Russia. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was first described as Armillaria albolanaripes by the American mycologist George F. Atkinson in 1908, from type specimens collected at Corvallis, Oregon, on November 6, 1906. It remained in Armillaria for several decades. In 1987 members of that genus with amyloid spores and no black rhizomorphs were transferred to Floccularia. MushroomExpert.Com also gives the former name Armillaria albolanaripes and the combination (Atkinson, 1908) Redhead, 1987. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

The two independent sources differ in detail. Wikipedia says the fruit bodies grow singly to scattered under conifers, in the Pacific Northwest and Rocky Mountains of North America, in spring and summer. It calls the species a snowbank mushroom, often found at the edge of melting snowbanks or shortly after the snow has melted. It also says the species occurs in Kashmir, India, where it grows in a suspected mycorrhizal association with Pinus wallichiana. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as probably mycorrhizal and western in distribution. It gives conifers in the Rocky Mountains, with fruiting in summer and fall, and hardwoods (especially oaks and alders) or occasionally conifers on the West Coast, with fruiting in fall and winter. It grows alone or scattered. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
