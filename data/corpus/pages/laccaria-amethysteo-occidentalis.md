## Summary

Laccaria amethysteo-occidentalis G.M. Muell. (1984) is a species in the family Hydnangiaceae, described from British Columbia. Sources describe it as an entirely purple mushroom with a deep purple cap, thick purple gills and a white spore print. It is associated with conifers in western North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) states that this mushroom previously went under the name Laccaria amethystina, and was separated from it on the basis of its darker cap color, larger size, and spore morphology. Kuo cites Mueller, 1984 as the reference. Wikipedia lists Mueller's 1984 paper "New North American species of Laccaria (Agaricales)", Mycotaxon 20(1): 101–116, as further reading. ## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, growing alone, scattered or gregariously, common, usually appearing in colder weather, and occurring west of the Rocky Mountains. Wikipedia describes it as found under conifers, usually pine, in western North America, and says it can be found on the ground near the North American West Coast from October to February. The 'usually pine' and October–February details come from Wikipedia alone. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
