## Summary

Tyromyces fumidiceps G.F. Atk. (1908) is a polypore in the family Incrustoporiaceae (Polyporales), with its type locality in New York and records from the US, Canada and several other countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a stemless bracket with soft, watery flesh, a whitish, off-white, grayish or brownish cap, and extremely small pores that become yellowish or pale olive in old age or when dried. The spore print is white. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) gives the translation of the Greek and Latin elements of the name as 'smoky-headed cheese fungus', with 'cheese fungus' referring to the cheese-like consistency of the fruiting body. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to MushroomExpert.Com (a single source), it is saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods and causes a white rot. It is annual and grows alone or gregariously. It is often found along riverbottoms, creeks and low-lying areas subject to floods, in summer and fall. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as fairly widely distributed east of the Great Plains. *[mushroomexpert]*
