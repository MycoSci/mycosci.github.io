## Summary

Cortinarius caperatus (Pers.) Fr., 1838, is a species in the family Cortinariaceae, known in English as the Gypsy and the Wrinkled Cortinarius. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe a buff to brownish-ochre cap that is often wrinkled and covered with whitish fibres, a thick whitish ring on the stem, and a rusty to ochre-brown spore print. MushroomExpert.Com notes it is one of only a few Cortinarius species with a sturdy, well-developed ring rather than a cortina. Wikipedia reports it from northern Europe, northern North America and temperate Asia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet caperatus is Latin for "wrinkled" and refers to the distinctive texture of the cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus caperatus by Persoon in 1796, who noted it grew in beech woods. Fries transferred it to Cortinarius in 1838. Gillet later moved it to Pholiota (1874), and Karsten erected the genus Rozites for it in 1879 on the basis of its double veil (a partial veil that leaves a ring on the stem, plus a universal veil). Quélet's treatment of Pholiota as a subgenus of Dryophila added Dryophila caperata to the synonymy. Wikipedia reports that genetic analyses in 2000 and 2002 showed Rozites was nested within Cortinarius, so the species was returned to Cortinarius. MushroomExpert.Com adds that the spelling "Rozites caperata" was an error in Latin grammar perpetuated by many field guides. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports fruiting bodies from August to October in conifer and beech woods, and on heather (often close to sphagnum) in Scotland. It gives September to November for North America and July and August for Alaska. Wikipedia describes the fungus as mycorrhizal but non-selective in its hosts, preferring acidic and sandy soils and avoiding chalky ones. Hosts and associates it names include Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris), Sitka spruce (Picea sitchensis), and, in North America, huckleberry. It also names dwarf birches (Betula nana, B. glandulosa) in Alaska and white birch (B. pubescens) in Greenland. MushroomExpert.Com independently describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, hardwoods, and bushes in the blueberry family, growing alone or more often gregariously, in summer and fall, and widely distributed in North America (illustrated collections from Kentucky and Michigan). These are single-source descriptions from Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com respectively. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
