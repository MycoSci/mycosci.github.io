## Summary

Phaeolepiota aurea (Bull.) R. Maire ex Konrad & Maubl., 1928, is a gilled mushroom (agaric) in the order Agaricales. Wikipedia describes it as a large, golden mushroom with a skirt-like ring on the stem and a cap up to 20 cm wide. It gives the common names Alaskan gold, Alaska gold, golden bootleg and golden cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies lists Agaricus aureus Bull. (Herb. Fr. 2: pl. 92, 1782) among the synonyms, along with later combinations in Pholiota (Saccardo, 1887) and Togaria (W.G. Smith, 1908). The sources do not say what material the name was based on or why the species was moved between genera. Wikipedia notes that recent molecular phylogenetics research shows Phaeolepiota is closely related to Cystoderma and may need to be merged into it. *[wikispecies, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states it is found throughout North America and Eurasia, often in groups and next to nettles.
