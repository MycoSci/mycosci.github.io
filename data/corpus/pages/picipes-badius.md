## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1801 by Christian Hendrik Persoon as Boletus badius. Lewis David de Schweinitz transferred it to Polyporus in 1832, and it was known under that name until 1997, when De transferred it to Royoporus, a genus he had described the year before. Zmitrovich & Kovalenko then proposed the genus Picipes for this fungus together with P. melanopus and P. tubaeformis. Wikipedia reports that Species Fungorum treats Picipes badius as the correct current name. Wikispecies gives the publication as Int. J. Med. Mushrooms 18(1): 35 (2016) and lists Boletus badius Pers. as the basionym, along with Grifola badia, Polyporellus badius, Polyporus badius and Royoporus badius among the synonyms. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a saprobic species that causes white rot and grows on standing or fallen trunks and branches of various hardwood genera, including Acer, Aesculus, Alnus, Betula, Castanea, Fagus, Fraxinus, Populus, Prunus, Robinia, Quercus, Salix, Tilia and Ulmus, in temperate regions of Asia, Europe and North America. The introduction to the same article also mentions conifers, but no conifer genus is listed in its ecology section. A GBIF materials record describes collections labelled white rot in a broad-leaved forest, on trunks of angiosperms, in Yunnan Province, China (specimens dated 12 January 2017 and 2 January 2019). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
