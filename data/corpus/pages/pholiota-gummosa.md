## Summary

Pholiota gummosa, commonly called the sticky scalycap, is a species in the family Strophariaceae, treated here as (Lasch) Singer 1951. Wikipedia describes it as a common saprotrophic species of Europe and North America. The same source gives the cap as straw-yellow to beige and 2–6 cm across, with crowded adnate gills that are pale yellow at first and turn brownish with age, and a brown spore print. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was first described by Wilhelm Gottfried Lasch in 1828 in the genus Agaricus. It was later moved through several other genera and was transferred to Pholiota by Rolf Singer in 1951. This account comes from a single independent source and does not say what material Lasch described or why each transfer was made. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it occurs in Europe and North America and grows as a saprotroph on the rotting wood of deciduous trees, including trunks and roots. It can also grow on wood buried near the surface, which makes it look as if it is fruiting in grass. This is a single-source statement, and no source supplied gives a fruiting season. *[wikipedia]*
