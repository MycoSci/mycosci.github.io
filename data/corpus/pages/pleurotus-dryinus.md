## Summary

Pleurotus dryinus (Pers.) P. Kumm. (1871), the veiled oyster, is a species of fungus in the family Pleurotaceae. Wikipedia describes it as growing on dead wood and as a weak pathogen, especially of broad-leaved trees. GBIF holds occurrence records from many countries, including Great Britain, Sweden and the United States. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name is a Latinised version of the Greek word "dryinos" (δρύῐνος), meaning "related to oak", referring to one of its main hosts. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was first defined as Agaricus dryinus by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1800. In 1871 Paul Kummer introduced Pleurotus as a genus and defined three similar ringed species: Pleurotus corticatus, P. albertini and P. dryinus. Kummer distinguished them because only P. corticatus has intertwined ("anastomosing") gills on the stem, and P. albertini is bigger and grows on conifer wood rather than oak. Wikipedia says all three are now considered forms of one species, and that the name dryinus takes precedence because it is the oldest. It also says that Elias Magnus Fries defined Pleurotus tephrotrichus in 1874, a species with a deeper grey colour. That name has also been incorporated into P. dryinus, but it may be distinguished as the variety tephrotrichus. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

A 2020 study in the Journal of the Faculty of Agriculture Kyushu University examined the biological characteristics and domestication cultivation of P. dryinus, using wild strains from Horqin Left-wing Back Banner of Tongliao City, Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, China. The optimal medium for mycelial growth was reported as D(+)-maltose, NaNO3, K2HPO4 and straw. The authors suggested 22 °C and pH 9 for good growth. The domestication experiments covered pre-culture of spawn, manufacture of cultural bags, spawn running, inducement to primordium, management of the fruiting period, and collection periods. This is a single research-paper source, and the supplied abstract gives no yields or commercial-scale detail. *[lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as saprobic on dead wood and also a weak parasite of trees (a "white rot"). It says the fungus occurs especially on oak, but also on beech, other broad-leaved trees, and occasionally on conifers. Wikipedia adds that it is often solitary or in small groups, appears from summer to autumn, and is distributed throughout Europe, where it varies locally between common and rare, and is also found in North America. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing alone or in small clusters on dead and living wood of hardwoods (especially oaks and beech), in summer and fall (winter in coastal California), and widely distributed in North America. The collections illustrated and described there are from Missouri, Kentucky and Québec. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
