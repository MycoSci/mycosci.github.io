## Summary

Pleurotus pulmonarius (Fr.) Quél., 1872, is a species in the family Pleurotaceae, recorded on GBIF from more than 40 countries. Wikipedia lists the common names Indian oyster, Italian oyster, phoenix mushroom and lung oyster, and describes it as closely related to Pleurotus ostreatus. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a shelf-forming, whitish to pale tan mushroom on hardwood, separated from P. ostreatus by paler and often smaller fruiting bodies, a more developed stem and warmer-weather fruiting. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the cap outline as lung-shaped to fan-shaped, 'hence its Latin name'. No source gives a fuller account of the derivation. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first published as Agaricus pulmonarius by Elias Magnus Fries in 1821, and Lucien Quélet reclassified it as Pleurotus pulmonarius in 1872. MushroomExpert.Com gives the same authorship, (Fries, 1821) Quélet, 1872. It also says the separation from P. ostreatus rests on mating incompatibility in the laboratory, on DNA evidence, and on differences in morphology and fruiting season. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states that P. pulmonarius is commercially cultivated, commonly in Europe and North America and also in New Zealand. It describes the method as transferring mycelium from a petri plate onto grain, then moving the colonized grain spawn to substrates such as straw, wood chips, sawdust, cardboard, coffee grounds and other cellulose-based materials. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing in shelf-like clusters on dead and living hardwood and causing a white rot. It gives the season as late spring through September, from collections in Illinois, Missouri and Kentucky, and says it is apparently widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia says it is found in temperate and subtropical forests worldwide, generally on hardwoods in the eastern United States and also on conifers in the west. A GBIF-hosted description gives growth on deciduous wood in temperate to subtropical regions, with Brazilian records from Paraná, Rio Grande do Sul, São Paulo, Mato Grosso do Sul and Tocantins. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
