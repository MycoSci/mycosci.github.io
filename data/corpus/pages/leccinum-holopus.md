## Summary

Leccinum holopus (Rostk.) Watling, 1960, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the white birch bolete, white bog bolete or ghost bolete. Wikipedia describes it as associating with birch and typically found in boggy or swampy areas, often among sphagnum moss. It is recorded from northern Asia, Europe and northeastern North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet holopus is Greek for "with perfect stalk". This is a single-source statement, and no source supplied here gives further detail on the derivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was first named as a species of Boletus by the German mycologist Friedrich Rostkovius in 1844 and was transferred to Leccinum by Roy Watling in 1960. Wikipedia lists a number of synonyms from transfers to other genera and describes several subtaxa. These include var. americanum, described by Alexander H. Smith and Harry Delbert Thiers in 1971 from collections made in Michigan, on the basis of flesh that stains reddish when injured. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) notes that Leccinum nucatum is a synonym and that var. americanum does not differ in DNA from var. holopus. Wikipedia likewise reports that DNA evidence suggests most of the described varieties are the same taxon. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes L. holopus as mycorrhizal and highly host-specific, associating with birch (Betula). It fruits on the ground, often among Sphagnum moss, singly to scattered in wet areas such as cedar swamps, bogs or soggy forests. Wikipedia gives the North American range as eastern Canada to New York and west to the northern Rocky Mountains, roughly matching the distribution of paper birch (Betula papyrifera), with fruiting from August to October there. It says the fungus is rare in southern Europe but more common in the Sphagnum swamps of the north, and has been recorded in Asia from Taiwan and Qinghai (China). MushroomExpert.Com independently describes it as circumboreal and mycorrhizal with birches: paper birch in North America and European white birch in Europe and Asia. It reports fruiting alone or gregariously in summer and fall, in northern North America from roughly the 43rd parallel northward, and also under water birch in the Rocky Mountains. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
