## Summary

Chamonixia caespitosa is a secotioid fungus in the family Boletaceae, described as new to science in 1899 by the French mycologist Léon Louis Rolland. It is described as a rare sequestrate species known from Europe and North America, with a first report from Japan. Its whitish outer surface (peridium) is reported to turn blue rapidly on exposure to air. *[wikipedia, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Habitat and ecology

In Poland, basidiocarps partially exposed from the humus layer were found in two localities: a spruce forest in the Tatra Mountains at 1540 m elevation, and a mixed spruce and fir forest in the Beskid Niski Mountains at about 400 m. In Japan, fruiting bodies were collected in a subalpine coniferous forest in the central part of the country. No season is stated in the supplied sources. *[lit_2, lit_3]*
