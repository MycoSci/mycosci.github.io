## Summary

Omphalotus olivascens (H.E. Bigelow, O.K. Mill. & Thiers, 1976) is a gilled mushroom in the family Omphalotaceae, described from California. Wikipedia describes it as a poisonous, orange-to-brown mushroom, and MushroomExpert.Com describes its gills as luminescent when fresh. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, this California "Jack O'Lantern" passed as Omphalotus olearius or Omphalotus illudens until 1976. Bigelow, Miller & Thiers then named it as a separate species. They based this on its duller orange colors, its olive overtones or stains, darker dried specimens, slightly larger spores, encrusted elements in the pileipellis, and differences in cultured mycelium. The same source says it has since been supported as a biological species (Petersen & Hughes 1998) and a phylogenetic species (Kirchmair et al. 2004). Wikipedia also mentions a blue-fleshed variety, O. olivascens var. indigo, described on live oak in Baja California, Mexico. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing in large clusters on the stumps or buried roots of hardwoods, especially oaks, in winter. It was originally described from California and is reported from California and Baja California. Wikipedia calls it a saprobe or parasite, usually found on oak or Eucalyptus and rarely on other hosts, and says it is endemic to the California Floristic Province. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
