## Summary

Phellinus tremulae (Bondartsev) Bondartsev & P.N. Borisov, 1953, is a polypore in the family Hymenochaetaceae, with occurrence records from many countries. Wikipedia reports that it grows on Populus tremula and on trembling aspen in Canada, and that it causes aspen trunk rot. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a parasitic fungus that decays aspen heartwood, with tough, woody, perennial fruiting bodies. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that the epithet "tremulae" refers to the species' association with the Eurasian aspen, Populus tremula, the host from which it was originally named in Europe. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was first described as Fomes igniarius f. tremulae by Appollinaris Semenovich Bondartsev in 1935. MushroomExpert.Com gives the same original citation (Bondartsev, 1935). MushroomExpert.Com reports that Wagner & Fischer (2002) found the species closely related to Phellinus igniarius, the type species of the genus, so it remains in Phellinus. A later study (Fischer & Binder, 2004) compared North American collections with European and Asian ones and found that the phylogenetic data suggested two separate taxa. Because the species was originally named from Europe, MushroomExpert.Com suggests the North American version may receive a new name. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as parasitic on the heartwood of quaking aspen and big-toothed aspen, and, in Europe and Asia, on trembling aspen. It causes a whitish to yellowish rot. It grows alone or gregariously from wounds and branch scars, and usually appears in the eye-shaped branch scars typical of aspens. The fruiting bodies are perennial. That source calls the species widely distributed in North America where aspens occur, and its illustrated and described collections are from Colorado. Wikipedia lists Populus tremula and trembling aspen in Canada as hosts. MushroomExpert.Com also reports that the fungus is important to gnats in the genus Sciophila, which may assist spore dispersal. It adds that yellow-bellied and red-naped sapsuckers use aspens infected by the fungus as nesting sites. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
