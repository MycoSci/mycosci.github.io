## Summary

Leucoagaricus americanus (Peck) Vellinga, 2000, is a member of the Agaricaceae, originally described by Peck as Agaricus americanus. Wikipedia reports that the species was moved in 2024 to the genus Macropsalliota as Macropsalliota americana, while MushroomExpert.Com still treats it as Leucoagaricus americanus. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a saprobic, scaly-capped lepiotoid mushroom with a swollen stem that reddens when rubbed, widely distributed in North America and more frequent east of the Rocky Mountains. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described as Agaricus americanus by Charles Horton Peck in 1872. Wikispecies gives the basionym as Agaricus americanus Peck, 1869, with a holotype at NYS collected at Clinton (New York) in August 1869, and lists Peck's report to the Regents of the University on the New York State Cabinet of Natural History among its references. The sources therefore differ on the year of Peck's description, and MushroomExpert.Com cites 'Peck, 1872'. According to Wikipedia, Saccardo transferred it to Lepiota in 1887, Redhead to Leucocoprinus in 1979, and Vellinga to Leucoagaricus in 2000. In 2024 Kun L. Yang, Jia Y. Lin and Zhu L. Yang moved it to the newly created genus Macropsalliota, for which it is the type species. Wikipedia and Wikispecies also list Lepiota bresadolae, Agaricus bresadolae and related names as synonyms. The sources do not say why the transfers were made. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes the species as saprobic, growing alone, scattered or gregariously on woodchips, in landscaping areas, near waste places, on disturbed ground and on stumps. It occasionally appears in woods, on stumps or around deadwood. He calls it a primarily urban mushroom that typically appears in summer and fall, and he describes the illustrated collections as coming from Illinois. He writes that it is common in eastern North America and appears occasionally in the West, where he thinks it has probably been introduced with woodchips. Wikipedia likewise says it is saprobic on sawdust, wood chips, stumps and the ground, and calls it native to North America and more common east of the Rocky Mountains. Wikispecies records the type collection as 'In grass by roadside: New York'. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, wikispecies]*
