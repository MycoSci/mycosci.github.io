## Summary

Cortinarius sanguineus (Wulfen) Gray, the Bloodred Webcap, is a small, bright red member of the Cortinariaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as associated with northern or montane conifers. It was first described from Europe and is also recorded in the northeastern United States and Canada. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet as the Latin word sanguineus, meaning "bloody". It also notes that Gray, in 1821, recorded the species as Cortinaria sanguinea, "the bloody curtain-stool". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Franz Xaver von Wulfen described the species as Agaricus sanguineus in 1781, reporting it from fir forests around Klagenfurt and Ebenthal in October. Samuel Frederick Gray established Cortinarius as a genus in 1821 and recorded the species as Cortinaria sanguinea. Friedrich Otto Wünsche described it as Dermocybe sanguinea in 1877. Wikipedia adds that most mycologists retain Dermocybe only as a subgenus of Cortinarius, because genetically all its species lie within Cortinarius. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the pigment can be used as a wool dye, giving shades of pink, purple or red, and names the major pigments as emodin, dermocybin and dermorubin. Mushroom World also calls it a traditional dye mushroom, though that claim is unsourced on that site and it is a separate source from Wikipedia only in the sense of being a different cluster. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, primarily spruces but also firs, eastern hemlock and northern white-cedar. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, in summer and fall. In North America it is known from boreal and sub-boreal ecosystems from Michigan to Newfoundland and New Jersey. The same source says it is fairly widely distributed in northern and montane Europe and was originally described from Austria. Wikipedia says it grows in conifer woodlands in autumn, and Wulfen's original report was from fir forests in October. Mushroom World says it is most often found in coniferous forests, particularly among spruces and pines. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
