## Summary

Lichenomphalia hudsoniana (H.S. Jenn.) Redhead, Lutzoni, Moncalvo & Vilgalys, 2002, is a basidiolichen in the family Hygrophoraceae. Wikipedia describes it as widely distributed in alpine and arctic regions, growing on moist soil amongst moss. It was first described in 1936 as Hygrophorus hudsonianus. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described as new to science in 1936 by the American mycologist Herbert Spencer Jennings, in the genus Hygrophorus (Wikispecies lists the basionym as Hygrophorus hudsonianus H.S. Jenn.). The type specimen was collected by George M. Sutton on Southampton Island in 1930, growing in the brown peaty parts of the moss Dicranum fuscescens. The taxon has since been transferred to several genera, including Clitocybe, Omphalina, Gerronema and Phytoconis. It was placed in Lichenomphalia in 2002, following a molecular phylogenetic-based revision of omphalinoid-like genera in the Agaricales. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to Wikipedia, the preferred habitat is damp soil amongst mosses, and in bogs. Less frequently it is recorded on decaying tree bark in shaded areas. It is described as one of the most common and widely distributed members of its genus. It occurs in Asia, Europe and North America, largely in alpine and arctic regions, and is also known from areas near the ocean in Norway and Great Britain. A GBIF description record cites Cossu et al. 2022 for a distribution in Colombia (Tolima) at 4150 to 4700 m a.s.l., and lists distribution entries for Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
