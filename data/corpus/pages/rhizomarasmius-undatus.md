## Summary

Rhizomarasmius undatus (Berk.) R.H. Petersen, 2000, is a member of the Physalacriaceae. According to Wikipedia, it is a small mushroom that grows on fern rhizomes. GBIF holds 778 occurrence records, spread over many European countries and some others, including the US and Canada. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the epithet undatus as meaning "wavy", the past participle of the Latin verb "undo" ("I undulate"). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Per Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1836 as Agaricus undatus by Miles Joseph Berkeley. In 1838 Elias Magnus Fries used the same name for a different mushroom (now Entoloma undatum), which made Berkeley's earlier use of the name illegitimate under the rules of precedence. In 1849 Fries renamed Berkeley's fungus Marasmius undatus, a name that stayed current for more than a century. In 2000 R. H. Petersen made it one of the two original species of the new genus Rhizomarasmius, the other being the type species R. pyrrhocephalus. All of this comes from a single source, Wikipedia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows on rhizomes of bracken (Pteridium aquilinum) and probably other ferns. It describes the species as widespread but rare in northern and western Europe, and reports it from North Africa, North America and the Altai Mountains. This is a single-source account.
