## Summary

Hygrocybe cantharellus (Schwein.) Murrill, 1911, is a small waxcap in the family Hygrophoraceae, called the chanterelle waxy cap by Wikipedia. Sources describe an orange to reddish-orange cap and a reddish-orange stem, with pale yellow gills running down the stem. Wikipedia lists it from North America and Australia. MushroomExpert.Com notes the name has been recorded from five continents and may cover several similar species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the European Hygrocybe lepida was previously referred to this name but is now known to be distinct. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo), noting records from diverse ecosystems on five continents, suggests the name may be a group of closely related, morphologically similar species rather than one widely travelled species. This is a single-source opinion. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it appearing in woods under hardwoods, growing gregariously or in loose troops, in summer and fall, and widely distributed east of the Great Plains. It says the precise ecological role is uncertain, and the illustrated collections are from Michigan, Pennsylvania and Québec. Mushroom World, an aggregator, instead describes it in mossy grasslands and open woodland areas, typically in late summer to autumn. Wikipedia gives North America and Australia, and iNaturalist, which derives from Wikipedia, says eastern North America and Australia. The GBIF distribution record lists Belgium (Flemish and Walloon Regions), DK, NO, SE and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, wikipedia, inaturalist, gbif_desc]*
