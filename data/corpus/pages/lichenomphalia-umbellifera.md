## Summary

Lichenomphalia umbellifera (L.) Redhead, Lutzoni, Moncalvo & Vilgalys, 2002, is a species in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the Heath Navel. It is a basidiolichen: the fungus forms a symbiosis with unicellular green algae of the genus Coccomyxa and produces small gilled mushrooms. Wikipedia also gives the names lichen agaric and green-pea mushroom lichen. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Carl Linnaeus first described the species in 1753 as Agaricus umbelliferus, and that it was transferred to Lichenomphalia in 2002. MushroomExpert.Com adds that Linnaeus thought it looked like a tiny parasol and said it grew among rotting leaves. That site notes that the mushroom grows in association with a green alga, which makes it a lichen. Wikipedia reports that the species has a wide geographic range and considerable phenotypic plasticity, but that phylogenetic research has confirmed these populations to be a single species. MushroomExpert.Com cites a 2012 study (Geml and collaborators) that found no significant genetic differentiation between North American and Eurasian populations of Lichenomphalia, although the researchers found northern and southern lineages within the species. The GBIF record notes a typification dispute. Redhead & Kuyper (1987) selected a Micheli plate as lectotype. Jorgensen & Ryman (1989) rejected that choice in favour of a Rudbeck drawing, because Fries, the sanctioning author, had excluded the Micheli element. Jorgensen & Ryman (1994) later designated an epitype in support of the Rudbeck figure (Fungi Exs. Suec. 1753, UPS). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes the species as mutualistic with a green alga (a Coccomyxa species), forming a lichen. The mushrooms grow alone or gregariously on soil, in moss, or on decaying conifer wood, from spring through fall, or over winter in warm climates. When no mushrooms are being produced, the lichen is described as inconspicuous, dark green and crust-like. That source says the species was originally described from Europe, is widely distributed in northern and montane areas of North America, and has also been recorded in Oceania; its illustrated collection is from California. Wikipedia says it occurs throughout most of the year on damp soil and rotting wood, and can be found in the Northern Hemisphere, particularly in the Arctic region. It says the species is common in the North American Pacific Northwest, northward from Santa Cruz. Mushroom World, an aggregator, additionally lists heathlands, moorlands, woodland clearings and other nutrient-poor habitats; this is a single-source statement. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
