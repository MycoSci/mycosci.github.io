## Summary

Lactarius tabidus Fr. (1838) is a milkcap in the family Russulaceae, known in English as the Birch Milkcap. Wikipedia describes a small brown-capped mushroom with white milk that dries yellow and stains fabric sulphur yellow, recorded from Europe and North America. Wikipedia, a single source cluster, calls the fruit bodies inedible. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia notes that authorship of the original description has been variously ascribed to Elias Magnus Fries and to Pierre Bulliard. It says the synonymous name L. theiogalus is variously credited to Fries and Samuel Frederick Gray. It also says L. chrysorrheus and L. hepaticus have been assigned to different Lactarius species. Wikispecies lists the name as Lactarius tabidus Fr. 1838, citing Fries's Epicrisis systematis mycologici, p. 346. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it can be found under pine or birch in autumn, growing best in leaf litter or Sphagnum moss. It also says it is common in southern Britain and rarer northwards, and that it occurs in North America. The same page's introduction instead says it grows at the base of pine. Mushroom World describes it as growing solitarily or in scattered groups on damp, humus-rich soil in mossy areas beneath broad-leaf trees, particularly favouring birch. Mushroom World gives its location as Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
