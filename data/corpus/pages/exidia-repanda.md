## Summary

Exidia repanda Fr. (1822), now placed by Index Fungorum as Descidia repanda, is a fungus in the family Auriculariaceae, described from Sweden and known in English as birch jelly. One source describes it as forming gelatinous, button-shaped, reddish-brown fruit bodies about 3 cm across, attached to dead birch wood. Only one descriptive source was available. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described from Sweden in 1822 as Exidia repanda by Elias Magnus Fries. Wikipedia reports that molecular research based on cladistic analysis of DNA sequences has shown it does not belong in Exidia sensu stricto and should be placed in the newly described genus Descidia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a wood-rotting species found on dead twigs and branches of birch, and states that the fruit bodies are attached to dead attached twigs and branches. It is described as widely distributed in Europe and also reported from China, Japan and the Russian Far East. This is a single-source description. *[wikipedia]*
