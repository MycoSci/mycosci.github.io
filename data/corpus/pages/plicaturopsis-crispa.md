## Summary

Plicaturopsis crispa (Pers.) D.A. Reid (1964) is a species in the family Amylocorticiaceae, known in English as the Crimped Gill. Wikipedia describes it as a saprotrophic fungus with small, bracket-like, semi-circular fruit bodies 1–3 cm in length, forming clusters on decomposing branches of typically deciduous trees. It has been recorded in Europe, Asia, Australia and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia (the only source in this set that tells the naming history), the species was originally described by Persoon in 1794 as Merulius fagineus and reclassified by him in 1800 as Merulius crispus. In 1821 Fries moved it into Cantharellus, then in 1862 moved it to Trogia, described as a genus of several tropical species with similar hymenial ridges. In 1922 Carleton Rea abandoned Trogia and moved the species into Plicatura. In 1964 Derek Reid emphasized the morphological differences between the two Plicatura species and erected the monotypic genus Plicaturopsis for P. crispa. Wikipedia adds that Merulius, Cantharellus, Trogia and Plicatura are not closely related, but belong to different orders. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only source with ecological text, describes P. crispa as saprotrophic, found in temperate regions year-round, often on hazel, alder and beech trees. Its ecology section describes it as colonizing predominantly dead branches of deciduous trees (Fagus and Betula) and as associated with a white rot. It takes part in the initial phase of decay. A few years into wood decomposition, strong competitors such as Trametes versicolor and Schizophyllum commune often displace it. GBIF's distribution entry lists Belgium (Brussels-Capital, Flemish and Walloon regions), Norway and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
