## Summary

Cryptococcus neoformans (Sanfelice) Vuill. 1901 is an encapsulated basidiomycetous yeast in the family Cryptococcaceae (Tremellomycetes), not a mushroom-forming fungus. Its teleomorph is a filamentous fungus formerly called Filobasidiella neoformans. It causes the disease cryptococcosis, primarily in immunocompromised hosts, and cryptococcal meningitis is most often fatal when left untreated. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Etymology

The genus name Cryptococcus comes from Ancient Greek κρυπτός (kruptós, 'hidden') and κόκκος (kókkos, 'grain'). The specific name neoformans comes from Latin neo- ('new') and fōrmāns ('shaping'). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

The species was first described in 1895 as Saccharomyces neoformans by Sanfelice, and Vuillemin transferred it to Cryptococcus in 1901 (Wikispecies). The teleomorph was first described in 1975 by K.J. Kwon-Chung, who obtained cultures of Filobasidiella neoformans by crossing strains of the yeast. Following changes to the nomenclature code, which discontinued separate names for teleomorph and anamorph forms, Filobasidiella neoformans became a synonym of Cryptococcus neoformans. Later classification work divided the former varieties into several species, and the name C. neoformans now refers to C. neoformans var. grubii (Wikipedia). The Wikispecies epitype is NYSD 1649, isolated by A. Proctor in Durham, North Carolina, USA, from a patient with Hodgkin's lymphoma. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes C. neoformans as an obligate aerobe that can live in both plants and animals, and as often found in bird excrement in its yeast state. It describes the fungus as normally a harmless soil fungus. Wikipedia also reports studies suggesting that colonies of C. neoformans and related fungi growing within the ruins of the Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant may be able to metabolize ionizing radiation. These are statements from a single independent source cluster (Wikipedia and its derivative iNaturalist entry). *[wikipedia]*
