## Summary

Amanita porphyria (Alb. & Schwein., 1805), the grey veiled amanita or porphyry amanita, is a mycorrhizal member of the Amanitaceae recorded across Europe and North America. Wikipedia describes it as a fairly common, inedible mushroom. MushroomExpert.Com separates it from similar species by its bulbous, rimmed or collared stem base, brown cap, and grayish veil remnants and ring. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet porphyria comes from the Ancient Greek porphúra (πορφύρα), meaning the Tyrian purple dye, and that this colour may be seen in the cap of the mushroom, though it is not always evident. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was described in 1805 under its current name by Albertini and Schweinitz in their Conspectus Fungorum. The name was later sanctioned by Fries, which gives it priority even though Schumacher had already described the same species as Agaricus gracilis in 1803. Wikipedia also notes there was some uncertainty over whether North American specimens belonged under a different name, but that DNA evidence now shows all the variants are the same species. MushroomExpert.Com, by contrast, says the North American versions of the species may be phylogenetically distinct, so the sources differ on this point. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it usually grows on poor soil under coniferous trees, especially spruce, but also fir, hemlock, and some deciduous trees such as birch, and that it is mycorrhizal. It fruits from summer to autumn, and is commoner in mountains or further north. In Europe it is very common in boreal or hemiboreal forests but less so in temperate areas. It is also found in northern North America, and Wikipedia says it has been recorded in Australia. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially spruces and hemlocks, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall, in northern North America from Maine to the Pacific Northwest. Mushroom World says it grows scattered on the ground under conifers and less commonly in mixed conifer-hardwood forests. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
