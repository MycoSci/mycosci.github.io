## Summary

Morchella tridentina Bres. is a species of morel in the family Morchellaceae, known in North America as the mountain blond or western blond morel. Its fruit bodies are conical, grey to buff, often rufescent, and up to 20 cm tall. It has been recorded in many countries, including Argentina, Chile, Cyprus, France, India, Israel, Spain, Turkey and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet derives from the Roman name of Trento, Tridentum. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Bresadola described the species in a work on fungi found in the region of Trento, Italy. Two original collections that Bresadola himself identified as M. tridentina are kept at the Swedish Museum of Natural History. They were found to match the protologue and typical morphology, but attempts to sequence them failed. The phylogenetic lineage provisionally called "Mel-2" was described as Morchella frustrata from Placer County, California, in a publication by Michael Kuo and colleagues. DNA studies by Richard and colleagues and by Loizides and colleagues showed that this lineage is identical to the southern European morels that match Bresadola's description. M. frustrata was therefore placed in synonymy with M. tridentina, which has chronological priority. Wikipedia also lists M. elatoides, M. quercus-ilicis, M. conica var. pseudoeximia and M. elatoides var. elegans as synonyms. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

A 2023 study in Acta Botanica Mexicana (lit_3) isolated M. tridentina mycelium from fruit bodies collected in an Abies religiosa forest in the State of Mexico. The mycelium was grown in vitro on PDA and propagated on wheat grains, where sclerotia formed after 21 days. The authors state that the strains could possibly be used to produce ascocarps, but that optimal conditions for fruiting still need to be determined. The source does not report fruit bodies produced in cultivation. *[lit_3]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is usually found in mountainous forests and Mediterranean shrubland, growing solitary, scattered or in small groups in spring. Its trophic mode is not yet known with certainty. Tree species associated with it in North America include Arbutus menziesii, Quercus spp., Pseudotsuga menziesii, Pinus ponderosa, Pinus lambertiana and Abies concolor. In Europe it is often found with Quercus ilex, Arbutus andrachne, Olea europaea, Abies pinsapo, Abies alba, Pinus brutia and Pinus sylvestris. In North America it has so far been confirmed only in Oregon and California. Other sources record it from an Abies religiosa forest in the State of Mexico (lit_3), from Neelum Valley in Azad Jammu and Kashmir, Pakistan (lit_1), and from Denizli province in southwestern Turkey (lit_2). *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*
