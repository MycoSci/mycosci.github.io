## Summary

Fistulina hepatica (Schaeff.) With., 1801, is a bracket fungus in the family Fistulinaceae (Agaricales), commonly called the beefsteak fungus. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as looking like a slab of meat, and both say the flesh exudes a red juice. It is recorded from many countries across Europe and North America, among others. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name is a diminutive of the Latin word fistula, meaning "small tube", and that the species name hepatica means "liver-like", referring to the consistency of the flesh. This derivation comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as fairly common in Britain and the rest of Europe from July to October, often on oaks and sweet chestnut, on living or dead wood, and as causing a brown rot. It adds that in Australia the species grows from wounds on Eucalyptus trees, and that it is also found in North America, North Africa and Southern Africa. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic and sometimes weakly parasitic on the wood of oaks and other hardwoods, causing a brown rot. It is annual, grows alone or in small groups near the bases of trees and on stumps in summer and fall, and is much more common east of the Rocky Mountains in North America. The collections MushroomExpert.Com illustrates and describes are from Kentucky and Ohio. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
