## Summary

Scutellinia scutellata (L.) Lambotte, 1887, the Common Eyelash, is a small cup fungus in the family Pyronemataceae (Pezizales, Ascomycota). Wikipedia describes it as a saprophytic species with small red cups edged with long, dark hairs or "eyelashes", and as the type species of Scutellinia. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific name scutellata is from the Latin for "like a small shield". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

MushroomExpert.Com says the common name "eyelash cup" comes from the long dark hairs on the cup margin, which show in its enlarged illustrations. Wikipedia likewise calls these "eyelashes" the most distinctive feature. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Carl Linnaeus first described the species in 1753 in Species Plantarum as Peziza scutellata. Jean Baptiste Émil Lambotte gave it its current name in 1887. Wikipedia also lists an 1902 name, Patella scutellata. According to Wikipedia, a 1996 study of British specimens found that S. crinita, described as Peziza crinita by Bulliard in 1789, was synonymous with S. scutellata. A GBIF nomenclatural note says Schumacher (1990) indicated a neotype, but original material exists, so under Art. 9.11 a neotype cannot be selected. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as saprobic, fruiting on rotten wood and damp soil, and sometimes on ashes, wet leaves or bracket fungi. It says the species typically fruits in groups, sometimes in dense clusters, and that in Alaska it has been found on tundra humus. Wikipedia gives the season as late spring to late autumn in Europe and winter and spring in North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on wet, rotted wood or nearby damp soil, growing gregariously or in clusters, from spring through fall, and widely distributed in North America. These two sources differ on the North American season. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
