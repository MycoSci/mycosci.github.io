## Summary

Lysurus mokusin (L. f.) Fr., 1823, is a stinkhorn in the family Phallaceae, known in English as the lantern stinkhorn. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe a hollow, spongy, ribbed stem topped by a head of arms that usually stay joined at the tips. The arms are coated in a foul-smelling, dark spore slime. It is recorded from the US, Australia, China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan and Italy among other countries. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Fries transferred the species to Lysurus in 1823 in his Systema Mycologicum, and that L. mokusin is the type species of the genus. Wikipedia attributes the original description, as Phallus mokusin, to Pierre-Martial Cibot in 1775 from material found near Peking, and says Persoon sanctioned the name in 1801. MushroomExpert.Com instead cites the basionym as (Linnaeus, 1782) Fries, 1823, so the sources differ on who authored the original name. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes L. mokusin as saprobic, growing solitarily or in small groups in forest litter, in wood chip mulch used in landscaping, and in compost. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously, usually in urban habitats such as lawns, landscaping and gardens. It gives summer as the fruiting season, or year round in warm climates, and calls the species most common in California and Australia. Wikipedia lists sightings in Australasia, the Canary Islands, Korea, Japan, China (Fujian Province) and the Bonin Islands, and says it was unknown in Europe until reported in Italy in 1979. The GBIF description record lists Madeira Island. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
