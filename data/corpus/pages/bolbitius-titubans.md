## Summary

Bolbitius titubans (Bull.) Fr., the Yellow Fieldcap, is a small, fragile, yellow-capped gilled mushroom in the family Bolbitiaceae. Wikipedia describes it as widespread and found on every continent except Antarctica. MushroomExpert.Com treats it as a species group awaiting modern taxonomic study. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Bulliard described the species in 1789 as Agaricus titubans, and MushroomExpert.Com likewise gives the original description as Bulliard 1789, from France. MushroomExpert.Com notes that several species (Bolbitius fragilis, B. variicolor and B. vitellinus) were described on putative morphological differences that the author finds inconsistent. It cites the only contemporary study it knows of (Malysheva et al. 2015), which found support for four subclades, mainly in Russian specimens. That study concluded the species is a complex of morphotypes and that whether they are independent taxa needs further research. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously on dung, compost, straw, and in fertilized grass, in summer and fall and over winter in warm climates. It says robust specimens occur on dung and heavily fertilized soil, and flimsier ones in grassy areas with less nitrogen. Wikipedia lists grass, woodchips, compost and dung, and also mentions meadows and lawns fruiting after rain in the warm season. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as widely distributed in Europe and North America, documented in Oceania, and reported from South America and Africa. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
