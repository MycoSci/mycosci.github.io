## Summary

Catathelasma imperiale (P. Karst.) Singer, 1940, is a species of gilled mushroom in the family Biannulariaceae, known in English as the imperial mushroom. Wikipedia describes stocky fruit bodies with a double ring and a tapering, often rooting stem, and an ectomycorrhizal association with conifers. It reports the species as widespread but uncommon, and assessed as globally "near threatened" on the IUCN Red List. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet "imperiale", meaning "imperial", refers to the (at times) imposing size of the fruit bodies. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia reports that in Ukraine the species is known as korban, or the Hutsul mushroom, because it is common among the Hutsuls, who are said to be experts at finding it. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described from Sweden in 1845 by the Norwegian botanist Nicolai Lund as Agaricus imperialis. That name is illegitimate because two earlier, different fungi had already been described under it. It was first legitimately described as Armillaria imperialis by the Finnish mycologist Petter Adolf Karsten in 1879. In 1922 Günther Beck von Mannagetta und Lerchenau created the genus Biannularia for it, giving Biannularia imperialis. In 1940 Singer transferred it to the existing genus Catathelasma, considering the two genera synonymous. Wikipedia adds that recent DNA research confirms this synonymy. This account comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes C. imperiale as ectomycorrhizal with conifers, particularly spruce. It says the species was originally described from Scandinavia and has been recorded from most northern countries of continental Europe as far south as Italy, and in Asia from China, Korea and Japan. For the Carpathian Mountains, Wikipedia says it grows in coniferous and spruce forests at 400–1,000 m altitude. The GBIF description records Chinese specimens (Gansu, Sichuan and Tibet, collected in August 2012–2013 at 2,700–4,200 m) under Picea. Wikipedia says North American records are uncertain: DNA sequencing of specimens determined as "C. imperiale" has so far shown them to be C. evanescens or C. ventricosum. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) defines the species for western North America. It describes it as presumably mycorrhizal, growing alone or scattered on the ground under conifers in late summer and fall, and common in the monsoon season in the Rocky Mountains, where it often grows under Engelmann spruce and subalpine fir. The sources therefore differ on whether the species occurs in North America, and the North American habitat description rests on that one field guide. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc, mushroomexpert]*
