## Summary

Phaeotremella foliacea (Pers.) Wedin, J.C. Zamora & Millanes (2016) is a species in the order Tremellales, based on Tremella foliacea Pers. Wikipedia describes it as producing brownish, frondose, gelatinous fruit bodies and as a parasite of the fungus Stereum sanguinolentum. It has been called leafy brain and jelly leaf in English. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet "foliacea" means "leafy", with reference to the shape of the fruit bodies. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Tremella foliacea was first published in 1800 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon. The name stayed in use until molecular research based on DNA sequences showed the species was not closely related to the type species of Tremella and belonged in a separate genus, Phaeotremella. Wikipedia also says that similar-looking fungi on broad-leaved trees that had been called Tremella foliacea were distinguished c. 2017 as Phaeotremella frondosa and P. fimbriata, and that the name P. foliacea is now restricted to the fungus that grows on conifers. Wikipedia further reports that the yeast Cryptococcus skinneri, described by Herman Phaff and L. do Carmo Sousa in 1962 from beetle frass on Western Hemlock, has been shown by DNA sequencing to be the yeast state of this species. All of this rests on a single independent source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes P. foliacea as a parasite of Stereum sanguinolentum, growing on the host's hyphae in the wood rather than on the host's fruit bodies, and says its fruit bodies are typically found on dead, attached or recently fallen branches of conifers. Wikipedia gives the range as North America, Europe and northern Asia; the GBIF description record lists only DK, NO, SE and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
