## Summary

Hericium abietis (Weir ex Hubert) K.A. Harrison, 1964, is a tooth fungus in the family Hericiaceae (Russulales), with occurrence records from the US, Canada, Japan, Poland, Bhutan, the UK, India and Ukraine. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a western species that grows on conifer wood. Its tightly packed branches are hung with many thin spines, giving a cascading appearance, and it is usually about the size of a softball or a soccer ball. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described as Hydnum abietis by the American botanist James Robert Weir in 1931. It says Weir collected the type specimens in 1916 near the Priest River in Idaho, and that Kenneth Archibald Harrison transferred the species to Hericium in 1964. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a white rot of conifers, with fruit bodies growing singly or occasionally in small groups on dead conifer wood, especially Douglas-fir. It says fruiting runs from after the start of the fall rains to mid-season, and that the species is found throughout North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and parasitic on the wood of conifers (including firs, Douglas-fir and hemlock), growing alone in fall and winter. It gives the distribution as northern California to Idaho and Alaska, with the illustrated collection from Oregon. The GBIF description record supplied lists only 'Vermont' under distribution. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
