## Summary

Calvatia cyathiformis (Bosc) Morgan 1890, the purple-spored puffball, is a large saprobic puffball whose spore mass matures from white through yellow to dull purple or purple-brown. Wikipedia gives the fruiting body as 5–20 cm high and/or broad, and MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) gives 8–17 cm high and 8–20 cm wide at maturity. Wikipedia reports it from North America and Australia, and Kuo describes it as widespread in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that the epithet cyathiformis means "shaped like a flask", but its author says he has trouble with that gloss. He notes the species is usually blob- or ball-shaped when young, and later develops a narrower basal portion that resembles an inverted pear or a loaf of bread. This is a single-source statement. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, gregariously, or in fairy rings in grass (lawns, golf courses, parks, pastures), in summer and fall. It says the species is widely distributed in North America but possibly absent on the West Coast, and that the illustrated collections are from Illinois and Québec. Wikipedia says it is found mostly in prairie or grassland environments in North America and Australia. For Australia it reports the species in grazing paddocks and grassed areas in the southwest of Western Australia, from Adelaide in South Australia to Cooktown on Cape York Peninsula, and in Darwin, Northern Territory. A GBIF description record lists the substrate as soil and the habit as terrestrial. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
