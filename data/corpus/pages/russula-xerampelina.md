## Summary

Russula xerampelina (Schaeff.) Fr. is a brittlegill in the family Russulaceae, known in English as the shrimp russula or crab brittlegill. Wikipedia describes a fresh odour of shellfish or crab and a cap in shades of wine-red, purple or green. MushroomExpert.Com notes that the name is often applied to a group of poorly studied North American 'shrimp russulas', while the name itself belongs to a purplish red European species. Wikipedia likewise reports that recent studies treat it as a species complex. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet as taken from the Ancient Greek meaning 'colour of dried vine leaves', from xeros ('dry') and ampelinos ('of the vine'). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was originally described in 1770 as Agaricus xerampelina from a collection in Bavaria by Jacob Christian Schaeffer, who noted the colour as fusco-purpureus ('purple-brown'). It was later given its present binomial by Elias Magnus Fries. Wikipedia also states that R. xerampelina is a species complex with at least three species (R. xerampelina, R. clavipes and R. graviolens), and that the former variety elaeodes is now classified as R. clavipes. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruiting bodies appear in coniferous woodlands in autumn in northern Europe and North America. In North America it is reported from July to October in the east and October to January in the west. It grows solitary or in groups with conifers, with an apparent preference for Douglas fir, more rarely pine or larch, and is sometimes found in deciduous woods such as beech and oak. MushroomExpert.Com describes the European species R. xerampelina as mycorrhizal with conifers, and cautions that North American 'shrimp russulas' under this name have not been adequately studied. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
