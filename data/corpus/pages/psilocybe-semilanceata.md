## Summary

Psilocybe semilanceata (Fr.) P. Kumm. 1871, the liberty cap, is a member of the family Hymenogastraceae. The English Wikipedia article describes it as a grassland species that produces psilocybin, psilocin and baeocystin, and as one of the most widely distributed psilocybin mushrooms. GBIF occurrence records include Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the generic name as derived from Ancient Greek psilos 'smooth, bare' and Byzantine Greek kubê 'head'. It gives the specific epithet as from Latin semi 'half, somewhat' and lanceata, from lanceolatus 'spear-shaped'. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

According to Wikipedia, the common name comes from the Phrygian cap, also called the 'liberty cap', which the mushroom resembles. The same article says P. pelliculosa shares this common name and is more or less indistinguishable from P. semilanceata in appearance. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Elias Magnus Fries first described the species as Agaricus semilanceatus in his 1838 Epicrisis Systematis Mycologici. Paul Kummer transferred it to Psilocybe in 1871, when he raised many of Fries's sub-groupings of Agaricus to genus level. Wikipedia also reports that after molecular studies showed Psilocybe to be polyphyletic, several mycologists proposed in 2005 to conserve the name Psilocybe with P. semilanceata as the type. The Nomenclature Committee for Fungi accepted the proposal unanimously in 2009. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the earliest reliably documented intoxication with this species was in 1799, when a British family prepared a meal with mushrooms picked in London's Green Park. It also reports that in the early 1960s Albert Hofmann chemically analysed fruit bodies collected in Switzerland and France, and that the 1963 publication was the first report of psilocybin in a European mushroom species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species fruits solitarily or in groups on rich, acidic soil, typically in grasslands such as meadows, pastures or lawns. It is often found in pastures fertilized with sheep or cow dung but does not typically grow directly on the dung. Wikipedia describes it as a saprobic species thought to live on decaying grass roots, and notes an association with sedges in moist areas of fields. Mushroom World, which shares a text cluster with Wikipedia, describes it as growing in scattered trooping groups on soil amongst grass in lawns and pastures, favouring hilly sites. For the United States, Wikipedia says it is most common in the Pacific Northwest west of the Cascade Mountains, where it fruits abundantly in autumn and early winter. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
