## Summary

Tricholoma vaccinum (Schaeff.) P. Kumm., in the family Tricholomataceae, was combined in Tricholoma in 1871 and has occurrence records from many countries across Europe, North America and Asia. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe a mycorrhizal fungus associated mainly with spruce. Its cap is shaggy, reddish- to orangish-brown, and breaks up into scales with age. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet derives from the Latin word vaccinus, meaning "pertaining to cows", probably in reference to the mushroom's color. This is a single-source statement, and Wikipedia itself hedges it with "probably". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Jacob Christian Schäffer first described the species in 1774 as Agaricus vaccinus. It also reports that Rolf Singer's 1986 infrageneric classification of Tricholoma places it in section Imbricata of subgenus Tricholoma. Marcel Bon described the variety fulvosquamosum in 1970, with squamules arranged concentrically on the cap, and Manfred Enderle published it as a form in 2004. MushroomExpert.Com notes that, like many Tricholoma species, it was originally described from Europe (Schaeffer 1774), and that North American collections may represent several unnamed species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies can be used to create yellow dyes to color wool or other fibers. No second source was found for this. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe a mycorrhizal species. Wikipedia gives association with coniferous trees, especially pine and spruce. MushroomExpert.Com gives primarily spruces, occasionally other conifers. Wikipedia says fruit bodies usually appear in groups or clusters on the ground, sometimes with moss, in late summer and autumn. MushroomExpert.Com says they grow alone, scattered, or gregariously, in late summer and fall, and also over winter in warm climates. Wikipedia reports it from northern Asia and Europe, widely in the United States and Canada, and from Mexico. It calls the species one of the most common Tricholoma in Central Europe, often in large groups in spruce forests. It says the species is rare in the United Kingdom, with most records from Scotland, and may be extinct in the Netherlands. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as widely distributed in northern and montane North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
