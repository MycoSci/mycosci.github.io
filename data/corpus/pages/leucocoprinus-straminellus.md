## Summary

Leucocoprinus straminellus (Bagl.) Narducci & Caroti (1995) is a species of the family Agaricaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a small dapperling with a whitish-yellow, striate-margined cap 1.5–2.5 cm wide that has a darker yellow centre, and a stem 2.4–5 cm tall. It is described there as similar to Leucocoprinus birnbaumii but smaller, a lighter shade of yellow, and with smaller spores that lack a germ pore. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Francesco Baglietto first described the species in 1865 as Agaricus straminellus. Pier Andrea Saccardo placed it in Lepiota as Lepiota straminella in 1887. Roberto Narducci and Vincenzo Caroti reclassified it as Leucocoprinus straminellus in 1995. Wikipedia adds that the name Leucocoprinus denudatus, dating from Rolf Singer in 1951, is still sometimes used. It also reports that a variant, var. albus, was described in 1999 by Vincenzo Migliozzi and Marcello Rava and is now considered a synonym. Wikipedia further notes that this species is often confused with Leucocoprinus flavescens, which has led to inconsistencies in descriptions. This account rests on a single independent source, Wikipedia; the iNaturalist text is a derivative of it. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that, like other Leucocoprinus species, it may have originated in a tropical climate but now occurs in plant pots, greenhouses and compost piles in many countries. It also says the species is more commonly found in Europe than in North America. The first North American observation, studied in 1981, was of specimens found in June growing in dense clusters at the University of Michigan Botanical Garden. A GBIF description record lists distribution for Austria, Belgium (Flemish Region), France, Great Britain, the Netherlands and Norway, plus the country codes BR, DE, DK, NO and SE.
