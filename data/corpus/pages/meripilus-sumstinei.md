## Summary

Meripilus sumstinei (Murrill) M.J. Larsen & Lombard, 1988, is a polypore in the family Meripilaceae, known as the giant polypore or black-staining polypore. Wikipedia describes it as found in eastern North America, and MushroomExpert.Com describes it as widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains and occasionally reported in the west. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

MushroomExpert.Com's Michael Kuo suggests that the species should be known as the "rooster of the woods", by analogy with the "hen of the woods" and the "chicken of the woods". This is a single-source proposal, and the source does not say how the other common names arose. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was originally described by William Alphonso Murrill as Grifola sumstinei in 1905 and transferred to Meripilus in 1988. MushroomExpert.Com cites the original authority as Murrill, 1904, so the two sources differ on the year of the original description. MushroomExpert.Com adds that North American field guides usually treated the species as "Meripilus giganteus", a name it says refers to a strictly European species, and refers readers to Larsen and Lombard (1988) for details. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is found in eastern North America from June to September. It says the fungus grows in large clumps on the ground around hardwood (including oak) trunks, stumps and logs, and also in clumps above dead logs, stumps and branches, and that it prefers shady areas. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as parasitic on living oaks and other hardwoods (also reported on Douglas-fir) and saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods, causing a white rot. It reports growth in large clusters of rosettes near the bases of trees, often reappearing in the same place in later years, in summer and fall. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
