## Summary

Pleurotus djamor (Rumph. ex Fr.) Boedijn, 1959, is commonly known as the pink oyster mushroom and belongs to the family Pleurotaceae. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as pink, with white forms also occurring. GBIF occurrence records place the species in dozens of countries across the tropics and subtropics and beyond. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Georg Eberhard Rumphius first named the species Boletus secundus arboreus in 1750. Elias Magnus Fries sanctioned it as Agaricus djamor in 1821 and later transferred it to Lentinus. Karel Bernard Boedijn transferred it to Pleurotus in 1959. The sources do not say why these transfers were made. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the species is commonly found in central Mexican communities and is the best-known mushroom in Tlayacapan, Morelos. Families collect it and often sell it at vendor markets, and mestizos also sell it door-to-door in mountain communities such as San Lorenzo de Atzqueltán and Izolta. Local names listed there are "seta", "cazahuate", "orejón", "hongo de pino", "blanco" and "oreja de cazahuate". This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states that the species is widely cultivated and can be grown at relatively warm temperatures of 25-30°C. It lists barley straw, sawdust, tea leaves and wheat straw as substrates, and says the species needs less water spraying during fruiting than the Italian oyster. This comes from a single source and gives no detailed protocol. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the species grows in tropical and subtropical areas, as far north as Japan and as far south as New Zealand. In Hawai'i it often grows on fallen coconuts and on palm frond stalks, and it has also been found on fallen ōhiʻa branches on Kaua'i. A GBIF-hosted description of a Brazilian specimen gives the substrate as dead trunks and says the species grows on deciduous wood. The same description calls it a worldwide species and reports a first record for the Cerrado region of Brazil. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
