## Summary

Tricholoma fracticum (Britzelm.) Kreisel is a species of Tricholoma in the family Tricholomataceae. Sources describe it as a sturdy agaric with a reddish-brown cap and a two-coloured stem with a flimsy ring zone. It is documented in North America from northern California. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states it was first described as Agaricus fracticus by Max Britzelmayr in 1893 and transferred to Tricholoma by Hanns Kreisel in 1984. Wikipedia notes it has occasionally been listed as a synonym of Tricholoma batschii, but it has larger spores and 2-spored basidia versus 4-spored in T. batschii, and the two are currently considered separate species. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) instead lists T. batschii and T. subannulatum as synonyms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states it is mycorrhizal with conifers, primarily Pinus, and can be found in California from October to January. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with pines and possibly other conifers, growing scattered or gregariously, sometimes in clusters, recorded from northern California in winter, and calls its overall North American distribution uncertain. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
