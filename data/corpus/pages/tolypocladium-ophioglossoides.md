## Summary

Tolypocladium ophioglossoides (J.F. Gmel.) C.A. Quandt, Kepler & Spatafora is a fungus in the family Ophiocordycipitaceae, order Hypocreales. Wikipedia describes it as parasitic on the fruit bodies of the truffle-like Elaphomyces, and lists it as commonly known as the goldenthread cordyceps. Wikipedia also states that the species is considered inedible. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet ophioglossoides is derived from Ancient Greek and means "like a snake's tongue". *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the species is used in traditional Chinese medicine as an herbal remedy for relieving postmenopausal syndrome in women. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is parasitic on fruit bodies of Elaphomyces, and that its distribution is throughout the Northern Hemisphere. It says the species fruits in late summer and fall, often under oak or pine trees because its host Elaphomyces prefers those tree species. These habitat statements come from Wikipedia alone. The GBIF description record lists distribution entries for DK, NO, SE and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
