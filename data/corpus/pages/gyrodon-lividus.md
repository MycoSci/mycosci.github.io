## Summary

Gyrodon lividus (Bull.) Sacc. 1888, the alder bolete, is a pored mushroom in the family Paxillaceae (order Boletales). Wikipedia describes it as bearing close affinity to the genus Paxillus, distinguished from other boletes by decurrent bright yellow pores that turn blue-grey on bruising. The same source states it is found predominantly in Europe, in a mycorrhizal association with alder, and that it has also been recorded from China, Japan and California. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the generic term Gyrodon is derived from the Ancient Greek gyros 'whorl' and odon 'tooth', and that the specific epithet lividus is Latin for 'lead-coloured'. This derivation comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia (a single-source account), Pierre Bulliard described the species in 1791 as Boletus lividus, and Pier Andrea Saccardo transferred it to Gyrodon in 1888. The same source says Lucien Quélet erected a genus for it in 1886 with Boletus lividus as the type. It also says Rolf Singer later determined that Elias Fries's Boletus sistotremoides (1815) was the same species. The source's own account of the intermediate genus names is internally inconsistent (Gyrodon, Gyroporus, Gyropus, Uliporus/Uloporus), so the finer details of the transfers should be checked against primary literature. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that Gyrodon lividus grows in a mycorrhizal association with alder. It says fruit bodies may be found alone or in clumps and appear in autumn. For North America it reports occurrence in California under alder (Alnus rhombifolia). It lists European records including Ķemeri National Park in Latvia, records from Turkey (Trabzon, Maçka and Sevinç), and records from China and Japan.
