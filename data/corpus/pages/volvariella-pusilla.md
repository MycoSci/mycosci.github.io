## Summary

Volvariella pusilla (Pers.) Singer is a species of agaric fungus in the family Pluteaceae, described by Rolf Singer in 1951. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as one of several tiny, whitish Volvariella species, with a cap 1–3 cm across and a stem base encased in a sack-like volva, and distinguishes it by its smooth stem and lined cap margin. Wikipedia reports it from Asia, Europe, North America, Africa and Australia, with the most sightings in Europe. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as apparently saprobic, growing alone or gregariously, usually reported from gardens, lawns and wood chips, but also found in woods in areas of disturbed ground such as pathsides, from spring through fall. It gives the distribution as primarily east of the Rocky Mountains, with occasional reports from western North America, and the collections it illustrates and describes are from Illinois. Wikipedia separately lists forests, parks, botanical gardens, allotment gardens and roadsides, sometimes close to houses, on the ground and in grass. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
