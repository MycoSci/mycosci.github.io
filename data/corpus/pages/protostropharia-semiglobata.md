## Summary

Protostropharia semiglobata (Batsch) Redhead, Moncalvo & Vilgalys is an agaric fungus in the family Strophariaceae, known in English as the dung roundhead. Wikipedia describes it as a common, widespread species that fruits on dung, with hemispherical straw-yellow to buff-tan caps 1–4 cm across, greyish gills that darken with age, and a slender stem with a fragile ring. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet semiglobata is Latin for "half-spherical" and refers to the shape of the cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia (the only source for this account), the species was first described as Agaricus semiglobatus by August Batsch in 1786 and was later moved through several genera, including Coprinus, Geophila, Psalliota and Psilocybe. Lucien Quélet transferred it to Stropharia in 1872, and that combination became its most commonly used name. In 2013 Scott Redhead made it the type species of the new genus Protostropharia, which was circumscribed for Stropharia species that form astrocystidia rather than acanthocytes on their mycelium. A form and two varieties described by F.H. Møller in 1945 are no longer considered taxonomically significant. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a saprobic species that fruits in small groups on dung, in soil containing manure, on lawns and grasslands, or in livestock corrals. It reports records from the dung of rabbit, sheep, cow, buffalo, moose, bear and wallaby. It adds that where the dung lies under moss the mushroom can look as if it grows from the moss, and that fruit bodies sometimes occur alongside Deconica coprophila. The same source reports records from northern Asia, Australia, Europe, Central and South America, North America, North Africa and New Zealand, and says the species is thought to have been introduced to Australia with imported domestic stock. The GBIF description record lists only the distribution entries DK, NO and Vermont. No supplied source gives a fruiting season. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
