## Summary

Clavulinopsis amoena (Zoll. & Moritzi) Corner is a clavarioid fungus in the family Clavariaceae. Wikipedia describes it as forming slender, cylindrical, golden-yellow fruiting bodies on the ground among plant litter, and says it was originally described from Java. Its identity is taxonomically unsettled: a later study of the type specimen found ellipsoid spores, unlike the globose-spored concept used in some earlier and later accounts. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was described from Java in 1844 by the Swiss mycologists Heinrich Zollinger and Alexander Moritzi. E.J.H. Corner, in his monograph of the clavarioid fungi, treated C. amoena as a globose-spored species of variable colour and form that was widespread in the tropics, particularly in Asia. Ronald H. Petersen at first agreed with Corner. His later study of the type specimen showed ellipsoid rather than globose spores, so the type was not the taxon described in earlier works. Petersen regarded Clavaria aurantia (described from Australia) and C. luteotenerrima (described from Indonesia) as synonyms. Wikipedia adds that the name has nonetheless continued to be used for a globose-spored species in some more recent taxonomic accounts. A separate 1976 note in Taxon, on the correct name for the type species of Clavulinopsis, lists Clavaria cardinalis Boud. & Pat. 1888 as equivalent to Clavulinopsis amoena. *[wikipedia, lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species typically occurs in small clusters on the ground in broadleaf woodland, and describes the fruiting bodies as growing on the ground among plant litter. This is a single-source statement; the iNaturalist text is a declared copy of the Wikipedia lead. The same article says the distribution is unclear because of identification confusion. It gives the type locality as Indonesia and notes reports from Australia and New Zealand. It quotes Petersen that the taxon 'seems to be distributed over the Southern Hemisphere, at least in temperate areas'. It also says that records from Brazil, and at least some records from elsewhere in America, refer to a different, globose-spored species. *[wikipedia]*
