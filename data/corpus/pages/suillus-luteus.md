## Summary

Suillus luteus (L.) Roussel, 1796, is a bolete in the family Suillaceae, known in English as slippery jack and in German as Butterpilz. It is the type species of Suillus. Sources describe a slimy brown cap and a ring on the stem, and an ectomycorrhizal association with pines. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com say it is native to Eurasia and has been introduced widely elsewhere. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is the Latin adjective lūteus, meaning "yellow", and that Suillus is an ancient term for fungi derived from a word for swine. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia notes that "slippery jack" is the name approved by the British Mycological Society. It says "sticky bun" refers to the fungus's resemblance to the pastry. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Linnaeus described the species as Boletus luteus in the second volume of Species Plantarum (1753). Roussel reclassified it in 1796 as the type species of Suillus. Batsch's Boletus volvatus is now treated as a synonym. Karsten (Cricunopus, 1881), Quélet (Viscipellis, 1886, and Ixocomus, 1888) and Hennings (Boletopsis, 1900) placed it in other genera. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both report that a collection from Sweden was designated neotype in 1986 (Palm & Stewart 1986). Wikipedia adds that Suillus and its allies were moved from the Boletaceae to the new family Suillaceae in 1997. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with pines, especially two-needle pines such as Scots and Austrian pine in Europe and red pine in North America. Kuo also reports collecting it repeatedly under eastern white pine in the Midwest, and says that either the species crosses the hard-soft pine divide or a soft-pine look-alike exists. He gives the growth habit as gregarious and the season as late summer and fall, or winter during warm spells or in warm climates. Wikipedia says it grows in coniferous forests in its native range and in pine plantations where it has been introduced, and that it fruits in spring, summer and autumn after wet weather. Wikipedia also says it appears to prefer acidic, nutrient-poor soil. A GBIF record classes it as ectomycorrhizal and lists Colombian collections at about 2640–2695 m elevation. Primary studies cited here sampled it under Pinus sylvestris (Pinus sylvestris stands; New Phytologist 1987, 2000) and on a copper mine spoil in Norway (Applied and Environmental Microbiology 2005). *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc, lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*
