## Summary

Climacodon septentrionalis (Fr.) P. Karst. is a species in the family Meruliaceae, order Polyporales, known in English as the Northern Tooth. It forms large shelf-like clusters on hardwood trees. The underside of each cap bears white spines rather than pores, and the spines turn yellowish with age. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Elias Magnus Fries described the species in 1821 under the genus Hydnum, and that Petter Karsten transferred it to Climacodon in 1881. MushroomExpert.Com notes that some authors use the spelling Climacodon septentrionale, and lists Steccherinum septentrionale as a synonym. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as a common cause of heart rot in hardwoods, found on living and recently dead trunks, especially beech (Fagus) and maple (Acer). It gives the range as northeastern North America, from southern Canada to Kentucky and west to the Great Plains, with an August–October season. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as parasitic on hardwoods, especially green ash, sugar maple and beech. It grows in large shelf-like clusters in the wounds of living trees or on recently dead stumps or trunks, in summer and fall. It gives the range as northeastern North America, south to Kentucky and Tennessee and west to the Great Plains. The GBIF description record lists distribution entries for Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont, with no habitat text. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
