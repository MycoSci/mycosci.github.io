## Summary

Pleurotus cornucopiae (Paulet) Quél. 1885 is a species of Pleurotaceae. Wikipedia describes it as quite similar to Pleurotus ostreatus, but distinguished by gills that are very decurrent and form a network on the stem. It is recorded across Europe and in the U.S. and Mexico according to that source. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name means "of the Cornucopia" (horn of plenty), and says this is appropriate because the mushrooms sometimes take on a shape similar to a drinking horn. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the basionym was published by Jean-Jacques Paulet in 1793 as Dendrosarcos cornucopiae. Paulet created the genus Dendrosarcos for gilled mushrooms with an excentric or missing stipe, at a time when most gilled mushrooms were placed in Agaricus. The same source says these fungi have not been found to be a closely related group, so the genus name now has only historical interest. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states the species is cultivated in a manner similar to P. ostreatus, though less extensively, and is sold in markets in Europe and China. It cites a Chinese paper reporting that, in the Shanghai area, cotton-seed hulls and wood-chips at 65% water content are an appropriate growth medium. A second paper, which actually treated the yellow-topped form, also suggested pasteurized switch grass, with lower yield than cotton-seed hulls and straw. These are single-source claims. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states the species is saprobic on dead wood and can also be a weak parasite. It occurs on stumps and fallen trunks of oak, beech, elm and other broad-leaved trees. It is reported as appearing from spring to late summer, and as distributed in the wild throughout Europe (from August to November), where it varies locally between common and fairly rare. It is also reported from the U.S. and Mexico. *[wikipedia]*
