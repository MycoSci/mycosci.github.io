## Summary

Hypomyces chrysospermus (Bull.) Tul. & C. Tul. is a parasitic ascomycete in the family Hypocreaceae that grows on boletes, covering the host in a mold-like layer that changes from white to golden yellow and finally to reddish brown. Wikipedia reports that both the mold and its afflicted hosts are not edible and may be poisonous. It is recorded in Eurasia and North America and in southwest Western Australia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that it is used in traditional Chinese medicine to stop bleeding, primarily by topical application onto open wounds. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as parasitic on various species of boletes, occurring in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. It gives the distribution as widespread in Eurasia and North America, also recorded in Central America and Oceania. Wikipedia adds that it is common in the southwest of Western Australia, where it is found in forest and coastal plant communities, and that it is also found in the Chinese provinces of Hebei, Jiangsu, Anhui and Fujian. Wikipedia also lists Paxillus and Rhizopogon among attacked hosts. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
