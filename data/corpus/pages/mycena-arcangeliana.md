## Summary

Mycena arcangeliana (Bres., 1904) is a species in the family Mycenaceae, first described from Italy and known in English as the angel's bonnet or late-season bonnet. Wikipedia describes small mushrooms with caps 1–5 cm across, whitish to darker grey-brown, on slender olive-greyish stems that fade with age. It is recorded across Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet arcangeliana may be in honour of Giovanni Arcangeli, who collected the species in the Orto botanico di Pisa. The source hedges this ('may be'), and no other source was found that discusses the derivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Giacomo Bresadola described the species in 1904. It was listed with 41 others found in Pisa, in an article by Egidio Barsali in the Bollettino della Società Botanica Italiana. Wikipedia also reports that the taxonomy is still somewhat disputed. Mycena oortiana, Mycena olivascens and Mycena vitilis var. olivascens have been treated variously as synonyms or as separate names. MycoBank and Index Fungorum are reported to disagree on where some of these belong. This account rests on a single source cluster (Wikipedia, with iNaturalist as a declared derivative). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species grows on dead deciduous wood, favouring beech and ash, in 'small troops'. It is recorded less frequently on conifers, bracken and Japanese knotweed. Reports from grassland are described there as probably misidentifications of another species. In the British Isles it is found from late summer to autumn, and is more commonly encountered in the south. Elsewhere in Europe its distribution is described as fairly widespread but less common. Wikipedia says it is listed as vulnerable on the Red List of Threatened Fungi in Norway, and that it has also been recorded in Scandinavia, Greenland and Italy. GBIF description records list distributions in Belgium (including its three regions), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. The habitat and season statements come from a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
