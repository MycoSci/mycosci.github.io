## Summary

Lactarius quietus (Fr.) Fr., the oak milkcap, is a member of the Russulaceae, first described by Fries as Agaricus quietus and transferred to Lactarius in 1838. Sources describe it as a brown milkcap with a zoned cap and an oily smell, growing under oak in Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet quietus means "quiet" or "calm" and is possibly used to refer to the unintrusive colours of the cap. This is a single-source, hedged derivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries first named the species Agaricus quietus in the first volume of his Systema Mycologicum (1821) and transferred it to Lactarius in his Epicrisis Systematis Mycologici (1838). Paul Kummer placed it in Galorrheus in 1871 and Otto Kuntze placed it in Lactifluus in 1891; the Lactarius placement is the one now accepted. Wikispecies lists the same basionym and synonyms. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes L. quietus as growing at the base of oak trees, solitarily or in scattered groups, in soil, and as common throughout autumn. It describes the species as ectomycorrhizal with oak, and notes that studies suggest it may also feed saprotrophically. Mushroom World, a separate source, says it is strongly associated with oak, typically in deciduous woodland among leaf litter, fruiting from late summer to autumn. For the variety incanus, Wikipedia reports it as commonly found in eastern North America under oak. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
