## Summary

Lycoperdon perlatum Pers. (1796), family Lycoperdaceae, is known in English as the common puffball, gem-studded puffball, warted puffball and devil's snuff-box. It is recorded in GBIF occurrences from dozens of countries across Europe, North America, Asia, Australasia and South America. Sources describe a whitish fruit body, pear-shaped to cushion-shaped and browning with age. It is covered in firm cone-shaped spines that leave pock-marked scars when they fall off, and it releases spores through a central opening. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet perlatum is Latin for "widespread". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described in 1796 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon and that it is the type species of the genus Lycoperdon. Wikipedia lists as synonyms Lycoperdon gemmatum (Batsch, 1783), L. gemmatum var. perlatum (1829), L. bonordenii (Massee, 1887) and L. perlatum var. bonordenii (Perdeck, 1950). Wikispecies gives the same list of names, and MushroomExpert.Com also treats L. gemmatum as a synonym. None of the supplied sources describes the original material or the reasons for the later transfers. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia notes that the species has been depicted on postage stamps from Guinea, Paraguay, Romania, Sierra Leone and Sweden. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, or in groups or clusters. Wikipedia says it grows on the ground, can also form fairy rings, and typically occurs in woods, grassy areas and along roads. It has been reported from Pinus patula plantations in Tamil Nadu, India. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says it grows in woods under hardwoods or conifers and is also common along roadsides and in urban settings. It gives the season as late spring through fall in temperate regions and also over winter in warm climates, and calls it widely distributed and common in North America. The collections it describes are from Illinois, Colorado and Italy. Mushroom World gives summer to fall, on soil or humus in forests, along roads or trails, or in open areas. GBIF descriptive records give the substrate as soil ("Suelo"), and one record gives "tierra y troncos, en claro de bosques" (soil and logs, in forest clearings). Wikipedia describes it as almost cosmopolitan and reports it from Africa, Asia, Australia, Europe, New Zealand and South America. In North America it ranges from Alaska to Mexico. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, gbif_desc]*
