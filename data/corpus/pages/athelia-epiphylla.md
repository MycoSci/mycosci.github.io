## Habitat and ecology

Wikispecies gives its life habit as facultatively lichenicolous. A 1981 Mycologia paper documents its occurrence on postharvest conifer residue (the title names subalpine fir foliage) under psychrophilic conditions, in close proximity to or under rotten snow pack, in a northern Rocky Mountain coniferous forest in Montana. A 2000 Canadian Journal of Forest Research study of decomposing mountain beech (Nothofagus solandri var. cliffortioides) logs in a New Zealand temperate forest names it, together with Marasmius otagensis, as characterizing decayed logs with relatively high nitrogen. *[wikispecies, lit_1, lit_2]*
