## Summary

Microstoma floccosum (Sacc.) Raitv. 1965 is a cup fungus in the family Sarcoscyphaceae (Pezizales). Sources describe a small, stalked, scarlet goblet- or funnel-shaped fruit body with dense white hairs on the outer surface. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains, and Wikipedia reports collections from the United States, India, China and Japan. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com lists Anthopeziza floccosa, Sarcoscypha floccosa and Plectania floccosa as synonyms, and says the spelling "Microstoma floccosa" is an error in Latin agreement that has been perpetuated in several field guides. Wikipedia reports that a fungus originally described as Microstoma floccosum var. macrosporum was recognized as an independent species in 2000 and renamed M. macrosporum. It differs from M. floccosum in fruiting season, ascus and ascospore size, and the ultrastructure of the hairs. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a saprobic species that grows scattered to clustered, attached to wood that is typically partially buried in the earth, and notes a preference for both oak and Shagbark hickory. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on decaying hardwood sticks and logs (sometimes buried), growing scattered, gregariously, or more often in clusters of 3-6. It gives the season as early summer and summer, a claim that appears in only this one source. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
