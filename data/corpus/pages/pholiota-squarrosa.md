## Summary

Pholiota squarrosa (Vahl) P. Kumm., the shaggy scalycap, is a species in the family Strophariaceae and the type species of the genus Pholiota. Wikipedia describes a cap and stem covered in pointed scales that angle downward and backward, with crowded gills that start yellowish and turn rust-brown. Wikipedia also reports that the mushroom has caused poisonings in people who ate it together with alcohol. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet squarrosa is derived from Latin and means "scurfy". MushroomExpert.Com states that the genus name Pholiota means "scaly" in Greek. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was transferred to the genus Pholiota by the German Paul Kummer, and that it is the type species of the genus. MushroomExpert.Com likewise calls it the type species of Pholiota. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and possibly parasitic, growing in clusters on the wood of hardwoods or conifers, often at the bases of living or dead trees, and especially common on aspens and spruces in the Rocky Mountains. It gives the season as summer and fall and the range as fairly widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia describes it as a secondary parasite on weakened trees and as a saprobe on dead wood. It lists sugar maple, red maple, yellow birch, paper birch, American beech and white ash among deciduous hosts and spruce among conifers. Wikipedia also says it occurs in North America and Europe, and that in the Netherlands it regularly fruits on ancient timber wharves. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
