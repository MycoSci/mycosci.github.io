## Summary

Stropharia melanosperma (Bull.) Gillet, 1878, is a member of the Strophariaceae. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as widespread in North America but not often reported, with a white cap fringed with tooth-like veil remnants, whitish-then-gray gills, a white ring with a lined upper edge, and fairly large spores with a slightly truncated end. GBIF's description record lists a distribution of Brazil, Norway and Sweden. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing scattered to gregariously in grassy areas, usually in parks, lawns, gardens, landscaping areas and waste places, and widely reported in North America; the collections it illustrates and describes are from Illinois. The same source says that, judging from field guides and herbarium records, it is more common in the southeast and in Kansas and Oklahoma than elsewhere on the continent. This is a single-source account. A GBIF description record separately lists a distribution of BR, NO and SE. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
