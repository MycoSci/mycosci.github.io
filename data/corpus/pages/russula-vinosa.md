## Summary

Russula vinosa Lindblad (1901) is a member of the Russulaceae, known in English as the darkening brittlegill. The type locality is Sweden. Wikipedia describes it as a coniferous-woodland species of Europe and North America that fruits in summer and early autumn. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia says the epithet "vinosa" derives from the Latin vinum, "wine". It says this likely alludes to the wine-coloured cap, which can act as a dye. This is a single-source statement and is hedged as "likely" in the source. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that in Sweden, where its edibility is noted, the species is known as "Vinkremla", alluding to its wine colour. This is the only source for the name's origin. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the species was originally described in the Swedish guide Svampbok ("Mushroom Book"), written by M. A. Lindblad and published in 1901. It says Romel, who came up with the synonymous name Russula obscura, was an editor of the 1913 release of that text. Mushroom World also gives Russula obscura as an alternative name. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia places it in coniferous woodlands and stands in Europe and North America in summer and early autumn. It names Great Britain, Southern Europe, New England and Fennoscandia as places where it is known. It says the species is usually understood to have a symbiotic relationship with evergreen tree roots, except in mountainous areas, where it has occasionally associated with birches. Mushroom World independently says it is "mostly found with pine". GBIF lists distribution records for Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, gbif_desc]*
