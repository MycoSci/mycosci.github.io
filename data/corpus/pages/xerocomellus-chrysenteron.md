## Summary

Xerocomellus chrysenteron (Bull.) Šutara, 2008, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the red cracking bolete. Wikipedia describes it as a small mushroom with tubes and pores rather than gills beneath the cap. MushroomExpert.Com cautions that the name as used by eastern North American authors refers to a different, probably unnamed species, and that the true European species differs from it. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet chrysenteron comes from the Ancient Greek words khrysos "gold" and enteron "innards". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard first described the mushroom as Boletus communis in 1789, and in 1791 gave it the epithet chrysenteron. Lucien Quélet placed it in the new genus Xerocomus in 1888, keeping the epithet chrysenteron. In 1985 Marcel Bon revived the epithet communis, giving Xerocomus communis. Wikipedia says phylogenetic analysis supports its placement as the type species of the genus Xerocomellus, described by Šutara in 2008. Wikispecies lists the same synonyms and dates. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it grows singly or in small groups in hardwood/conifer woods from early summer to mid-winter. It describes the species as mycorrhizal with hardwood trees, often beech on well-drained soils. Wikipedia also says it has been recorded in Taiwan and introduced to New Zealand, where it grows in groups under introduced deciduous trees. Mushroom World says it grows with deciduous trees on well-drained, humus-rich soil. MushroomExpert.Com describes the eastern North American concept rather than the European species: mycorrhizal with hardwoods, especially oaks, and sometimes reported with conifers, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall, widely distributed from the Great Plains eastward in the United States. The same page states that the true European Xerocomellus chrysenteron is associated primarily with conifers. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, mushroomexpert]*
