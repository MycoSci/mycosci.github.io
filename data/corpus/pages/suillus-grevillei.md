## Summary

Suillus grevillei (Klotzsch) Singer, 1945, is a bolete in the family Suillaceae, commonly called Greville's bolete or larch bolete. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a European species associated with larch trees, characterized by a sticky yellow cap and a whitish, flimsy ring on the stem. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from Robert Kaye Greville. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states the species was originally described from Great Britain. It cites the original authority as J. F. Klotzsch, 1832, and the combination in Suillus as R. Singer, 1945. MushroomExpert.Com and Wikipedia both refer to a 1992 study (Barua et al.) that used molecular evidence to show the secotioid, truffle-like Gastrosuillus laricinus to be a derivative of S. grevillei. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as mycorrhizal with larch (Larix), especially European larch, growing gregariously on the ground in summer and fall. It calls the species widespread in Europe and occasional in North America, especially with planted European larch, and reports it from New Zealand. It adds that the extent of its actual occurrence in North America is undetermined. Wikipedia states that it grows only under larch trees, gives a season of July–November, and describes it as widespread in North America and Europe, with a record from Taiwan. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
