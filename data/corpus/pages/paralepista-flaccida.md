## Summary

Paralepista flaccida (Sowerby) Vizzini, 2012, is a species in the family Tricholomataceae, known in English as the tawny funnel cap. Wikipedia describes it as found across the Northern Hemisphere and known to form fairy rings. For a long time it was treated as two species, a 'flaccida' form associated with broad-leaved trees and an 'inversa' form associated with conifers, which modern authorities merge into one. *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the earliest description was by Giovanni Antonio Scopoli in 1772 as Agaricus inversus in Flora Carniolica. In 1799 James Sowerby described it as Agaricus flaccidus in Coloured Figures of English Fungi or Mushrooms. Later combinations were Clitocybe flaccida (Kummer, 1871), Clitocybe inversa (Quélet, 1872), and Lepista flaccida and Lepista inversa (Patouillard, 1887), followed by long-standing confusion over whether Clitocybe or Lepista was the appropriate genus. Jörg H. Raithelhuber had proposed the name Paralepista in 1981, but it was accepted only as a subgenus. In 2012 Alfredo Vizzini published a molecular analysis with a phylogram showing that these mushrooms and Lepista gilva form a clade separate from other Lepista and from Clitocybe species. He adopted Paralepista at genus level. Older references generally listed the flaccida and inversa forms as separate species. This account comes from a single encyclopedia source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes P. flaccida as a saprobe growing on humus-rich soil, compost or conifer needles from summer to autumn. The flaccida form is reported from deciduous woods and the inversa form from under conifers. Many fruit bodies often appear together, and it may form fairy rings. The same source says it is most frequent and common in Europe, also occurs in the U.S., and has been reported from Mexico and Australia. A GBIF description record lists distribution as DK, NO, SE and Vermont, with no further ecological text. The ecological details rest on a single independent source cluster. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
