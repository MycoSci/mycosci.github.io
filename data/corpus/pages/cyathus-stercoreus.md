## Summary

Cyathus stercoreus (Schwein.) De Toni, 1888, is a bird's nest fungus in the family Agaricaceae, with occurrence records from many countries. Its fruiting bodies are small goblet- or funnel-shaped cups, roughly 1 cm tall, holding tiny blackish 'eggs' (peridioles); Wikipedia calls the cups 'splash cups' that use falling water drops to eject the peridioles. MushroomExpert.Com separates it from the similar Cyathus striatus by its smooth rather than lined inner surface. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Sources give two different accounts of the epithet. Wikipedia states that it derives from the Latin word stercorarius, meaning 'of dung'. Michael Kuo (MushroomExpert.Com) writes that his Latin dictionary translates stercoreus as 'filth', and links the name to the fungus's association with dung. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as coprophilous, growing on dung, in soil containing dung, and at bonfire sites, and says it has also been recorded on sand dunes. It quotes Curtis Gates Lloyd as writing that the fungus 'probably occurs in every country where manure occurs'. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic and gregarious or in dense clusters, on wood chips, organic debris (straw, sawdust and so on), manured soil, or dung. It fruits in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates or greenhouses, and Kuo says it is widely distributed in North America and just as likely to be found on wood chips or organic debris as on dung. A GBIF description record gives the substrate of one record as horse dung ('escremento de caballo') and lists distribution records including Belgium, Brazil, Chile (Maule Region), Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Slovenia and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
