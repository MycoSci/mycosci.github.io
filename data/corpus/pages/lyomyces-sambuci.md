## Summary

Lyomyces sambuci (Pers.) P. Karst. 1882 is a member of the Corticiaceae, known in English as the elder whitewash. Wikipedia describes it as a resupinate, very thin, white, pruinose or chalky-looking crust on deadwood, especially elder. The only other fetched descriptive text is a GBIF distribution stub. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (under the name Hyphodontia sambuci) describes it as a fungus on deadwood, especially elder, and says that in northern Europe it occurs mostly on Sambucus nigra. It says a much wider range of substrates is used in warmer, more southern regions. The same source lists dead but still-hanging branches of Fraxinus, Berberis, Nothofagus, Ulmus, Populus, Hedera, Ribes and Symphoricarpos, and rarely conifers such as Cryptomeria. It says the fruit body often grows on dead branches of trees and bushes that are still attached to the tree. GBIF's description record lists only the distribution entries DK, NO and Vermont. This is single-source information. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
