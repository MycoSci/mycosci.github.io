## Summary

Russula virescens (Schaeff.) Fr. is a member of the Russulaceae, with GBIF occurrence records from many countries. Wikipedia describes it as having a pale green cap up to 15 cm across, covered with darker green angular patches, with crowded white gills and a firm white stipe. MushroomExpert.Com reports that the traditional concept of the species has proven inaccurate, and that R. virescens in the strict sense is European. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet virescens is Latin for "becoming green". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the characteristic pattern of the cap surface earned the species common names such as green-cracking russula, quilted green russula and green brittlegill, and notes that "green brittlegill" usually refers to Russula aeruginea. It also says the species is known locally as the "moldy russula" in the mid-Atlantic United States. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Jacob Christian Schaeffer first described the species in 1774 as Agaricus virescens, and that Elias Fries transferred it to Russula in 1836. It also reports that under Rolf Singer's 1986 classification, R. virescens is the type species of subsection Virescentinae in section Rigidae. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says the traditional concept, covering Europe and North America, has turned out to be inaccurate. It treats R. virescens in the strict sense as European, and describes North American material as a complex of mostly unpublished DNA-defined species, of which Russula parvovirescens is one that can be fairly easily recognized. Wikipedia describes the species' North American distribution as subject to debate. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that R. virescens fruits on soil in deciduous and mixed forests, forming ectomycorrhizal associations with trees including oaks (Quercus), European beech (Fagus sylvatica) and aspen (Populus tremula). Fruit bodies may appear singly or in groups and may reappear in the same spots year after year. In Europe, fruiting occurs mainly from summer to early autumn. Wikipedia also describes preliminary investigations suggesting an association with at least ten species of Dipterocarpaceae in Southeast Asia. These habitat details rest on a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
