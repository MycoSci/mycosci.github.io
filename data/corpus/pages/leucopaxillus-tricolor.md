## Summary

Leucopaxillus tricolor (Peck) Kühner, 1926 is a member of the family Tricholomataceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a brownish cap, pale yellow gills and a stout white stem, and says its odor is strong and foul, reminiscent of coal tar. That page describes an illustrated and described collection from Kentucky. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com's Michael Kuo presumes that the epithet tricolor refers to the three colors of the brownish cap, pale yellow gills and stout white stem. This is a single-source presumption, not a documented derivation. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com credits the species to Charles Peck (1888), and the combination in Leucopaxillus to Kühner (1926). It quotes Peck as calling it "[r]emarkable for its varied colors and for the peculiar hue assumed by the lamellae in the dried state". The page says the gills turn from pale yellow to pale purplish brown or pinkish when dried. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, decomposing the litter of hardwoods, growing gregariously in summer and fall. It is described as widely distributed east of the Great Plains but apparently more common in the middle southeast. The described collection is from Kentucky. Separately, the GBIF description record lists distribution entries for Belgium (Flemish Region), Denmark, Norway and Sweden, without habitat detail. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
