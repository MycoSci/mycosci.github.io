## Summary

Chrysomphalina chrysophylla (Fr.) Clémençon is a mushroom in the family Hygrophoraceae, described by Elias Magnus Fries in 1821 and placed in the genus Chrysomphalina by Heinz Clémençon in 1982. Wikipedia gives it a north temperate distribution and describes yellow caps 1–5 cm wide on stems 1–4 cm long, with a yellowish spore print. MushroomExpert.Com describes yellow gills that run down the stem and a finely scaly brown cap. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Fries described the species in 1821 and that Heinz Clémençon placed it in Chrysomphalina in 1982. MushroomExpert.Com lists Gerronema chrysophylla and Omphalina chrysophylla as synonyms. It also reports that recent DNA studies support a fairly close relationship to the waxy caps, despite the species sharing virtually none of the features that used to define them. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Single-source account: MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a saprobic decomposer of well decayed conifer wood. It grows alone, scattered, or more often gregariously, in summer and fall. The same source calls it fairly widely distributed but not common in North America. Wikipedia gives only a north temperate distribution. The GBIF description record lists the distribution as DK; NO; SE; Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
