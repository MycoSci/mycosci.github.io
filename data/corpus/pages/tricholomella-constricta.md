## Summary

Tricholomella constricta (Fr.) Zerova ex Kalamees, 1992, is a species in the family Lyophyllaceae, known in English as the Mealy Domecap. Wikipedia describes the genus Tricholomella as monotypic, with this as its only species. Wikipedia also describes the fruiting body as a white, fleshy, tricholomatoid mushroom with a cap 3–6 cm across. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was described as new to science in 1979 by the Ukrainian mycologist Mariya Yakovlevna Zerova. Her original publication was invalid, and Kuulo Kalamees later republished it validly in 1992. Wikipedia adds that the genus's taxonomic placement has been contested. The disputed point is whether the basidia carry siderophilous (iron-staining) granulation. That character would suggest Lyophyllum or Calocybe, but Zerova denied its presence in Tricholomella. Wikipedia also says some mycologists regard the character as unreliable. This account comes from a single independent source (Wikipedia; the iNaturalist text is a copy of its introduction). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species inhabits deciduous and mixed forests, steppes, meadows and pastures throughout Eastern Europe and Asia, fruiting from June to October, and calls it rare. It lists records from Estonia (seven localities), Latvia, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, and Russia (Kirov Oblast, Leningrad Oblast and Primorsky Krai).
