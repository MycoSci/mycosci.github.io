## Summary

Multiclavula mucida (Pers.) R.H. Petersen, 1967 is a species in the family Hydnaceae, first described as Clavaria mucida by Persoon in 1797. Wikipedia describes it as a basidiolichen, a fungus that lives in association with green algae, typically on decaying wood. It has been recorded in many countries across Europe, the Americas, Asia and Oceania. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet mucida comes from the Latin mucidum, meaning "mouldy" or "slimy", and that the genus name Multiclavula means "many clubs". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Christiaan Hendrik Persoon first formally described the fungus in 1797 as Clavaria mucida. It was later proposed for transfer to Typhula (Elias Fries, 1827), Calocera (Richard Wettstein, 1886), Pistillaria (Costantin and Dufour, 1891), Lentaria (E. J. H. Corner, 1950) and Stichoclavaria (Eckehard Paechnatz, 1987). Ron Petersen transferred it to the newly circumscribed genus Multiclavula in 1967, after finding that several species in Clavulinopsis and Lentaria could be congeneric. Wikispecies independently records the basionym Clavaria mucida (1797) and the Lentaria (1950), Stichoclavaria (1987) and Petersen (1967) combinations. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that the species is commonly found on decomposing wood, often in the presence of algae, on a wide variety of substrates including bamboo, beech, cedar, poplar and oak. It says coniferous wood is a particularly favoured substrate in Central Europe. It also relays Persoon's original note that the fungus appeared in autumn on decaying, moist wood. Wikipedia describes the species as rare in Europe and, in Estonia, an indicator of old-growth forest. All of this comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*
