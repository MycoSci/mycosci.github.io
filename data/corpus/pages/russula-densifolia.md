## Summary

Russula densifolia Secr. ex Gillet (1876) is a species of Russulaceae with a type locality in France. Wikipedia describes robust, squat fruit bodies with caps up to 14.5 cm across. The flesh stains red and then black when bruised. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a blushing russula, and notes that this group of Russula badly needs revision. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet refers to the closely spaced gills. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Louis Secretan first described the species in 1833 as Agaricus adustus var. densifolius, and that Claude-Casimir Gillet transferred it to Russula in 1876. Wikipedia also reports that Robert Shaffer defined four forms in a 1962 monograph, and that Index Fungorum treats these forms as synonyms. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the mushroom is sold as an edible species in Phayao and Chiang Mai Provinces in northern Thailand, and that it is used in traditional Chinese medicine for its purported antirheumatic activity. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows on the ground, singly, scattered or in groups, in mixed and deciduous forests, and tends to appear in summer and autumn. It describes the species as mycorrhizal, and reports it with oak in Mexico and in dune pine forests in Spain. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers, fruiting in summer and fall (also over winter in warmer climates), and widely distributed in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
