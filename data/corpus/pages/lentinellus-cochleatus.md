## Summary

Lentinellus cochleatus (Pers.) P. Karst. (1879) is a species in the family Auriscalpiaceae (order Russulales), known in English as the aniseed cockleshell. It is a wood-inhabiting fungus; MushroomExpert.com describes funnel-shaped to folded caps on well-developed stems, with saw-toothed gills and a white spore print. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.com both describe an anise-like odor, though MushroomExpert.com notes the odor can also be not distinctive. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.com describes it as saprobic, growing in clusters on the wood of hardwoods, in summer and fall. It says the species is distributed in Europe and North America, especially in northern and montane areas but also in temperate zones. That description is based on a collection from Michigan. Wikipedia says it is widespread in Britain and the rest of Europe, on broadleaf wood, from July to December. GBIF's description record lists distribution entries for Belgium (Flemish and Walloon regions), Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
