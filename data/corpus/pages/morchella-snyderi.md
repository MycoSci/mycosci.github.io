## Summary

Morchella snyderi M. Kuo & Methven (2012) is a morel in the family Morchellaceae, with its type locality in Idaho. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as one of western North America's "natural" morels, meaning it appears in non-burned forests. Its cap ridges are yellowish when young and usually darken to brown or black with maturity or on drying. The stem usually becomes ridged and pocketed as it matures. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

The specific epithet honors Leon C. Snyder, who described similar morels in Washington state in the 1930s. Michael Kuo says he chose the epithet so that Snyder's work on this species would not be forgotten. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Morchella snyderi was described as new to science in 2012 in the journal Mycologia, together with 13 other morels from the United States and Canada. The study came out of the Morel Data Collection Project. It had earlier been identified as phylogenetic species Mel-12 in a 2011 study. According to Michael Kuo, a coauthor of the description, the species should have been named Morchella crassistipa, a name published by Snyder in 1938 from Washington collections. DNA testing of Snyder's type collection showed at least two separate phylogenetic species within it, so that name was designated doubtful. One of the specimens Snyder saved as a type matched the mushroom later named M. snyderi. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Fruit bodies are reported growing alone, scattered, or in groups on the ground under conifers, including Douglas fir (Pseudotsuga menziesii), ponderosa pine (Pinus ponderosa) and white fir (Abies concolor). Fruiting is reported from April to June. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a "natural" morel of non-burned forests and lists it as documented from California, Idaho, Montana, Oregon and Washington. Wikipedia additionally lists New Mexico and Arizona. Both sources say it is possibly (Wikipedia: "suspected") saprobic and mycorrhizal at different points in its life cycle. Wikipedia alone also reports that M. snyderi, identified as phylogenetic species Mel-12, has been shown to colonize the non-native grass Bromus tectorum (cheatgrass) as an endophyte. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
