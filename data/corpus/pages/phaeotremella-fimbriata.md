## Summary

Phaeotremella fimbriata (Pers.) Spirin & Malysheva, 2018, is a species in the family Tremellaceae, with the basionym Tremella fimbriata. Wikipedia describes it as producing blackish, frondose, gelatinous fruit bodies up to 4 cm across, and as a parasite of the mycelium of the fungus Stereum rugosum. GBIF's description record lists distribution entries for Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Tremella fimbriata was first published in 1800 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon, together with Tremella foliacea. Most later authors treated the two as synonyms, usually preferring T. foliacea. Molecular work based on DNA sequences showed that Tremella foliacea sensu lato covered several similar but distinct species, and the name Phaeotremella fimbriata was then recognized in its current sense. This account comes from a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that P. fimbriata is a parasite of Stereum rugosum, growing on the host's hyphae in the wood rather than on the host's fruit bodies. It says that, following its host, the fruit bodies are typically found on dead, attached or recently fallen branches of broad-leaved trees, particularly alder. This is a single-source description (Wikipedia). GBIF's description record lists distribution entries for Norway, Sweden and Vermont, with no habitat text. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
