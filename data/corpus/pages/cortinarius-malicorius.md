## Summary

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having orange young gills, and says identification also requires young specimens and microscopic confirmation of its small spores. *[gbif_desc, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, what is now called Cortinarius malicorius once covered two species. Cortinarius croceofolius (also spelled croceifolius) was described by the American mycologist Charles Peck in 1911. It lacked the olive colorations on the cap and in the flesh that the European C. malicorius shows, and it was treated as distinct. The two have since been proclaimed synonymous, and the older name prevails. The author of that page expects this to change once DNA studies are done; this is a single-source opinion. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (single source) describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially pines. It grows scattered or gregariously, often in wet areas or with sphagnum. Fruiting is in fall, and in winter on the West Coast. That source calls it widely distributed in North America but more common in the Northeast. *[mushroomexpert]*
