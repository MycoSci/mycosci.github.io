## Summary

Infundibulicybe squamulosa (Pers.) Harmaja, published in 2003 in Index of Fungi 7: 634, is a species in the family Tricholomataceae (order Agaricales). MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a clitocyboid mushroom that appears under conifers, with a vase-shaped, orange-brown cap and stem and pale gills. The same source describes it as primarily northern and montane in North America. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com gives the name references as C. H. Persoon, 1801 and H. Harmaja, 2003, and lists Clitocybe squamulosa as a synonym. It also reports that Bigelow (1985) named two western North American varieties, var. montana and var. sicca, separated on spore morphology. Bigelow thought the typical variety was limited to northeastern North America and the upper Midwest. According to the same source, it is unclear whether these varieties can be applied in a contemporary, DNA-informed setting. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as saprobic, growing scattered or gregariously under conifers on moss or in needle duff, from spring through fall. It says the species was originally described from Europe, where it is widespread but more common in northern and montane regions, and that in North America it is also primarily northern and montane. The illustrated and described collections on that page are from Colorado. GBIF's distribution entries list Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
