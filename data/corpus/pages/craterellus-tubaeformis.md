## Summary

Craterellus tubaeformis (Fr.) Quél. (1888) is a species in the family Hydnaceae, known in English as the Funnel Chanterelle, Trumpet Chanterelle and Winter Chanterelle, and recorded from dozens of countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small chanterelle with a brown cap, well developed false gills that often develop grayish shades, and a yellow stem with a waxy surface. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com quotes Redhead and collaborators (2002), who state that the spelling tubaeformis should not be changed to tubiformis because 'tuba' refers to a trumpet shape and 'tubus' to a cylindrical tube. This is the only source that discusses the derivation of the name. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the species is found in great numbers late in the mushroom season, and that this earned it the common name 'winter mushroom'. This is a single-source account and covers only that one common name. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the species was reclassified from Cantharellus to Craterellus, a move supported by molecular phylogenetics. MushroomExpert.Com credits the 2000 LSU-gene study by Dahlman and collaborators with showing that tubaeformis belongs among the Craterellus species rather than in Cantharellus. Wikipedia reports that molecular work indicates two genetic populations traditionally called tubaeformis, one in Europe and eastern North America and one in western North America. It says that if they were split, the eastern one would keep the name tubaeformis because the type specimens came from Sweden. Wikipedia adds that in 2017 scientists from the Oregon Biodiversity Information Center suggested the name Craterellus neotubaeformis for the western form but had not yet written the paper. MushroomExpert.Com treats the West Coast form as a separate, as-yet unnamed species (its 'Craterellus species 02'). It also notes that a later ITS-based study (Guo-ying and collaborators, 2011) complicated the picture when eastern North American specimens were added, and that further systematic study is needed. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com states that it is mycorrhizal with conifers and grows alone, gregariously, or in loose clusters in moss or on well-decayed, moss-covered logs in conifer bogs. It gives the range as northern and montane North America and also northern and montane Europe, and the season as fall. Wikipedia says it is most common in forests with much well-rotted coarse woody debris, growing on moss or rotten wood, and in North America mostly in conifer bogs. Wikipedia also says that in the Pacific Northwest it can be found from November to January, and that the western North American form has been shown to form ectomycorrhizal associations with western hemlock (Tsuga heterophylla) and Douglas-fir (Pseudotsuga menziesii). Mushroom World likewise says it grows on moss and is mostly found in conifer bogs. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
