## Summary

Hydnum oregonense is a tooth fungus in the family Hydnaceae, described in 2018 by Norvell, Liimatainen & Niskanen from Oregon, and recorded in the US and Canada. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as relatively small and slender, with a 2–4 cm pale orange cap that often develops a small central "navel", and with round, distinctively large spores. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, western North American authors had treated this fungus as "Hydnum umbilicatum", because it appeared to match Peck's 1902 description of a small species with a "small but usually deep and distinct umbilicus". Peck's species was collected in the Adirondacks and has smaller spores (averaging 8.4 x 8.0 µm). Norvell and collaborators (in Niskanen 2018) therefore described Hydnum oregonense as a separate species. This account comes from a single source. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, including Douglas-fir, Bishop pine and western hemlock. It grows gregariously on the ground, often near stumps and decaying logs, in winter and spring, and is distributed from northern California to Alaska. The illustrated collection is from California. Wikipedia separately states that it grows near the west coast of North America from November to March under conifer and tan oak trees. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
