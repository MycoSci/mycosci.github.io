## Summary

Irpex lacteus (Fr.) Fr. is a crust fungus in the family Irpicaceae and the type species of the genus Irpex. Wikipedia describes it as common and distributed throughout temperate areas of the world. It is considered a polypore, but depending on growth conditions it can also produce a hydnoid (toothed) hymenophore. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Elias Magnus Fries first described the fungus in 1818 as Sistotrema lacteum, and made it the type species of the genus Irpex in 1828. The same source attributes the extensive synonymy to the species' variability and abundance, which led to it being described as new to science numerous times. This account comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a white-rot fungus that inhabits mainly angiosperm branches and trunks, and as one of the most common wood-rotting fungi in urban North America. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, spreading across the bottoms and sides of fallen hardwood logs, occasionally on conifer wood, causing a white rot of the sapwood and rarely the heartwood, and occasionally reported as parasitic on living cherry wood. The same source says it is annual, appears year-round, and is widely distributed in North America but rare or absent in the Southwest. GBIF description records list specimens from Yunnan Province, China, on angiosperm stumps, fallen trunks and branches, and dead bamboo, at an altitude given as 2000-2600 (units not stated). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
