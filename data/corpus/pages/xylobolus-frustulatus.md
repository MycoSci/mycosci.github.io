## Summary

Xylobolus frustulatus (Pers.) P. Karst., the ceramic parchment or ceramic fungus, is a crust fungus in the family Stereaceae. Its fruit body forms hard, flat, cracked patches that resemble pieces of ceramic tile. Wikipedia describes the species as inedible. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Persoon first described the species in 1801 as Thelephora frustulata, and it was later moved among several genera, including Auricularia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a saprobic species that grows only on well-rotted oak wood, mostly on branches and trunks lying on the ground and more rarely on attached branches. It gives the range as northern Europe and North America, with records from Asia including Japan and Taiwan. Wikipedia says sporulation generally occurs in summer, and that most autumn and early-spring specimens are sterile. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on dry, well-decayed wood of oaks, and rarely on other hardwoods. It says the fungus grows in dense patches, causes a white pocket rot, and fruits in spring, summer, fall and winter. Kuo gives the range as eastern North America, Texas and Mexico. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
