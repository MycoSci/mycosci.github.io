## Summary

Tricholoma argyraceum (Bull.) Gillet, 1874, is a grey-capped mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae. Wikipedia reports it is often confused with the similar-looking Tricholoma scalpturatum. MushroomExpert.Com describes a radially fibrillose, brownish gray cap, a mealy odor, and very narrow spores under the microscope. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Pierre Bulliard described the species as Agaricus argyraceus in 1779, and that Claude Casimir Gillet gave it its current name in 1874. MushroomExpert.Com gives the basionym date as 1789 instead. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states the species occurs across Europe but is uncommon overall, with fruit bodies appearing from June to December (occasionally earlier in spring), and that it is ectomycorrhizal with birch (Betula), Carpinus, oak (Quercus) and Tilia. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in late spring and summer in Europe. Its North American distribution is described there as uncertain, with recorded collections from Midwestern and eastern hardwood forests and from cottonwood riverbottoms in western mountains; the illustrated collections are from Illinois. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
