## Summary

Clitopilus prunulus (Scop.) P. Kumm. (1871) is a member of the Entolomataceae, known in English as the miller and the sweetbread mushroom. Wikipedia describes it as having a grey to white cap, decurrent gills and pink spores, and MushroomExpert.Com gives a brownish pink spore print and a strongly mealy odor. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the common names 'the miller' and 'sweetbread mushroom' are derived from the mushroom's distinctive smell. MushroomExpert.Com likewise calls them references to its mealy odor, and adds that the smell is sometimes compared to that of an old grain mill. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Giovanni Antonio Scopoli described the species as Agaricus prunulus in 1772, and Pierre Bulliard called it Agaricus orcella in 1793. Paul Kummer erected the genus Clitopilus and gave the species its current name in 1871, and C. prunulus is the type species of the genus. Wikipedia also reports that populations from Yunnan and Taiwan previously considered consistent with C. prunulus were described as a separate species, Clitopilus amygdaliformis, in 2007. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows on the ground in hardwood and coniferous woods in summer and autumn. It also describes the species as found in grasslands in Europe and North America, growing solitary to gregarious in open areas of conifer/hardwood forests, common under Bishop pine (Pinus muricata) along the coast north of San Francisco and fruiting shortly after the fall rains. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered or gregariously under hardwoods or conifers in grassy areas and open woods, in summer and fall (or in winter in warmer climates), and apparently widely distributed in North America. Mushroom World, an aggregator, describes it as saprotrophic and commonly found in deciduous forests among oaks, beeches and birches; this is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
