## Summary

Lactarius aspideus (Fr.) Fr. 1838 is a milkcap in the family Russulaceae, with occurrence records from many countries. The two independent descriptive sources that fit this name (MushroomExpert.Com and a GBIF description) describe a whitish to pale yellow or straw-yellow milkcap whose milk stains tissues lilac and which grows with willows (Salix). *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the name Lactarius aspideus has been applied in a few North American contexts, but that the species probably does not occur on that continent. It says a 1907 Vermont description (Burlingham) belongs to the North American species Lactarius aspideoides, which is morphologically very similar but has a sticky stem surface and a bitter taste. Kuo adds that later North American authors applied the name aspideoides to varied similar collections with little attention to mycorrhizal tree associations, and that molecular and ecological study will be needed before names in this group can be applied with confidence. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with willows, often in wet, grassy areas and near lakes, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall. It calls the species widely distributed in Europe but not common. The illustrated collection came from Italy's lake district. A GBIF description, which does not name the area it covers, reports fruiting from the end of June to October with Salix, in grass or on naked soil in humid localities, often at lake shores. It calls the species widely distributed but rather uncommon. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
