## Summary

Stereum sanguinolentum (Alb. & Schwein.) Fr. is a fungus in the family Stereaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a plant pathogen that causes red heart rot on conifers, and its fruit bodies as a thin leathery crust on wood that exudes a red liquid when injured. It is recorded from many countries in Europe, North America and elsewhere. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that fresh fruit bodies bleed a red-colored liquid when injured, and that this is reflected in the common names "bleeding Stereum" and "bleeding conifer parchment". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described scientifically by Albertini and Schweinitz in 1805 as Thelephora sanguinolenta. It lists Phlebomorpha, Auricularia, Merulius and Haematostereum among the other genera to which it has been transferred. This account comes from a single source, and it gives no reasons for the transfers. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that fruit bodies are produced on dead wood or on dead branches of living trees. It states that the fungus causes red heart rot on conifers, particularly spruces and Douglas-firs, and has also been recorded on balsam fir, Douglas fir and western hemlock. It says the fungus enters hosts through open wounds caused by mechanical damage or grazing wildlife. It also says the fungus has been recorded from North America, Europe, east Africa, New Zealand and Australia. These statements come from one independent source (Wikipedia); the iNaturalist text is a derivative of it. *[wikipedia]*
