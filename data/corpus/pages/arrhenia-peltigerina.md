## Summary

Arrhenia peltigerina (Peck) Redhead, Lutzoni, Moncalvo & Vilgalys (2002) is placed in the family Hygrophoraceae. Wikispecies lists it as a lichenicolous fungus growing on Peltigera lichens. A 2024 phylogenetic study found that material identified under this name falls into a complex of seven divergent clades, and reports that collections are often misidentified. *[wikispecies, lit_1]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Agaricus peltigerinus Peck, 1878. It was later transferred to Clitocybe by Saccardo (1887), to Omphalina by Collin (1994), and to Arrhenia by Redhead, Lutzoni, Moncalvo & Vilgalys (2002). Omphalina cupulatoides P.D.Orton (1976) is listed as a synonym. A 2024 study reports that the type specimens of Agaricus peltigerinus and Omphalina cupulatoides fell into separate clades, and that the latter was recombined as Arrhenia cupulatoides. The same study described four new species from the complex: Arr. baltica, Arr. fennoscandica, Arr. mohniensis and Arr. talpoides. A fifth clade, represented by a single specimen, was left formally undescribed. In that study Arr. peltigerina in the narrow sense was found only in North America. *[wikispecies, lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikispecies gives the life habit as lichenicolous, a parasite on living lichens, on Peltigera species. A 2024 study of the A. peltigerina complex reports that all but one collection came from soil over calcareous bedrock. The Peltigera host was P. hymenina for Arr. cupulatoides and P. rufescens for Arr. baltica, and varied for the other segregate species; the study does not give a host for Arr. peltigerina in the narrow sense. GBIF's distribution entry for the species lists Denmark and Norway. A 2017 species list for the Yuganskiy Nature Reserve (Western Siberia) records the name there and reports it as one of three species in that reserve listed in the Red Data Book of Khanty-Mansi Autonomous Okrug – Yugra. These regional records use the name in its broad sense and may include other species from the complex. *[wikispecies, lit_1, gbif_desc, lit_2]*
