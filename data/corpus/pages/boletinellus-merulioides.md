## Summary

Boletinellus merulioides (Schwein.) Murrill, 1909, the ash-tree bolete, is a bolete in the family Boletinellaceae. It was first described as Daedalea merulioides by Schweinitz in 1832. Sources place it on the ground near ash trees in eastern North America, with a described association with a leafcurl ash aphid. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Lewis David de Schweinitz first described the species as Daedalea merulioides in 1832, from collections made in Salem, and that William Alphonso Murrill transferred it to Boletinellus in 1909. It also states that Rolf Singer placed it in Gyrodon, though it is not closely related to that genus genetically. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says the species has been moved among Boletinellus, Gyrodon, Boletinus and Boletus, and that the most current data it cites (Nuhn and collaborators, 2013) supports Boletinellus, in a basal position to the Boletinae. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia, the only source to mention any human use, states that the fruit bodies can be used in mushroom dyeing, giving light brown or dark orangish brown colors depending on the mordant. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the fruit bodies grow on the ground, scattered or in groups, almost always near ash (Fraxinus spp.) and rarely near maple and white pine. It describes the range as eastern North America, from eastern Canada south to Alabama and west to Wisconsin, and also Mexico, with reports from Japan. It gives the fruiting season as summer and autumn. MushroomExpert.Com independently describes it under green ash and other ash trees, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall, and widely distributed in eastern North America (perhaps also the southwest). Both sources describe an association with an ash-feeding aphid. Wikipedia calls it a mutualism in which the aphid shelters in hollow sclerotia formed by the fungus. MushroomExpert.Com says the fungus is not actually mycorrhizal and is 'probably' in symbiosis with the leafcurl ash aphid, Meliarhizophagus fraxinifolii. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
