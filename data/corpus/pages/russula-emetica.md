## Summary

Russula emetica (Schaeff.) Pers., 1796, in the family Russulaceae, is commonly called the sickener, emetic russula or vomiting russula. Wikipedia describes it as the type species of the genus Russula, with a red cap and white gills and stem. It is recorded across the Northern Hemisphere, and Wikipedia notes that many similar red-capped russulas exist, some separable only by microscopy. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Ancient Greek emetikos, meaning 'emetic' or 'vomit-inducing'. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the common names (sickener, emetic russula, vomiting russula) refer to the gastrointestinal distress the mushroom causes when consumed raw. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, it was first described as Agaricus emeticus by Jacob Christian Schaeffer in 1774, in his series on the fungi of Bavaria and the Palatinate. Christian Hendrik Persoon placed it in Russula in 1796. Wikipedia also reports Agaricus russula Scopoli (1772) as a synonym, but says that name is unavailable because Persoon's name is sanctioned. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the mushroom used to be widely eaten in Russia and eastern European countries after parboiling and then salting or pickling, and that in some regions of Hungary and Slovakia the cap cuticle is removed and used as a spice for goulash. The same source says consumption is not recommended. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as mycorrhizal, with conifers (especially pines) as preferred hosts. Fruit bodies grow singly, scattered or in groups in sphagnum moss near bogs and in coniferous and mixed forests, and occasionally on humus or very rotten wood. It fruits from late summer until early winter. A Scottish Scots pine plantation study cited there found productivity highest from August to October. Wikipedia says it is known from North Africa, Asia and Europe, and that its range in North America is uncertain because some sightings refer to R. silvicola. Sightings in Australia are now referred to R. persanguinea. Mushroom World independently says it is found mainly beneath conifers (pines and spruces in particular) and occasionally on mossy, fairly wet heathland. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
