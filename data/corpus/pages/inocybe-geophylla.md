## Summary

Inocybe geophylla (Bull.) P. Kumm. 1871, the White Fibrecap, is a small white to cream mushroom in the family Inocybaceae with a fibrous, silky, umbonate cap and adnexed gills. A lilac variety, lilacina, is also known. Wikipedia describes the species as poisonous, and it is recorded across Europe and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Ancient Greek geo- 'earth' and phyllon 'leaf'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was given its current binomial name in 1871 by Paul Kummer. The lilac form, var. lilacina, was originally described as Agaricus geophyllus var. lilacinus by Charles Horton Peck in 1872 from Bethlehem, New York, and was given its current name by Claude Casimir Gillet in 1876. Kauffman classified it as a separate species in 1918. A 2005 nuclear-gene study found I. geophylla closely related to I. fuscodisca, and I. lilacina more closely allied with I. agglutinata and I. pudica. This is single-cluster (Wikipedia) material. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods and conifers, growing alone, scattered or gregariously, in summer and fall (and winter in warm climates), and widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia likewise describes it as mycorrhizal, fruiting in deciduous and coniferous woodlands in summer and autumn, in grassy areas, near pathways, and often on rich, bare, disturbed soil at roadsides and near ditches. Wikipedia adds that in western North America it is found under live oak, pine and Douglas-fir, and that in the Canadian Arctic the nominate form occurs in dryish tundra heath communities. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
