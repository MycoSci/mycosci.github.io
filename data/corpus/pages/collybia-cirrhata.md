## Summary

Collybia cirrhata (Pers.) Quél., the piggyback shanklet, is a small whitish agaric in the family Tricholomataceae. It is described as saprobic, growing in clusters on the decayed or blackened remains of other mushrooms, and as lacking the sclerotium found at the stem base in the other two Collybia species. It is recorded across Europe, northern Asia and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin cirrata, meaning "curled". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species first appeared in the literature in 1786 as Agaricus amanitae, described by Batsch. The first valid name was Agaricus cirrhatus, published by Schumacher in 1803. It was later transferred to Microcollybia by Métrod in 1952 and again by Lennox in 1979, because Métrod's transfer was considered a nomen nudum. Microcollybia has since been wrapped into Collybia. Wikipedia also records that Charles Horton Peck called it the "fringed-rooted Collybia". *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Saprobic, growing gregariously on the decaying or blackened remains of other mushrooms (MushroomExpert.Com; Wikipedia). Wikipedia adds that fruit bodies are occasionally found on moss or soil with no apparent connection to decayed mushrooms, and lists Lactarius, Russula, Meripilus giganteus and Bovista dermoxantha as known hosts. MushroomExpert.Com reports it under hardwoods or conifers in summer and fall, widely distributed at least in northern and montane North America. Wikipedia describes it as known from temperate, boreal, and alpine or arctic habitats. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
