## Summary

Morchella esculenta (L.) Pers. is a species in the family Morchellaceae (Pezizales, Ascomycota). Wikipedia describes the fruit body as a yellowish, pitted, sponge-like cap on a white hollow stem. Wikipedia and two journal articles (lit_2, lit_3) call it an edible mushroom, and Wikipedia describes it as among the most highly prized. *[wikipedia, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin esculenta, meaning "edible". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was originally named Phallus esculentus by Carl Linnaeus in Species Plantarum (1753), and Christiaan Hendrik Persoon later gave it its current name. Wikipedia also reports that in 2014 Richard et al. used DNA analysis to restrict the name M. esculenta to a single species of yellow morel commonly found in Europe and also reported from China, and that other yellow morels, including those in North America, have received new scientific names. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that in Jammu and Kashmir, wild mushrooms locally known as Gucchi, including Morchella conica and M. esculenta, are gathered and supplied as a medical remedy. It also states that M. esculenta was named state mushroom of Minnesota, the first state mushroom of any state. Its Nepali name is given as Guchi chyau. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia reports that in 1901 Repin obtained fruit bodies in a cave where cultures had been established in flower pots in 1892. It also describes a method by Taylor Piercefield that used inoculated hardwood sapling beds treated with potassium hydroxide; it produced large mature fruits but was not commercially viable at large scale. According to the same source, small-scale commercial growers have had success with partially shaded rows of mulched wood, inoculated with spores in a water and molasses solution and later treated with a dilute wood-ash solution to trigger fruiting. All of this comes from a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fungus fruits under hardwoods and conifers for a short period in spring, and is also associated with old orchards, woods and disturbed ground. It grows on the ground, sometimes solitary but more often in groups, in forests, orchards, yards and gardens, and sometimes in recently burned areas. Wikipedia notes a preference for limestone-based (alkaline) soil, though it has also been found in acidic soils. In North America it is called the "May mushroom" but fruits from February to July depending on locality. Wikipedia also says the name M. esculenta has been applied to many similar yellow morels worldwide, so some of this habitat information may describe taxa now treated as separate species. The Index Fungorum-derived GBIF record quotes the original habitat statement as "Habitat in Sylvis antiquis". *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
