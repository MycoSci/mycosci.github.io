## Summary

Schizophyllum commune Fr. (1815) is a species in the family Schizophyllaceae, recorded from dozens of countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having small, stemless, pleurotoid fruiting bodies 1–4 cm across that attach like tiny bracket fungi. Its gill-like folds on the underside look split or doubled. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that Schizophyllum derives from the Greek schíza, 'split', because of the radial, centrally split, gill-like folds. It glosses commune as meaning common, shared ownership or ubiquitous. MushroomExpert.Com separately notes that commune refers to shared ownership 'in an odd way', since the species is one of the most widely distributed and common mushrooms on the planet. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the gills split when the mushroom dries out, and that this earned it the common name 'split gill'. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on deadwood, or occasionally parasitic on living wood. It grows alone or, more often, gregariously to clustered, on decaying hardwood sticks and logs, including planks and boards, and on the deadwood of deciduous trees. It is described as fruiting year-round, surviving by shriveling up and waiting for more moisture. Wikipedia likewise gives rotting wood and year-round occurrence in places such as North America. A GBIF-hosted descriptive record says 'growing on deciduous wood', and other GBIF-hosted records list dead wood, decaying trunks, a burned trunk and dead bamboo trunks as substrates, with gregarious habit. Both MushroomExpert.Com and Wikipedia describe it as occurring throughout the world. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
