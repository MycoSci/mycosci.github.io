## Summary

Russula delica Fr. (1838) is a species in the family Russulaceae, recorded in GBIF occurrence data from many countries across Europe, Asia and North America. Wikipedia describes it as mostly white with ochraceous or brownish cap markings and a short, robust stem. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet delica is Latin for "weaned". No other supplied source discusses the derivation of the name. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries first described the species in 1838, and older names include Christian Hendrik Persoon's Lactarius piperatus var. exsuccus. The same source says the species has undergone many taxonomic changes. Russula chloroides is now treated as a distinct species, based on very dense lamellae and a blue/green zone at the stem apex in some specimens. Wikipedia also notes that a form John Burton Cleland described in 1935 as R. delica, collected under eucalypts in the Mount Lofty Ranges of South Australia, was reclassified as the new species R. marangania by Cheryl Grgurinovic in 1997. All of this comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruiting bodies are often found half buried in the soil, or sometimes growing hypogeously. It describes the species as widespread in the northern temperate zones, including Europe and Asia, and particularly common in the Eastern Mediterranean. It calls it a thermophilic species that appears during hot spells in summer and autumn, in broadleaved and coniferous woods. Wikipedia adds that in North America it is rare and largely replaced by R. brevipes. These habitat statements come from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*
