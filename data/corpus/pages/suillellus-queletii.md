## Summary

Suillellus queletii (Schulzer) Vizzini, Simonini & Gelardi, in the family Boletaceae, is commonly known as the deceiving bolete. It was formerly placed in Boletus. It is described as uncommon in Europe and has also been recorded in Taiwan. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet queletii was given in honour of Lucien Quélet. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Stephan Schulzer von Müggenburg described the species in 1885 as a Boletus, and that it was transferred to Suillellus in 2014. The same source notes that the name Boletus erythropus, long applied to a red-pored species, was later found to be invalid for that usage because Persoon's 1796 mushroom had orange pores. It adds that, according to Funga Nordica, the fungus Persoon described was actually Suillellus queletii. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a mycorrhizal mushroom found in deciduous forest at lower altitudes, under beech, oak or hazel. It is described as uncommon in Europe and considered endangered in the Czech Republic, and it has been recorded in Taiwan in Asia. The iNaturalist text, which derives from Wikipedia, gives the host as mostly beech. *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*
