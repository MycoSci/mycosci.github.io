## Summary

Strobilomyces strobilaceus (Scop.) Berk., commonly known as old man of the woods, is a species of fungus in the family Boletaceae. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as characterized by soft, dark scales on the cap surface, with caps 4–11 cm wide. The GBIF distribution record lists Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont, among others. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that it was first described scientifically by the Italian naturalist Giovanni Antonio Scopoli in 1770 as a species of Boletus. No supplied source gives the reasons for later transfers. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia notes that on 27 February 2014 the postal administration of Switzerland issued a 50-centime definitive postage stamp depicting the species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it is found solitary or in groups in deciduous as well as coniferous forests in low mountain ranges and alpine areas of eastern North America, Europe, and Asia (Iran and Taiwan), and is less common in lowlands. It gives the season as July to October, under oak and beech trees. Mushroom World describes it as commonly found in forests with a mix of coniferous and deciduous trees in moist and shaded environments. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
