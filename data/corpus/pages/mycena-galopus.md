## Summary

Mycena galopus (Pers.) P. Kumm., the milking bonnet, is a small saprobic mushroom in the family Mycenaceae. Wikipedia describes it as having a grayish-brown, bell-shaped, radially grooved cap up to 2.5 cm wide and a slender stem that exudes a whitish latex when broken. It is reported from North America and Europe and is described as a leaf-litter decomposer. Wikipedia lists it as inedible; this comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet galopus is derived from the Greek γαλα 'milk' and πονς 'foot'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described as Agaricus galopus by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1800 and transferred to Mycena by Paul Kummer in 1871. An Australian taxon formerly treated as a variety, M. galopus var. mellea, was raised to species level and renamed M. thunderboltensis in 1998. The variety candida was described by Jakob Emanuel Lange in 1914 from specimens found in Denmark, and the variety nigra was named by Carleton Rea in 1922. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Per Wikipedia, all varieties fruit in summer and autumn on leaf litter in coniferous and deciduous habitats. The fruit bodies grow in groups to scattered on humus under hardwoods or conifers. In the United States it is described as very abundant along the Pacific Coast from Washington to California, and also in Tennessee and North Carolina. It extends north to Canada (Nova Scotia). In Europe it has been collected from Britain, Germany, Ireland and Norway. This habitat information comes from a single source (Wikipedia, with iNaturalist as a derivative copy). *[wikipedia]*
