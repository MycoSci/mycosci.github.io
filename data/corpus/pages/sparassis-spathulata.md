## Summary

Sparassis spathulata (Schwein.) Fr. 1828 is a fungus in the family Sparassidaceae, known as the eastern cauliflower mushroom. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as an eastern North American species whose flattened, more or less straight branches form a loose rosette, usually with concentric color zones. It contrasts with Sparassis americana, whose branches are tightly packed, curled and "noodly". *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com says the species was originally described from North Carolina. It recognizes Sparassis spathulata f. herbstii, previously treated as Sparassis herbstii, as a morphological form with thickened branch tips. The same source says European records of S. spathulata probably represent the similar Sparassis brevipes, which Petersen et al. (2015) treat as endemic to Europe, while S. spathulata is found only in North America. Wikipedia also notes that f. herbstii was previously considered S. herbstii. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as pathogenic and saprobic, growing from the roots or bases of trees, found under hardwoods (especially oaks) or pines, and causing a brown rot or butt rot. It fruits in summer and fall, and is fairly widely distributed in North America east of the Great Plains and below the Great Lakes. Wikipedia says it can be found between eastern Texas and northeastern North America from July to September. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
