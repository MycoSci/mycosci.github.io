## Summary

Phlebopus portentosus (Berk. & Broome) Boedijn, 1951, is a bolete-like fungus in the family Boletinellaceae, order Boletales. The only descriptive source supplied, Wikipedia, treats it under the name Phlebopus marginatus, which it regards as the same taxon as P. portentosus. ## Etymology

The genus name Phlebopus is said by Wikipedia to derive from the Greek for "vein" and "foot". No supplied source discusses the derivation of the species epithet. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Berkeley and Broome described Boletus portentosus in a report on the fungi of Ceylon published in 1873. They based it on a specimen with a 25 cm cap collected on June 15, 1869, and held it to be related to Boletus aestivalis. Boedijn noted the spore shape, lack of cystidia and short tubes, and allocated it to Phlebopus in 1951. Wikipedia also says New Zealand botanist Robert McNabb used the combination Phaeogyroporus portentosus, which was in use for some years. In 2001 Roy Watling proposed the name Phlebopus marginatus over P. portentosus. He considered the two taxa the same, because the former name (Boletus marginatus, 1845) predated the latter. Watling also queried whether a single species occurs over so wide a range. All of this comes from a single encyclopedia source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

This is a single-source account from Wikipedia, written under the name P. marginatus. It says the fungus occurs in eucalypt forests in Australia and may be found any time after rain, with fruit bodies isolated, in groups, or in fairy rings. It is also reported from rainforest in Queensland. In New Zealand it is possibly associated with Nothofagus truncata. In China, in Yunnan, Guangxi and Hainan, it is said to grow in association with Delonix regia, Mangifera indica, Coffea arabica, Citrus grandis, Artocarpus heterophyllus and Quercus species. The same source describes it as a pantropical species and gives Indonesia, Malaysia and Sri Lanka among its range. *[wikipedia]*
