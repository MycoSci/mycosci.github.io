## Summary

Flammulaster carpophilus (Fr.) Earle ex Vellinga (1986) is a species in the family Tubariaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a tiny, fibrillose-scaly mushroom found in Europe and North America, with a cap 5–12 mm across in the collection described there. The GBIF description record contains only a distribution entry, listing Sweden. *[gbif_desc, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was originally described from Scandinavia (Fries 1815). According to that source, North American authors treated it as Cystoderma rhombosporum until at least the late 1970s, and it has also been placed in Phaeomarasmius and other genera. The same source says that several versions of the species have been, and sometimes still are, treated as separate species. The version with papillate spores is sometimes called Flammulaster rhombosporus, and a pale, pinkish version with capitate cheilocystidia is sometimes called Flammulaster subincarnata. Its author follows Vellinga (1986) in treating carpophilus as a broadly defined species that includes these as forms or varieties. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (single source) describes the species as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously in leaf litter or conifer duff in hardwood forests or in mixed woods, from spring through fall. It is described as fairly widespread in Europe, especially in beech forests. The source calls the North American distribution uncertain, but usually reported from northern and montane regions. The collection illustrated and described there is from Colorado. *[mushroomexpert]*
