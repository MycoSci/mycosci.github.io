## Summary

Cortinarius traganus (Fr.) Fr. 1838, the gassy webcap, is a species in the family Cortinariaceae with records from many countries across Europe, North America and Asia. Wikipedia describes it as lilac-coloured, with rusty-brown gills and spores. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first named Agaricus traganus by Elias Magnus Fries in 1818. Fries's protologue was very brief. It mentioned fruity-smelling basidiomata, a pale lilac cap, a purplish-white bulbous stipe and yellow flesh, and it referred to an illustration by Schaeffer (1774), which became the lectotype. That illustration mixed features of more than one species. Liimatainen and colleagues therefore designated as epitype a collection by Lindström of 13 September 1988, from a dry, sandy pine forest at Myran, Sweden. Wikipedia also notes that some authorities treat the American variant as a separate species, Cortinarius pyriodorus, and reserve the name C. traganus for the European form. This account rests on a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species grows in coniferous forests, seems to prefer poorer soils (both siliceous and non-calcareous), and is widespread across the temperate zone of the Northern Hemisphere. It says the species is fairly common in North America, generally appearing from August to September, or somewhat later on the West Coast. Mushroom World, which is in the same source cluster as Wikipedia, instead describes it as mycorrhizal, usually with deciduous trees but also found with conifers, often on acidic soil. A GBIF descriptions record lists distribution as Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, gbif_desc]*
