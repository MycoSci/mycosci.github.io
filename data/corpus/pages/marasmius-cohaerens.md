## Summary

Marasmius cohaerens (Pers.) Cooke & Quél., 1878, is a gilled mushroom in the family Marasmiaceae, listed on this site under the common name Bristled Parachute. Wikipedia describes it as fairly common in European woods. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small brown-capped mushroom with a white spore print and a stem that darkens from the base. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Two supplied sources explain the epithet differently. Wikipedia says it has the same origin as the English word "coherent" and means "keeping together", which it glosses as the mushroom being difficult to pull apart. MushroomExpert.Com instead links the name to the mushroom growing in clusters of two or three. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Christiaan Hendrik Persoon originally described the species in 1801 as Agaricus cohaerens. It adds that in 1878, in a work published in London, Mordecai Cubitt Cooke and Lucien Quélet assigned the current name. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a saprobic mushroom growing singly or in small groups on humus and litter in beech forests or with other deciduous trees, and only occasionally in coniferous forests. It says the species is widely distributed and fairly common in Europe and in eastern Asia, and occurs rarely in North America. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on leaf litter and woody debris in hardwood forests, primarily oak-hickory and beech-maple, and also in hemlock forests where beech is present. It says the mushroom grows alone or, more typically, in clusters of two or three, from early summer through fall, and is widely distributed in eastern North America. For central Illinois, the same source reports it as a regular in late spring and early summer that also appears in late summer and fall. GBIF description records list the substrate as leaf litter ("Sobre hojarasca"). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
