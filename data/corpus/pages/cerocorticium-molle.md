## Summary

Cerocorticium molle (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Jülich, 1975, is a crust fungus placed in the family Pterulaceae (order Agaricales). GBIF holds 130 occurrence records from 19 countries, including Brazil, the United States, Cuba, Taiwan, Kenya, Tanzania, New Zealand and Russia. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Miles Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis first described the fungus in 1868 as Corticium molle. It says they described the fruit body of the type specimen as resembling "a thin coating of wax poured over the surface", and that Walter Jülich transferred it to Cerocorticium in 1975. A 2008 study of Bresadola's type specimens treats Corticium aureolum as a synonym of Cerocorticium molle. A 2015 Mycotaxon study found that Chrysoderma alboluteum, described from Reunion, is conspecific with it. *[wikipedia, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only independent source for this, states that it grows on the dead bark and wood of a variety of angiosperms. It says the fungus has occasionally been recorded on or under the bark of living trees, and that it is found in tropical and subtropical regions of Africa, Asia, North America and South America. A 2003 Mycoscience paper reports it from mangrove forests on the islands of Iriomote and Okinawa, Japan, as a new record for Japan. *[wikipedia, lit_1]*
