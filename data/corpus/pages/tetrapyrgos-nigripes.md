## Summary

Tetrapyrgos nigripes (Fr.) E. Horak is a very small gilled mushroom in the family Marasmiaceae, with a white cap and a slender, tough stalk that turns black from the base upward. It is described as saprobic on leaf litter and twigs. Sources place it mostly in the Americas, with additional records from Australia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name Tetrapyrgos derives from the Greek tetra ('four') and pyrgos ('tower'), referring to the sharply projecting nodules or corners on the spores of species in the genus. It states that the species name nigripes derives from the Latin niger ('black') and pes ('foot'). This is a single-cluster source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries formally described the species in 1838 as Marasmius nigripes. He noted that he had examined a specimen sent by Lewis David von Schweinitz, who had earlier described it in his Synopsis fungorum Carolinae superioris (1822). Schweinitz's earlier name was later recognized as illegitimate, because Jakob Gabriel Trog had already used it for the taxon now known as Macrocystidia cucumis. MushroomExpert.Com lists Marasmiellus nigripes and Heliomyces nigripes as synonyms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on leaf litter and twigs in hardwood forests, growing alone or gregariously in summer and fall, and widely distributed east of the Mississippi River. It says the stem is inserted directly into the substrate, usually an oak leaf or a twig. Wikipedia likewise gives a twig or leaf as the usual substrate, mainly oak in North America, and reports an association with cashew trees in Cuba under high relative humidity. GBIF description records (Spanish-language, unattributed) list gregarious growth, on wood and on leaf veins within humid tropical forest, at altitudes of 1250–1900. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
