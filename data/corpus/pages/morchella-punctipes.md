## Summary

Morchella punctipes, the half-free morel, is a species in the Morchellaceae described by Charles Horton Peck in 1903 from Michigan. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a morel whose cap is attached to the stem about halfway down, and places it in hardwood forests from the Great Plains eastward. North American half-free morels were long called Morchella semilibera, until DNA studies (O'Donnell et al. 2011) supported treating M. semilibera as a strictly European species. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

According to MushroomExpert.Com, Peck coined the epithet punctipes ("spotted stem") for a Michigan half-free morel with what he called a "squamulose stem", whose adornment "consists of small conical points which are sometimes darker colored than the stem". Peck considered the usual half-free morel (M. semilibera) to have a smooth stem. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) reports that Peck (1903) named the species from a Michigan half-free morel sent to him; he was based in New York state. The original description gives the locality as Agricultural College, Michigan, June, collected by B. O. Longyear. Peck separated it from M. semilibera on the basis of its larger ascospores and squamulose stem. Kuo argues that stem ornamentation is too variable to separate species, but that Peck's name still applies under the rules of taxonomy. The holotype is held at the New York State Museum herbarium (NYS). DNA useful for PCR could not be extracted from it. Kuo et al. (2012) applied Peck's epithet to the eastern North American half-free morel, based on morphological match with the type and on the phylogenetic species Mel-4 of O'Donnell et al. (2011), rather than naming a new species. Wikipedia states that M. punctipes was once thought to be the same species as M. semilibera, and that DNA studies disproved this. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
