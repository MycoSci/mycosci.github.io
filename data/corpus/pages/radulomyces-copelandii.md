## Summary

Radulomyces copelandii (Pat.) Hjortstam & Spooner (1990) is a fungus in the family Radulomycetaceae, known in English as the Asian beauty. It is a toothed crust fungus with whitish to pale yellowish teeth that darken to brownish with age, growing on dead wood. It is native to Asia and has been recorded in North America since 2009. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com gives the original authority as N. T. Patouillard (1914) and the combination in Radulomyces as K. Hjortstam & B. M. Spooner (1990), and says the species was originally described from the Philippines. It notes that Radulomyces paumanokensis, described from Long Island, is a synonym of R. copelandii (Nakasone et al. 2021). It says that species was first described as separate because of its thick clumps of tightly packed spines, which are often flattened or branched. It also lists Radulodon copelandii as a former name. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as typically found on logs and decaying wood. It gives the native range as Asia (Russian Far East, China, Japan, Korea, the Philippines, Malaysia and Sri Lanka). It reports the first North American find in Massachusetts in 2009, by J. Ginns and Lawrence Millman. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on standing or fallen deadwood of hardwoods, generally where bark is still attached, in cracks and fissures, causing a white rot. That source gives the season as summer through winter and records it in the northeastern and Mid-Atlantic United States. The seasonal and hardwood-substrate statements come from that one source only. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
