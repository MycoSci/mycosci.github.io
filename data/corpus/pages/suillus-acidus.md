## Summary

Suillus acidus (Peck) Singer, 1945, is a bolete in the family Suillaceae, recorded in the US, Russia, Canada, Mexico and Finland. Wikipedia says it was first described by Charles Horton Peck as Boletus acidus. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a fairly distinctive bolete with a whitish to dull yellow, slimy-when-fresh cap that often has partial veil remnants along its edge, and flesh in the stem that is rosy orange. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) states that the taste of the slime on the fresh cap is acidic, hence the species epithet acidus. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Charles Horton Peck first described the species as Boletus acidus in 1905. MushroomExpert.Com instead cites Peck 1906 and says the species was originally described from New York. Its reference line credits R. Singer, 1945, for the combination in Suillus. Under the current definition MushroomExpert.Com follows (Nguyen and collaborators, 2016), the species includes Suillus intermedius, Suillus lutescens and Suillus subolivaceus. Suillus subalutaceus is described there as morphologically very similar but phylogenetically separate. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes S. acidus as mycorrhizal with red pine, eastern white pine or western white pine. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously in late summer and fall. It is described as distributed in the northern Midwest, northeastern North America and the Pacific Northwest, and the collections illustrated there are from Michigan. A GBIF description record lists a distribution of Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
