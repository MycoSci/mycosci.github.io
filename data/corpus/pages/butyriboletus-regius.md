## Summary

Butyriboletus regius, the royal bolete, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, described by Krombholz and transferred to Butyriboletus by Arora & Frank in 2014. Wikipedia describes it as having a pink cap, yellow flesh and a reticulate pattern on the stem, and records it from North America, Europe and China. It was formerly placed in Boletus. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was first described and illustrated by Julius Vincenz von Krombholz in 1832. It was formerly placed in section Appendiculati of the genus Boletus. Molecular analysis showed that it and related 'butter bolete' species, including Boletus appendiculatus, are phylogenetically distinct from Boletus, and the new genus Butyriboletus was created for them. Wikipedia also notes that Harry D. Thiers described a similar mushroom from California as B. regius, though it is not the same species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes Butyriboletus regius as an ectomycorrhizal species with a broad host range, associating with oak and conifers, especially fir. Fruit bodies grow singly, scattered, or grouped together. In North America they usually appear from August to November, and also between May and June. The North American distribution includes the Pacific Northwest states of California, Oregon and Washington, where frequency ranges from 'rare to locally abundant'. The same source says it is rare in Europe, appears on the Regional Red List of several countries, and is considered endangered in the Czech Republic. It has also been recorded from China.
