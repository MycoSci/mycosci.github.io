## Summary

Russula sanguinea (Bull.) Fr. is a brittlegill in the family Russulaceae, recorded in northern Europe, North America and parts of Asia. Wikipedia describes a bright blood-red, robust-capped mushroom associated with conifers, and treats R. sanguinaria as the currently used name for it. MushroomExpert.Com applies the name to a red-capped, red-stemmed, acrid russula associated with pines, and notes that the North American concept may not match the original European species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithets sanguinaria and sanguinea both derive from the Latin sanguis ('blood'), referring to the mushroom's colour. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus sanguinarius by Schumacher in 1803 and redescribed under the binomial Russula sanguinaria by Rauschert in 1989. Agaricus sanguinea was described by Bulliard and renamed Russula sanguinea by Fries. Wikipedia says it was previously widely known as Russula sanguinea. MushroomExpert.Com notes that some mycologists treat R. sanguinaria as a synonym, and that Russula rosacea, in the sense of many authors, is also a synonym. It further says the North American concept may include more than one phylogenetic species, none of which is probably the same as the original European R. sanguinea. David Arora (1986) was unsure whether the European species was the same as the American Russula rosacea, while a 2012 field guide, per Wikipedia, treats them as the same. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports it appearing in summer and autumn, mycorrhizal with softwood trees, often Pinus in coniferous woodland on sandy soils, and widespread in the northern temperate zones. MushroomExpert.Com (North American concept) describes it as mycorrhizal with pines, especially two-needled pines such as Bishop pine and red pine, and perhaps with other conifers. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
