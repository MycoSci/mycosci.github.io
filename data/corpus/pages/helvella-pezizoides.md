## Summary

Helvella pezizoides Afzel. (1783) is an ascomycete in the family Helvellaceae, order Pezizales. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a black, loosely saddle-shaped cap with the margin rolled upwards when young, and a black smooth stem. It separates it from other Helvella species with smooth rather than ribbed stems by a combination of features, including a densely fuzzy cap undersurface. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (a single source) describes it as probably mycorrhizal, growing alone or gregariously under hardwoods or conifers, on the ground or on rotting wood, in summer and fall, in northern forests from Michigan to Alaska. The same source says the North American range appears limited to boreal or nearly boreal regions, but that because the species is so infrequently collected it is unclear whether it might be expected elsewhere. A GBIF distribution record lists Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
