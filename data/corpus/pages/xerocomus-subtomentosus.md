## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name Xerocomus is derived from the Ancient Greek xeros 'dry' and kome 'hair', referring to the velvety surface of the cap. It also states that the specific name subtomentosus is Latin for 'finely haired', referring to the cap. Both derivations come from that one source. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Carl Linnaeus described the species in 1753 as Boletus subtomentosus. Lucien Quélet later placed a number of Boletus species in the genus Xerocomus and made X. subtomentosus its type species. Wikipedia says this classification was disputed and that many authorities continued to use Boletus subtomentosus. MushroomExpert.Com says Europeans have generally used Xerocomus since Quélet, while most North American authors avoided it. Both sources say that later DNA studies show the X. subtomentosus group is not closely related to Boletus edulis, the type species of Boletus. MushroomExpert.Com says Linnaeus's concept was a sub-velvety bolete with a yellowish cap and stem. It says the 'true' X. subtomentosus must come from Sweden, where Linnaeus first described it. A 2006 study of the subtomentosus group (Taylor and collaborators) found new species and potential new species in Europe and North America. It named Xerocomus chrysonemus from the United Kingdom, and a 2007 publication named Xerocomus silwoodensis. MushroomExpert.Com therefore doubts that the North American and southern European populations are the same species as the Swedish type. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as ectomycorrhizal with a wide range of hardwood and conifer trees, and Mushroom World likewise says it grows in association with hardwood and conifer trees. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with a wide variety of hardwoods, including oaks, beech, birches and aspens, growing alone, scattered or gregariously. Wikipedia gives the season as autumn. MushroomExpert.Com gives summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. Wikipedia reports it from forests across Eurasia, North America and Australia. It cites a record near Tangier, Morocco, under cork oak (Quercus suber), and says the species is common in subarctic western Greenland, associated with white birch (Betula pubescens). Wikipedia also says it is associated with bilberry and other heath-family (Ericaceae) shrubs. MushroomExpert.Com says it was originally described from Sweden and is widely distributed in Europe and North America, and also known from Asia and Oceania. It notes that its illustrated collections come from California, Colorado, Illinois, Indiana and Washington. MushroomExpert.Com cautions that populations on different continents, associated with very different trees, may not be the same species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
