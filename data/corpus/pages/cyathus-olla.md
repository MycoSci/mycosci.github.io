## Summary

Cyathus olla (Batsch) Pers. is a bird's nest fungus, known as the field bird's nest. Wikipedia describes it as a saprobic species whose fruit bodies resemble tiny bird's nests filled with "eggs" (spore-containing peridioles). It is recorded from many countries, and Wikipedia calls it relatively common, with a worldwide distribution. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin word olla, meaning "pot". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Peziza olla Batsch, published in Elenchus Fungorum in 1783, with the combination Cyathus olla credited to Persoon. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com also record a large form, Cyathus olla f. anglicus. Wikipedia says this form was originally reported from England by Curtis Gates Lloyd. *[wikispecies, wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes C. olla as saprobic and usually found on woody debris. It says specimens on soil are usually attached to bits of wood or stems in the soil, and that the species usually grows in moist, shaded places. It cites Brodie for this and notes a record from the arid region of Lima, Peru. MushroomExpert.Com (single source) describes it as growing scattered, gregariously or in dense clusters, sometimes terrestrially but often on woody debris, and frequently on dead plant stems, including corn husks in cornfields and debris in canola fields. It gives the season as summer and fall in North America. A GBIF descriptions record lists the habit as terrestrial and the substrate as soil. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
