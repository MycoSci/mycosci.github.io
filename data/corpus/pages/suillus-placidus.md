## Summary

Suillus placidus (Bonord.) Singer, 1945, is a bolete in the family Suillaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a Suillus with a bald, sticky white cap and a slender stem that develops conspicuous glandular dots and smears, apparently associated exclusively with eastern white pine. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was originally described from Germany by Bonorden in 1861. It also notes that Boletus albus Peck (1872) is probably the same mushroom, according to Snell (1933, 1970). *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes S. placidus as mycorrhizal with Pinus strobus (eastern white pine) and apparently exclusively associated with it. It grows alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall. It is fairly widely distributed in northeastern North America, west to the Dakotas and south along the Appalachians, corresponding with the natural range of the host tree. The same source says that in Europe it appears only with imported Pinus strobus, and that reports from China and Japan probably represent a different species. Wikipedia gives a broader picture. It says the species occurs only with five-needled pines of the subgenus Strobus, including Pinus cembra and introduced P. strobus in Europe, and Pinus sibirica, P. pumila and P. koraiensis in the Russian Far East, and that it has also been reported in China. Wikipedia says fruiting is in summer and autumn, singly or in small groups. The two sources differ on the Asian records and the range of host trees. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
