## Summary

Russula albonigra (Krombh.) Fr., 1874, is a brittlegill in the family Russulaceae, commonly known as the blackening russula. Wikipedia describes the flesh as white, turning black or sooty. GBIF holds occurrence records from many countries, including the US, Sweden, France and the UK. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet comes from the Latin albus and niger, meaning white and black. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described by the mycologist Julius Vincenz von Krombholz in 1838. Wikispecies instead lists the basionym Agaricus alboniger Krombholz 1845 and the combination Russula albonigra (Krombh.) Fr., 1874. The two sources disagree on the date of Krombholz's description. Wikispecies also lists two later varietal combinations: Russula nigricans var. albonigra (Cooke & Quélet 1878) and Russula adusta var. albonigra (Massee 1893). *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows under hardwood and conifer trees in North America, and gives the Pacific Northwest as an example of where it is found. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*
