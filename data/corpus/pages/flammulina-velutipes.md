## Summary

Flammulina velutipes (Curtis) Singer, 1951 is a gilled mushroom in the family Physalacriaceae, with GBIF occurrence records from many countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as fond of cold weather, often appearing in late fall or winter, and growing from the wood of hardwoods. Both MushroomExpert.Com and Wikipedia give its spore print as white. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was originally described from England by the botanist William Curtis in 1782 as Agaricus velutipes, and transferred to Flammulina by Rolf Singer in 1951. Wikipedia also says that until recently F. velutipes was considered conspecific with the Asian F. filiformis, but that DNA sequencing shows the two are distinct. MushroomExpert.Com says the species was originally described from England (Curtis) and was epitypified from England by Bas (via Redhead & Petersen 1999). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultivation

MushroomExpert.Com states that the 'enoki' or 'enokitake' seen commercially is a cultivated form of Flammulina velutipes and that it looks nothing like the wild mushroom: pale, long-stemmed and tiny-capped. The iNaturalist text, which is a declared derivative of Wikipedia, likewise calls enokitake cultivars of F. velutipes. The Wikipedia article on F. velutipes itself attributes the cultivated 'enokitake' to the Asian F. filiformis, a separate species. No cultivation method or tek is given in the supplied sources. *[mushroomexpert, inaturalist, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as saprotrophic, especially on dead or dying elm, and also on ash, beech and oak. It gives the season as October to February in Europe and through May in the east in North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the stumps, logs, roots and living wood of hardwoods and more rarely conifers, growing in clusters. It says the mushrooms sometimes appear terrestrial when the wood is buried, and usually appear from fall to spring. It says the species is widely distributed and that its illustrated and described collections come from Illinois and Québec. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
