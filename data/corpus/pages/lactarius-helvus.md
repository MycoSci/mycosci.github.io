## Summary

Lactarius helvus (Fr.) Fr. 1838 is a milkcap in the family Russulaceae, known in English as fenugreek milkcap and burnt-sugar milky. GBIF records it from many countries, chiefly in northern Europe and North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes a medium-sized, light-brown-capped mushroom with scant, watery milk and a strong curry-like or burned-maple-syrup odor. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet helvus is derived from the Latin for 'honey-yellow'. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries first described the species in 1821 as Agaricus helvus and placed it in Lactarius in 1838, and Peck's Lactarius aquifluus has been deemed a synonym. MushroomExpert.Com adds that North American field guides have generally used the name Lactarius aquifluus, which comes from a New York collection named by Peck in 1876 and was used by Hesler & Smith (1979). It says European researchers (Heilmann-Clausen and collaborators, 1998) treat that name as a synonym of L. helvus without molecular evidence. Kuo also suggests that if molecular data ever separate a North American species, L. aquifluus would probably be the best name for it. This account of the naming dispute comes from a single source. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports fruiting bodies in groups in woodlands near conifers such as Picea and Pinus, and near birch (Betula) and rarely beech (Fagus). It says they occur in wet places, often in Sphagnum, in late summer and autumn, and that the species is found across Europe and Asia. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers (rarely birch) in boggy, wet locations, usually growing in sphagnum, in summer and fall. It calls the species widely distributed and fairly common in northeastern North America from Nova Scotia to Minnesota, and also documented in the southern Appalachians, Texas, Montana and Idaho. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
