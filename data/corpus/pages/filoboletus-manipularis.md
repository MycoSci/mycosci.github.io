## Summary

Filoboletus manipularis (Berk.) Singer, 1945, is a species of agaric fungus in the family Mycenaceae, order Agaricales. Wikipedia describes it as occurring in tropical Asia, Australia and the Pacific islands, with mycelium and fruit bodies that can be bioluminescent, and with pores rather than gills on the underside of the cap. These descriptive details come from a single encyclopedia source. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that in Japan the species is called Ami-hikari-také ("reticulated luminous mushroom") and has become part of Japanese folklore. It adds that fungal bioluminescence in general was regarded in the post-Edo period as eerie and attributed to yōkai (supernatural creatures), and that the glow was called mino-bi ("raincoat fire"), a term associated with fungi growing on rotting wood and straw raincoats. This is a single-source account, and the passage speaks of fungal bioluminescence generally rather than of this species alone. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that populations are found in tropical regions of Asia, Australia and the Pacific Islands, and that fruit body clusters are typically found in forests on rotted or rotting wood. The same source reports it as endangered in Chiba Prefecture, Japan, and listed as "near threatened" in Miyazaki Prefecture. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*
