## Summary

Pisolithus arenarius Alb. & Schwein. (1805) is a species in the family Sclerodermataceae (order Boletales). MushroomExpert.Com describes a puffball-like fruiting body that is ball-shaped when young and matures into a top-shaped, stumplike or otherwise irregular form 4–10 cm high and 3–8 cm across. The mature fruiting body releases cinnamon brown spore powder. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says North American field guides probably call this mushroom Pisolithus tinctorius or Pisolithus arhizus. It cites recent research (Martin et al. 2002; Lebel et al. 2018) as indicating that Pisolithus arenarius is the more appropriate name. Its reasons are that Pisolithus arhizus appears to be limited to Europe, Africa and Asia, and that the name Pisolithus tinctorius, as traditionally applied, is illegitimate. Coker and Couch (1928) applied that name without knowing it had already been used (Fischer 1900) for a species associated with rockroses on the Canary Islands. The page refers readers to Lebel and collaborators (2018) for a fuller account, and says that North American material shows considerable variability in physical features and mycorrhizal hosts. This account comes from a single source. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the species is mycorrhizal with various hardwoods and pines. It grows alone, scattered, or in small groups, and is often found in gravel, sandy soil, ditches and lawns. It fruits in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. It is described as widely distributed in North America but more common on the West Coast and in the southeast. The collections described there come from California, Indiana, Kentucky and Virginia. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*
