## Summary

Syzygospora mycetophila (Peck) Ginns, 1986, is a fungus in the family Filobasidiaceae (Tremellomycetes). Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a parasite of the agaric Gymnopus dryophilus, producing gelatinous growths on the host's fruit bodies. MushroomExpert.Com, the only descriptive source independent of Wikipedia, calls it Collybia jelly and says the growths are creamy yellow, brain-like or saucer-shaped, with up to 35 on a single host mushroom. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia (which treats the species under the name Carcinomyces mycetophilus and says its generic placement is currently uncertain) states that it was first described by Charles Horton Peck from North America. MushroomExpert.Com cites the authority as (Peck, 1874) and lists Christiansenia mycetophila and Tremella mycetophila as former names. Neither source describes the type material or the reasons for the later transfers. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a parasite on Gymnopus dryophilus; Wikipedia says it produces extensive gelatinous galls on the host's fruit bodies. MushroomExpert.Com adds that the jelly-like growths appear to be made of the host mushroom's own tissue, induced by the parasite, and that they occur on the stem, gills and cap. Wikipedia states that it is widespread in North America and has also been recorded in continental Europe. The GBIF description record lists the distribution as DK, NO, SE and Vermont. MushroomExpert.Com says only that the host grows in just about any kind of woods at just about any time. No source gives a season for the parasite itself. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
