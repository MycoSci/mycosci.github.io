## Summary

Calocera viscosa (Pers.) Bory is a species in the family Dacrymycetaceae, with GBIF occurrence records from many countries, mostly in Europe. Wikipedia describes it as a small, gelatinous, bright golden-yellow, branched jelly fungus that grows on dead wood of conifers. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Clavaria aurea by Alexander von Humboldt in 1793, but that name is illegitimate because it had already been used for a different species. It was then legitimately described as Clavaria viscosa from Germany in 1794 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon. A white fungus from Italy was described as Calocera cavarae in 1896. Later studies suggested it was only an aberrant form of C. viscosa lacking yellow pigments. All of this comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a wood-decay fungus found on logs and dead wood of conifers. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing in troops and clusters on moss-covered deadwood of conifers. The wood is often buried, so the fruit bodies can appear terrestrial. A GBIF description record (in Spanish) gives the substrate as dead trunks or branches in sunny places. On season, Wikipedia gives October to March on the West Coast of North America and July to September elsewhere on the continent. MushroomExpert.Com gives summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. Mushroom World, an aggregator, says it fruits throughout the year and is most commonly seen in autumn. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc, mushroomworld]*
