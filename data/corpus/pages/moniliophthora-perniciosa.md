## Summary

Moniliophthora perniciosa (Stahel) Aime & Phillips-Mora, 2006, in the family Marasmiaceae, was previously placed in Crinipellis as Crinipellis perniciosa. According to Wikipedia, it is the fungus that causes witches' broom disease of the cocoa tree (Theobroma cacao). The same source describes it as currently limited to South America, Panama and the Caribbean. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the fungus was first recorded in the Brazilian Amazon in 1785 and is thought to have co-evolved with cocoa in its centre of origin. It notes the earlier placement in Crinipellis but gives no account of the reasons for the transfer. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes M. perniciosa as a plant pathogen (hemibiotroph) that evolved in the Amazon, with susceptible hosts being tropical plants of rain forests, particularly in the Upper Amazon River basin on the eastern side of the Andes. The economically important C-biotype infects species of Theobroma and Herrania (Malvaceae). Other biotypes are reported on a liana (Arrabidaea verrucosa, Bignoniaceae) in Ecuador and on Solanaceae, including Solanum rugosum, in Brazil. Wikipedia states that the disease favors humid, warm tropical weather. Spores are wind-dispersed but must land in water to germinate, so spread is mainly during rainy periods. This is a single-cluster account (Wikipedia, which iNaturalist copies in part); GBIF's only descriptive entry lists Brazil as a distribution record. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
