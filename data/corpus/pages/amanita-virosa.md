## Summary

Amanita virosa Bertill. (1866) is a species of Amanitaceae known as the destroying angel or European destroying angel. Its fruit bodies are pure white with a ring on the stipe and a sack-like volva at the base. It is reported from Europe and northern Asia, and Wikipedia describes it as highly toxic. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states the specific epithet is derived from the Latin adjective virōsus, meaning 'toxic'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries first described the species in 1838 as Agaricus virosus, but that name is illegitimate because it had already been used for an earlier and different species. Louis-Adolphe Bertillon legitimately published Amanita virosa in 1866. Wikipedia says the species was originally described from Sweden. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports it in woodland in late summer and autumn, especially with beech and chestnut but also with pine, spruce and fir, forming an ectomycorrhizal relationship with these trees. It is reported as known throughout Europe, with confirmed records from northern Asia (China). Mushroom World describes it as growing solitary or scattered on soil in broad-leaf or mixed woods. Wikipedia notes the name was formerly used for similar-looking North American agarics that research has shown to be distinct species. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
