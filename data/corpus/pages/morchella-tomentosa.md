## Summary

Morchella tomentosa M. Kuo (2008) is a morel in the family Morchellaceae, known in English as the gray morel, fuzzy foot morel or black foot morel. Sources describe it as a fire-associated species from western North America. Young caps and stems are densely covered in fine hairs, which gives a fuzzy, sooty look, and colour shifts from black toward gray, brownish, yellowish or whitish with age. The name "gray morel" is also used by collectors in eastern North America for a different group of morels, so the common name alone does not identify this species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was formally described as new to science in 2008; MushroomExpert.Com, written by the describing author Michael Kuo, cites Kuo 2008 and lists a holotype collection. MushroomExpert.Com also reports that the name "Morchella atrotomentosa" had been applied to this species by McKnight & McKnight (1987). It calls that name invalid for several reasons and says it represents a European mushroom that may or may not be the same. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as growing alone, scattered or gregariously in burned conifer forests in western North America (Colorado to northern California and Alaska). It fruits primarily in the spring following the fire, sometimes in dwindling numbers for a few years afterward, and usually at high elevation. It says the species is possibly saprobic and mycorrhizal at different points in its life cycle. A GBIF-hosted description reports eight specimens from a burnt pine forest on a mountainside at 1,130 m elevation, at Arischia. MushroomExpert.Com also mentions unvouchered photos of an apparent match from a jack pine burn in Michigan's Upper Peninsula in 2008, which it says cannot be documented because no specimen was preserved. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
