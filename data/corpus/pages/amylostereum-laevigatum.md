## Summary

Amylostereum laevigatum (Fr.) Boidin, 1958, is a crust fungus in the family Echinodontiaceae (order Russulales), with the English name "Yew Duster". It is recorded from many countries, mainly in Europe and North America. Published work on it in the supplied sources is mostly about its association with horntail wasps and conifer hosts. *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_2]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was originally named Thelephora laevigata by Elias Fries in 1828, and that French mycologist Jacques Boidin transferred it to the genus Amylostereum in 1958. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports the fungus from Norway, Sweden, France, Switzerland, Canada and the United States, on Abies, Juniperus, Taxus and Thuja. Two Mycoscience papers from Japan describe it as associated with horntails: in Kochi, Kagawa and Ehime Prefectures it was isolated from the mycangia of adult female Urocerus japonicus, and in Ibaraki, Kochi and Nagasaki Prefectures from the mycangia of adult female Urocerus antennatus. The 1997 paper reports basidiocarps on felled logs of Cryptomeria japonica and calls this the first record of the species from Japan. In its inoculation experiments, Chamaecyparis obtusa and Cryptomeria japonica trees inoculated with isolates showed wood discoloration, and the fungus was reisolated from the discolored areas. Wikipedia describes the horntail as injecting the fungus into sapwood when the female lays eggs beneath the bark. *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_2]*
