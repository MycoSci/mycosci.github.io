## Summary

Leucocoprinus cretaceus (Bull.) Locq. is a species in the family Agaricaceae, combined into Leucocoprinus by Locquin in 1945. Wikipedia describes it as likely tropical in origin but first documented in Europe, where it was found in greenhouses and bark beds. MushroomExpert.Com calls it a fairly uncommon little lepiotoid mushroom that appears in greenhouses, with potted plants, and in planters. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet cretaceus means 'chalky' and comes from the Latin creta, 'chalk'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus cretaceus by Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard in 1788. Bulliard illustrated it as 'L'Agaric Cretacé' in Herbier de la France, called it fluffy and as white as chalk, and reported finding it only in hot greenhouses and planters under cold frames. Wikipedia adds that his illustration may be more consistent with Leucocoprinus cepistipes. Later treatments listed it as a variant of Agaricus cepaestipes (Persoon, 1801) and as Coprinus cepaestipes var. cretaceus (Gray, 1821). Marcel Locquin made the combination Leucocoprinus cretaceus in 1945. Moser placed it in Leucoagaricus in 1953. Wikipedia says the species is still often conflated with L. cepistipes because of source material based on outdated classifications, and that several early descriptions filed under its synonyms may actually describe other species. This account rests on a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as growing in clusters on woodchips, sawdust and compost heaps, and also directly from the ground or on trees. It is documented in plant pots and greenhouses. Wikipedia describes it as widespread in tropical areas, with a scattered distribution in South America. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing alone, in groups, or in loose clusters in potted plants, greenhouses and planters. It reports fruiting year-round, and says the North American distribution is uncertain but apparently uncommon, more likely in tropical and subtropical areas. The collections it illustrates and describes are from California and Florida. Bulliard, as reported by Wikipedia, noted the mushrooms in July and August in hot greenhouses and planters. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
