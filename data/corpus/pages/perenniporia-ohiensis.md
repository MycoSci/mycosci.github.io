## Summary

Perenniporia ohiensis (Berk.) Ryvarden, 1972, is a polypore in the family Polyporaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a tough, woody polypore with an ivory-white pore surface, either resupinate (without a cap) or, more commonly, with a hoof-shaped cap that darkens from whitish to brownish and eventually black with age. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (a single source) describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods, common on fence posts and rails (especially those of locust wood), and causing a white rot. It is perennial and found year-round (especially in warmer climates), though generally appearing in summer and fall. Kuo describes it as fairly widely distributed in North America from the East Coast to the Rocky Mountains and the Southwest, but apparently absent or rare on the West Coast and in the Pacific Northwest. *[mushroomexpert]*
