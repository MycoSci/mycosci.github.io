## Summary

Calostoma cinnabarinum Desv. (1809) is a gasteroid fungus in the order Boletales, with a bright red spore case surrounded by a yellowish jelly layer and set atop a spongy stalk. It is reported from the Americas and East Asia. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

The specific epithet cinnabarinum is derived from the Ancient Greek kinnábari (κιννάβαρι) and refers to the fungus's cinnabar-red colour. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Persoon gave the first modern description in 1809, as Scleroderma callostoma. Later that year Desvaux created the genus Calostoma and, to avoid a tautonymous name, called the type species C. cinnabarinum. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Ectomycorrhizal; the only host trees identified are Quercus oaks (Wilson et al. 2007, and Wikipedia's summary of it). MushroomExpert.Com describes it as growing alone or gregariously, often in moss beds or in low-lying wet areas, from spring through fall. Wikipedia describes fruiting as most common in late summer and fall, with spring occurrences known, and says the species appears restricted to wetter forests. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_1]*
