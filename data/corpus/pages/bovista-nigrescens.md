## Summary

Bovista nigrescens Pers. (1794) is a puffball in the family Lycoperdaceae, with its type locality in Great Britain. Wikipedia describes the fruit body as 3–6 cm across and roughly spherical, attached to the substrate by a single mycelial cord. The white outer wall flakes off in large scales at maturity, exposing a dark purple-brown to blackish inner wall, and spores leave through an apical pore. Mushroom World, which overlaps heavily with Wikipedia, gives the same description. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that Bovista nigrescens is often found in grass and pastureland, and also on the ground, in fields, on lawns and on roadsides, at altitudes up to 2,500 m. It says fruiting is most abundant in late summer to autumn and that old, dried fruit bodies persist for many months. It describes the species as uncommon in most areas but frequent in North and West Europe. Mushroom World gives a similar account (grass and pastureland, late summer to autumn, North and West Europe), but it belongs to the same source cluster as Wikipedia and is not independent confirmation. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
