## Summary

Trametes versicolor (L.) Lloyd, 1920, is a polypore in the family Polyporaceae, recorded in GBIF occurrence data from dozens of countries. Wikipedia describes it as a common polypore found throughout the world, and it is commonly called turkey tail. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the meaning of the epithet versicolor as 'of several colors' and says it describes the fungus's blend of markings. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the common name turkey tail comes from the fungus's shape resembling a wild turkey's tail feathers. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) likewise says it looks vaguely like a turkey's tail. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it commonly grows in tiled layers, in groups or rows, on logs and stumps of deciduous trees, and that it is a white rot fungus. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods and rarely on conifer wood. It is annual, causes a white rot of the sapwood, and grows in dense overlapping clusters or rosettes on logs and stumps. Kuo gives its season as year-round and calls it very widely distributed and common in North America; his described collections are from Illinois. A GBIF-hosted description citing Ryvarden (2016) gives 'growing on deciduous wood' and a worldwide distribution. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
