## Summary

Bovista aestivalis (Bonord.) Demoulin 1979 is a species of small puffball in the family Lycoperdaceae. Wikipedia describes the fruit body as 1.5–3.0 cm broad, spherical to cushion-shaped, with a white exoperidium that breaks up into buff, fine warts and exposes a thin brown endoperidium with age. Spores are released through a ragged pore at the top. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet, aestivalis, is derived from Latin and means "pertaining to the summer". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1851 by the German mycologist Hermann Friedrich Bonorden as Lycoperdon aestivale, and Vincent Demoulin transferred it to the genus Bovista in 1979. No source supplied here gives the material it was described from or the reasons for the transfer. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (a single source) describes it as generally scattered or in small groups on the edges of grassy areas along paths, and also in coastal dunes among herbs and shrubs. It says the species is generally found in the coastal regions of California and was reported from Korea in 2015. The same source says that when watered, it fruits in the summer months, and through autumn and winter after periods of rain. The GBIF description record lists distribution entries including Belgium (Flanders), Brazil, Denmark, Norway, Madeira Island, Sweden and Vermont, with no habitat or substrate text. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
