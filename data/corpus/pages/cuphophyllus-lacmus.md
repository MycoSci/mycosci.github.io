## Summary

Cuphophyllus lacmus (Schumach.) Bon, 1985, is a species of agaric (gilled mushroom) in the family Hygrophoraceae, with the recommended English name grey waxcap. Wikipedia describes it as having a European distribution, occurring mainly in agriculturally unimproved grassland, and reports it assessed as globally "vulnerable" on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species because of threats to that habitat. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was first described from Denmark in 1803 by the naturalist Heinrich Schumacher as Agaricus lacmus, and was transferred to the genus Cuphophyllus by the French mycologist Marcel Bon in 1985. It also reports that molecular (DNA-based) research has confirmed C. lacmus as a distinct species, but within a group that includes Cuphophyllus subviolaceus and that requires further research. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the grey waxcap as widespread but generally rare throughout Europe, and also known from Greenland. It states that, like most other European waxcaps, the species occurs in old, agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns). This is a single-source account (Wikipedia; iNaturalist repeats its opening text). *[wikipedia]*
