## Summary

Austroboletus gracilis (Peck) Wolfe is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, originally described as Boletus gracilis by Charles Horton Peck in 1872. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a slender-stemmed bolete that is mycorrhizal with eastern hemlock and other conifers in eastern North America. A GBIF record lists a distribution in Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com says the species is, "as its name suggests, graceful in stature," with a stem that is typically long and slender. No source gives a formal derivation of the epithet. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Peck originally described the species as Boletus gracilis in 1872, and Carl B. Wolfe later transferred it to Austroboletus. MushroomExpert.Com notes that it was previously placed in Tylopilus and is called "Tylopilus gracilis" in many field guides. The same source suggests that several distinct species may be included under the current concept of A. gracilis. It reports that the species is highly variable in color and that its stem is sometimes reticulate and sometimes not. It also says some authors recognize var. flavipes (more yellowish, slightly smaller spores, no stem reticulation) and var. pulcherripes (deeper colors, shorter and coarsely reticulate stem). Whether these varieties correspond to phylogenetic differences remains to be seen. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with eastern hemlock and other conifers, growing alone or scattered in summer and fall. It is fairly widely distributed in eastern North America but more common in the north and in the Appalachians. The collections described and illustrated there are from Michigan and Pennsylvania. A GBIF description record lists a distribution in Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
