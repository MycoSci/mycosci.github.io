## Summary

Amanita abrupta Peck (1897) is a white, warty Amanita in the family Amanitaceae, described from Alabama and recorded mainly in eastern North America. It is named for its abruptly swollen stem base. English Wikipedia describes it as possibly toxic and considers it inedible. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet abrupta refers to the shape of the swollen base, which is abruptly enlarged rather than gradually tapering. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Charles Horton Peck first described the species in 1897 from a specimen found in Auburn, Alabama. Because the volva remains are not present on the bulb of dried, mature specimens, Peck thought it should be grouped with Amanita rubescens and A. spissa. Jean-Edouard Gilbert later transferred it to Lepidella (1928) and Aspidella (1940); both genera have since been subsumed into Amanita. Wikipedia also says A. abrupta is the type species of section Lepidella. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the fruit bodies grow on the ground, typically solitary, in mixed conifer and deciduous forests, usually in autumn. It describes the species as widely distributed in eastern North America and thought to be mycorrhizal, and it cites Tulloss for beech, birch, fir, tsuga, oak and poplar as preferred hosts. It adds that the species was shown experimentally not to form mycorrhizae with Virginia Pine. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) gives the ecology as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers, in summer and fall, and widely distributed in eastern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
