## Summary

Trametes gibbosa (Pers.) Fr. 1838, the lumpy bracket, is a polypore in the family Polyporaceae. Sources describe it as a saprobic bracket fungus on dead hardwoods that causes a white rot. It is recorded across Eurasia and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia links the common name "lumpy bracket" to the lumpy, uneven upper surface of the fruit body; no source narrates how the name arose beyond this. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) states the species was originally described from Europe (Persoon 1795), with Fries's 1838 combination cited. It also reports that North American collections were long misidentified as Trametes elegans. Mating studies by Kout & Vlasak (2007) found two elegans-like species in North America. One paired consistently with Czech collections of T. gibbosa. The other is not T. elegans, which the source describes as tropical, and it currently lacks a valid name (referred to there as Trametes species 01). This account is from a single source. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes T. gibbosa as saprobic on dead hardwoods, found on beech stumps and the dead wood of other hardwood species, and says it grows year round. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods, annual, causing a white rot of the sapwood, and growing alone or gregariously on logs and stumps, from spring through fall. The two sources differ on season. Both give the range as Eurasia and North America, in North America from the Great Plains eastward and in the Pacific Northwest. MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as Eurasian and introduced to North America, where it is said to be spreading quickly. Wikipedia notes that the fruiting bodies are frequently attacked by boring beetle larvae. GBIF's description record lists distribution entries for Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Madeira, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
