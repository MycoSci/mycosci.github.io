## Summary

Suillus lakei (Murrill) A.H. Sm. & Thiers, 1964, is a bolete in the family Suillaceae. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as growing in mycorrhizal association with Douglas-fir. Wikipedia gives its native range as the Rocky Mountains and western North America and notes that it has been introduced elsewhere along with Douglas-fir. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

According to Wikipedia, Murrill named the species Boletinus lakei after the mycologist E.R. Lake of Oregon Agricultural College (now Oregon State University). This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that William Alphonso Murrill first named the species Boletinus lakei, from a type specimen that E.R. Lake collected at Corvallis, Oregon, in late November 1907. MushroomExpert.Com cites the original description as Murrill 1912, from Oregon. According to Wikipedia, Rolf Singer later transferred the species to Ixocomus (1940) and Boletinus (1945). Smith and Thiers moved it to Suillus in their 1964 monograph of North American Suillus species. Wikipedia adds that Smith and Thiers described the variety pseudopictus in the same work, and that Index Fungorum and MycoBank treat the later variety landkammeri as a synonym of S. lakei. MushroomExpert.Com notes that authors disagree on whether a dry-capped form with slightly smaller spores belongs under pseudopictus or should be lumped with S. lakei. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe S. lakei as mycorrhizal with Douglas-fir (Pseudotsuga menziesii). MushroomExpert.Com says it grows only in association with this tree, both within the tree's natural range and where it has been introduced. Wikipedia says fruit bodies grow solitarily or in groups on the ground, in young conifer stands or grassy parkland. It also says the fungus has been noted to prefer poor, exposed soil such as road banks and campgrounds. Both sources place fruiting in late summer and autumn in the Rocky Mountains. For the West Coast, Wikipedia says fruiting is later, and MushroomExpert.Com says fall and winter. Both sources also say the fungus is found in Europe and elsewhere, where Douglas-fir has been introduced. Wikipedia lists Argentina, Chile and the South Island of New Zealand among these places. In the Rocky Mountains, MushroomExpert.Com reports it growing alongside Gomphidius subroseus, another Douglas-fir associate. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
