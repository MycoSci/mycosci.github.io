## Summary

Agaricus subrutilescens (Kauffman) Hotson & D.E. Stuntz, the wine-colored agaricus, is a mushroom in the family Agaricaceae. Wikipedia describes the cap as 5–15 cm across, dry, with many wine to brown fibrils, especially near the center. It describes the stalk as white with a skirt-like ring and soft woolly scales below the ring. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described scientifically in 1925 as Psalliota subrutilescens and was transferred to Agaricus in 1938. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the mushroom fruits in undisturbed mixed woods in Western North America and Japan. It grows by itself or scattered in small clusters, often under redwood, pine, or alder. Wikipedia adds that it has recently been identified in New Zealand and Australia. *[wikipedia]*
