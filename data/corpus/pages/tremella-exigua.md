## Summary

Tremella exigua Desm. (1847) is a species of fungus in the family Tremellaceae, originally described from France. According to Wikipedia, it produces small, dark, pustular, gelatinous basidiocarps and is parasitic on pyrenomycetous fungi (Diaporthe and Cucurbitaria species) on dead branches of trees and shrubs. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Desmazières first published the species in 1847, based on a collection from France on a dead branch of ash (Fraxinus excelsior). It reports that Elias Magnus Fries had earlier described Agyrium atrovirens from Sweden on the same host tree, a species interpreted as synonymous with T. exigua. The name is not available in Tremella, because the combination Tremella atrovirens is an illegitimate homonym of the earlier, unrelated T. atrovirens Bull. Wikipedia also lists Tremella genistae (described from Belgium on broom) as a further synonym, and says the name Tremella virescens Schumach. has been used for this species but its interpretation is doubtful. It adds that initial molecular research suggests T. exigua is not closely related to Tremella sensu stricto and belongs in a separate, as yet unnamed, genus in the family Bulleraceae. All of this comes from a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes T. exigua as a parasite on lignicolous pyrenomycetes, including species of Diaporthe and Cucurbitaria. It says the species was originally described from ash but is more commonly found on dead branches of gorse (Ulex europaeus), broom (Cytisus scoparius) and barberry (Berberis vulgaris). Wikipedia states it has been widely recorded in Europe and also reported from Canada and Ecuador. The GBIF record lists a distribution of Denmark, Norway and Sweden. The habitat description rests on a single independent source (Wikipedia). No season is stated in any supplied source. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
