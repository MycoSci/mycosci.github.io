## Summary

Lactarius argillaceifolius is a species of milkcap in the family Russulaceae, described by Hesler and A.H. Smith in 1979 with its type locality in Michigan. Wikipedia describes it as having a convex to flattened drab lilac cap and cream-colored gills, and as producing off-white latex that stains tissue brownish. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks in eastern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by Lexemuel Ray Hesler and Alexander H. Smith in their 1979 monograph on the North American species of Lactarius. The type specimen was collected by Smith from Oak Grove, Livingston County, Michigan, in July 1972, and is housed at the University of Michigan Herbarium. Hesler and Smith published the varieties dissimilis (from South Carolina) and megacarpus (from California) at the same time. MushroomExpert.Com says var. dissimilis was based on a single collection. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe the species as mycorrhizal with oaks. Wikipedia says fruit bodies grow scattered or in groups on the ground under hardwoods, especially oak, from July to October, and that it is often one of the first mycorrhizal mushrooms to fruit in oak and hickory forests. MushroomExpert.Com gives the season as spring, summer and fall, and the range as widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains. Wikipedia says it is found from eastern Canada south to Florida and west to Minnesota and Texas, and that it has been reported from pine (Pinus elliottii) plantations in southern Brazil, where it is probably introduced. Variety megacarpus is reported from the Pacific Coast states and Baja California under coast live oak and tanoak. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
