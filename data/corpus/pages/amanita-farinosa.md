## Summary

Amanita farinosa Schwein. (1822) is a small, ringless species in the family Amanitaceae, with the type locality given as North Carolina. Wikipedia describes it as a poisonous North American mushroom. The cap and stem are dusted with fine mealy powder. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com notes that A. farinosa in the sense of Thiers (1982) is a much larger and stockier West Coast mushroom that is probably a separate species, and that a similar western species is probably undescribed. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as uncommon, found in eastern North America from summer to autumn among the underwood of hardwood trees. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers, sometimes in urban grassy areas near trees, growing alone, scattered or gregariously from early spring to late fall, and widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains from Quebec to Costa Rica. A GBIF record lists a distribution in Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
