## Summary

Clavulinopsis helvola (Pers.) Corner, with the combination published in 1950, is a clavarioid fungus in the family Clavariaceae. Wikipedia describes it as forming slender, cylindrical, yellow fruiting bodies up to 60 by 3 mm. It is widely distributed in Europe and has also been reported from India, Japan, China, the United States and Brazil. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was originally described from Europe in 1797 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon, and that E.J.H. Corner placed it in the genus Clavulinopsis in 1950. In 1978 Ron Petersen revised the clavarioid genera and placed it in Ramariopsis, a move followed by some later researchers. Wikipedia also notes a recent epitype from Slovakia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow on the ground, singly or in small clusters, in agriculturally unimproved grassland or in woodland litter, and that the species is presumed to be saprotrophic. In Europe it is said generally to occur in agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns), while in America and Asia it grows in woodland. This habitat account rests on a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
