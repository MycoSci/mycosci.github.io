## Summary

Marasmiellus affixus (Berk.) Singer, 1973, now placed by Index Fungorum as Collybiopsis affixa, is a species in the family Omphalotaceae. Wikipedia gives its common name as the "little stinker" and describes it as occurring in Asia and Australia. Some researchers have treated it as a basidiolichen, but that status remains unproven. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the species is colloquially known as the "little stinker" because of its characteristic odour, described as resembling "strong rotting cabbage" or "old-wet-nappy". It also reports that the Dayak ethnic group uses the local name kulat kerang, which translates to "shell mushroom" or "shell fungus". Both statements come from a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Miles Joseph Berkeley described the species in 1848 as Agaricus affixus. The type was collected in 1846 by Ronald Campbell Gunn from Penquite at Launceston, Tasmania, where it was growing on the bark of a young Eucalyptus amygdalina tree. It was later moved to Marasmius, Pleurotus, Chamaeceras and Dendrosarcus. Rolf Singer transferred it to Marasmiellus in 1973, and it was generally known by that name for about five decades. Following a molecular-phylogenetics-led resurrection and restructuring of Collybiopsis, Jerry Cooper transferred it to that genus in 2023. Wikipedia gives this account; Wikispecies independently lists the same synonyms and the 1973 Singer combination. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to Wikipedia, in Tasmania (Australia) it grows in sclerophyll forest on dead woody material, especially wood that has lost its bark. It has also been recorded in cool-temperate rainforest growing on dead fronds of Dicksonia antarctica. Its Australian distribution also includes Queensland, South Australia and Victoria, and it has been reported from India and from North Sumatra (Indonesia). This habitat information comes from a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
