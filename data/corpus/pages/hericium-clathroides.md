## Summary

Hericium clathroides (Pall.) Pers. (1797) is a species in the family Hericiaceae, order Russulales, with the English vernacular name Coral Tooth. Its circumscription has been contested. Some authors separate it from H. coralloides on the basis of substrate, and others have doubted that the two are distinct species. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Hydnum clathroides by Peter Simon Pallas in the second volume of Reise durch verschiedene Provinzen des rußischen Reichs (1773), and Christiaan Hendrik Persoon moved it to Hericium in 1797. Fries later treated it in Merisma within Hydnum. In 1959 Rudolf Arnold Maas Geesteranus argued that Fries had misapplied the epithet. Until then, specimens on fir (Abies) had traditionally been called H. clathroides. Maas Geesteranus assigned the name to the species fruiting on beech (Fagus) and proposed classifying the fir-inhabiting species as H. coralloides. Domański, Marchand and Jahn accepted the new definitions. Nils Hallenberg's 1983 analysis of European Hericium, which included interbreeding experiments with beech and fir forms, doubted the separation of the two species and proposed a neotype for H. coralloides. Some authors, such as Władysław Wojewoda, followed him. Wikipedia notes that Index Fungorum considers the taxon verified. All of this comes from a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that some authors distinguish H. clathroides from H. coralloides by substrate. On that view H. clathroides grows on beech wood (rarely other hardwoods), and H. coralloides is additionally said to bear its hymenophore spines in tufts. Wikipedia also reports that specimens on fir (Abies) were traditionally called H. clathroides, and that Hallenberg doubted the separation of the beech and fir forms. The substrate is therefore tied to a disputed species boundary. This is a single-source statement, and no source supplied here gives a season or a region. *[wikipedia]*
