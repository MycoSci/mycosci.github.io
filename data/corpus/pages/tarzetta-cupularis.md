## Summary

Tarzetta cupularis (L.) Lambotte 1887 is a small cup fungus in the family Tarzettaceae, recorded from many countries, mainly in Europe and North America. Wikipedia describes it as brown to cream-coloured flask-shaped cups up to 2 cm across and 2.5 cm tall, while MushroomExpert.Com gives a goblet-shaped cup 5-15 mm across with an indistinct stem-like base. MushroomExpert.Com also notes that several tiny Tarzetta species may be passing under this name. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a species of northern Europe with occasional records from Spain and Morocco, also occurring in North America, appearing from spring to autumn in groups in damp woodland. MushroomExpert.Com (describing North American material) lists it as terrestrial, growing alone, gregariously or in small clusters, often reported from burn sites and also from moss or bare soil under conifers or hardwoods, in late spring and early summer. It calls the ecology uncertain and cites a 2006 investigation suggesting that at least some Tarzetta species are mycorrhizal. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
