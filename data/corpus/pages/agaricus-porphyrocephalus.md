## Summary

Agaricus porphyrocephalus F.H. Møller (1952) is a species in the Agaricaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a brownish, fibrillose mushroom with gills that are pink before turning dark brown, a flimsy quickly collapsing ring, and a dark brown spore print. That source records it from lawns and meadows in eastern North America and reports it as widespread in Europe. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was originally described from Denmark (Møller 1952). It also states that, despite resembling members of the Agaricus campestris group, its ITS sequences clearly separate it from them. The same source treats a white variety, Agaricus porphyrocephalus var. pallidus, as very similar to campestris-group species and best separated from them by its smaller spores. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (single-source) describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in meadows, fields, lawns, and grassy areas, in fall. It reports the species as widespread in Europe and in North America east of the Great Plains, with the illustrated and described collections coming from Illinois, New Jersey, and North Carolina. *[mushroomexpert]*
