## Summary

Aleuria aurantia (Pers.) Fuckel (1870) is a cup fungus in the family Pyronemataceae, known in English as the orange peel fungus. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe bright orange, cup-shaped fruiting bodies that are often irregular or flattened where they grow in clusters. GBIF records the species from dozens of countries, mainly in Europe and North America. A fucose-specific lectin from its fruiting bodies has been purified and sequenced in the biochemical literature. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet as the Latin word aurantia, meaning "orange". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the bright orange, cup-shaped fruiting bodies often resemble orange peels strewn on the ground, which gave the species its common name. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Christiaan Hendrik Persoon described the species as Peziza aurantia in 1800, and Karl Wilhelm Gottlieb Leopold Fuckel placed it in the genus Aleuria in 1870. Wikispecies lists it as the type species of Aleuria. MushroomExpert.Com notes that Aleuria wisconsinensis, described by Rehm (1904) from Wisconsin, is a synonym according to Haffner (1993). *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the fungus fruits mainly on bare clay or disturbed soil. It gives the North American season as November to March in the West and May to November in the East, and says the fungus is also found in south Chile and Europe. MushroomExpert.Com says it usually grows in clusters on the ground, often in clayey soil or disturbed ground such as roadbanks and trails. It also reports the species in urban landscaping and occasionally on woodchips. It gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates, and says the species is traditionally labeled saprobic but possibly mycorrhizal (Hobbie et al. 2001). Mushroom World, an aggregator, describes clusters on bare soil in wooded areas or among low grasses, often on gravelly ground. These three sources differ in detail, and each statement above is single-source. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
