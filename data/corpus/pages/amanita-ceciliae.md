## Summary

Amanita ceciliae (Berk. & Broome) Bas is a species in the family Amanitaceae. It was first described by Berkeley and Broome in 1854 and given its current name by Bas in 1984. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a ringless Amanita with a lined brown cap bearing grey patches of universal veil. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia, the only source that discusses the name, says the common name 'Cecilia's ringless amanita' is after Cecilia Berkeley, wife of M. J. Berkeley. It quotes the intent of the name as 'to record the services which have been rendered to Mycology by many excellent illustrations and in other ways'. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says 'strangulated amanita' refers to the tightly clasping volva, and that 'Cecilia's ringless amanita' is after Cecilia Berkeley, wife of M. J. Berkeley. No supplied source narrates the origin of 'snakeskin grisette'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Per Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1854 by Miles Joseph Berkeley and Christopher Edmund Broome and placed in section Vaginatae. The name Amanita inaurata, given by Louis Secretan in 1833, was also used for it. In 1978 that name was declared nomenclaturally incorrect under the botanical code, and Cornelis Bas gave the present name in 1984. MushroomExpert.Com calls Amanita inaurata and Amanita strangulata probable synonyms. It says the 'real' A. ceciliae is European, and that eastern North American material under this name looks like a large group of potentially separate species. Wikipedia likewise notes speculation that North American collections may be an undescribed species differing from the European one. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods and conifers, growing alone, scattered or in groups in summer and autumn. Wikipedia reports that in Europe it often occurs in deciduous forest with hornbeam, oak, beech and birch, rarely with pine, fir, spruce and cedar, and prefers neutral to calcareous soils. For North America, Wikipedia gives mainly east of the Mississippi River, with reports from the Pacific Northwest, the Southwest and Texas (apparently associated with pecan). MushroomExpert.Com gives 'east of the Rocky Mountains' for the species group. GBIF description records list the habitat as mature forest ('Bosque maduro'). A 2010-2011 survey listed the species in Hirpora Wildlife Sanctuary, Kashmir, among ectomycorrhizal macrofungi. A separate laboratory study achieved in vitro ectomycorrhizal synthesis with Pinus gerardiana seedlings. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc, lit_3, lit_1]*
