## Summary

Hygrophorus eburneus (Bull.) Fr. is a species in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the ivory waxy cap or cowboy's handkerchief. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as medium-sized and pure white, and covered in a layer of slime when wet, and it names the species as the type species of the genus Hygrophorus. Records span Europe and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet eburneus is a Latin adjective meaning "of ivory". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia (a single-cluster source), the species was first named Agaricus eburneus by Jean Bulliard in 1783. Elias Fries later divided Agaricus into tribes and placed A. eburneus in the tribe Limacium. Samuel Frederick Gray named it Gymnopus eburneus in 1821. Paul Kummer named it Limacium eburneum in 1871, when he raised Fries's tribes to the rank of genus. Wikipedia states that H. eburneus is the type species of Hygrophorus, in section Hygrophorus, subsection Hygrophorus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (a single independent cluster) states that the fruit bodies grow on the soil, mostly in coniferous woods, thickets and grassy areas. It says the fungus prefers moist, mesic, loamy, calcareous soil. It also says that, like all Hygrophorus species, the fungus is mycorrhizal with tree roots. Wikipedia describes the species as widely distributed in North America and also found in Europe (Poland and Portugal), Israel and North Africa. The GBIF description record lists distribution entries for Belgium (including the Brussels-Capital, Flemish and Walloon regions), Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. No source supplied here states a fruiting season. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
