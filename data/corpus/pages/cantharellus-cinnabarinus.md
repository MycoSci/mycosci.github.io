## Summary

Cantharellus cinnabarinus (Schwein.) Schwein., 1832, is a chanterelle in the family Hydnaceae, known in English as the red chanterelle or cinnabar chanterelle. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as small, with a flamingo-pink to cinnabar-red cap 1–4 cm wide and false gills that run down the stem. Both MushroomExpert.Com and Wikipedia describe it as widely distributed in eastern North America. MushroomExpert.Com also notes a nearly identical species, Cantharellus texensis, described from Texas in 2011 on the basis of DNA and microscopic differences. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species is named after cinnabar, which has a similar red color. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, especially beech and oaks but also shagbark hickory and big-toothed aspen. It grows alone, scattered, or gregariously, in summer and fall, and is widely distributed in eastern North America. Its illustrated collections are from Michigan, Québec, and Illinois. Wikipedia, in a separate source cluster, describes it as fruiting from June to October, mostly on the ground in broadleaf and mixed broadleaf/conifer forests, usually scattered or in small groups. It also says the species prefers acidic soils. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
