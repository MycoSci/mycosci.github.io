## Summary

Geastrum melanocephalum (Czern.) V.J. Staněk (1956) is an earthstar fungus in the family Geastraceae. Wikipedia reports that it is on the Red List of threatened macrofungi in Poland, where it is listed as in danger of extinction. The same source describes its inner spore sac (endoperidium) as evanescent, so that the dark spore mass is left fully exposed as the fruiting body matures. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

According to Wikipedia, the epithet melanocephalum derives from the Ancient Greek words for "black" (melas) and "head" (kephale). It refers to the dark gleba that is exposed when the endoperidium ruptures. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described as Trichaster melanocephalus by Vassilii Czernajew in 1845, from specimens collected in Ukraine, and that Czernajew made it the type of the genus Trichaster. In 1913 László Hollós questioned this treatment and suggested that T. melanocephalus was only an abnormal form of Geastrum triplex. In 1925 Heinrich Lohwag compared Geastrum and Trichaster and showed the species to be distinct, though he did not treat it as a separate genus. Wikipedia says the species was moved into Geastrum in 1956, giving the combination Geastrum melanocephalum. It adds that molecular phylogenetic analyses place the species within Geastrum, most closely related to European and North American populations of G. triplex. This account comes from a single independent source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes G. melanocephalum as primarily a Eurasian species, occurring across Europe and into Central and Eastern Asia. It reports the species as typically growing in well-drained, calcareous, sandy, alkaline environments, in open habitats rather than dense forest. Wikipedia describes it as a saprotroph that does not appear to form mycorrhizal relationships. Collection sites it names include Belgium, Czechoslovakia, Denmark, Germany, Hungary, Poland, Serbia, Spain, Sweden and Ukraine in Europe, and Kazakhstan and the desert regions of Western China in Asia. All of this rests on one source (Wikipedia). No season or fruiting time is stated in any supplied source. *[wikipedia]*
