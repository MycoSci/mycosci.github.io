## Summary

Tyromyces chioneus (Fr.) P. Karst. 1881, the white cheese polypore, is a polypore in the family Incrustoporiaceae. Wikipedia says it was first described as Polyporus chioneus by Elias Fries in 1815 and moved to Tyromyces by Petter Karsten in 1881. Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a white-rot decomposer of dead hardwood, especially birch. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia (single source) states that the specific epithet chioneus means "snow", referring to the fungus's white color. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Polyporus chioneus by Elias Fries in 1815 and transferred to the genus Tyromyces by Petter Karsten in 1881. Wikipedia also states it is the type species of Tyromyces. MushroomExpert.Com lists Tyromyces albellus and Polyporus albellus as synonyms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as growing on dead hardwood and causing a white rot, with birch the most common host. Wikipedia reports it as common on Betula pubescens in Greenland. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, annual, growing alone or with two or three other fruiting bodies in summer and fall. It says the species is fairly widely distributed from the Great Plains to the Maritime provinces, apparently absent in the southeastern United States, and also reported in the Pacific Northwest and northern California. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
