## Summary

Suillus bovinus (L.) Roussel, the Jersey cow mushroom or bovine bolete, is a pored mushroom (bolete) in the family Suillaceae, order Boletales. Wikipedia describes it as a Eurasian species introduced to North America and Australia. It gives the cap as sticky, grey-yellow or ochre and 3–10 cm across, with a compound pore layer and a ringless stipe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin word bos, meaning "cattle", and that Suillus is an ancient term for fungi derived from the word "swine". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia lists "euro cow bolete" alongside Jersey cow mushroom and bovine bolete, and says the mushroom's colour is similar to that of a Jersey cow. It gives no further account of how the common names arose. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Carl Linnaeus first described the species as Boletus bovinus in the second volume of Species Plantarum (1753). Henri François Anne de Roussel later reclassified it into Suillus, of which it became the type species. Other listed transfers are Agaricus (Lamarck, 1783), Viscipellis (Quélet, 1886) and Ixocomus (Quélet, 1888). Josef Šutara placed it in Mariaella in 1987, but molecular studies do not support that genus, which is now treated as synonymous with Suillus. In 1951 Arthur Anselm Pearson described Boletus bovinus var. viridocaerulescens from Western Cape Province, South Africa, distinguished by flesh that turns greenish-blue on injury; Rolf Singer transferred it to Suillus in 1961, and Index Fungorum does not recognise it as independently significant. Wikipedia also reports that Suillus was moved from the Boletaceae to the newly circumscribed Suillaceae in 1997 on the basis of pigment chemistry and later molecular work. All of this comes from one source (the Wikipedia cluster). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that S. bovinus is found in conifer woods and plantations across Europe, including subalpine regions in the Alps up to about 800 m. It gives the preferred soils as often acidic and sandy, or sometimes calcareous and moraine. Associations named are Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris) in Lithuania, Japanese red pine (Pinus densiflora) in Japan and Pinus radiata in South Africa. It has been recorded in Taiwan and in several Chinese provinces. Where introduced, Wikipedia says it is thought to have arrived in North America with Scots pine and is found in the eastern United States. It has also been recorded infrequently under Scots pine in Australia and has been recorded in New Zealand. The species is described as mycorrhizal, forming sheaths around the roots of living trees. Mushroom World, an independent source, says it occurs often in large groups beneath pine trees, beside forest paths, in clearings and at woodland edges. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
