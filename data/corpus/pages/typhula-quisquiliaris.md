## Summary

Typhula quisquiliaris (Fr.) Henn. (1896) is a species of club fungus in the family Typhulaceae, known in English as the bracken club. Wikipedia describes it as producing small, white, club-shaped fruit bodies, each with a single distinct head and stem, with the stem attached to a sclerotium embedded in the substrate. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific name quisquiliaris as Latin meaning "pertaining to refuse". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by James Sowerby in 1803 as Clavaria obtusa. That name was illegitimate because Christiaan Hendrik Persoon had already used it for a different species in 1797. Elias Magnus Fries had named the species Clavaria quisquiliaris in 1818, and in his 1821 Systema Mycologicum he named it Pistillaria quisquiliaris, which is the name sanctioned there. Samuel Frederick Gray reclassified Sowerby's Clavaria obtusa as Geoglossum obtusum. Paul Christoph Hennings transferred the species to Typhula in 1896, giving the currently accepted name. Wikipedia adds that Pistillaria quisquiliaris was still sometimes used into the 20th century, for example by Carleton Rea in 1922. All of this rests on Wikipedia alone. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies are typically found in rows, growing from plant detritus. It says the species favours bracken, especially Pteridium aquilinum, and that colonisation of dead matter from other plants is not unknown. It describes the species as feeding saprotrophically on these substrates. In Europe, Wikipedia says the fruit bodies can be encountered from April to December. GBIF's description record lists a distribution of Belgium, Denmark, Norway and Sweden. The habitat and season details come from a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
