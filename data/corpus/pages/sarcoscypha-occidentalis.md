## Summary

Sarcoscypha occidentalis (Schwein.) Sacc. is a cup fungus in the family Sarcoscyphaceae (order Pezizales), known in English as the stalked scarlet cup or western scarlet cup. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as a small scarlet cup, up to about 2 cm across, on a slender stem, growing on hardwood sticks and twigs. MushroomExpert.Com notes that its small size means it is easily overlooked. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet occidentalis derives from the Latin word for "western", and says it may refer to the species' distribution in the Western Hemisphere. This is a single-source statement and is hedged in that source ("may refer to"). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was originally collected in Muskingum County, Ohio, and named Peziza occidentalis by Lewis David de Schweinitz in 1832; Pier Andrea Saccardo later assigned it its current name. Andrew Price Morgan renamed it Geopyxis occidentalis in 1902 because of a perceived similarity to Geopyxis hesperidea, but later authors did not adopt that name. In 1928 Fred Jay Seaver applied the genus name Plectania to Sarcoscypha coccinea and other red cup fungi, and Richard P. Korf later reinstated the genus name Sarcoscypha. MushroomExpert.Com's reference line is consistent with the Schweinitz 1832 basionym and a Saccardo combination. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as saprobic on decaying hardwood sticks and logs, sometimes on buried sticks (so that it appears terrestrial) and, apparently, sometimes on leaf litter. It gives the season as late spring through fall and the range as widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains. Wikipedia likewise describes it as saprobic on hardwood twigs, particularly partially buried ones in moist, shaded, humus-rich soil, fruiting from late spring to early autumn in the United States. Wikipedia places it in North America east of the Rocky Mountains, at higher elevations in Central America and the Caribbean, and reports collections from Japan and Taiwan. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
