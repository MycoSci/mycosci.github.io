## Summary

Guepinia helvelloides (DC.) Fr. (1828) is a jelly fungus in the order Auriculariales, known in English as apricot jelly or salmon salad. Wikipedia describes it as producing salmon-pink, ear-shaped, gelatinous fruit bodies, and MushroomExpert.Com describes a funnel-shaped or irregular pinkish-orange "cap" with rubbery flesh and a white spore print. GBIF holds occurrence records for it from many countries across Europe, North America and Asia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described and illustrated as Tremella rufa by Nicolaus Joseph von Jacquin in 1778. Elias Magnus Fries later (1828) called it Guepinia helvelloides in his Elenchus Fungorum, based on Augustin Pyramus de Candolle's Tremella helvelloides. Wikipedia reports that Lucien Quélet later erected the separate monotypic genus Phlogiotis for Jacquin's species, and that Julius Oscar Brefeld placed it as Gyrocephalus rufa in Persoon's genus Gyrocephalus. The same source says the correct genus name was debated for some time because Guepinia was a homonym: Toussaint Bastard had used it in 1812 for a genus of flowering plants. It reports that in 1982 changes to the International Code for Botanical Nomenclature gave protected status to names adopted by Fries in the Elenchus Fungorum, which established Guepinia as the correct genus name. This account comes from a single independent source cluster (Wikipedia). MushroomExpert.Com separately lists Phlogiotis helvelloides and Tremiscus helvelloides as synonyms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe the fungus as saprobic. Wikipedia says the fruit bodies grow solitarily or in small tufts on soil, usually in association with buried rotting wood, and are more commonly found in summer and autumn than in spring. It also says that in North America the fungus is associated with coniferous forests. MushroomExpert.Com says it grows on the ground or on well-rotted wood, almost always under conifers, in summer and fall (also winter in warmer climates). MushroomExpert.Com describes it as widespread in Europe and widely distributed in North America, more common in the Northeast, the northern Midwest, Mexico and the Pacific Northwest. It adds that the fungus is also found in the Caribbean and South America and is reported rarely from Asia. Wikipedia additionally lists Iran, Turkey, Brazil, Puerto Rico and the Qinling region of China. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
