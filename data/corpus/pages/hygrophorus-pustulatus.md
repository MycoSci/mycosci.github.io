## Summary

Hygrophorus pustulatus (Pers.) Fr. 1838 is a member of the Hygrophoraceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a grayish brown cap, white gills that begin to run down the stem, and a whitish stem covered in tiny brown dots. The same source says western North American material is more robust than eastern material and has larger spores, and that it may constitute a separate species. GBIF's distribution record lists Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com notes that Hygrophorus tephroleucus, sometimes considered a synonym of H. pustulatus, is separated on the basis of its more slender stature and less conspicuous stem ornamentation. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, particularly firs, and also reports an association with Engelmann spruce and with redwood. It fruits in late summer and fall. The same source calls it common from the Rocky Mountains westward and occasional in north-central and northeastern North America; the collection it illustrates is from Colorado. Mushroom World says it grows scattered or in groups on soil under conifers, especially firs, but its text closely tracks the MushroomExpert.Com description and adds no distinct habitat detail. *[mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
