## Summary

Lactarius pyrogalus (Bull.) Fr. is a milkcap in the family Russulaceae, described in Wikipedia as a greyish species with widely spaced gills. Both Wikipedia and Mushroom World associate it with hazel woodland. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet "pyrogalus" translates as "fire milk", referring to the species' very hot, acrid taste. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the English name "fire-milk lactarius" comes from the species' very hot, acrid taste. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described by Pierre Bulliard as Agaricus pyrogalus in 1792, and received its current name from Elias Magnus Fries in 1838. Wikispecies lists Agaricus pyrogalus Bull. as the basionym. It also lists later transfers to Galorrheus (Kummer, 1871) and Lactifluus (Kuntze, 1891). It records that uses of the name by Ricken and by Schaeffer (var. pyrogalus) are excluded and referred to Lactarius circellatus. None of the supplied sources give the reasons for these transfers. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as fairly common on the forest floor in mixed woodland, generally at the base of hazel trees, alone or in scattered groups. It says the species is particularly common in hazel woodland managed for coppice, and that it fruits in August, September and October. Mushroom World independently describes a strong association with hazel, most often in hazel woodland and coppice and occasionally along hedgerows, fruiting from late summer to autumn. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
