## Summary

Climacodon pulcherrimus (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Nikol., 1961, is a toothed fungus in the family Meruliaceae, recorded in GBIF occurrence data from many countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a hairy, shell-shaped cap that resembles a polypore from above, with densely packed spines on the underside. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Miles Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis first described the species as a Hydnum in 1849. MushroomExpert.Com adds that it was originally described from South Carolina. T. L. Nikolajeva later transferred it to Climacodon. Moreno and collaborators (2007) concluded that it does not belong in Climacodon, because other species in that genus have hymenial cystidia and lack multiple clamp connections. They did not transfer it to another genus. MushroomExpert.Com also lists Hydnum kauffmanii, described by Peck (1907) from Michigan, among its synonyms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods (occasionally conifers), causing a white rot. It grows alone, gregariously, or in shelving clusters, in summer. It is documented from eastern North America, Texas and New Mexico, but is described as apparently more common in tropical and subtropical areas. Wikipedia likewise describes it as widely distributed in subtropical and tropical areas, on decomposing hardwoods. A 2004 study reported it as a new record for the Atlantic Rain Forest of Northeast Brazil. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, lit_3]*
