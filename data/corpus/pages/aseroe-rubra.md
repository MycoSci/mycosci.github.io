## Summary

Aseroe rubra Labill. (1800) is a stinkhorn in the family Phallaceae, with its type locality in Tasmania. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe a stalk topped by a crown of forked arms, with a foul-smelling brown spore slime that attracts flies. It is recorded from Australia, New Zealand and many other countries. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the scientific name is derived from the Ancient Greek words asē ('disgust') and roē ('juice'), and the Latin ruber ('red'). This derivation comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia gives the Māori name puapua-a-Autahi, 'blossom of Canopus', and says the name refers to the fungus typically being seen after Canopus rises in New Zealand. The origins of the English common names are not narrated in any supplied source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Aseroe rubra was collected in 1792 in southern Tasmania and named by the French botanist Jacques Labillardière. Wikipedia calls it the first native Australian fungus to be formally described. It also notes that the species is placed in Phallaceae, though some mycologists have placed it in a separate family, Clathraceae. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes A. rubra as a saprotroph on decomposing plant matter, woodchips and mulch, common in gardens and amenity plantings and also occurring in alpine grasslands and woodlands. It gives the Australian range as southeastern Queensland through New South Wales and eastern Victoria to Tasmania, and says it is also found on many Pacific islands including New Zealand. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on plant litter and woody debris, growing alone or gregariously, found in woods or, more commonly, in cultivated areas and compost. It says the species appears year-round, is common in Hawaii and frequently found in the southeastern United States, and is also found in tropical and subtropical areas worldwide (especially Australia, New Zealand and southern Africa). Its illustrated and described collections there came from North Carolina, South Carolina and Georgia. Two papers report it as a new record for Aurora Province in the Philippines (a 2011 macrofungi survey of the Bazal-Baubo watershed) and as a first record for the Pampa biome in Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil (2025); the Pampa is described there as predominantly grassland vegetation. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_1, lit_2]*
