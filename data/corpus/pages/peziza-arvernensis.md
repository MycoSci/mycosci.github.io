## Summary

Peziza arvernensis Roze & Boud. (1880) is a cup fungus in the family Pezizaceae. Wikipedia gives the common names "boring brown cup fungus" and "fairy tub" and describes brown cups with a pale tan exterior, up to about 8 cm across. MushroomExpert.Com says the species is likely to require microscopic examination to identify with certainty. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies lists Aleuria silvestris Boud., Galactinia sylvestris (Boud.) Svrček, 1962, and Peziza silvestris (Boud.) Sacc. & Traverso, 1911 as synonyms. MushroomExpert.Com notes that the species is called "Peziza sylvestris" in many field guides. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (English) states that it often appears in small groups on soil in broad-leaved woodland, especially with beech, and is widespread in Europe with a few records from North and South America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or in clusters on the ground in hardwood and conifer forests, usually where woody debris has accumulated. It fruits from spring through fall, or in winter in coastal California, and MushroomExpert.Com calls it widely distributed in North America. The two sources differ on how common it is in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
