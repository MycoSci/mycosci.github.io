## Summary

Lysurus periphragmoides (Klotzsch ex Hook.) Dring, 1980, is a stinkhorn in the family Phallaceae, known in English as the chambered stinkhorn or stalked lattice stinkhorn. It was first described as Simblum periphragmoides in 1831 and transferred to Lysurus in 1980. MushroomExpert.Com states that the name has usually been applied to a very different mushroom, and that 2018 research by Hernández Caffot and collaborators clarified what the true species is. Descriptions filed under this name may therefore refer to more than one concept. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet periphragmoides as meaning "fenced in all around" and says it refers to the latticed structure of the cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the basionym Simblum periphragmoides was described by Johann Friedrich Klotzsch in 1831 from specimens collected in Bois Chéry, Mauritius, and designated as the type species of Simblum. Wikipedia reports that Donald Malcolm Dring's 1980 monograph on the Clathraceae transferred the taxon to Lysurus, on the grounds that a distinction between Simblum and Lysurus could not easily be maintained because intermediate states exist. Dring is said to have lumped 18 synonyms under it. Wikipedia describes the species as morphologically variable and attributes its many synonyms to authors treating different forms as new species. Its example is Atkinson's 1902 Dictybole texense, which Curtis Gates Lloyd considered a decomposing or insect-damaged specimen of this species, and which William H. Long moved to Simblum in 1907. MushroomExpert.Com separately notes that recent research (Hernández Caffot et al., 2018) shows the name had usually been applied to a different mushroom. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a saprobic fungus with a pantropical distribution that grows on fertile ground and mulch. It says the species is typically found solitary or in groups on lawns, mulch, pastures and open woods, and that a North American field guide notes an association with apple orchards and cornfields. Wikipedia also says it is fairly common in South America but usually appears only in wet weather in southern North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously, usually in urban habitats (lawns, landscaping, gardens, compost heaps) but also in woods, in summer or year round in warm climates. It adds that the distribution is uncertain because of confusion with another species, and that the collection it illustrates is from the Philippines. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
