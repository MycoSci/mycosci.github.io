## Summary

Crucibulum laeve (Huds.) Kambly, 1936, the common bird's nest fungus, is a member of the Nidulariaceae. Its fruiting body is a small cup holding egg-like peridioles. It is recorded from many countries across Europe, the Americas, Asia, Australasia and Africa. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin laeve, meaning "smooth". This is a single-cluster claim. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the Tulasne brothers used the name Crucibulum vulgare in their 1844 monograph of the Nidulariaceae, and that the species was known by that name until it was deemed invalid under the rules of fungal nomenclature. It also reports that Crucibulum was long treated as containing only this one species, partly because Curtis Gates Lloyd and other early 20th-century mycologists doubted that new species could be justified given intermediate forms. In 1970–71 Brodie described two variants as new species, which ended the monotypic view. This account comes from Wikipedia alone. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes the species as saprobic, growing alone, scattered or densely gregarious on woodland debris (sticks, leaves, nutshells, needles), woodchips, old furniture and dung. It says the species is not typically found on the ground alone or on larger logs. It fruits from spring through fall, or in winter in warmer climates, and is widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia independently lists twigs, lignin-rich vegetable debris, wood chips, old matting and manure as substrates. It quotes Brodie as saying the species is "never" found on soil or large logs. Wikipedia also describes it as a temperate-zone species with a circumpolar distribution. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
