## Summary

Pluteus petasatus (Fr.) Gillet, 1876 is a species in the family Pluteaceae (order Agaricales), listed in English as Scaly Shield. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a pink-spored, wood-associated species with a whitish to very pale brown cap that has fine brown scales over the center, and gills that stay white for quite a while before turning pink. That description comes from a single field guide source. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the species was originally described from Sweden (Fries 1838) and was neotypified from St. Petersburg, Russia by Justo et al. (2014). A Korean mycological journal paper (1994) listed it as new to Korea from Ullung Island collections made in 1992. A 2014 study of wood-inhabiting Basidiomycetes on cork oak in the Mamora forest reported it as new to Morocco. *[mushroomexpert, lit_1, lit_2]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (single source) describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods, but frequently found in woodchips, woody mulch and sawdust piles. It often appears in urban areas, especially where trees have been removed within one or two years. It sometimes grows from buried wood and so appears terrestrial. It grows alone, gregariously, or more often in loose clusters, from late spring through fall, or over winter in warm climates. The same source calls it widely distributed in Europe and North America and reported from Oceania, and its illustrated collections are from Illinois, Indiana and Georgia. A 2014 inventory of lignicolous Basidiomycetes on cork oak trunks in the Mamora forest reports it there as a first record for Morocco. A 1994 paper reports it as new to Korea from Ullung Island. *[mushroomexpert, lit_1, lit_2]*
