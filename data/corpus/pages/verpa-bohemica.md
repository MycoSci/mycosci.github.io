## Summary

Verpa bohemica (Krombh.) J.Schröt., 1893, is a fungus in the family Morchellaceae. Wikipedia reports it from northern North America, Europe and Asia, fruiting on the ground in woods in early spring. The synonym Ptychoverpa bohemica is used by some European mycologists, and English common names include early false morel and wrinkled thimble-cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

According to Wikipedia, the specific epithet bohemica refers to Bohemia (now part of the Czech Republic), where Krombholz originally collected the species. The same source says the synonymous genus name Ptychoverpa derives from the Ancient Greek ptyx (genitive ptychos), meaning "fold", "layer" or "plate". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described by the Czech physician and mycologist Julius Vincenz von Krombholz in 1828 as Morchella bohemica, and that Joseph Schröter transferred it to Verpa in 1893. It also says that Jean Louis Émile Boudier published the synonym Ptychoverpa bohemica in 1907, considering the large, curved ascospores and the rare, short paraphyses distinct enough to warrant a new genus for this single species. The same source reports that Alfred Brooker Klugh first found the species in Canada shortly before 1910, where it was referred to by the synonym Morchella bispora. These details come from a single source cluster (Wikipedia) and were not checked against the primary literature. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes fruit bodies growing singly or scattered on the ground in woods in early spring, often before the true morels appear, and often along riverbanks near cottonwoods, willows and aspens, buried in plant litter. It adds that the fungus prefers moist, sunny areas, and that a carbon and nitrogen isotope study indicated a saprobic lifestyle, although it has also been suggested that the fungus is mycorrhizal for at least part of its life cycle. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently gives the ecology as "probably mycorrhizal", found under hardwoods and sometimes under conifers in early spring, widely distributed throughout northern North America; the collections it illustrates and describes are from Michigan. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
