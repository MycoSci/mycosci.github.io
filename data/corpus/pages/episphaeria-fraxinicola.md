## Summary

Episphaeria fraxinicola (Berk. & Broome) Donk, 1962, is placed in the family Crepidotaceae. Wikipedia describes it as the only species in the monotypic genus Episphaeria, and as a rare species found in Europe. Its tiny fruit bodies resemble minute white cups growing scattered or in groups on the bark of ash trees. The same source says the placement of the genus within the Agaricales is not certain. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet fraxinicola is derived from Fraxinus, meaning "ash", and "colo", meaning "I inhabit". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described as Cyphella fraxinicola by Miles Joseph Berkeley and Christopher Edmund Broome in an 1875 publication. Otto Kuntze transferred it to Chaetocypha in 1891, and Carleton Rea moved it to Phaeocyphella in 1922. Marinus Anton Donk circumscribed the genus Episphaeria in 1962 with E. fraxinicola as the type species. The same source reports that the genus's family placement has varied between authors. Rolf Singer (1986) included it in the Crepidotaceae on morphological similarity. The Dictionary of the Fungi (10th edition, 2008) includes it in the Inocybaceae and notes that the Strophariaceae may be appropriate. A 2010 publication includes it in the Crepidotaceae without molecular support, because the authors could not obtain sequence data from their material. All of this comes from a single independent source cluster (Wikipedia, with iNaturalist as a declared derivative). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes Episphaeria fraxinicola as a rare wood-decay fungus that grows on the bark of ash trees (Fraxinus species). It says the fungus prefers thin twigs at high heights and is widely distributed in Europe. The fruit bodies are described as growing scattered or in groups. This is a single-source account. GBIF distribution records list Belgium (Flemish Region), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
