## Summary

Gymnopilus aeruginosus (Peck) Singer, the magic blue gym, is a species of Hymenogastraceae. Wikipedia describes it as a clustered, wood-growing fungus with a rusty orange spore print, common in the Pacific Northwest. Scientific literature also reports it from India and Taiwan, and Wikipedia adds Japan and Korea. *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_2]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet aeruginosus refers to the bluish staining caused by psilocin polymerization. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was given its current name by the mycologist Rolf Singer in 1951. A 1970 paper in the Indian Journal of Forestry reports it, with two other Gymnopilus species, as a new record for India from the plains of Jammu. A 1975 paper in Taiwania lists it among eleven lignicolous fungi reported for the first time from Taiwan. *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_2]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows gregariously to cespitosely on stumps, logs, and woodchip mulch or sawdust, on hardwood and conifers. It gives the season as spring, fall, and winter, and says the species is common in the Pacific Northwest. It also grows in some southern US states such as Tennessee and Georgia, and is found in Japan and Korea. Separate primary literature records it from Jammu, India (1970) and Taiwan (1975). *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_2]*
