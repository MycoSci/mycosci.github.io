## Summary

Agaricus hondensis, the felt-ringed agaricus (Agaricaceae), was described by William Alphonso Murrill in 1912 from California material. English Wikipedia describes it as a species of the Pacific Coast of North America and states that it is poisonous. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

English Wikipedia states that the epithet hondensis refers to the type location, La Honda, California. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to English Wikipedia, Murrill described the species in 1912 from collections he made in November 1911 under Californian redwoods. In the same publication he described A. bivelatoides, A. hillii and A. macmurphyi. In 1944 Alexander H. Smith examined the type material and concluded that there were no characters separating A. hondensis from those three names. He also concluded that Zeller's A. glaber (1938) was the same species. MycoBank treats these names as synonymous. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

English Wikipedia describes A. hondensis as saprobic, with fruit bodies growing scattered or in groups under conifers or in mixed forests, and reports that it has also been reported in fairy rings. It is stated to occur on the Pacific Coast of North America from British Columbia south to California, and to be most common in California. The same source gives fall fruiting, September to October through much of its range, but November to February in California. This is a single-source account (Wikipedia; iNaturalist text is derived from it). *[wikipedia]*
