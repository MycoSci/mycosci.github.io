## Summary

Clavaria fragilis Holmsk. (1790) is a fungus in the family Clavariaceae, described from Denmark. Wikipedia describes it as the type species of the genus Clavaria, producing unbranched, tubular white fruit bodies that typically grow in clusters and reach up to about 15 cm tall. Clavaria vermicularis is a later synonym that is still frequently used. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the Latin epithet fragilis refers to the brittle fruit bodies. It also gives the meaning of the synonym's epithet vermicularis as "wormlike". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described from Denmark in 1790 by Theodor Holmskjold and was sanctioned under this name by Elias Magnus Fries in his 1821 Systema Mycologicum. Olof Swartz redescribed it in 1811 as Clavaria vermicularis. That name is a later synonym and so obsolete under the principle of priority, but it is still frequently used. This account comes from a single independent source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only independent source with habitat text, says the fungus grows in woodland litter and in old, unimproved grassland, on moist soil. It is presumed to be saprobic, rotting fallen leaf litter and dead grass stems. Fruit bodies tend to grow in groups, tufts or clusters. Wikipedia gives the range as the Northern Hemisphere (Europe, Asia and North America), with records from Australia and South Africa. It reports the European season as July to October. In North America it is said to be more common east of the Rocky Mountains. In northern Europe it is described as one of the "CHEG" fungi, which are considered indicators of old, unimproved grassland. *[wikipedia]*
