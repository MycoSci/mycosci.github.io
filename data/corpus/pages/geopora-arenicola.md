## Summary

Geopora arenicola (Lév.) Kers, 1974, is a species of ascomycete fungus in the family Pyronemataceae. Wikipedia gives its common name as the "hole in the ground truffle" and describes it as an uncommon species reported from Europe and Uzbekistan. The fruit body is a rounded ascocarp that forms underground and later opens at the surface as a cream-coloured cup. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Peziza arenicola Lév., published in 1848 in Annales des Sciences Naturelles, Botanique, ser. 3, 9: 140, with the host/substratum and locality recorded as "On soil: France". The same entry lists later combinations of the epithet in Lachnea (Gillet, 1880), Humaria (Brunaud, 1886), Scutellinia (Kuntze, 1891), Sepultaria (Massee, 1895), Sarcoscypha (Rehm, 1895, nom. inval.) and Sarcosphaera (Lindau, 1896). ## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fungus forms a rounded ascocarp underground on sandy loam soils. The fruit body stays subterranean for most of the year and breaks the surface in the spring, forming a cream-coloured cup (apothecium) up to 3 cm wide and tall. Wikipedia says it is likely mycorrhizal but that the host species is difficult to determine. Wikispecies records the original (basionym) collection as "On soil: France". GBIF's distribution entry lists Belgium (Flemish Region), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, gbif_desc]*
