## Summary

Trametes villosa (Sw.) Kreisel, 1971, is a polypore in the family Polyporaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small, densely hairy bracket with a cap zoned in gray and buff shades, and a pore surface of large angular pores (1-3 per mm) that become elongated or nearly tooth-like with age. A GBIF-hosted description gives its range as subtropical areas of the United States to Argentina, and calls it common in the Neotropics. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods and conifers (especially eastern red cedar), annual, causing a white rot, growing in clusters on logs and stumps in summer and fall, and widely distributed in southeastern North America. A description hosted by GBIF states that it grows on deciduous wood and is known from subtropical areas of the United States to Argentina, common in the Neotropics; specimen data in the same record give the substrate as dead wood. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
