## Summary

Xylobolus subpileatus (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Boidin is a crust fungus in the family Stereaceae, order Russulales. Wikipedia describes it as a wood-decay fungus of fallen oak logs that leaves a honeycomb-like decay pattern, and reports it as listed as endangered in several European countries. ## Discovery and naming

Per Wikipedia, the fungus was first described in 1849 by Miles Joseph Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis, who placed it in Stereum as a species closely related to but distinct from Stereum rugosum. The original collections came from dead trunks in Ohio and South Carolina, United States. French mycologist Jacques Boidin gave it its current name in 1958 by transferring it to the genus Xylobolus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (single-source claims) describes X. subpileatus as a saproxylic wood-decay fungus that colonises fallen oak wood, producing a honeycomb-like decay pattern by selectively degrading lignin. It states that fruiting bodies appear only on logs of Quercus species, and that the likelihood of fruiting rises as the wood advances through decay. For old-growth oak forests in Corsica, it reports that fruiting probability increases from around 7 years after treefall to a peak at roughly 40 years, within natural canopy gaps. In eastern North America it is described as known from mature deciduous forests on fallen oak logs. In Europe its populations are described as often scarce and highly localised, listed as endangered in several countries (e.g. Austria, Hungary) and critically endangered in the Czech Republic. A GBIF description record gives the habitat as a Quercus humboldtii forest ("Bosque de Quercus Humboldtii"). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
