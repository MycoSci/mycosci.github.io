## Summary

Pterulicium gracile (Desm. & Berk.) Leal-Dutra, Dentinger & G.W. Griff. (2020) is a species in the family Pterulaceae, order Agaricales. Wikipedia describes it as a small whitish coral fungus with a delicate branching structure. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet gracile (originally gracilis) derives from the Latin 'gracilis', meaning graceful. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the earliest classification of this species was as Typhula gracilis in 1838 by Desmazières and Berkeley, and the epithet gracilis was the one retained. It was later placed in Clavaria and Typhula, and in 1950 E.J.H. Corner reclassified it as Pterula gracilis. In 2020 the Pterulaceae were reclassified on the basis of phylogenetic analysis, and the species was moved to Pterulicium by Leal-Dutra, Dentinger and Griffith. Wikipedia attributes the large number of synonyms to repeated reclassification and to a lack of significant distinguishing features separating it from other coral fungi. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only independent source with descriptive text, reports the species on decaying vegetable matter, leaves and the stems of herbaceous plants in deciduous woodland and marshes, and says it may grow on other plant matter. It is described as growing solitary or in small trooping groups. For the United Kingdom, Wikipedia says it is not commonly recorded and is said to be widespread but rarely reported. *[wikipedia]*
