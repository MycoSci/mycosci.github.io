## Summary

Hypsizygus ulmarius (Bull.) Redhead, 1984, is a species in the family Lyophyllaceae (order Agaricales). Wikipedia gives the common names elm oyster mushroom and, less commonly, elm leech. GBIF holds occurrence records from a wide range of countries, and Wikipedia describes it as widespread in temperate forests globally. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia glosses the genus name Hypsizygus as combining 'high up' (Hypsi-) and 'yoke' (-zygus), referring to where the mushroom is found attached to its host tree. It states that the species name refers to elm (Ulmus spp.), a tree the fungus commonly grows on. This derivation comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1791 as Agaricus ulmarius by Jean Baptiste Francois Pierre Bulliard, at a time when most gilled mushrooms were placed in Agaricus. After Paul Kummer created the genus Pleurotus for oyster mushrooms, it was placed there as Pleurotus ulmarius, most likely because of the similar appearance of the fruiting bodies. Wikipedia says Robert Kühner later moved it to Lyophyllum as Lyophyllum ulmarium, and that Scott Redhead moved it to Hypsizygus in 1984, based on ecological and morphological similarity to another species in that genus. It adds that a later DNA study of the D2 region of the large ribosomal subunit RNA in Lyophyllum species, together with culture morphology, agreed with Redhead's placement. It also notes that further DNA evidence suggests Hypsizygus is polyphyletic. This account comes from a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states that the species is easily culturable on a variety of media and can be grown on substrates similar to those used for true oyster mushrooms, such as paper, straw and logs. It also says commercially available kits exist for growing it at home. This comes from a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species commonly grows on elm (Ulmus spp.), box elder (Acer negundo) and beech, and may be found on other trees as well. It says the mushrooms grow high up in the wounds or scars of the host tree, and that the species is found in temperate forests across North America, Europe and Asia. The same source says it is found on live trees, that it is unclear whether it feeds on live or dead tissue, and that it causes brown rot on damaged areas of its host. GBIF's distribution entry lists Belgium (Flemish Region), Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. No supplied source gives a season. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
