## Summary

Gymnopus fusipes (Bull.) Gray, 1821 is a gilled mushroom in the family Omphalotaceae and the type species of the genus Gymnopus. Wikipedia describes it as a parasitic species that is quite common in Europe and often grows in large clumps. It also describes the stipe as soon becoming distinctively tough, bloated and ridged. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name fusipes indicates a spindle-shaped stem, from the Latin fusus meaning "spindle" and pes meaning "foot". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia reports that Gray had already given the English name "Spindle naked-foot" in 1821, but that this suggestion never gained much popularity. It says the English name "Spindle Shank" has also been given to the species. It does not say who coined "Spindle Shank". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Bulliard originally described the species as Agaricus fusipes in his "Herbier de la France", when all gilled mushrooms were assigned to Agaricus. In 1821 Samuel Frederick Gray, in his "Natural Arrangement of British Plants", placed it in the existing genus Gymnopus. Gray's book was not very popular, and in 1872 Lucien Quélet moved it to Collybia as Collybia fusipes, the name by which it was generally known for many years. Wikispecies lists Collybia fusipes (Bull.) Quél., 1872 among the synonyms. Wikipedia says Antonín and Noordeloos, in work culminating in 1997, found Collybia as then defined to be polyphyletic and resurrected Gymnopus for some species including fusipes. It says subsequent DNA studies led modern authorities, including Species Fungorum and GBIF, to accept the current name. It adds that an alternative move to Rhodocollybia has not generally been accepted. This account rests on a single Wikipedia article plus a Wikispecies synonym list. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that this mushroom grows, often in large clumps, at the base of trees or on roots or stumps. It says the fungus is always associated with wood, which may be buried and not immediately visible. Its main host is given as oak, with occasional records on beech. Wikipedia describes it as saprobic on dead wood and also a serious parasite, causing a root rot of oak. It is said to appear from summer to autumn and to be distributed throughout Europe, where it varies locally between quite common and quite rare. Wikipedia also says the fungus is spreading as a disease to North America, particularly on Northern Red Oak.
