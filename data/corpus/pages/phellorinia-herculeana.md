## Summary

Phellorinia herculeana (Pers.) Kreisel was published in 1961 in Index of Fungi 3: 122 and is placed in the family Agaricaceae. Wikipedia describes the genus Phellorinia as monotypic, with this as its only species. GBIF holds 473 occurrence records from about 40 countries and territories. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was described by the English naturalist Miles Joseph Berkeley in 1843 as P. inquinans. It also states that the epithet comes from the basionym Scleroderma herculeanum Pers. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the spores were used for body decoration by Australian Aboriginal groups in desert areas. A 2017 ethnomycological study of the Wayuu people of the Alta Guajira in Colombia reports that the spores of the three fungi it studied, this species among them, are used as a cosmetic by women and as a skin protector in religious rituals and dances. The study notes that the treatment of the spores, and the other materials added to them, vary with the geographic location of the communities. *[wikipedia, lit_3]*

## Habitat and ecology

One 2022 study (Processes) reports that it grows commonly in coastal soil with saline conditions, in barren land soil unfit for cultivation, and in desert soil. It forms its basidiocarp singly around xerophytic trees and annual plants, and also in soil without vegetation. The same study describes a rhizoid that grows horizontally in the soil, thick at the base of the stipe and thinning into the mycelial strand. A separate 1988 paper describes the species from Los Monegros (Spain). *[lit_1, lit_2]*
