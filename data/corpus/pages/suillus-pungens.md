## Summary

Suillus pungens Thiers & A.H. Sm. (1964), the pungent slippery jack, is a bolete in the family Suillaceae. It is an ectomycorrhizal species associated mainly with Monterey pine and bishop pine in coastal California, where it was first collected. Its cap changes colour markedly as it develops, starting whitish and passing through gray and olive stages to brown and yellow shades. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin pungens and refers to the pungent aroma of the fruit bodies. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) likewise says the epithet refers to the odor and taste of the mushroom, and that the original authors (Thiers & Smith 1964) considered these 'not easily overlooked'. Kuo adds that in his own collections the odor was strongly fragrant. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described by Harry D. Thiers and Alexander H. Smith in their 1964 monograph on North American Suillus species. The type collection was made on the campus of San Francisco State University in San Francisco, a locality MushroomExpert.Com also gives. Wikipedia adds that Smith and Thiers placed it in section Suilli. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe S. pungens as mycorrhizal, associated primarily with Monterey pine (Pinus radiata) and bishop pine (Pinus muricata). Wikipedia adds that some collections have been made under knobcone pine and ponderosa pine, but only within the range of Monterey pine. It also occurs with planted and introduced pines in California, including P. pinea, P. pinaster and P. halepensis, particularly in urban areas. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as growing solitarily, scattered or in groups in humus. MushroomExpert.Com gives alone, scattered or gregarious, in both urban and woodland areas. Both sources place fruiting in late fall and winter. Wikipedia adds that the species also fruits sporadically through the rest of the year, especially in wet weather. Both describe it as common in the San Francisco Bay Area, and Wikipedia also reports it from the southeastern Sierra Nevada and Santa Cruz Island. The sources differ on Oceania. Wikipedia, citing an IUCN assessment, reports introduced occurrences in New Zealand (common with introduced P. radiata) and Australia. MushroomExpert.Com says it is 'also reported (perhaps erroneously) from Oceania'. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
