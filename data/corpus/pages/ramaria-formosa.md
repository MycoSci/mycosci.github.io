## Summary

Ramaria formosa (Pers.) Quél. 1888 is a coral fungus in the family Gomphaceae. Its fruit body is much-branched, with pinkish to coral-pink branches and yellow tips. It is described from Europe, and sources disagree about how far the name applies to North American collections. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the generic name comes from Latin rāmus 'branch' and the specific epithet from Latin formōsus 'beautiful'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Persoon described the fungus in 1797 as Clavaria formosa. Fries sanctioned the genus name Clavaria in 1821 and treated Ramaria as a section of it. Quélet placed the species in Ramaria in 1888. Synonyms arose from transfers to the now obsolete genera Merisma (Lenz, 1831) and Corallium (Hahn, 1883). Wikipedia also says the binomial appears to have been applied loosely to coral fungi fitting the description, and that collections from North America are now considered a different species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as fruiting in autumn in Europe, associated with beech, and says it is thought to form mycorrhizal associations with golden oak (Quercus alnifolia) in Cyprus. MushroomExpert.Com (a North American account, written for collections that Wikipedia says are now considered a different species) describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods and occasionally conifers, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
