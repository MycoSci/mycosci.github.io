## Summary

Exidia recisa (Ditmar) Fr. is a jelly fungus in the family Auriculariaceae, given the recommended English name amber jelly in the UK. Wikipedia describes gelatinous, orange-brown, top-shaped fruit bodies on dead willow twigs in Europe, and notes the species has long been confused with the North American Exidia crenata. MushroomExpert.Com describes North American collections as brown to purplish brown, and its account differs from Wikipedia's on colour and host. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet "recisa" means "cut-off", with reference to the shape of the fruit bodies. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was originally found growing on willow in Germany and was described in 1813 by L.P.F. Ditmar as Tremella recisa, then transferred to Exidia by Fries in 1822. It says Tremella salicum has long been considered a synonym. It also says molecular work based on DNA sequences places E. recisa in a complex of similar species that includes E. crenata in North America and E. yadongensis in eastern Asia. MushroomExpert.Com gives the same authority citation, (Ditmar, 1813) Fries, 1822. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

The two independent sources describe the habitat differently. Wikipedia describes a wood-rotting species typically found on dead attached twigs and branches, originally recorded on willow and most frequent on it, with reports also from alder and Prunus. It says the species typically fruits in autumn and winter, is widely distributed in Europe, and has an uncertain distribution elsewhere because it has only recently been distinguished from similar species in North America and Asia. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes a saprobe on recently fallen hardwood sticks and branches, especially oak wood, fruiting from spring through fall. It reports the species as apparently widely distributed in North America and much more common east of the Rocky Mountains, with the illustrated collections from Illinois and Québec. Each of these accounts comes from a single source. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
