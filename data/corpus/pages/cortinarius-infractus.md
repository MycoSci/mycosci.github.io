## Summary

Cortinarius infractus (Pers.) Fr. 1838 is a species of Cortinariaceae, recorded in GBIF from many countries, including Norway, Sweden, Denmark, the UK, the US and Canada. Sources describe a sticky, grayish-olive to olive-brown cap with olive-tinged gills, a rusty-brown spore print and a bitter taste. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first named Agaricus infractus by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1799 and transferred to Cortinarius by Elias Magnus Fries in his 1838 Epicrisis Systematis Mycologici. Differing opinions on how Cortinarius should be organized led to the names Phlegmacium infractum and Pholiota infracta. Wikipedia also reports that Moser and Ammirati described a variety, C. infractus var. flavus, in 1999 from Shoshone National Forest, Wyoming. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states the fruit bodies grow scattered in deciduous forests of both oak and beech. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as a European species that is also widely distributed in North America. It calls the species mycorrhizal with hardwoods or, less frequently, conifers. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, in late summer and fall, or over winter in warmer climates. These are two separate sources with differing emphasis, and no other habitat information was supplied. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
