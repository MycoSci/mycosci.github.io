## Summary

Panellus pusillus (Pers. ex Lév.) Burds. & O.K. Mill. (1975) is a species in the family Mycenaceae, order Agaricales. Wikipedia describes it as a small, fan- or kidney-shaped bracket with pores rather than gills, and reports weak bioluminescence in both the fruiting body and the mycelium. It is recorded in Australia, Asia, Europe, North America and South America. Wikipedia notes that the identity of Australian and Asian material is under discussion. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet pusillus refers to the fungus's small size, meaning "very small". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia gives the common name "little ping-pong bat", from the cap's resemblance to a pong paddle. It also gives the Japanese name Hinano-uchiwa, meaning "princess fan", which it attributes to the shape. Both come from the same single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Gloeoporus pusillus by Joseph-Henri Léveillé in 1844, in a description that cites Persoon's manuscript name and gives the habitat as Hispaniola, on trunks. It was later moved through several genera, including Dictyopanus, Favolus, Polyporus and Laschia. Burdsall and Miller (1975) treated Dictyopanus as a subgenus of Panellus and transferred its species to Panellus, which produced the current name. Wikipedia also reports that Chew et al. (2015) raised concerns about morphological anomalies in Old World material. They described Malaysian specimens as Panellus luxfilamentus and stated that this species has been commonly misidentified as P. pusillus in Asia, Australia and New Zealand. Whether the Tasmanian form matches mainland Australian or Malaysian material is described as unclear. All of this comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the Japanologist Katsumi Masuda theorized P. pusillus to be the source of the glowing bamboo in the opening of The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter, and that the historian Michihisa Hotate agreed. It presents this as a hypothesis and speculation, not an established fact. It is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes P. pusillus as a saprobic species that causes a white rot of wood. It reports the fungus growing in dense groups and overlapping clusters on decaying broadleaf wood in shade, favoring hardwoods. It also reports growth on rotten bamboo culm, eucalyptus and pine, and says the species is distributed mostly in Florida and prefers the tropics and subtropics (citing Wassink 1979). GBIF description records for this taxon list the growth habit as clustered or overlapping (cespitose or imbricate), the habit as wood-dwelling (lignicolous), and the substrate as dead wood and living trunk. One GBIF-listed collection is from Tolima, Colombia, at 3000–3100 m elevation, collected on 25 Nov 2005. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
