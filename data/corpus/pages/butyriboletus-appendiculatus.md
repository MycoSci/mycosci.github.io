## Summary

Butyriboletus appendiculatus (Schaeff.) D. Arora & J.L. Frank (2014) is a member of the Boletaceae, recorded in GBIF from many European countries as well as the US, Canada, Mexico and Japan. It is commonly known as the butter bolete or oak bolete. Wikipedia describes a pored mushroom with yellow pores and stipe, and flesh that may stain blue when cut or bruised. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

## Where the common name comes from

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia attributes the first scientific description to Jacob Christian Schäffer as Boletus appendiculatus, dated 1774. Wikispecies lists Boletus appendiculatus Schaeff. with the date 1763, so the two sources give different years. Wikipedia adds that Charles Horton Peck later used the same name, in 1896, for a species he found in Washington, and that this use was illegitimate because Schäffer's name has priority. According to Wikipedia, the species stayed in Boletus until 2014, when molecular phylogenetic analysis showed that it and the other members of Boletus section Appendiculati were distinct from Boletus, and the genus Butyriboletus was created for them. Wikispecies gives the current combination as Butyriboletus appendiculatus (Schaeff.) D. Arora & J.L. Frank, Mycologia 106(3): 466 (2014). *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that it grows under oaks and other broad-leaved trees such as beech, and that fruit bodies grow singly, scattered, or in groups under hardwoods. It says the species often forms large colonies beneath oaks and is frequently found with old oaks in ancient woodland, and that it is relatively rare in Britain. Wikipedia gives its range as Europe and North America. In North America it says the species is more common in the Pacific Northwest, where it often associates with live oak and tanoak.
