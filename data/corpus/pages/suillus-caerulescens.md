## Summary

Suillus caerulescens A.H. Sm. & Thiers (1964) is a bolete in the family Suillaceae, known as the Douglas-fir suillus and fat jack. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a western North American species that is mycorrhizal with Douglas-fir. It gives the recognition features as blue-staining flesh, a reddish brown staining pore surface, a yellowish to tawny cap and a ring on the stem. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was first described by Alexander H. Smith and Harry D. Thiers in 1964. MushroomExpert.Com adds that Smith & Thiers (1964) also described Suillus imitatus, and that a variant, viridescens, was described later by Smith & Trappe (1972). According to that source, sequencing of the type collections by Nguyen and collaborators (2012) showed these taxa to represent Suillus caerulescens and Suillus ponderosus, respectively. The MushroomExpert.Com passage does not say which of the two names each refers to beyond that order, so this is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both independent sources associate it with Douglas-fir. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with Douglas-fir, growing alone or gregariously in fall and winter. It reports the species as originally described from Washington and distributed throughout the Pacific Northwest and northern California, with the illustrated collection from California. Wikipedia states it grows with Douglas-fir in western North America from October to December. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
