## Summary

Amylocystis lapponica (Romell) Bondartsev & Singer 1944 is a bracket fungus in the family Dacryobolaceae (order Polyporales). Wikipedia describes it as having medium-sized, soft, annual fruit bodies with a strong, distinct smell, growing as a brown-rot saprophyte on fallen coniferous wood. The same source calls it a rather rare species that occurs only in old-growth forest, and says it is red-listed in 7 European countries. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was first described by the Swedish mycologist Lars Romell in 1911 as Polyporus lapponicus, from a type collection made at Nattavaara, Sweden, where it was growing on fir. Romell initially thought it might be Climacocystis borealis but rejected that idea, because that species has an easily breakable fruit body and spores of a different size and shape. The species was later placed in Ungulina (Pilát, 1934), Leptoporus (Pilát, 1938) and Tyromyces (J.Lowe, 1975). Wikipedia says it has microscopic characters typical of Tyromyces but differs in having thick-walled amyloid cystidia in the hymenium, and that this is why A. Bondartsev and Rolf Singer created the genus Amylocystis in 1944 to contain it. Polyporus ursinus, proposed by Curtis Gates Lloyd in 1915, is now considered a synonym. This account comes from a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that Amylocystis lapponica decomposes fallen conifer wood, in which it causes brown rot. Its preferential hosts are given as spruce and larch, with occasional records on fir. It is described as circumboreal in coniferous forests. In Europe it is said to be restricted almost exclusively to old-growth forests that have vegetative continuity, natural tree species composition, multi-aged structure, abundant dead wood in various stages of decay and a stable, cold, humid climate. Wikipedia reports it from only one virgin forest in the Czech Republic (Boubínský prales) and, in Poland, only from Białowieża Forest. It is known from hundreds of localities in Finland and Sweden and dozens in Norway, is widely distributed in western North America, and is also found in China.
