## Summary

Omphalotus subilludens (Murrill) H.E. Bigelow, the southern jack-o'-lantern, is a species in the family Omphalotaceae. Wikipedia describes it as definitively recorded in Florida and Texas, fruiting in clusters on dead and dying wood, and as poisonous to humans when eaten. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first collected by William A. Murrill in 1944 in Gainesville, Florida, and described as Monodelphus subilludens. It was later moved to the genus Omphalotus by H.E. Bigelow. This account comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes O. subilludens as a saprobe on the stumps and roots of dead and dying hardwoods and palms. It notes that some sources suggest a specialization to palms, but that both formal and informal observations counter this, with the species recorded on various wood types. It is reported as fruiting in spring, summer and fall, with most observations in late summer and fall. It is definitively recorded in Florida and Texas, with possible reports from Arizona and Mexico. This is a single-source description. *[wikipedia]*
