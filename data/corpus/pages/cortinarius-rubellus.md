## Summary

Cortinarius rubellus, the deadly webcap, is a species of Cortinariaceae described by Cooke in 1887 from material collected in Great Britain. Wikipedia places it in the Orellani, a group of Cortinarius species it describes as highly toxic, where eating them results in kidney failure that is often irreversible. Two peer-reviewed papers on its toxin chemistry and a clinical case report also describe it as poisonous. *[wikipedia, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Mordecai Cubitt Cooke described C. rubellus in 1887 from material collected by a Dr. Carlyle at Orton Moss near Carlisle, Cumbria, and that the name was rarely used before 1980. C. orellanoides (Henry, 1937) and C. speciosissimus (Kühner and Romagnesi) are treated as the same species under different names. Klaus Høiland concluded that C. orellanoides and C. speciosissimus were identical, and Høiland and others noted that C. rubellus was likely the same species as well. Gasparini queried this because Cooke's original illustrations show triangular or fig-shaped spores. Wikipedia reports that both C. rubellus and C. orellanoides lack a type specimen, and that a 2007 proposal to conserve the name C. speciosissimus had no consensus as of 2022. This account rests on a single encyclopedia source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports it from high-latitude temperate to subalpine forests of the northern hemisphere, including subalpine conifer forest in the Yatsugatake Mountains, Japan, and British Columbia and western Washington in North America. In Scandinavia and the north of the British Isles it is described as generally found in wet areas of conifer or mixed conifer and broadleaf woodland, though uncommon. Mushroom World, a single aggregator source, describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, pine and spruce in particular, on damp acid soil, often fruiting in small groups. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
