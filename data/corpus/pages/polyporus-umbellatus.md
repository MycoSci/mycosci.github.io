## Summary

Polyporus umbellatus (Pers.) Fr., 1821, is a species in the family Polyporaceae, known in English as the umbrella polypore. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a butt rot fruiting near the bases and roots of hardwoods in northern North America, with many small smoky brown caps whose branches fuse into one solid structure below. GBIF description records list distribution entries for Belgium, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com notes that the species is sometimes called "Grifola umbellata" in older field guides and may move to a new genus (perhaps "Dendropolyporus") once DNA-informed taxonomy of Polyporus is better settled. It also cites Xing and collaborators (2013), who report substantial genetic variation among Chinese populations, so that several or many species may be involved. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the sclerotium stage of the life cycle (a compact mass of hardened mycelium with food reserves) is the stage used in Chinese medicine. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic or parasitic as a butt rot on the roots and wood of hardwoods, causing a white rot and fruiting at the bases of trees, often reappearing in the same place in later years. It reports fruiting in spring, summer and fall, and calls the species rare but fairly widely distributed in northern North America, southwards to Tennessee and Kansas. The author's own collection was at the base of a white ash in oak-hickory woods (illustrated collections from Illinois). Wikipedia separately says it grows on roots of old beeches or oak and can be found in eastern North America from May to September; iNaturalist repeats the beech/oak statement and is a declared derivative of Wikipedia. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, inaturalist]*
