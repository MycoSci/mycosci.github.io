## Summary

Leucopholiota decorosa (Peck) O.K. Mill., T.J. Volk & Bessette (1996), the decorated pholiota, is a species in the family Agaricaceae that Peck first described as Agaricus decorosus in 1873. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe a fruit body with pointed brown scales on the cap and stem, white gills and a white spore print. GBIF records it from several countries, including the US, Canada, Japan and France. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name Leucopholiota means "white Pholiota", referring to the gills and the spores, and that it was proposed in 1980 by Henri Romagnesi, who originally described it as a subgenus of Armillaria. It also states that the epithet decorosa, though intended for "elegant" or "handsome", actually means "decent", "respectable", "modest" or "decorous". This derivation comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Charles Peck described the species in 1873 from a specimen found in New York State and placed it in Tricholoma, then considered a subgenus of Agaricus. In 1947 Smith and Walters moved it to Armillaria. Smith later moved it to Tricholomopsis, and in 1987 it was transferred to Floccularia. Wikipedia says a specimen appearing at a 1994 mushroom foray in North Carolina led Tom Volk, Orson K. Miller Jr. and Alan Bessette to rename it Leucopholiota decorosa in a 1996 Mycologia publication. They raised Leucopholiota from a subgenus of Armillaria to genus level to accommodate it, and it became the type species. These details come from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods, often in forests of beech, hemlock and sugar maple. It grows alone, gregariously or in clusters in late summer and fall, and is fairly widely distributed in eastern North America. Wikipedia describes it as saprobic on rotting branches and stumps of deciduous trees, notes that one field guide reports a preference for sugar maple, and says that in Ohio it typically fruits from late September to mid November. Wikipedia also says it has been collected in France and was reported in 2007 from the Astore District of Pakistan at about 3,600 m. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
