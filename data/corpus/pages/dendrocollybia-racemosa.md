## Summary

Dendrocollybia racemosa (Pers.) R.H. Petersen & Redhead, 2001, is a member of the Tricholomataceae (Agaricales), known in English as the branched collybia or branched shanklet. Wikipedia describes it as the only species in its genus, with small grayish caps and a thin stem covered in short lateral branches, arising from a black sclerotium. Its distribution in Wikipedia and GBIF is the Northern Hemisphere, including Europe and the Pacific Northwest of North America. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet racemosa comes from the Latin racemus, meaning 'a cluster of grapes'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus racemosus by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1797 and sanctioned under that name by Elias Magnus Fries in 1821. Samuel Frederick Gray called it Mycena racemosa in 1821, and Joanne Lennox's 1979 Microcollybia racemosa is also treated as a synonym. Fries placed it in his tribe Collybia, which Lucien Quélet raised to generic rank in 1873. The species was later treated as one of four Collybia species. Phylogenetic work by Karen Hughes and colleagues on ribosomal DNA internal transcribed spacer sequences did not recover a clade containing all four. That result, together with differences in stem projections, fruit body pigmentation and macrochemical reactions, led to the genus Dendrocollybia being established in 2001 to hold this species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only independent source with descriptive text, reports the following. It is a saprobic species that usually grows on the well-decayed fruit bodies of other agarics, often suspected to be Lactarius or Russula, although the host is often unidentifiable because of advanced decay. A 2006 molecular study in the Pacific Northwest of the United States confirmed Russula crassotunicata as a host. It is also reported less commonly in deep coniferous duff, in groups or small clusters. In North America it is restricted to the Pacific Northwest, where fruit bodies appear in late summer to autumn. In Europe it is named from the United Kingdom, Scandinavia and Belgium. It is described as widely distributed in temperate parts of the Northern Hemisphere but rarely collected. GBIF's distribution record lists Belgium (Flemish and Walloon Regions), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
