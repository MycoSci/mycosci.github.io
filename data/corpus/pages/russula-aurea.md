## Summary

Russula aurea Pers. (1796) is a member of the family Russulaceae, known in English as the gilded brittlegill. Wikipedia describes it as an uncommon species found in deciduous woodland in Europe in summer and early autumn; this is a single-source claim. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that both specific epithets, aurea and the later aurata, are derived from the Latin aurum, 'gold'. This is a single-source claim. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was known for many years as Russula aurata, which was originally published in 1801 as Agaricus auratus by William Withering and placed in Russula by Elias Magnus Fries in 1838. The binomial Russula aurea had been coined by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1796 and takes precedence. Wikispecies lists Agaricus auratus With., Agaricus aureus (Pers.) Pers. and Russula aurata Fr. as synonyms. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species occurs under deciduous trees in summer and early autumn, in particular beech, oak and hazel. It describes the species as found in Europe, uncommon in Britain, and recorded as far east as the Black Sea region of eastern Turkey. The GBIF distribution record lists Belgium (Flemish and Walloon Regions), Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont, and gives no habitat or substrate information.
