## Summary

Trichaptum biforme (Fr.) Ryvarden, 1972, is a poroid bracket fungus in the order Hymenochaetales, known in English as the violet-toothed polypore. Sources describe it as a saprobe on hardwood logs and stumps. Its pore surface is purple to lilac when fresh and fades with age. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a saprobe that decomposes hardwood stumps and logs, widely distributed and recorded on at least 65 tree hosts. It rarely grows on conifers but has been recorded from pine (Pinus) deadwood. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing in overlapping clusters on hardwood logs and stumps in late spring, summer and fall. It reports the species from all 50 US states and all the Canadian provinces, and calls it one of the most commonly encountered fungi in eastern North America. The same source says it causes a straw-colored sapwood rot in standing trees. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
