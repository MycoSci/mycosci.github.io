## Summary

Cortinarius croceus (Schaeff.) Gray, 1821, is a member of the Cortinariaceae, known in English as the Saffron Webcap. GBIF's description record lists its distribution as Belgium (Flemish and Walloon Regions), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. As Michael Kuo treats it on MushroomExpert.Com, it has yellow gills, a yellowish brown to olive brown cap, and a dark red to reddish black reaction to KOH on the cap surface. *[gbif_desc, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says the name is officially applied to a European species, and that a common North American version has been labeled Cortinarius incognitus, which it describes as having slightly larger spores and a mild taste. The same page lists Dermocybe crocea as a synonym. It also says Cortinarius cinnamomeus, as some field guide authors treat it, may be the same mushroom, but that the original European description of C. cinnamomeus gives a reddish cinnamon cap, reddish cinnamon gills and a yellowish stem. The page places the species in subgenus Dermocybe and describes species delineation in this group as unsettled pending DNA research. This is the treatment of a single field guide author. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com states that it is mycorrhizal with conifers, growing alone or scattered, often in dry areas, in summer and fall, and that it is widely distributed in North America. Mushroom World, which gives its location as Europe, says it grows solitarily or in scattered trooping groups on soil in coniferous woods. *[mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
