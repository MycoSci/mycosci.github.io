## Summary

Xeromphalina campanella (Batsch) Kühner & Maire, 1934, is a species in the family Mycenaceae, recorded from many countries across North America and Eurasia. It is a small mushroom with a yellowish to orange cap that has a central depression, and it grows in dense clusters on decaying conifer wood (MushroomExpert.Com). Common names in use include fuzzy-foot, golden trumpet and bell Omphalina. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name Xeromphalina means "little dry navel" and that campanella means "bell-shaped", describing the mature and young shapes of the cap respectively. This is a single-cluster claim; the iNaturalist text repeats Wikipedia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as saprobic on the deadwood of conifers, typically growing in dense clusters on stumps and logs but occasionally alone or gregariously. Wikipedia likewise describes fruiting in clumps or very dense clusters on decaying logs, stumps and woody debris of coniferous trees, at times almost entirely covering old stumps. On season, MushroomExpert.Com reports it nearly year-round, especially in warmer climates, but most common in summer and fall. Wikipedia and Mushroom World say it can be found in any wet season of the year. Wikipedia describes it as cold-tolerant and found in both North America and Eurasia. MushroomExpert.Com's illustrated collections are from Michigan, Colorado and Texas. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
