## Summary

Hygrophorus olivaceoalbus (Fr.) Fr. is a species of waxcap in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the Olive Wax Cap or Glutinous Woodwax. Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe a sticky or slimy cap 3–12 cm wide, streaked with fibres beneath the slime, and a stem sheathed by a slimy outer veil and a brown fibrous inner veil that stretches into concentric bands as the stem grows. Gills are white and waxy, and the spore print is white. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet olivaceoalbus is derived from the Latin words for olive-brown (olivaceus) and white (albus). This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first officially described as Agaricus olivaceoalbus by Elias Fries in 1815. It had earlier been published as Agaricus adustus by Batsch in 1783, but that was an illegitimate renaming of Agaricus brunneus, published by Schäffer in 1774. Fries transferred it to Hygrophorus in 1838. Paul Kummer moved it to Limacium in 1871, a genus since sunk into synonymy with Hygrophorus. MushroomExpert.Com's reference line ('Fries, 1815) Fries, 1838') is consistent with the 1815 description and the 1838 transfer. MushroomExpert.Com adds that a number of similar species and varieties have been named on the basis of minor differences in physical features, and that whether these taxa are phylogenetically distinct has not, to the author's knowledge, been investigated. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com says the species is traditionally thought to be mycorrhizal with conifers, but that recent investigations (Lodge and collaborators, 2013) suggest it may be parasitic on the roots of spruces and other conifers. Both MushroomExpert.Com and Wikipedia report it under redwood and Sitka spruce on the West Coast of North America, Engelmann spruce in the Rocky Mountains, and eastern hemlock or spruces in the northeast. Wikipedia also lists blue spruce in the Rocky Mountains. Both describe fruiting singly, scattered or in small clusters, late in the year: MushroomExpert.Com gives late summer and fall (over winter in warmer climates), and Wikipedia gives late summer to early winter, occasionally as early as June or through December depending on location and climate. Wikipedia describes the range as northern and western North America, Europe (except the Mediterranean region) and Russia, and says the species favours acidic and chalky ground with mosses at higher altitudes, in conifer forests and occasionally mixed forests. These habitat details are single-source statements from Wikipedia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
