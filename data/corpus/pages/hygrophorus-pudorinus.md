## Summary

Hygrophorus pudorinus (Fr.) Fr. is a species of waxy cap in the family Hygrophoraceae, with the combination dated 1836. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a stocky, sticky-capped waxy cap with a pinkish-orangish cap and thick white gills, associated with spruces and other conifers in North America. The same source notes that the name is considered by Lodge and collaborators (2013) to be generally misapplied, and that North American material may represent several genetically distinct species. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name is the Latin word pudorinus, meaning "blushing". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries described the species as Agaricus pudorinus in his 1821 Systema Mycologicum, and it became Hygrophorus pudorinus when Hygrophorus was raised to genus rank. Wikipedia places it in subsection Pudorini of Hygrophorus, alongside H. erubescens and H. purpurascens. MushroomExpert.Com cites Lodge et al. (2013), who report that the type of H. pudorinus matches H. persicolor, that the name has been misapplied to H. abieticola, and that the North American taxon called H. 'pudorinus' falls in a sister clade to H. persicolor in their ITS analysis. MushroomExpert.Com also lists several North American varieties (var. fragrans, var. fragrans f. pallidus, var. subcinereus). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with spruces and other conifers, growing scattered to gregariously in late summer and fall. It reports the species from Colorado to the West Coast of western North America, and in central and northeastern North America from about the Great Lakes northward; the collections it illustrates are from Engelmann spruce forests in Colorado and from Québec. Wikipedia separately says it grows under fir and spruce in coniferous woodland in western and northeastern North America, appears in groups or fairy rings, and often grows in boggy places in sphagnum moss. The sphagnum and fairy-ring details come from Wikipedia alone. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
