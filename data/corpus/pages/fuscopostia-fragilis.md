## Summary

Fuscopostia fragilis (Fr.) B.K. Cui, L.L. Shen & Y.C. Dai (2018) is a polypore in the order Polyporales, placed by MushroomExpert.Com in the family Fomitopsidaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as common on the deadwood of spruces and other conifers in Europe and North America, recognizable by a cap and pore surface that bruise reddish brown and by pores that often become elongated and tooth-like. That description is a single field-guide source. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the species was originally described from Sweden, with the basionym attributed to E. M. Fries in 1828. Tyromyces fragilis, Postia fragilis and Oligoporus fragilis are listed among its many synonyms. The genus Fuscopostia was proposed by Shen et al. (2018). The MushroomExpert.Com author notes that this was done without following several of the guidelines for establishing new genera set out by Vellinga et al. (2016), and that a better taxonomic solution may appear later. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of spruces and other conifers, causing a brown rot. It grows alone or gregariously and is annual, fruiting from spring through fall, or over winter in warm climates. That source describes it as widely distributed in Europe and North America, and its illustrated and described collections are from Illinois, Ohio and Wisconsin. The GBIF description record lists distribution only as DK and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
