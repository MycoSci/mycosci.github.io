## Summary

Stereum hirsutum (Willd.) Pers. 1800, in the family Stereaceae, is a bracket-forming crust fungus that grows on dead wood, often in overlapping, fused groups. MushroomExpert.Com notes that mycologists generally apply the name to a group of species or forms that blend into one another, so collections may not fit neatly into S. hirsutum, S. complicatum or S. gausapatum. Wikipedia also records it as a plant pathogen of peach trees. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia gives the substrates as dead limbs and trunks of both hardwoods and conifers, and lists North America, Europe and Australia among its range. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on the dead wood of hardwoods, especially oaks, growing densely gregariously, often from gaps in the bark, and causing a white rot of the heartwood. It gives the season as spring, summer, fall and winter, and describes it as widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia also states that it infects peach trees as a plant pathogen. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
