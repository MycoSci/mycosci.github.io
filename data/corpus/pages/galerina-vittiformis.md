## Summary

Galerina vittiformis (Fr.) Singer, 1950, is a small agaric in the family Hymenogastraceae. Wikipedia describes it as the type species of the genus Galerina and gives the common name hairy leg bell. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes tiny caps of 2–6 mm, orangish brown and fading as they dry, with a lined margin. It notes that many Galerina species are more or less identical to the naked eye, so microscopic examination is required for certain identification. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in moss in summer and fall. The author finds it rising above moss beds in central Illinois from May to September, and the described collections are from Illinois. The same source says it is probably widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia states that it is widely distributed in temperate regions, where it typically grows in moist locations, often among mosses. It adds that in North America it can generally be found from June to September, or somewhat later on the West Coast, where it is more common. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
