## Summary

Lactarius hygrophoroides Berk. & M.A. Curtis (1859) is a milk-cap in the family Russulaceae, with its type locality in Maine. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a dull orange, finely velvety cap, widely spaced gills and copious white milk. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com reports that Hesler and Smith (1979) described four varieties, distinguished by odor and by staining of the gills from the latex. It adds that Lalli and Pacioni (1992) studied the type collections of all hygrophoroides taxa and found that "they cannot be considered autonomous entities". They concluded that "L. hygrophoroides should be regarded as a single entity, with some variability in smell, latex and context colour-change". *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and perhaps with other hardwoods, growing scattered or gregariously in summer. It gives the range as widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains, from Texas to Kansas and the northeastern states. Wikipedia states that it grows under oak trees in eastern North America from June to September. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
