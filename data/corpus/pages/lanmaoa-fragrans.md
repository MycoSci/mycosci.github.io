## Summary

Lanmaoa fragrans (Vittad.) Vizzini, Gelardi & Simonini (2015) is a bolete in the family Boletaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a rare species found in Europe, with a brownish convex cap 5–12 cm across that is velvety when young and later smooth, and a spindle-shaped stipe that is yellow at the top, flushed red lower down and black at the very base. This description comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the fungus was originally described in 1835 by the Italian mycologist Carlo Vittadini as a species of Boletus, and was transferred to Lanmaoa in 2015 by Alfredo Vizzini. Wikispecies gives the basionym as Boletus fragrans Vittad., Descr. fung. mang. Italia: 153 (1835), and the current combination as Lanmaoa fragrans (Vittad.) Vizzini, Gelardi & Simonini, in Vizzini, Index Fungorum 235: 1 (2015). It also lists homotypic combinations in Leccinum (Šutara, 1989), Suillus (Kuntze, 1898), Tubiporus (Ricken, 1918), Versipellis (Quél., 1886) and Xerocomus (Konrad & Maublanc, 1952). *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that L. fragrans is most often reported from southern Europe, where it grows in a mycorrhizal association with oaks. The same source says it has occasionally been reported from North America, but that those reports involve a different mycorrhizal associate (conifers) and it is not known whether that form is the same as the European species. This habitat information comes from one source only. *[wikipedia]*
