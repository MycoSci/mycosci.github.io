## Summary

Stropharia aeruginosa (Curtis) Quél., 1872, is a species in the family Strophariaceae, recorded in many countries, mostly in Europe and North America. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as a slimy mushroom with a blue-green cap that fades to yellowish with age. Its edibility is disputed between sources; see the disagreements field. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet aeruginosa as Latin for "covered in verdigris". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described as Agaricus aeruginosus by William Curtis in 1784 and given its current binomial by Lucien Quélet in 1872. It also says Machiel Noordeloos placed it in Psilocybe in 1995. MushroomExpert.Com dates Curtis's original description to 1774 and quotes Curtis as writing that the cap loses its verdigris green, "alas!", and is often found pale yellowish brown. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously under hardwoods or conifers, on soil or woody debris, in summer and fall. It gives the range as widely distributed in Europe and, in North America, northern and montane areas, the Pacific Northwest and the Midwest. Wikipedia describes it as found on lawns, mulch and woodland from spring to autumn, and says it particularly favours wood-chip mulches in gardens and parks. Mushroom World says it grows in small trooping groups on soil, often amongst grass, in woods, pastures and heaths. The sources differ in detail on season and substrate. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
