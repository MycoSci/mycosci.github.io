## Summary

Ganoderma curtisii (Berk.) Murrill, 1908, is a polypore in the family Polyporaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a wood-decaying polypore found in eastern North America, and a GBIF-hosted description reports it as the first record of the species in Costa Rica and Central America. Whether it is a species distinct from North American material called G. lucidum (G. sessile) has been debated in the literature. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the name was established by Miles Berkeley in 1849 as Polyporus curtisii and transferred to Ganoderma by W. A. Murrill in 1908. It says the species' viability as distinct from North American specimens described as G. lucidum (G. sessile) is debated. It reports that Volk, Gilbertson and Ryvarden, and Bessette et al., do not treat it as a separate species, while several recent molecular studies show it to be genetically distinct from G. lucidum. A GBIF-hosted description lists the type as from South Carolina, USA. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) reports that Ganoderma curtisii meredithiae, a form growing on pine wood that was previously recognized as a separate species, was not supported at species level by DNA work (Loyd and collaborators 2018). Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com are separate sources, and the naming history above rests mainly on Wikipedia. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and sometimes parasitic, growing alone or in groups on decaying hardwood logs and stumps, or from the wounds of injured living trees. It says the fungus is annual, fruiting spring through fall (and over winter in warm climates), and widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains; the collections it illustrates and describes are from Illinois and New York. Wikipedia gives decaying stumps and roots of hardwoods as the habitat and says the species is primarily found in the Southeastern United States, with Craig and Levetin claiming to have observed it in Oklahoma. The two sources differ in how widely they describe the range. A GBIF-hosted description reports that in Costa Rica the species is found only in the highlands, always associated with decaying wood in Quercus or Pinus forests, and that it occurs in the same forest types in Mexico. The same description says that G. curtisii f. sp. meredithiae was erected for forms occurring exclusively on pines (Loyd et al. 2018). *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
