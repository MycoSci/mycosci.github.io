## Summary

Sphaerosporella brunnea (Alb. & Schwein.) Svrček & Kubička is a small cup fungus in the family Pyronemataceae (order Pezizales). Wikipedia describes it as a pyrophilic species that commonly grows on burned substrates, and both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe fruiting bodies only a few millimetres across (1-6 mm and 3-5 mm respectively). Wikipedia also reports it as a contaminant of truffle (Tuber) nurseries and orchards. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by Albertini and Schweinitz in their Conspectus fungorum (1805) as Peziza brunnea, and the current name was given by the Czech mycologists Svrček and Kubička in 1961. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both report that S. brunnea and S. hinnulea were long kept apart on habitat (burned ground versus moss or moist or sandy soil) and on colour (brown to yellow-brown versus orange-brown or reddish-brown). Both cite Yao & Spooner (1996) as the most recent treatment to consider the question, which treats the two as synonyms. Wikipedia adds that charcoal was found in the substrate of the S. hinnulea type specimen, and that Trichophaea brunnea is also a synonym. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that S. brunnea grows on burnt substrates, including soil containing charcoal or other burnt matter, and has also been found in sandy soils and among mosses and peat. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) likewise says it is often collected on burned substrates but is also found among mosses and on bare soil. It describes the fungus as growing alone, gregariously or in loose groups, fruiting spring through fall (a single-source statement), and as apparently widely distributed in North America. Its illustrated collection came from mossy soil under white oak in Illinois. Both sources describe it as mycorrhizal and possibly also saprobic. Wikipedia calls it ectomycorrhizal with low host specificity, and names hosts including Pinus banksiana, Picea, Larix, Populus, Quercus robur, Quercus ilex and Castanea sativa. For distribution, Wikipedia lists Australia, Asia, the eastern United States and parts of Europe (Germany, Austria, Poland, the United Kingdom, Sweden). The GBIF description record lists Belgium (Flemish Region), Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
