## Summary

Clavulinopsis sulcata Overeem (1923) is a clavarioid fungus in the family Clavariaceae and the type species of the genus Clavulinopsis. Wikipedia describes it as forming very long, slender, cylindrical pinkish or orange fruiting bodies that grow on the ground among plant litter. "Flame fungus" is a vernacular name that has been used for it. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was originally described from Java in 1923 by the Dutch mycologist Casper van Overeem, who made it the type species of the newly circumscribed genus Clavulinopsis. It also reports that E.J.H. Corner treated C. sulcata as a synonym of Clavaria miniata Berkeley (1843), a name that is illegitimate because it is a homonym of the earlier, unrelated Clavaria miniata Purton (1821). Ronald Petersen at first agreed with Corner. A study of the C. miniata type specimen by Derek Reid found ellipsoid rather than globose spores, so the two may not be synonymous, as Petersen later acknowledged. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows on the ground among leaf litter. It was initially described from Indonesia and has been reported from eastern Australia (Queensland, New South Wales, Victoria and Tasmania) and from both islands of New Zealand. In Tasmania it is found in mixed Nothofagus and Leptospermum woodland. This is a single-source account (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
