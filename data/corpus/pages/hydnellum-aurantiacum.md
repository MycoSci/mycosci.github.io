## Summary

Hydnellum aurantiacum (Batsch) P. Karst. is a tooth fungus in the family Bankeraceae, with reddish-orange to rusty red fruit bodies that bear spines rather than gills under the cap. Wikipedia lists it as critically endangered in the United Kingdom and describes it as inedible. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet aurantiacum is derived from the Latin for "orange". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the names "orange spine" and "orange hydnellum" refer to the species' reddish orange or rusty red colored fruit bodies. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, August Batsch first described the fungus in 1789 as Hydnum suberosum var. aurantiacum, and Petter Karsten transferred it to Hydnellum in 1879. Wikipedia also lists the synonyms Hydnum stohlii (Rabenhorst, 1873), Hydnellum complectipes (Hall, 1972), Hydnum aurantiacum, Calodon aurantiacus (Karsten, 1881) and Phaeodon aurantiacus (Schröter, 1888). *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the species is used in mushroom dyeing, where it produces grayish to greenish-gray colors depending on the mordant used. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it is typically found growing solitary or in clusters on the ground in conifer and mixed woods. It reports the species from Australia, Europe, North America and Asia, and as one of the most frequently encountered Thelephorales species in the Sverdlovsk region of Russia. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially pines and eastern hemlock, and fir or Douglas-fir in the Pacific Northwest, and perhaps occasionally with hardwoods. It fruits alone or gregariously in summer and fall (or over winter in warm climates) and is apparently widespread in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
