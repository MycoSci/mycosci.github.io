## Summary

Daedalea quercina (L.) Pers., the oak mazegill, is a bracket polypore in the family Fomitopsidaceae. Index Fungorum currently lists it as Fomitopsis quercina, and Wikipedia reports that it was transferred to Fomitopsis in 2024. Wikipedia describes it as inedible due to its toughness. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet refers to the oak genus Quercus, on which the species frequently grows. MushroomExpert.Com says the genus name refers to the maze-like pore surface, noting that Daedalus designed the maze that hid the Minotaur. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com gives the authority as (Linnaeus, 1753) Persoon, 1801. Wikipedia reports that the species, previously placed in Daedalea, was transferred to the new genus Fomitopsis in 2024 on molecular phylogenetic data, as Fomitopsis quercina (L.) Spirin & Miettinen (2024). The GBIF record notes that original material was an illustration in Buxbaum (1740) and that no type was designated. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the fruit bodies have been used as a natural comb and as an aphrodisiac, and to brush down horses with tender skin. It also cites Gilbertson for the use of smoldering fruit bodies in England to anesthetize bees. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or in small groups with fused caps on decaying oak wood, occasionally on other hardwoods, and causing a brown rot of the heartwood. It says the species is widely distributed in North America but rare west of the Mississippi. Wikipedia says it prefers Quercus but has also been found on Fagus grandifolia, Fraxinus americana, Juglans nigra and Ulmus americana. Wikipedia also reports it from nearly all European countries, following the distribution of oak, and from Northern Africa, North America, Asia from the Caucasus to India, and Australia. The GBIF description has an old note 'Habitat in Quercubus'. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
