## Summary

Butyriboletus frostii (J.L. Russell) G. Wu, Kuan Zhao & Zhu L. Yang, 2016, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, recorded in the US, Mexico, Costa Rica and Canada. Sources describe a sticky bright red cap, red pores, a coarsely net-patterned (reticulate) red stem, and flesh and pore surface that bruise blue. Its current genus is disputed: Index Fungorum lists it as Exsudoporus frostii, and Wikipedia also uses Exsudoporus, whereas MushroomExpert.Com uses Butyriboletus. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that John Lewis Russell named the species for his friend, the amateur American mycologist Charles Christopher Frost, who published a description of it in his 1874 survey of the boletes of New England. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia (a single source for this account), the species was named by the Unitarian minister John Lewis Russell of Salem, Massachusetts, from specimens found in Brattleboro, Vermont. Frost published the description in 1874, so older literature cites the name as Boletus Frostii Russell apud Frost. Mycologist Roy Halling, while attempting to establish a lectotype, examined Russell's original material and notes. He concluded that Frost made the original species determinations and that there is no evidence Russell ever collected the species or wrote a description of it. Wikipedia lists later transfers: Suillellus (Murrill, 1909), Tubiporus (Imai, 1968), and Exsudoporus (2014, following molecular studies). In 2016 Wu and colleagues, citing insufficient sequences, treated Exsudoporus as a synonym of Butyriboletus and made the combination Butyriboletus frostii. Wikipedia adds that later phylogenetic and morphological analyses resolved Exsudoporus as a distinct genus sister to Butyriboletus. Rolf Singer first described a Florida bolete as a subspecies of this species in 1945, then later published it as a separate species, Boletus floridanus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe a mycorrhizal species associated with hardwoods, especially oaks. Fruit bodies grow alone, scattered or in groups on the ground and appear in summer and fall (Wikipedia: summer to early autumn, June to September in eastern North America). Wikipedia gives the US distribution as Maine south to Georgia, west to Tennessee and Michigan, and southern Arizona. It also reports the species under madrone in Mexico and with the oaks Quercus copeyensis, Q. costaricensis, Q. rapurahuensis and Q. seemanii in Costa Rica. MushroomExpert.Com gives the range as eastern North America, Texas, pockets of the Southwest, Mexico and Central America, and says its illustrated collections are from Kentucky. Wikipedia quotes William Murrill and Alexander H. Smith on a preference for thin oak woods, or thin sandy soil under scrub oak, where light is enough for grass to grow. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
