## Summary

Entoloma abortivum (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Donk, 1949, is a species in the family Entolomataceae, known in English as the aborted entoloma and shrimp of the woods. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a steely gray entolomatoid mushroom found near decaying wood in the hardwood forests of eastern North America, usually found parasitizing a species of Armillaria. Lumpy masses of tissue often found alongside the mushrooms were long thought to be an 'aborted' form of the Entoloma. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that it was first named Clitopilus abortivus by Miles Joseph Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis, and that the Dutch mycologist Marinus Anton Donk gave it its current name in 1949. MushroomExpert.Com dates the Berkeley and Curtis name to 1859. No supplied source describes the original material or the reasons for the transfer to Entoloma. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as parasitic on species of Armillaria, and probably also saprobic on woody debris and leaf litter. It grows scattered or gregariously near decaying wood in hardwood forests, or in leaf litter near decaying wood, and is often found with Armillaria species fruiting nearby. The season is given as late summer and fall, and the range as widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains. The same source says that irregular 'aborted' masses of tissue, 2-10 cm high, are often found alongside the gray mushrooms. It also says these masses were once thought to result from Armillaria mellea parasitizing the Entoloma. A 2001 study (Czederpiltz, Volk & Burdsall), as reported by MushroomExpert.Com, suggested the reverse: the masses are malformed Armillaria fruiting bodies permeated by E. abortivum hyphae. Wikipedia likewise states that research indicates the Entoloma parasitizes the honey mushroom. A GBIF description record lists a distribution of Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
