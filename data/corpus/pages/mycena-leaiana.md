## Summary

Mycena leaiana (Berk.) Sacc. (1891) is a species in the family Mycenaceae, known in English as the orange mycena. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as a saprobic species with bright orange caps that grows in dense clusters on hardwood deadwood. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states the species was named after Thomas Gibson Lea (1785–1844), a mushroom collector from Ohio who had sent a collection of specimens to Berkeley for identification. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally named Agaricus leajanus by the English biologist Miles Joseph Berkeley in 1845. Pier Andrea Saccardo moved it to Mycena in 1891, when the large genus Agaricus was divided. Wikipedia also reports that M. leaiana var. australis was proposed as a varietal name after specimens were found in Australia. The reasons given are the wide geographical separation from the eastern United States populations and a difference in cap color. MushroomExpert.Com's reference line likewise gives the authorship as (Berkeley, 1845) Saccardo, 1891. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods, growing in tight, dense clusters. The author notes rare occasions of finding it growing alone, binding leaf litter under hardwoods. That source gives the season as summer and fall and the range as widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains ("from the Great Plains eastward"). Wikipedia independently describes it as growing in dense cespitose clusters on hardwood logs and branches, and as reported throughout the eastern and central U.S. and Canada. For var. australis, Wikipedia gives Australia and New Zealand. A GBIF descriptive record lists the ecosystem as disturbed secondary forest and pasture ("Bosque secundario perturbado | Potrero"), with distribution given as Global, BR and Vermont. It is a single database record with no further detail. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
