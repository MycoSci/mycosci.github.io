## Summary

Lactarius fallax A.H. Sm. & Hesler (1962), the velvety milk cap, is a species of Russulaceae described from Oregon. Wikipedia describes it as having velvety, brown to blackish caps 2–9 cm across with a distinct pointed umbo. It exudes a white latex that slowly stains the flesh and gills vinaceous. It is reported from the western United States and Canada, north to Alaska. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet fallax is derived from the Latin word for "deceptive". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was described by Alexander H. Smith and Lexemuel Ray Hesler in a 1962 publication. Smith made the initial collection in late October 1944 in Rhododendron, Oregon. The same publication described the variety L. fallax var. concolor from specimens collected on Mount Hood. Smith and Hesler said this variety had until then generally been identified as Lactarius lignyotus in the Pacific Northwest. They also described L. lignyotus var. americanus, but in their 1979 monograph of North American Lactarius they treated it as equivalent to, and synonymous with, L. fallax var. concolor. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that the fruit bodies grow scattered to grouped on the ground, or on very rotten conifer logs in alpine areas under standing conifers. It also reports them in spruce forests and mixed conifer forests. It describes the species as fairly common and typically found between August and October. Wikipedia gives the range as the western United States and Canada, extending north to Alaska, and says the eastern range is bounded by the Great Plains. It says field observations suggest the fungus can form ectomycorrhizal associations with Tsuga heterophylla, and that Hesler and Smith noted var. concolor was prevalent under fir species. All of this comes from a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
