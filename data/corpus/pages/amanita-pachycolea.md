## Summary

Amanita pachycolea D.E. Stuntz (1982), the western grisette or Stuntz's great ringless amanita, is a species in the family Amanitaceae with its type locality in California. It belongs to Amanita section Vaginatae and, like others in that group, lacks a ring on the stem and has a sacklike volva and a strongly lined cap margin. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as the largest North American member of the Amanita vaginata group, with a dark brown cap up to about 18 cm wide and a stem 10-25 cm long. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was recognized as distinct by mycologist Daniel Elliot Stuntz and published in 1982 by Harry Delbert Thiers. MushroomExpert.Com cites the authority as 'Stuntz in Thiers & Ammirati, 1982'. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in fall and winter, in coastal Northern California and northward along the Pacific Coast. It says the species is found primarily in northern California and is also documented from the Pacific coasts of Oregon, Washington and British Columbia. Wikipedia says it is found in western North America, associated with conifers in coniferous and mixed forests. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
