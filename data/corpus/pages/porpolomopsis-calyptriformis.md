## Summary

Porpolomopsis calyptriformis (Berk.) Bresinsky (2008) is a species of agaric in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the Ballerina waxcap or Pink Waxcap. Wikipedia describes it as a mainly European species of agriculturally unimproved grassland, assessed as globally "vulnerable" on the IUCN Red List. It describes the cap as narrowly conical and pale rose-pink to lilac-pink. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet as coming from Greek καλὐπτρα (a woman's veil) plus Latin forma (shape), hence "veil-shaped". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1838 by the Rev. Miles Joseph Berkeley as Agaricus calyptraeformis (so spelt), from specimens he collected locally in England. In 1889 the Swiss mycologist Victor Fayod moved it to Hygrocybe. In 2008 Bresinsky proposed the genus Porpolomopsis to accommodate it. Wikipedia says recent molecular work based on DNA sequences found that it does not belong in Hygrocybe sensu stricto and confirmed its removal to Porpolomopsis. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the species is widespread but generally rare across Europe, with its stronghold in the United Kingdom, and that like other waxcaps it occurs in old, agriculturally unimproved short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns). It also says the species has been reported from North America, but that DNA-sequenced specimens from there are not the same as the European P. calyptriformis. Wikipedia notes that recent research suggests waxcaps are neither mycorrhizal nor saprotrophic but may be associated with mosses. *[wikipedia]*
