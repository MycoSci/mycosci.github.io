## Summary

Cortinarius semisanguineus (Fr.) Gillet, 1876, is a webcap in the family Cortinariaceae, known in English as the Surprise Webcap. Sources describe a medium-sized mushroom with a yellowish-brown to ochre cap and bright blood-red gills that turn cinnamon-brown to rusty with age. It is associated with conifers, and Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com report it from Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name semisanguineus means 'half blood-red', a reference to the gill colour, as compared with C. sanguineus, which is wholly blood-red. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia describes 'surprise webcap' as a recently given, 'fanciful' common name, and says the species was called the 'red-gilled webcap' in the past. No source explains how either name arose. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says some authorities place the species in the genus Dermocybe, but most mycologists retain Dermocybe as merely a subgenus of Cortinarius. MushroomExpert.Com lists Dermocybe semisanguinea as a synonym. It also notes variants based on gill colour (yellowish to blood red to orangish) and stem colour, and variable spore size according to Cortinarius specialist Joseph Ammirati (1972, 1984). No supplied source describes the original material or the reasons for later transfers. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that Cortinarius semisanguineus can be used as a dye for textile yarns. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species appears in conifer or mixed conifer and birch woods in autumn, and that it has a mycorrhizal relationship with birch (Betula). It says the species is often abundant under young spruce in plantations on acid soil, appearing from September to November. It calls the species occasional in Britain, Europe and Scandinavia, and common in parts of North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially pines, growing alone or scattered, often in moss, in summer and fall, and apparently widely distributed in North America. Mushroom World says it grows typically with conifers and birch. The GBIF description record lists distribution only, naming Belgium (including its Brussels-Capital, Flemish and Walloon regions), Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, gbif_desc]*
