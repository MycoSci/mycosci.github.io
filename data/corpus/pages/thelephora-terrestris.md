## Summary

Thelephora terrestris (Ehrh. ex Fr.) is a fungus in the family Thelephoraceae, known in English as Earth-Fan or Earthfan. It is recorded in many countries across Europe, North America and elsewhere. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a spreading fungus that forms fan-shaped, dark brown caps in small clusters and rosettes. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

iNaturalist says the name Common Fiber Vase refers to the circular, overlapping cap, and notes that it has also been called the Earthfan fungus. *[inaturalist]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the fungus was first described by Jakob Friedrich Ehrhart in 1787. MushroomExpert.Com says the North American version is probably not the same as the true European one originally described in the 18th century, and that DNA studies suggest hidden species within the genus. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it grows on soil in North America and Europe, commonly in sandy soils under pine, and that it forms ectomycorrhizal associations especially with Pinus. It also reports associations with alder, birch, oak, beech and poplar. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, spreading over the ground and fruiting singly, in groups or in clusters. It gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates, and calls it widespread and common in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
