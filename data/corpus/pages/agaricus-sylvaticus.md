## Summary

Agaricus sylvaticus Schaeff. (1774) is a species in the family Agaricaceae. Its type locality is Germany and the catalog records it as growing on soil. GBIF holds occurrence records from many countries. Wikispecies places it in Agaricus subg. Pseudochitonia, sect. Sanguinolenti. *[wikispecies]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name sylvaticus (or silvaticus) means "of the woods". It notes that both spellings are found in the literature and that Species Fungorum gives sylvaticus as the current name. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the species was first validly described under the current name, as Agaricus silvaticus, in 1774 by Jacob Christian Schäffer. It adds that most gilled mushrooms were then grouped in Agaricus, and that A. sylvaticus has kept the same name while the genus was later restricted. Wikispecies gives the original reference as Schaeffer, J.C. 1774, Fungorum qui in Bavaria et Palatinatu circa Ratisbonam nascuntur Icones, 4:1–136. This is a single-source account from encyclopedic sources. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Cultivation

A 2012 nutritional study (lit_2) describes A. sylvaticus, there called the "Sun Mushroom", as cultivated in Brazil. Its material was obtained in dehydrated form from a producer in Minas Gerais State. The abstract gives no cultivation method. *[lit_2]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is often found in groups in coniferous forests, from early summer or from September through to November, in Europe, North Africa and North America. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*
