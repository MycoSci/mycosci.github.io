## Summary

Itajahya galericulata Möller (1895) is a stinkhorn in the family Phallaceae, with occurrence records from South America, North America, Africa, India and Australia. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as fairly easy to recognize because of a calyptra, a cap-like patch of whitish tissue, at the apex of a head whose surface is made of small wrinkled knots of tissue. Wikipedia names the same calyptra as the main feature separating Itajahya from other stinkhorns. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the generic name Itajahya derives from the Itajahy River in Brazil, where the fungus was originally discovered. It states that the species name comes from the Latin galericulatus, 'provided with a little helmet-like skull-cap (galericulum) or galerum', and interprets this as referring to the bonnet-like calyptra atop the head. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the 'jacaranda' in the English name 'jacaranda helmet stinkhorn' comes from the South American tree Jacaranda mimosifolia, which is widely planted along streets and elsewhere in English-speaking South Africa. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first published in 1895 by Möller in Botanische Mitteilungen aus den Tropen (Bot. Mitt. Trop. 7: 79, 148). In 1996 Kreisel designated it Phallus galericulatus (Czech Mycol. 48(4): 275), but Wikipedia says recent studies returned the taxon to Itajahya. MushroomExpert.Com notes that preliminary DNA studies (Marincowitz et al. 2015, Melanda et al. 2021) have upheld its separation from the near-identical, pink-stemmed Itajahya rosea. Its author calls separation of the genus Itajahya from Phallus more tentative, in his view. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both MushroomExpert.Com and Wikipedia describe the species as saprobic. Both say it grows gregariously in gardens and cultivated areas, and Wikipedia says this is reported for North America. MushroomExpert.Com gives the season as summer and fall in the Northern Hemisphere and winter and spring in the Southern Hemisphere. It lists the illustrated collection as from Arizona. Wikipedia says that in Australia the species is described as inhabiting dry mulga country with an annual rainfall of less than 50 cm. It also says that in South Africa the species commonly occurs in association with the planted ornamental tree Jacaranda mimosifolia, and that it may have been introduced there in pots of that tree. MushroomExpert.Com notes a disjunct distribution (South America, Texas and the southwestern United States, South Africa, India) and says this suggests human-mediated transport, leaving the original range uncertain. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
