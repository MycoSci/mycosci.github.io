## Summary

Entoloma vernum S. Lundell (1937) is a species in the family Entolomataceae, described from Sweden. MushroomExpert.Com treats the name as an aggregate for pointy, brown, springtime entolomatoid mushrooms with a deep pink spore print, and says that applying the European name to North American collections has not been tested with DNA-based study. GBIF's distribution description lists Belgium, the Flemish Region, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com says the species was originally described from Sweden (Lundell 1937). It reports that Hesler's 1967 southeastern United States treatment does not include E. vernum. It says Hesler's Entoloma tortuosum, described from a March collection in Tennessee, was synonymized with E. vernum by Machiel Noordeloos in 1988 after he studied Hesler's type, and that no contemporary DNA-based study has investigated this. It also says Largent (1994) recorded the species as Nolanea verna from Alaska, and named a variety with isodiametric spores, Nolanea verna var. isodiametrica, from Oregon, Idaho and Wyoming. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Kuo) describes the European species as a springtime denizen of conifer plantations, sometimes also under hardwoods. It gives the ecology as saprobic or possibly mycorrhizal, growing scattered to gregariously, especially in pine plantations (including red pine plantations), and also in hardwood forests under tulip trees and oaks, in spring. It calls the species widespread in Europe and reported across North America, and its described collections are from Illinois, Indiana, Michigan and Pennsylvania. Mushroom World, a separate aggregator, gives a different account: mainly spring but also summer and autumn, solitary or in small trooping groups in grasslands, often near conifers. GBIF's distribution description lists Belgium, the Flemish Region, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, gbif_desc]*
