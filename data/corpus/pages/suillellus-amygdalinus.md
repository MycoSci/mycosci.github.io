## Summary

Suillellus amygdalinus (Thiers) Vizzini, Simonini & Gelardi (2014), formerly Boletus amygdalinus, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae recorded from western North America. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as having thick, red to brown caps, red pores, and a strong blue staining reaction when cut or injured. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia notes that in Latin, amygdaline means relating to or resembling an almond. It does not say this is the reason for the epithet. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first named Boletus puniceus by Harry D. Thiers in 1965, from specimens he found in Napa County, California, on 23 November 1963. In 1975 Thiers changed the name to Boletus amygdalinus, because the epithet puniceus had already been used for a different bolete from Yunnan, China, published in 1948. The fungus was transferred to Suillellus in 2014, after molecular phylogenetics showed it to be in a lineage distinct from Boletus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that the fruit bodies grow on the ground, in groups or scattered. The fungus has been reported from low-elevation hardwood forests of live oak, manzanita and madrone in California and Oregon. Fruiting occurs after the onset of autumn rains, usually between October and January. Wikipedia adds that the mushroom is hard to spot because its cap resembles madrone leaves in color and it is often buried under leaves. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*
