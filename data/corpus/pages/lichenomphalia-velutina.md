## Summary

Lichenomphalia velutina (Quél.) Redhead, Lutzoni, Moncalvo & Vilgalys (2002) is a basidiolichen in the family Hygrophoraceae. Wikipedia describes it as known mainly from Europe and North America, with a first Asian record from China in 2018, and also from Greenland. Its fruiting bodies are small and mushroom-like, with caps 4–12 mm across. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first formally described by the French mycologist Lucien Quélet, who placed it in the genus Omphalia, and that it was transferred to Lichenomphalia in 2002. Wikispecies lists the basionym Omphalia velutina Quél. under 1885 and the combination Omphalina velutina (Quél.) Quél. under 1886. Neither source says why the transfers were made. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows on soil and on moss. It reports Chinese collections at elevations above 3,000 m and European collections generally below 1,500 m. It gives the distribution as European and North American, adds that it also occurs in Greenland, and says it was first recorded from China (and Asia) in 2018. This is a single-source account (Wikipedia; the iNaturalist text is a copy of it). *[wikipedia]*
