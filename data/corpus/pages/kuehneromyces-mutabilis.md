## Summary

Kuehneromyces mutabilis (Schaeff.) Singer & A.H. Sm. (1946) is a species in the family Strophariaceae, commonly called the sheathed woodtuft. Sources describe it as growing in clumps on dead wood, with a hygrophanous cap that changes colour markedly as it dries and a ringed, scaly stem. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) states that the genus Kuehneromyces was established by Singer & Smith (1946) with K. mutabilis as its type species. The same page notes that the species is frequently treated as a Pholiota, and that DNA studies it cites (Matsumoto et al. 2003; Jacobsson & Larsson, 2007) support placing it apart from Pholiota, in Kuehneromyces. It lists Pholiota mutabilis and Galerina mutabilis as synonyms and credits the original epithet to Schaeffer, 1774. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species always grows on wood, generally on stumps of broad-leaved trees (especially beech, birch and alder) and rarely on conifers. It gives a fruiting period of April to late October, and also winter months where conditions are mild, and lists North America, Australia, Asia (the Caucasus, Siberia, Japan) and Europe as its range. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing in clusters, rarely alone, on the wood of hardwoods or conifers. It gives spring, summer and fall as the season, plus winter in warm climates. The author reports finding it on both alder and conifer wood. That page calls it widely distributed and common in montane western North America and occasionally reported from the Appalachian Mountains, with the described collections from Colorado. Mushroom World says it grows clustered on stumps and logs of broad-leaf trees, favouring birch. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
