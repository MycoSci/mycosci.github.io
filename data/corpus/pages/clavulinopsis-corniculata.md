## Summary

Clavulinopsis corniculata (Schaeff.) Corner is a clavarioid fungus in the family Clavariaceae, given the recommended English name meadow coral in the UK. Wikipedia describes it as forming branched, cylindrical, ochre fruit bodies and as part of a species complex that has not yet been resolved. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as fairly distinctive among corals and clubs because of its yellow colours and delicate branching. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was originally described from Germany in 1774 by Jacob Christian Schäffer, and that E. J. H. Corner placed it in the genus Clavulinopsis in 1950. It adds that a recent epitype comes from Slovakia, and that initial molecular work on DNA sequences indicates C. corniculata belongs to a complex of related species. GBIF's description records list the original material as an illustration in Ray's Synopsis Methodica Stirpium Britannicarum (ed. 3, 1724) and note that no type was designated. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species grows on the ground, singly or in troops, and is presumed to be saprotrophic. It says that in Europe it generally occurs in agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns) from July to November, and that in America and Asia it grows in woodland. It also gives woodland litter as a habitat. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo), writing about North America, describes it as saprobic and growing alone, scattered, or gregariously under hardwoods or conifers in woods, or in grassy areas, often on disturbed ground. It fruits in summer and fall, or over winter in warmer climates. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
