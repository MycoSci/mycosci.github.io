## Summary

Leucoagaricus leucothites (Vittad.) Wasser, 1977, is a species in the family Agaricaceae, known in English as the White Dapperling. Sources describe it as a saprobic mushroom of lawns and other disturbed ground with a nearly worldwide distribution. Index Fungorum currently treats the accepted name as Leucocoprinus leucothites. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was originally described as Agaricus leucothites by Carlo Vittadini in 1835, that Solomon Wasser transferred it to Leucoagaricus in 1977, and that in 2023 it was reclassified as Leucocoprinus leucothites. Wikispecies gives the basionym publication as Agaricus leucothites Vittad., Descr. fung. mang. Italia: 310 (1835), and the Leucoagaricus combination as Ukr. bot. Zh. 34(3): 308 (1977). MushroomExpert.Com notes the species is also known as Leucoagaricus naucinus and, in older sources, Lepiota naucina. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously in grassy areas or on disturbed ground such as roadsides and cultivated areas, often in the vicinity of conifers and occasionally in woods, from late summer through fall and sometimes in spring. Kuo describes it as widely distributed and common throughout North America, with the illustrated collections from Illinois, Michigan and Québec, and describes its overall distribution as nearly global, occurring primarily in urban, cultivated areas. Wikipedia independently describes a worldwide distribution with most occurrences in Europe and North America, generally in disturbed, grassy areas such as gardens and parks and sometimes in forests. Wikipedia also records it as a secondary food source for adults of the pleasing fungus beetle Tritoma biguttata affinis. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
