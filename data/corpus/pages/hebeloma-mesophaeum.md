## Summary

Hebeloma mesophaeum (Pers.) Quél. (1872) is a species of mushroom in the family Hymenogastraceae. A GBIF-hosted description calls it widely distributed and very common in Europe, ranging from the Mediterranean through the temperate and boreal zones to the Arctic and alpine zones, and less common in colder areas. It reports only a few records from the Rocky Mountains and arctic Canada. *[gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com notes that Hebeloma strophosum, traditionally separated on the basis of its smaller spores and more persistent veil, is regarded as a synonym by Vesterholt (2005). A GBIF-hosted discussion reports that Beker et al. (2016) could not delimit H. mesophaeum on several loci and suspected several species hidden within the sample assigned to it. It suggests that H. excedens and H. alpinicola are likely among these 'cryptic' taxa. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (a single source) describes it as mycorrhizal with various conifers, including pines, spruces and firs, and usually growing gregariously. It gives the season as fall, winter and spring, and also summer at higher elevations. It says that in North America the species appears limited to association with conifers, whereas the European concept (Vesterholt, 2005) is quite broad and allows 'nearly all types of habitat'. Wikipedia says it can be found near trees, including conifers. Mushroom World says it grows on soil, predominantly in the presence of conifers, in late summer to autumn. A GBIF-hosted description gives its range as circumpolar, arctic-alpine, temperate, boreal and subarctic, and apparently uncommon in Greenland (11 records). *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld, gbif_desc]*
