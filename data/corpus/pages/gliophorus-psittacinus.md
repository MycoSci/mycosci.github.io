## Summary

Gliophorus psittacinus (Schaeff.) Herink, 1958, is a waxcap in the family Hygrophoraceae, commonly called the parrot waxcap. Wikipedia describes it as a small, colourful mushroom with a cosmopolitan distribution. MushroomExpert.Com cautions that the name probably covers a species complex in need of further study. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was formerly known as Hygrocybe psittacina, and that a molecular phylogenetics study found it belongs in Gliophorus; it had already been placed in Gliophorus but had been considered a synonym of Hygrocybe. MushroomExpert.Com adds that it was also previously known as Hygrophorus psittacinus, and that Lodge and collaborators (2013) resurrected the genus Gliophorus for it and its close relatives. The same source quotes those researchers on the "extraordinarily high sequence divergence among collections identified as H. psittacinus", which they take to indicate a species complex. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that Gliophorus psittacinus is widely distributed in grasslands in western Europe, the United Kingdom, Iceland, Greenland, North America, South Africa and Japan. It gives the fruiting season as late summer to autumn (September to November), and says the species is apparently in decline in Europe due to habitat degradation. MushroomExpert.Com describes North American collections (illustrated and described collections are from Illinois, Michigan and Québec) appearing in hardwood and conifer forests, growing scattered to gregariously, frequently in moss or on mossy embankments along wooded roadsides, from spring through fall or over winter in warmer climates. That source says the precise ecological role is uncertain. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
