## Summary

Hohenbuehelia petaloides (Bull.) Schulzer, 1866, is a species in the family Pleurotaceae, known in English as the Floury Oyster or Shoehorn Oyster. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as distinctively shaped, with a "petaloid" habit that looks like a shoehorn with gills or a rolled-up funnel. That source also lists fairly crowded whitish gills, a white spore print, and a mealy odor and taste. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard and was later assigned to the genus Hohenbuehelia in 1866 by Stephan Schulzer von Müggenburg. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (single-source description, North American collections) calls it saprobic and terrestrial. It grows alone, gregariously, or in clusters, often in the vicinity of woody debris, and is often found in gardens, parks, lawns, or even potted plants, but also in woods. That source gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in warmer climates, and says it is widely distributed in North America; the described collections are from Illinois, Kentucky, and California. It also says the species does not usually grow directly from dead wood. Wikipedia instead describes it as saprobic on decaying wood, found alone or in small groups around woody debris or the occasional stump. Wikipedia calls its distribution cosmopolitan, with regions named as North America, Venezuela, Europe, Japan, and New Zealand. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
