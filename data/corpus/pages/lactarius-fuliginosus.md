## Summary

Lactarius fuliginosus (Fr.) Fr. 1838, the sooty milkcap, is a species in the family Russulaceae. Wikipedia describes medium-sized fruit bodies with velvety, grayish-brown caps and crowded gills. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet fuliginosus derives from the Latin word for "sooty". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by Karl von Krapf in 1782 as Agaricus fuliginosus. Fries sanctioned that name in his 1821 Systema mycologicum and transferred it to Lactarius in 1838. Later synonyms are Kummer's 1871 Galorrheus fuliginosus and Kuntze's 1891 Lactifluus fuliginosus; Wikispecies lists the same synonyms. Wikipedia also reports that a 2012 molecular analysis found L. fuliginosus and L. picinus to be sibling species that cannot be reliably distinguished by morphology alone. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that L. fuliginosus is ectomycorrhizal with deciduous trees and that its fruit bodies grow on the ground in deciduous forests of Europe and North America. In Asia it has been recorded from the Kashmir Valley in India, China, and Japan. *[wikipedia]*
