## Summary

Rickenella fibula (Bull.) Raithelh. (1973) is a small orange-to-yellow mushroom in the family Rickenellaceae, with GBIF occurrences recorded across Europe, the Americas, Asia and Australasia. Sources describe it occurring among moss, with a cap usually under about 1 cm across. Wikipedia reports that molecular analysis places it closer to certain polypores and crust fungi than to other gilled mushrooms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the fruit body occurs among moss, which is why it is sometimes called 'moss sentinel'. This is a single-source statement, and no source narrates the origin of the other common names. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in moss beds, from spring through fall, or over winter in warm climates. It gives the species as widely distributed in North America, with the described collections from Illinois. Kuo calls it probably saprobic but apparently involved in some sort of mutualism with moss, and notes it also occurs in commercial 'Frog Moss' used in terrariums and vivariums. In Kuo's area it is often a springtime species, appearing during late morel season. Wikipedia likewise says the fruit body occurs among moss. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
