## Summary

Panellus stipticus (Bull.) P. Karst. is a small, tough, fan- or kidney-shaped wood-decay mushroom in the family Mycenaceae. It is the type species of the genus Panellus. Strains from eastern North America are described as bioluminescent, while strains from the Pacific coast of North America and from other continents are reported not to be. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet stipticus refers to the fungus's purported value in stopping bleeding. It describes the epithet as a Greek equivalent of the Latin astringens, from στυπτικός (styptikós), itself from the verb στύφειν (styphein), 'to contract'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first named Agaricus stypticus by Jean Bulliard in 1783 and sanctioned under that name by Elias Magnus Fries in 1821. Fries later moved it to Panus as Panus stipticus. Petter Karsten assigned the current name in 1879. Wikipedia says the species has passed through many genera, and that Panellus stypticus remains in use as a variant spelling. MushroomExpert.Com also notes the alternate spelling and gives the original description as Bulliard 1783, from France. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies are reputed to have been used in traditional Chinese medicine as a styptic to staunch bleeding, and also as a 'violent purgative'. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both MushroomExpert.Com and Wikipedia describe it as a saprobe of hardwood deadwood. Wikipedia adds that it causes a white rot and usually grows in tightly overlapping clusters on logs, stumps, fallen branches and the sides of hardwood trees. It says the fungus can also grow in wounds of living trees. Wikipedia lists preferred hosts in North America as oak, birch, maple, hickory, pecan and American hornbeam. For Europe it lists oak, birch, alder, beech, hazel, chestnut and ash. It also reports occasional records on loblolly pine and eastern white pine. Wikipedia gives fruiting as September through November in Europe, the Canary Islands and North America, sometimes in spring, with long-lasting fruit bodies that may be found year-round. MushroomExpert.Com gives spring through fall, also over winter in warm climates or during winter warm spells in temperate areas. Wikipedia reports it as common in northern temperate Europe and also collected in Australia, New Zealand, Anatolia, Japan and China. In North America it is reported as more common in the east than the west, ranging from Alaska to Costa Rica. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
