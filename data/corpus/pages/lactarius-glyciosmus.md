## Summary

Lactarius glyciosmus (Fr.) Fr. 1838, the coconut milkcap, is a member of the Russulaceae. GBIF records it from Europe, North America and elsewhere, including Japan, China and New Zealand. The supplied sources describe it as a small to medium-sized Lactarius with pinkish buff colours and a strong smell of coconuts, associated with birch (Betula). *[gbif_desc, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific name derives from the Ancient Greek words glukos, "sugar(y)", and osmos, "smell". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the species was first described by the Swedish mycologist Elias Magnus Fries as Agaricus glyciosmus in 1818, and MushroomExpert.Com likewise gives Fries, 1818. Wikispecies lists the basionym Agaricus glyciosmus as Fr., 1815, so the sources differ on the year. MushroomExpert.Com adds that it has not been established whether the North American version of L. glyciosmus is the same as the original European species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with paper birch (possibly also with other birches) and also reported with alder. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, in late summer and fall, and is widely distributed in North America wherever the host trees occur. Its illustrated collections are from Michigan and Wisconsin. Wikipedia describes it as growing in soil under broad-leaved trees, particularly birch and often within sphagnum moss, from late summer to autumn. A GBIF-hosted taxonomic treatment says it is typically associated with Betula, though Neuhoff (1956) reported it under Salix and Alnus as well. The same treatment calls it common from the end of May through October in wet places with Sphagnum and on grassy roadsides. A second GBIF-hosted treatment lists Montana and Wyoming specimens collected near Betula glandulosa. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
