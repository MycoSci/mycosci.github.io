## Summary

Alloclavaria purpurea (O.F. Müll.) Dentinger & D.J. McLaughlin is a club or coral fungus in the family Rickenellaceae, formerly known as Clavaria purpurea. Wikipedia gives the common names purple fairy club and purple coral. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having dull purple, unbranched, cylindrical to nearly spindle-shaped fruiting bodies growing in tightly packed clusters, with a white spore print. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that "Alloclavaria" means "the other Clavaria". *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com say the species was moved out of Clavaria into its own genus on the basis of molecular/phylogenetic analysis. MushroomExpert.Com credits Dentinger & McLaughlin with creating the genus Alloclavaria for it. It says the genus is set apart by prominent cystidia, which it says are absent from the related genera Clavaria, Clavulina and Clavulinopsis, and by molecular data. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says fruit bodies are found in spruce-fir forests, October to December on the West Coast of North America and July to October further inland. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes tightly packed clusters under conifers, often in moss, in summer and fall (also winter in warmer climates), and widely distributed in northern, montane and western North America. The same page says the species is traditionally presumed to be saprobic, but that Dentinger & McLaughlin suggested it might be mycorrhizal or associated with mosses. A 2025 study in the Siberian Journal of Forest Science reports it as a new record among protected fungi for Krasnoyarsk Stolby National Park; the supplied abstract gives no location or substrate details for it. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_2]*
