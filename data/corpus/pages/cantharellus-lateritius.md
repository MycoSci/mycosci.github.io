## Summary

Cantharellus lateritius (Berk.) Singer 1951, the smooth chanterelle, is a species in the family Hydnaceae, recorded in the United States, Costa Rica, Mexico, Colombia, Australia and other countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a medium-sized to large chanterelle with a smooth to shallowly wrinkled undersurface and a single, well-defined stem. The same source says it is mycorrhizal with oaks in eastern North America. It also quotes a 2011 paper by Buyck & Hofstetter saying the species complex needs more morphological and molecular study. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific name lateritius means "bricklike" and refers to the smooth hymenium. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Thelephora cantharella by Lewis David de Schweinitz in 1822, from specimens collected in Ohio. Fries transferred it to Craterellus in 1838. In 1856 Berkeley and Curtis called it Craterellus lateritius, and Berkeley used that name again in 1873, citing Alabama as the type locality. Ronald H. Petersen (1979) treats Berkeley's name as a nomen novum rather than a new species. Petersen could not locate Schweinitz's original specimen, so Berkeley's epithet takes precedence as the earliest published name with an associated type specimen. Otto Kuntze's Trombetta lateritia (1891) is listed as another synonym, and Rolf Singer transferred the species to Cantharellus in 1951. Wikipedia says the motivation for Berkeley's name change is unclear. The account rests on this single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and sometimes hickories in eastern North America. It grows alone, scattered, gregariously, densely gregariously, or in loose clusters, in summer and fall. It is widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains, and the collections described are from Pennsylvania and Illinois. The same source says it usually appears in July. Wikipedia reports it appearing from June to September in North America, extending north to Michigan and New England, under hardwood trees. Wikipedia also cites Howard Bigelow for New England growth on road shoulders in grass near oaks, and says it prefers sloping creek banks. Outside North America, Wikipedia reports it on forest soil in Malaysia, mostly under Shorea, and in the Western Ghats of Kerala, India, forming ectomycorrhizal associations with Vateria indica, Hopea parviflora, Diospyros malabarica and Myristica malabarica. A GBIF description record gives a Colombian collection from Tolima at 2350 m elevation on 24 November 2005, and another GBIF record lists Vermont. Wikipedia's Asian and African records may not refer to the same entity as the North American one: MushroomExpert.Com notes that Buyck (2014) described several similar smooth chanterelles from Africa, Asia and New Caledonia as new species. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
