## Summary

Mythicomyces corneipes (Fr.) Redhead & A.H. Sm. is the only species in the genus Mythicomyces, family Mythicomycetaceae, and was first described as Agaricus corneipes by Elias Fries in 1861. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as having shiny yellowish-orange to tawny caps 1–3 cm across on slender stems 1–2 mm thick. GBIF records it from North America and northern Europe, including Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Fries described Agaricus corneipes in 1861 from collections made in a fir forest near Alsike, Sweden. Andrew Price Morgan later recorded it in northwestern North America in 1907, and Smith recorded it several times after that. It was placed in Psilocybe by Petter Karsten in 1879 and in Geophila by Lucien Quélet in 1886. Gastón Guzmán excluded it from Psilocybe in 1983, citing roughened spores without a germ pore, a pale spore print, the stem texture and the tawny basal mycelium. Redhead and Smith erected the genus Mythicomyces in 1986 and first placed it in the Strophariaceae. Later authorities moved it to the Psathyrellaceae on molecular evidence, and in 2019 the family Mythicomycetaceae was recognized for Mythicomyces and Stagnicola. All of this comes from a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes Mythicomyces corneipes as saprobic, using plant debris (usually bits of wood) as substrate. Fruit bodies appear in autumn and grow in groups among mosses in moist habitats, such as near the edges of bogs, or under conifers or birch in soil wet from spring flooding. Wikipedia reports it as most common in the Pacific Northwest of North America, and rare but widespread across northern Europe. It also quotes Smith calling the species 'extremely rare' in 1938. GBIF distribution data lists Norway and Sweden. The habitat description rests on one source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
