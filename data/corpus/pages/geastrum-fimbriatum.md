## Summary

Geastrum fimbriatum Fr. (1829) is an earthstar in the family Geastraceae, known in English as the sessile earthstar. Wikipedia describes it as distinguished from other earthstars by the delicate fibres lining the pore at the top of its spore sac. It is recorded across Europe, Asia and the Americas. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet fimbriatum means "fringed", referring to the characteristic edge of the apical pore of the spore sac. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Elias Magnus Fries described the species (as Geaster fimbriatus) in his 1829 Systema mycologicum. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia notes that the species was depicted on a Nigerien postage stamp in 1985. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to Wikipedia, G. fimbriatum is saprobic, and its fruit bodies grow on the ground in groups or clusters, usually near the stumps of hardwood trees. Wikipedia lists it from Europe, Asia (India and Mongolia), eastern North America (including Mexico), Central America (Costa Rica) and South America (Brazil). This is a single-source account; the GBIF description record lists only distribution areas. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
