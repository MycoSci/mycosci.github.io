## Summary

Boletus sensibilis is a species of bolete in the family Boletaceae, described by Charles Horton Peck, with its type locality in New York. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as one of many North American red-capped, blue-staining boletes, with flesh and pores that turn dark blue almost instantly when handled or cut. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe a dirty pinkish red cap that fades to reddish cinnamon and a spore print that is grayish olive. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com, quoting Peck's original description, says Peck named the species "sensibilis" (sensitive) because of "the ease and rapidity" of its blue bruising. Wikipedia likewise says the name was adapted from the Latin word for sensitive and refers to the near-instant blue staining. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com says the species is frequently misunderstood and that Peck's original description (1880) is the best guide. It reports that Peck described the stem as lemon yellow, often stained with red or rhubarb-color, and mentioned no odor. Kuo states that the popular ideas that the species must smell like curry, or can have a red stem with a yellow apex, are mistakes that lead to curry-scented specimens of Boletus bicolor being mislabeled as B. sensibilis. He says a third species, Boletus pseudosensibilis (Smith & Thiers, 1971), was named nearly a hundred years later and is easily confused with it. Wikipedia gives 1879 as the year of Peck's description; MushroomExpert.Com cites 1880. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, especially oaks and beech, growing scattered or gregariously in summer and fall, and "possibly widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains". The collection it illustrates and describes is from Illinois. Wikipedia likewise describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, often near oaks or beech, in fall and summer east of the Rocky Mountains. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
