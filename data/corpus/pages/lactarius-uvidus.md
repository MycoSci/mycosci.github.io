## Summary

Lactarius uvidus (Fr.) Fr. (1838) is a milk-cap in the family Russulaceae, known in English as the purple-staining milk cap or shiner. Its milk and cut flesh stain lilac to violet, and the cap is slimy or sticky when fresh. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both say it occurs in Europe and North America. Wikipedia and iNaturalist say the fungi identified under this name are a complex of closely related species and varieties that are hard to delimit. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, inaturalist]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says Fries first described the species as Agaricus uvidus in 1818 and gave it its current binomial in 1838. Wikispecies dates the basionym Agaricus uvidus to 1815, and MushroomExpert.Com cites Fries 1818. The supplied sources therefore disagree on the year of the basionym. Wikipedia adds that the species gives its name to section Uvidi of Lactarius, whose members have milk that discolours violet and a greasy cap surface. Wikispecies lists transfers to Galorrheus (Kummer, 1871) and Lactifluus (Kuntze, 1891). It also lists two misapplications of the name: L. uvidus sensu Kühner is treated as Lactarius robertianus, and L. uvidus sensu Quélet as Lactarius aspideus. None of the sources say why the transfers were made. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (single-source claim) calls it a European species found under birch, spruce and willows. In eastern North America it describes the fungus as mycorrhizal with quaking aspen, big-toothed aspen and birch, sometimes reported under conifers, and as also reported under pines and willows in the Appalachian Mountains. It grows alone or gregariously in summer and fall. It is fairly common in eastern North America from roughly the 41st parallel northward, and is also reported from the southern Appalachians. MushroomExpert.Com says the western North American version is usually called Lactarius montanus. Wikipedia describes the species complex as occurring in deciduous and coniferous woods, with a preference for acidic soils, and as widely distributed in Europe, North Africa and North America. Wikipedia says it is fairly common in some regions and rare in others, and that it is classified as vulnerable or endangered on the Danish and Dutch Red Lists of Fungi. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
