## Summary

Rubroboletus rhodoxanthus (Krombh.) Kuan Zhao & Zhu L. Yang (2014) is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the Ruddy Bolete. Wikipedia describes it as a species native to Europe, more widespread in warm southern regions and rare in the north. It is generally regarded as inedible and may cause gastrointestinal symptoms if eaten. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species epithet is derived from the Ancient Greek words ρόδο (rhódo, "rose" or "pink") and ξανθός (xanthós, "blonde" or "fair"). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the fungus was first described in 1836 by Julius Vincenz von Krombholz, who considered it a variety of Boletus sanguineus. In 1925 Franz Joseph Kallenbach recombined it as a distinct species, and it remained in Boletus until 2014. It was then transferred to the newly erected genus Rubroboletus on the basis of DNA data. A placement in Suillellus, following an online recombination, was not supported by molecular data and has been rejected by later authors. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to Wikipedia, it forms ectomycorrhizal associations with members of the Fagaceae, particularly oak (Quercus) and beech (Fagus), and sometimes chestnut (Castanea). It is described as rare in northern Europe and more frequent in warm southern regions. Molecular testing has confirmed it in France, Italy, Portugal, Cyprus and Sardinia. On Cyprus it is reported as locally frequent, appearing in seasons with early rainfall and growing on serpentine soil under the endemic golden oak (Quercus alnifolia). It is considered critically endangered in the Czech Republic and reported as extinct in England. In the British Islands it is known only from Northern Ireland. *[wikipedia]*
