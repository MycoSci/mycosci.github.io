## Summary

Amanita ocreata Peck (1909) is a species in the family Amanitaceae, described from California. Wikipedia describes it as a deadly poisonous destroying angel of the Pacific coast of North America that associates with oak trees. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states the specific epithet is derived from the Latin ocrĕātus 'wearing greaves', from ocrea 'greave', referring to its loose, baggy volva. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Charles Horton Peck described the species in 1909 from material collected by Charles Fuller Baker in Claremont, California, and that Amanita bivolvata is a botanical synonym. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports fruiting from January to April, in mixed woodland on the Pacific coast of North America from Washington south through California to Baja California, Mexico. It is described as ectomycorrhizal and found in association with coast live oak (Quercus agrifolia) and hazel (Corylus spp.); in Oregon and Washington it may also be associated with Garry oak (Quercus garryana). *[wikipedia]*
