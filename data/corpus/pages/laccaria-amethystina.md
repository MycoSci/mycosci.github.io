## Summary

Laccaria amethystina (Cooke, 1884), the amethyst deceiver, is a small mushroom in the family Hydnangiaceae. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe a purple cap and gills whose colour fades with age or drying, which is the basis of the common name. GBIF records it across Europe, North America, Asia and parts of Central and South America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the bright amethyst colour fades with age and weathering, which makes the mushroom difficult to identify, hence the name "deceiver". It adds that the name is shared with the close relative Laccaria laccata, which also fades and weathers. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1778 by William Hudson as Agaricus amethystinus. Mordecai Cubitt Cooke later placed it in Laccaria, and the combination Laccaria amethystina was first used by Cooke in 1884. MushroomExpert.Com gives the same authorship, "(Hudson, 1778) Cooke, 1884". Wikipedia also says it was once placed in Collybia, as Collybia amethystina, and was treated in 1922 as a variety of L. laccata, var. amethystina (Cooke) Rea, which is now a synonym. Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com say the name L. amethystina was formerly applied to purplish North American collections. Wikipedia says Gregory M. Mueller described several of these as distinct species in 1984 and 1988. MushroomExpert.Com says the name was misapplied on the West Coast to Laccaria amethysteo-occidentalis. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as mycorrhizal, growing solitary to scattered with a variety of deciduous and coniferous trees, most commonly trees in the Fagales and often with beech. It gives the season as late summer to early winter, and June–December in Europe. It says that in Central and South America the species more commonly grows with oak, and that it has been shown to be an "ammonia fungus", one that grows abundantly on soil after nitrogen-containing material is added. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, especially partial to oaks and beech, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in late spring and summer, and widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains. These two sources differ on season and on whether conifers are hosts. Mushroom World says it typically grows in scattered groups on soil in broadleaf and mixed woodland, often among leaf litter. A GBIF descriptions record gives the habit as mycorrhizal and the substrate as Quercus humboldtii, alongside a Colombian (Tolima) collection. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, gbif_desc]*
