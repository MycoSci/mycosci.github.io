## Summary

Rhizomarasmius setosus (Sowerby) Antonín & A. Urb. (2015) is a species in the family Physalacriaceae. The English Wikipedia entry describes it as a tiny whitish mushroom with a distinctive hairy stem. GBIF holds occurrence records from many countries, mostly in Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia, the only independent source for this, says the species epithet comes from the Latin adjective "saetosus" or "setosus", meaning "coarsely hairy". It says that word in turn derives from "saeta" (or "seta"), a bristle. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia (a single-source account), the species was originally described in 1801 as Agaricus setosus by James Sowerby in his work "Coloured Figures of English Fungi or Mushrooms". Lucien Quélet independently described it as Marasmius recubans in 1873. In 1987 Machiel Noordeloos determined that Sowerby's name takes precedence and made the combination Marasmius setosus. In 2015 Vladimír Antonín and Alexander Urban transferred it to the new genus Rhizomarasmius. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that this mushroom grows on dead beech leaves, and occasionally on other deciduous leaves such as willow or birch. It describes the species as widely distributed in central and western Europe. This is a single-source statement, and the source gives no season. *[wikipedia]*
