## Summary

Cystodermella cinnabarina (Alb. & Schwein.) Harmaja is a small agaric in the family Agaricaceae, with a reddish-orange cap covered in grainy or mealy granules. It was transferred from Cystoderma in 2002, and the spine lists the English name Cinnabar Powdercap. It is recorded widely across Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described as Agaricus granulosus var. cinnabarinus by Albertini and Schweinitz in 1805. The GBIF description text says no holotype was preserved or designated, and that a neotype was designated from Estonia (Saaremaa, in Pinus sylvestris forest on sand dunes). MushroomExpert.Com says it was originally described from Portugal and neotypified from Estonia (Saar et al. 2024b). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a saprotrophic species fruiting in coniferous and deciduous forests, on ground among moss, grass and litter, solitary or in small groups in summer and autumn. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a saprobe growing alone or gregariously under conifers, sometimes fruiting from well-rotted wood. It gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. Wikipedia lists records under pine, oak, spruce, fir and chestnut in Greece and Turkey. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
