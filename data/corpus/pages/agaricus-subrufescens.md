## Summary

Agaricus subrufescens Peck is a species in the family Agaricaceae, commonly known as the almond mushroom, with its type recorded from New York. Wikipedia reports that the name Agaricus blazei was long misapplied to it, and that genetic and interfertility testing placed the Brazilian strains called A. blazei and A. brasiliensis, and European samples called A. rufotegulis, in the same species. Wikipedia lists records from North and South America, Europe, Asia and Australia. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Agaricus subrufescens was first described by the American botanist Charles Horton Peck. It says the fungus was found again in Brazil in the 1970s and misidentified as Agaricus blazei Murrill, a species originally described from Florida. According to the same source, Didukh and Wasser rejected the name A. blazei for this species in 2002 but called the Brazilian fungus A. brasiliensis, a name already used for a different species, Agaricus brasiliensis Fr. (1830). Wikipedia then reports that Richard Kerrigan's genetic and interfertility tests on several strains showed the Brazilian strains and North American populations of A. subrufescens to be genetically similar and interfertile, and that European samples called A. rufotegulis were the same species. It concludes that A. subrufescens is the oldest name and so has priority. Wikispecies records the type locality as New York (on soil), lectotype NYS 599340, and lists synonyms with their authors and dates, including A. blazei (Murrill, 1945), A. rufotegulis (Nauta, 1999) and A. brasiliensis (Wasser et al., 2002, nom. illegit.). *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only independent source describing habitat, states that A. subrufescens forms fruit bodies singly or in clusters in leaf litter in rich soil, often in domestic habitats. Wikispecies records the type collection as on soil in New York. Wikipedia says the species was originally described from the northeastern U.S. and Canada, and lists records from California, Hawaii, Great Britain, the Netherlands, Taiwan, Japan, the Philippines, Iran, Australia, Brazil and Uruguay. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*
