## Summary

Geastrum saccatum Fr. (1829) is an earthstar in the family Geastraceae, known in English as the sessile earthstar, bowl earthstar or rounded earthstar. The mature fruiting body is described as a small round spore case sitting on 4-9 (Wikipedia: five to eight) rays that curve back, measuring 2-5 cm across when the rays are expanded. It is distinguished from similar earthstars by a circular ridge or depression around the pore of the spore case. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as saprobic, growing scattered or clustered in humus leaf litter, usually in late summer and fall, and also states it is found on rotting wood. It gives a North American season of September-December on the west coast and July-October elsewhere. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously under hardwoods or conifers, often around stumps, from spring through fall (over winter in warmer climates). GBIF description records list substrates including leaf litter, soil, and dead trees or soil. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
