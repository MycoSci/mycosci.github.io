## Summary

Asterophora lycoperdoides (Bull.) Ditmar (1809) is a species of Lyophyllaceae that grows as a parasite on other mushrooms, mainly Russula species. Wikipedia describes it as found in North America and Europe. MushroomExpert.Com describes a small mushroom whose cap soon becomes covered in orangish brown powder made of asexual chlamydospores. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the asexual spores (chlamydospores) are star-shaped, "hence the name 'star bearer'". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard first named the species in 1784 as Agaricus lycoperdonoides. Wikispecies lists this as the basionym (Herb. Fr. 4, tab. 166, 1784) and gives the combination in Asterophora by Ditmar in Neues Journal für die Botanik 3(3-4): 56 (1809). MushroomExpert.Com says it was originally described from France and lists Nyctalis asterophora as a former name. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultivation

One laboratory study (Mycoscience, 2006) reported that A. lycoperdoides formed fruiting bodies on potato dextrose agar medium in approximately 1 week, with an optimal temperature of 25 °C for mycelial growth and fruiting-body formation. The same study reported that the fungus showed high host specificity to Russula nigricans in nature, and that growth was faster on media supplemented with freeze-dried R. nigricans. This is a single laboratory study, not a described cultivation method. *[lit_3]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as parasitic on species of Russula and Lactarius (especially Russula dissimulans, Russula densifolia and closely related blushing russulas). It grows alone or gregariously, usually appearing when the host has begun to blacken and decay, and is found in a variety of forests. A Japanese study (Mycoscience, 2006) reported high host specificity to Russula nigricans in nature. An Indian record (Biodiversity, 2007) reports it growing on Russula sp. in Madhya Pradesh. Wikipedia gives the season as July to September in eastern North America and somewhat later on the West Coast, and August to November in temperate Europe, where it can be locally common. MushroomExpert.Com gives summer, fall and winter (in warmer climates), and says it is widely distributed in North America but apparently more common east of the Great Plains. *[mushroomexpert, lit_3, lit_2, wikipedia]*
