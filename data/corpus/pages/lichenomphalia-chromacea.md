## Summary

Lichenomphalia chromacea (Cleland) Redhead, Lutzoni, Moncalvo & Vilgalys (2002) is a basidiolichen in the family Hygrophoraceae. Wikipedia describes it as producing yellow-orange, mushroom-like fruiting bodies with a cap width typically under 4 cm, alongside a greenish, granular lichen thallus of fungal hyphae and algae on the soil around the base of the stipe. That source gives its range as southern Australia (South Australia, New South Wales and Tasmania). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by John Burton Cleland from specimens collected in South Australia. Wikispecies lists the basionym as Omphalia chromacea Cleland, Trans. Roy. Soc. South Australia 48: 239 (1924). Scott Redhead and Thomas Kuyper later transferred it to Botrydina and then to Phytoconis, following changes to the rules of botanical nomenclature. Tom May and Alec Wood proposed placing it in Omphalina in 1995. In 2002 the basidiolichen genus Lichenomphalia was circumscribed and this species was among those transferred to it. Botrydina and Phytoconis have since been synonymized with Lichenomphalia. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (the only independent source with habitat text) reports it from South Australia, New South Wales and Tasmania. It is said to fruit in clusters on the ground, occasionally on bark, and often amongst mosses. In Tasmania it is described as typically found in sandy or peaty soil in heathland and woodland, usually at higher elevations. *[wikipedia]*
