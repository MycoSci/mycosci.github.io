## Summary

Hydnophlebia chrysorhiza (Eaton) Parmasto, 1967, is a crust fungus in the family Meruliaceae (Polyporales). The fruiting body is a resupinate, membranous crust, orange-brown when dry and reddish orange to deep orange when fresh, with a toothed (hydnoid) surface of conical to subcylindrical spines 1–1.6 mm long. It is recorded in GBIF from the United States, Canada, Japan and a number of other countries. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the taxon was originally described as Hydnum chrysorhizum by Amos Eaton in 1817, and that it is the type species of the genus Hydnophlebia. The GBIF description text gives the type as Hydnum chrysorhizon Torr. in Eaton's Manual, 3rd edition, with a lectotype in herbarium NY designated by Burdsall in 1985. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a white rot organism infecting dead deciduous trees, found in eastern North America from July to November. A specimen listed in the GBIF description material was collected on Quercus sticks in Hamilton County, Ohio, in October 1973. These habitat statements come from one encyclopedia entry and one specimen record, not from a habitat survey. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
