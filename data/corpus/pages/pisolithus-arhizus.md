## Summary

Pisolithus arhizus (Scop.) Rauschert, 1959, is a member of the Sclerodermataceae (Boletales). Wikipedia describes it as a widespread earthball-like fungus. Common names include dead man's foot, dyeball, pardebal and Bohemian truffle. MushroomExpert.Com documents it from southern Europe, Africa and Japan and says the name is widely applied in North America without support from recent research. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Lycoperdon arrizon Scop. (Delic. Fl. Faun. Insubr. 1: 40, 1786). Homotypic synonyms listed there include Scleroderma arhizum (Pers., 1801), Pisocarpium arhizum (Link, 1816) and Lycoperdodes arrhizon (Kuntze, 1891). Heterotypic synonyms include Polysaccum crassipes DC. (1807). The combination in Pisolithus is by Rauschert (Zeitschr. Pilzk. 25(2): 51, 1959). The sources give no account of the reasons for these transfers. *[wikispecies]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the black viscous gel is used as a natural dye for clothes. It also states that the species is a major component of mycorrhizal fungus mixtures used in gardening as root stimulators. The same source records the name pardebal in South Africa and Bohemian truffle in Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with pines, oaks and cedars. It grows alone, scattered or in small groups, often in gravel, sandy soil, ditches and lawns. Fruiting is in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. The range given there is southern Europe, Africa and Japan, and the illustrated collection came from an urban stone pine grove on the Italian Riviera. That page states that recent research (Martin et al. 2002, Lebel et al. 2018) does not support applying the name arhizus to North American material. Its authors point to Pisolithus arenarius for the North American species. The iNaturalist text, a derivative of Wikipedia, says the species may in fact be several closely related species. *[mushroomexpert, inaturalist]*
