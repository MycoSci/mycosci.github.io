## Summary

Pulveroboletus ravenelii (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Murrill is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, first described as Boletus ravenelii in 1853 and moved to Pulveroboletus in 1909. It is known as Ravenel's bolete or the powdery sulfur bolete. Wikipedia reports it from Asia, Australia and the Americas. MushroomExpert.Com describes young fruit bodies as having a powdery, bright yellow partial veil covering the pore surface and sheathing the stem, with greenish-blue bruising on the pores. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet honors Henry William Ravenel, the American botanist who collected the original specimens in South Carolina. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Miles Joseph Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis described it as Boletus ravenelii in 1853 from specimens sent to them by the American botanist Henry William Ravenel, who collected them in South Carolina. They called it "a most splendid species closely allied to B. hemichrysus, and, like that, remarkable for the pulverulent veil". William Alphonso Murrill transferred it to the genus Pulveroboletus in 1909, giving the name in use today. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the fruit bodies have been used in traditional Chinese medicine and in mushroom dyeing, where the colour obtained (yellow, gold, greenish yellow, orange or olive) depends on the mordant used. This is a single-source statement from an encyclopedia entry. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Sources agree that this is a mycorrhizal species fruiting on the ground alone, scattered, or in groups (MushroomExpert.Com adds occasionally in small clusters). Wikipedia describes it as growing in woods under conifers, with pine, hemlock and rhododendron as preferred hosts, and its introduction also mentions oak. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal primarily with conifers (especially pines, eastern hemlock and spruces) but also with hardwoods, including oaks, manzanita and rhododendron. Both give summer to fall fruiting; Wikipedia gives July to October, and both say later on the North American West Coast, with MushroomExpert.Com adding early winter there. Wikipedia describes the North American range as eastern Canada south to the Gulf of Mexico and west to Texas, Michigan and California, and MushroomExpert.Com calls it very widely distributed in North America but more common in the south. Wikipedia also reports it from a Mexican beech (Fagus mexicana) forest in Hidalgo (2010), from Costa Rica and Colombia, from Indonesia and China in Asia, and from northeast Australia. A GBIF distribution record lists Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
