## Summary

Chroogomphus helveticus (Singer) M.M. Moser (1967) is a species in the family Gomphidiaceae. A 1977 study describes it as a European species with a southern Hercynian-Alpine-Carpathian and Illyrian-Macedonian distribution that grows together with conifers. A 1973 survey described it as rare in Czechoslovakia. *[lit_1, lit_2]*

## Discovery and naming

A 1973 survey in Česká Mykologie states that the species was first described as a variety of Gomphidius viscidus by A. Pilát in 1926 and was later recognized as a distinct species. The same paper reports that its taxonomy was clarified using new and herbarium specimens. *[lit_2]*

## Habitat and ecology

A 1977 paper in Feddes Repertorium says the species grows in association with various conifers. It names Picea abies, Pinus cembra, Pinus peuce, Pinus strobus and Abies alba as possible mycorrhizal partners. It gives an altitude range of 300 m to more than 2000 m above sea level. It says the species is not bound to acidic soils but can be called acidophilic. It says distribution centers lie in areas where the five-needled pines Pinus peuce and Pinus cembra are native. A 1971 paper in the same journal lists it among alpine elements of the fungal flora recorded in the south of the former DDR (East Germany). Both habitat sources come from one publisher cluster, so this is effectively single-source information. *[lit_1, lit_3]*
