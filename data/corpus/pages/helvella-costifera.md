## Summary

Helvella costifera Nannf. (1953) is a cup fungus in the family Helvellaceae, order Pezizales. Wikipedia describes it as having stalked, cup-shaped fruiting bodies 1.5–4 cm across, with a greyish to greyish-brown cup and yellowish-white ribs running from a hollow stalk onto the underside of the cup. Wikipedia lists records from China, North America including Mexico, and Europe (Spain, Denmark, Norway, Sweden). MushroomExpert.Com also describes it as widely distributed in northern and eastern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) cites Nannfeldt, 1953 for the name. It says North American mycologists have separated H. costifera from H. acetabulum using four characters: a gray rather than yellow-brown cap, generally rounded and blunt-edged rather than sharp-edged stem ribs, sometimes slightly smaller spores, and pigment located only in the cell walls of the excipular surface rather than inside the cells. Kuo writes that Nancy Weber (1972) used the last character to separate what she called "Helvella griseoalba" from H. acetabulum and from H. costifera, which she believed to be strictly European. Abbot & Currah (1997) found that distinction insignificant and synonymized Weber's species. Kuo says he goes back and forth on whether H. costifera is truly distinct from H. acetabulum. He suspects the North American members of the group may be several species that do not match the European H. acetabulum or H. costifera, and he says the group needs contemporary study. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*
