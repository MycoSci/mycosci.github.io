## Summary

Pholiota nubigena (Harkn.) Redhead is a species in the family Strophariaceae. According to Wikipedia, it is a secotioid fungus whose fruit bodies look like unopened mushrooms, 1–4 cm tall, with whitish to brownish caps 1–2.4 cm across and a short whitish stipe that runs through the internal spore mass into the cap. It is recorded from mountainous areas of the western United States. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet nubigenum derives from the Latin roots nub, meaning "cloud", and gen-, meaning "born of" or "originating from". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described in 1899 by Harvey Willson Harkness as Secotium nubigenum. Harkness's type collection came from logs of lodgepole pine (Pinus contorta) in the Sierra Nevada at about 2,100 m (7,000 ft). Curtis Gates Lloyd discussed it in 1903 under the spelling rubigenum, arguing that nubigenum was an error carried down from Saccardo. Rolf Singer and Alexander H. Smith erected the genus Nivatogastrium in 1959 with N. nubigenum as its type and only species, and treated Lloyd's spelling as a misprint. Wikipedia adds that the holotype was destroyed in the 1906 San Francisco earthquake. A 2015 Mycotaxon paper (lit_1) reports that phylogenetic analyses and morphology show the secotioid Nivatogastrium nubigenum [=Pholiota nubigena] is nested within Pholiota, and that the species has retained ballistospores with the typical curved sterigmata. Wikipedia says Scott Redhead transferred the species to Pholiota in 2014. *[wikipedia, lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species fruits singly, in groups, or in small clusters on rotting conifer wood, especially fir and lodgepole pine. It fruits in spring and early summer as a "snowbank fungus", often near melting snow or soon after the snow has gone. It is described as common in the Sierra Nevada and Cascade Mountains, usually at 1,650–2,400 m (5,410–7,870 ft). It has been collected in California, Idaho, Oregon and Washington.
