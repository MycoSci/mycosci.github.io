## Summary

Laccaria bicolor (Maire) P.D. Orton, 1960, is a member of the family Hydnangiaceae, known in English as the Bicoloured Deceiver. Sources describe it as an ectomycorrhizal fungus, and it was the first ectomycorrhizal fungus to have its genome sequenced. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that, like others in its genus, it carries the common name 'Deceiver' because of its propensity to fade and become hard to identify. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the taxon was first described by the French mycologist René Maire in 1937 and raised to species rank by P.D. Orton in 1960. MushroomExpert.Com cites the same authorities, (Maire, 1937) Orton, 1960. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as mycorrhizal with a range of trees, found in the temperate zones of the world in summer and autumn. It says the species seems to prefer birch and pine woods, and names red pine, jack pine and black spruce among its ectomycorrhizal partners. Wikipedia places it in temperate and boreal forests of North America and 'probably Northern Europe'. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) instead describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, growing scattered or gregariously, often in moss, in summer and fall. Kuo gives its range as western North America and the Great Lakes region, and notes it has also been reported from West Virginia. The two sources differ in emphasis (birch and pine versus conifers) and were not reconciled. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
