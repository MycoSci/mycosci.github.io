## Summary

Panus conchatus (Bull.) Fr. 1838 is a gilled wood-rotting fungus in the family Panaceae, known as the lilac oysterling. Wikipedia describes it as having a smooth lilac to tan cap and decurrent gills, and MushroomExpert.Com notes a white spore print and a usually off-center stem. Molecular work cited by both sources places it with the polypores rather than the gilled mushrooms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet conchatus is derived from a Latin word meaning "shell-like". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described as Agaricus conchatus by Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard in volume 7 of his 1787 Herbier de la France, and that Elias Magnus Fries transferred it to Panus in 1838. The same source says the fruitbody changes greatly with age: young specimens are pliable, fleshy and lilac to purple, while old ones lose the colour and become tough. It attributes the many synonyms to this variability. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as saprobic, on hardwood stumps, logs and sticks, usually crowded in clusters. It lists beech, poplar, birch and oak as typical hosts, with ash and elm less often, and also conifers (fir, spruce, pine, yew). It reports fruiting in eastern North America from April to August and on the West Coast from December to June. MushroomExpert.Com describes it growing alone or more often gregariously to clustered on decaying hardwood sticks and logs, from spring through fall and over winter in warm climates. It gives the range as widespread in Europe, widely distributed in North America from Canada to Mexico but apparently absent or rare in the southern Rocky Mountains, and reported from the Caribbean and South America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
