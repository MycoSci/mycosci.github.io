## Summary

Buglossoporus quercinus (Schrad.) Kotl. & Pouzar (1966) is a polypore in the family Fomitopsidaceae, known in English as Oak Polypore. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as growing on oak wood in Europe and in eastern North America from New York to Virginia. It has a yellowish cap with no clearly differentiated cuticle and a cream-colored pore surface with very small pores, and its surfaces and flesh bruise pink to purplish. The same source says it is treated as a rare find in European literature. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com credits the species to H. A. Schrader (1794) and the combination to F. Kotlába & Z. Pouzar (1966), and says it was originally described from Germany. The same source lists Buglossoporus pulvinus and Piptoporus quercinus as synonyms. It gives no account of the material or the reasons for the transfers. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and parasitic on living and dead oaks, causing a brown rot. It is annual, grows alone or gregariously, and appears in summer. That source reports it as widely distributed in Europe and, in North America, from New York to Virginia; its illustrated collection is from Maryland. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*
