## Summary

Suillus quiescens T.D. Bruns & Vellinga (2010) is a pored mushroom in the genus Suillus, family Suillaceae, with California recorded as its type locality. Wikipedia describes it as forming ectomycorrhizae with pines and as first collected in 2002 on Santa Cruz Island. It reports records from California and Oregon and an introduced population in New Zealand. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet quiescens refers to the organism's ability to wait dormant (quiescent) in the soil until it encounters pine roots. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Per Wikipedia, fruit bodies were first collected in 2002 on Santa Cruz Island, in Santa Barbara County, California. The species was named provisionally as a new species, Suillus quiescens, in conference proceedings published in 2005, and was officially described and named in a 2010 Mycologia publication. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that fruit bodies grow together in small groups on the ground in association with bishop pine (Pinus muricata). A multigene phylogenetic study of Suillus also sampled it in California with Monterey pine (Pinus radiata). Wikipedia reports that the IUCN Red List assessment describes it as ectomycorrhizal with two- and three-needle pines, and as also occurring with lodgepole pine (Pinus contorta) on the northern California and Oregon coast. Wikipedia says it is the most common Suillus species on Santa Cruz Island and has also been collected at Santa Rosa Island and Point Reyes National Seashore in California. Wikipedia reports an introduced population in New Zealand, growing with non-native Monterey pine.
