## Summary

Coprinellus micaceus (Bull.) Vilgalys, Hopple & Jacq. Johnson (2001) is a species in the family Psathyrellaceae, formerly known as Coprinus micaceus. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a saprobe that grows in clusters on decaying or rotting wood, and Wikipedia gives it a cosmopolitan distribution. Both sources note that the fine, mica-like granules on young caps distinguish it from similar coprinoid mushrooms, though rain often washes them off. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet micaceus derives from the Latin mica, meaning "crumb, grain of salt", plus the suffix -aceus, "like, similar". It adds that the modern application of "mica" to a very different substance comes from the influence of micare, "glitter". This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the names "shiny cap", "mica cap" and "glistening inky cap" all refer to the mealy particles on the cap that glisten like mica. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described scientifically by Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard in 1786 as Agaricus micaceus, in Herbier de la France. It later became known as Coprinus micaceus after Fries raised Persoon's section Coprinus to genus rank. It was transferred to Coprinellus in 2001, when molecular studies led to Coprinus being split into four genera. Wikipedia also says C. micaceus is virtually indistinguishable from C. truncorum by external appearance and that many reported collections of the former may be of the latter. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) likewise says that the macrofeature description matches several official species, all commonly called "Coprinus micaceus" in North American field guides. Kuo says preliminary DNA results raised the possibility that C. micaceus and C. truncorum are genetically identical, but that Keirle and collaborators (2004) found two of the tested "C. micaceus" specimens had initially been identified as C. truncorum. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes C. micaceus as saprotrophic, growing in and around stumps or logs of broad-leaved trees or attached to buried wood, with a preference for bark (particularly the secondary phloem) over the wood itself. It calls the species a late-stage colonizer of wood that is already softened by decay. It also says the species is associated with disturbed or developed ground such as roadsides, paths, gardens, building sites and parking-lot edges. It has been noted growing indoors on rotting wood in humid environments, and once in an abandoned coal mine about 120 m underground. Wikipedia gives a fruiting period of April to September in North America and May to December in Europe, and says it is more prevalent in spring and fall. MushroomExpert.Com independently describes it as saprobic, growing in clusters on decaying wood that may be buried, so the mushrooms appear terrestrial. Kuo gives its season as spring, summer and fall (sometimes winter) and says it is frequently urban but also found in woods, and widely distributed in North America. GBIF description records list distribution localities in Colombia (Tolima, 3000–3100 m a.s.l.), Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Madeira, Faial Island and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
