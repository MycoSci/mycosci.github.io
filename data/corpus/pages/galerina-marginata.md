## Summary

Galerina marginata (Batsch) Kühner is a species in the family Hymenogastraceae, order Agaricales, known by common names including funeral bell, deadly galerina, autumn skullcap and deadly skullcap. Sources describe it as an extremely poisonous, wood-rotting 'little brown mushroom' that contains amatoxins, the same toxins as the death cap. It is recorded across the Northern Hemisphere, in Australia and in Antarctica. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet marginata is derived from the Latin word for 'margin' or 'edge'. It also states that autumnalis, the epithet of a now-synonymized name, means 'of the autumn'. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the common name 'marginate Pholiota' resulted from the species' synonymy with Pholiota marginata. It gives no account of how the other common names arose. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the oldest names in the synonymy are Agaricus marginatus, described by August Batsch in 1789, and Agaricus unicolor, described by Martin Vahl in 1792. Agaricus marginatus has priority as the oldest validly published name. Before 2001, G. autumnalis, G. oregonensis, G. unicolor and G. venenata were treated as separate species. A 2001 DNA study by Gulden and collaborators found no genetic differences between them and G. marginata, and they were reduced to synonyms. MushroomExpert.Com also notes that older field guides list this mushroom as 'Galerina autumnalis'. Wikispecies lists Agaricus marginatus Batsch (1789) as the basionym. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

The sources differ in emphasis on substrate and season. Wikipedia describes a saprobic, wood-rotting fungus typically reported on or near conifer wood, also seen on hardwoods. It says fruit bodies are solitary but more often in groups or small clusters, appearing in summer to autumn, and that those growing on buried wood may appear to be on soil. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on rotting wood of fallen hardwoods and conifers, causing a stringy white rot, usually in clusters, most frequent in spring and fall but found year-round in North America. Mushroom World says it grows in clusters on stumps and logs of broad-leaf trees. A GBIF-listed description records one collection growing in a coastal moss field dominated by Sanionia uncinata. Wikipedia gives the range as the Northern Hemisphere (North America, Europe, Japan, Iran, continental Asia, the Caucasus), Australia and Antarctica. A 2024 paper reports a record from Byers Peninsula, Livingston Island, in the South Shetland Archipelago, Antarctica. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, gbif_desc, lit_2]*
