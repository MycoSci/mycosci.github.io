## Summary

Amanita phalloides (Vaill. ex Fr.) Link, 1833, is in the family Amanitaceae and is commonly called the death cap. It is described as a deadly poisonous ectomycorrhizal mushroom, native to Europe and introduced to other parts of the world, including North America and the Southern Hemisphere. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Sébastien Vaillant described the species in 1727 under a phrase name. Elias Magnus Fries described it as Agaricus phalloides in 1821, but included all white amanitas in that description. Johann Heinrich Friedrich Link settled on the name Amanita phalloides in 1833, after Persoon had named it Amanita viridis about 30 years earlier. This history comes from a single encyclopedia source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states the species is ectomycorrhizal, most commonly under oaks in Europe. It is also recorded under beeches, chestnuts, horse-chestnuts, birches, filberts, hornbeams, pines and spruces. It fruits in summer and autumn. In coastal California it is associated with coast live oak. Where introduced, it has mostly been recorded with the exotic trees it would associate with in its native range. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks, growing alone, scattered or gregariously, on the West Coast and in the eastern United States from Pennsylvania and Virginia to Maine. Pringle et al. (2009) describe two distinct North American ranges: the West Coast (California to British Columbia) and the East Coast (Maryland to Maine). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_3]*
