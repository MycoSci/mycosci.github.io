## Summary

Meripilus giganteus (Pers.) P. Karst., 1882, is a polypore fungus in the family Meripilaceae, commonly known as the giant polypore or black-staining polypore. Wikipedia describes it as a bracket fungus that causes a white rot in broadleaved trees, particularly beech, and forms large multi-capped clumps at the base of trees. Its pore surface quickly darkens black when bruised or injured. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (single independent source; iNaturalist repeats its text) states that M. giganteus grows on hardwoods, more rarely on conifers, and is often found in large clumps at the base of trees, sometimes some distance from the trunk where it parasitizes the roots. It cites Ryvarden and Gilbertson as reporting it especially on Quercus and Fagus, and also on a longer list of hardwoods (including Acer, Betula, Populus, Tilia, Ulmus) and on the conifers Abies, Larix and Pinus. The fruiting body is said to appear in autumn and to collapse into a slimy black mass after the first frosts. The same source describes it as circumboreal in the northern hemisphere and widely distributed in Europe, and says that North American field-guide records are due to confusion with the related M. sumstinei. *[wikipedia]*
