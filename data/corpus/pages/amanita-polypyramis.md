## Summary

Amanita polypyramis (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Sacc. (1887) is a species in the family Amanitaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a large white mushroom found in the Eastern United States, with a chlorine-like scent and pyramid-shaped warts covering the cap. MushroomExpert.Com places it in Amanita section Lepidella and gives mature cap widths of 7-21 cm, a fragile ring that soon disappears, and a large, abrupt, powdery basal bulb. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

The iNaturalist taxon description states that the species name, polypyramis, refers to the pyramid-like warts on the surface of the cap (pileus). This is a single-cluster claim: iNaturalist is a declared derivative of Wikipedia, and the Wikipedia text supplied here does not itself state the derivation. *[inaturalist]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com credits the basionym to Berkeley & M.A. Curtis (1853), with the combination by Saccardo (1887). A 1999 Mycotaxon revision studied the holotype of Amanita alexandri along with other collections of both species. It reported no significant difference between the two taxa and proposed A. alexandri as a posterior taxonomic synonym of A. polypyramis. *[lit_1, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks, often found in pine-oak forests, usually growing alone or scattered, in summer and fall. It gives the distribution as the United States from Texas to New Jersey and Florida, and southward through Mexico to Central America (Costa Rica). Wikipedia describes it as found in the Eastern United States. It notes that although some sources list the range as New Jersey to Costa Rica, the species has been found as far north as New England, especially near Cape Cod. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
