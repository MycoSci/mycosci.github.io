## Summary

Lactarius rufus (Scop.) Fr. is a member of the family Russulaceae, recorded mainly across Europe and North America. One source describes it as a common, medium-sized milkcap with a dark brick-red cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

One source states that the specific epithet rufus is a reference to the mushroom's colour. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

One source states that the species was described originally by Giovanni Antonio Scopoli and later by Elias Magnus Fries. Wikispecies lists the basionym as Agaricus rufus Scop., 1772, and the combination Lactarius rufus (Scop.: Fr.) Fr. 1838. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species appears from late spring to late autumn and is frequent in the northern temperate zones of Europe and North America. It says the species is most commonly found with pine, but can also appear with birch, conifer, or spruce, and that it is common in northern California and the Pacific Northwest from late summer to early winter. A GBIF-hosted description lists Montana and Wyoming collections made under Abies lasiocarpa, Picea engelmannii, Pinus albicaulis and Pinus contorta. That description calls the species extremely morphologically and ecologically variable. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
