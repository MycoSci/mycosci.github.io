## Summary

Lactarius gerardii Peck (1873) is a milk-cap in the family Russulaceae, described from New York. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a brown-capped Lactarius with distant white gills that run down the stem, and with white, unchanging milk. The same source reports that recent molecular work shows the name as traditionally applied may cover more than one species. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the species was originally described from New York by Peck in 1873. That page reports that Stubbe and collaborators (2010) studied gerardii-like collections worldwide and confirmed five gerardii-like species for North America. Three of these line up with traditional morphological species: L. gerardii var. fagicola, L. subgerardii and L. petersenii. The other two are described as mixtures of specimens identified as L. gerardii, L. atro-olivaceus and L. gerardii var. subrubescens. These two cannot be separated by morphology and need DNA testing. Kuo writes that one of the two is the species Peck originally described, but that it may never be known which, because informative DNA is unlikely to be recoverable from Peck's specimens. This account comes from a single source. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods (especially oaks), also sometimes reported under conifers. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall. The same source gives its distribution as widely across the region east of the Great Plains, extending at least as far south as Costa Rica. A GBIF description record for the species lists Vermont as a distribution. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
