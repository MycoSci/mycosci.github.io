## Summary

Agaricus xanthodermus Genev. (1876) is a species of Agaricaceae, known in English as the Yellow Stainer. Sources describe a whitish-capped Agaricus whose flesh, especially at the base of the stem, turns bright yellow when cut or scraped, and which has a phenolic odor. Sources disagree about whether it is safe to eat; see the disagreements section. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet xanthodermus is derived from the Ancient Greek words for "yellow" and "skin", given a Latin adjective ending. It notes that the form Agaricus xanthoderma is also often seen and is legal under the nomenclatural rules. MushroomExpert.Com likewise glosses the epithet as "yellow-skin". *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Léon Gaston Genevier first defined the species in 1876, in a letter published in the bulletin of the French Botanical Society. He wrote about the Agaricus mushrooms of the region of Nantes and proposed a detailed reclassification into 5 species, this one among them. According to the same source, yellow-staining mushrooms had until then been treated as varieties of A. edulis, A. arvensis and A. silvicola. This is a single-source account (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a saprobic species occurring in woods, lawns, gardens and hedgerows in autumn, and as widely distributed in North America, Europe, West Asia, North Africa and southern Africa, and introduced into Australia. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing scattered or in large groups, sometimes clustered or in arcs. It says the species usually appears in grassy, cultivated and urban settings, occasionally in thin woods (especially on the West Coast), in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. The MushroomExpert.Com collections described are from California and Illinois. Mushroom World, an aggregator, lists gardens, parks and roadside verges and late summer to autumn. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
