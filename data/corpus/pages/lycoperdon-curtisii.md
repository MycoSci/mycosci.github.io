## Summary

Lycoperdon curtisii (Berk.), known as Curtis's Puffball, is a puffball in the family Lycoperdaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a tiny, spiny puffball, roughly 1–3 cm across, that often grows in clusters and becomes an olive-colored dust inside at maturity. The same source notes that several look-alikes exist and that it confirms identifications microscopically. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both list Vascellum curtisii as a synonym, published by Hanns Kreisel in 1963. MushroomExpert.Com also lists Lycoperdon wrightii as a synonym. Neither source gives an account of the material or the reasoning behind these transfers. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, usually growing in tight clusters in grass, often in disturbed ground such as ditches, and sometimes on woodchips in landscaping areas. It gives the season as late summer and fall and the distribution as widespread in North America, with the illustrated and described collections coming from Illinois. Wikipedia records the fruit bodies growing in fairy rings. A GBIF distribution entry lists Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
