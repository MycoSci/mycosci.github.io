## Summary

Clavulinopsis umbrinella, the beige coral, is a coral fungus in the family Clavariaceae, transferred to Clavulinopsis by Corner in 1950. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as multiply branched, 2–4.5 cm tall, white at first and darkening to pale brown or umber, usually with darker tips. The same source calls it an uncommon species known from Europe and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by Miles Joseph Berkeley in 1860 as Clavaria umbrina, a name that was an illegitimate homonym. Pier Andrea Saccardo then described it as Clavaria umbrinella in 1888, and E.J.H. Corner transferred it to Clavulinopsis in 1950. This account comes from a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies grow in grass. It reports the species as uncommon in Europe, with records from Great Britain, France and the Czech Republic, and also found in North America. GBIF's distribution listing names Belgium (Flemish Region), Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. Wikipedia also reports a 2014 claim of a first Scottish record on a lawn at Napier University's Craiglockhart Campus, but adds that the National Biodiversity Network Gateway lists about twenty earlier Scottish records.
