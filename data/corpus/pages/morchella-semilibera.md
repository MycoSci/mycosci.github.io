## Summary

Morchella semilibera DC. (1805), the half-free morel, is a fungus in the family Morchellaceae. Wikipedia describes it as native to Europe and Asia. It also reports that DNA analysis has shown the macroscopically near-identical half-free morels to be a cryptic species complex, and that the name M. semilibera should be restricted to the European species. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the original publication as de Candolle's description in Lamarck & de Candolle, Flore française, 3rd edition, vol. 2, p. 212 (1805). Wikipedia says de Candolle based the species on specimens from Europe. It says DNA work has shown the half-free morels to be a cryptic complex of at least three geographically isolated species, so the name M. semilibera should be restricted to the European one. Per Wikipedia, Morchella populiphila was described from western North America in 2012, and Peck's 1903 name Morchella punctipes was reaffirmed for eastern North American half-free morels. Wikipedia also reports a proposal to conserve M. semilibera against earlier synonyms sanctioned by Fries (Phallus crassipes, P. gigas and P. undosus), which it says have since been shown to be the same species. Wikispecies lists Mitrophora semilibera (DC.) Lév. (1846) as a synonym. These are single-cluster statements from Wikipedia and Wikispecies. *[wikispecies, wikipedia]*
