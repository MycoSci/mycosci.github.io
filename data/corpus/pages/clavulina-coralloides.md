## Summary

Clavulina coralloides (L.) J. Schröt. (1888) is a species in the family Hydnaceae, order Cantharellales. Wikipedia, which treats it mainly under the synonym Clavulina cristata, describes it as a white- or light-colored coral mushroom present in temperate areas of the Americas and Europe, and as the type species of the genus Clavulina. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the widely used epithet cristata was coined in 1790 by the Danish mycologist Theodor Holmskjold, as Ramaria cristata. It says Carl Linnaeus had described apparently the same fungus as Clavaria coralloides in Species Plantarum in 1753. Because of this, under the International Code of Nomenclature for algae, fungi, and plants, Clavulina coralloides should be used in preference to Clavulina cristata, although the latter is in more common use. A GBIF description record lists the original material as illustrations in Sterbeeck (1675), Tournefort (1700) and Vaillant (1727), and states that no type was designated. The same record notes that Donk (1954) identified the cited Vaillant figure as Clavulinopsis corniculata. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (a single independent source for this field) says the fungus grows solitary or in clusters on the ground, sometimes on rotten wood, in both coniferous and hardwood forests in temperate areas of the Americas and Europe. It gives fruiting times of October–March on the west coast of North America, July–October further inland in North America, and June–November in Europe. It also calls the fungus common and says it typically fruits from late summer to winter. *[wikipedia]*
