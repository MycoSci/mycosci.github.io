## Summary

Mycetinis alliaceus (Jacq.) Earle ex A.W. Wilson & Desjardin, 2005, is a member of the family Omphalotaceae and is commonly known as the garlic parachute. Wikipedia describes it as one of the larger mushrooms formerly placed in Marasmius, with a beige cap of up to 4 cm, a long tough slender stipe and a strong smell of garlic. The same source says it is distributed throughout Europe, fairly common in some areas and quite rare in others. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the strong smell of garlic is the significance of the Latin species name, alliaceus. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally documented by Nikolaus Joseph Freiherr von Jacquin in 1773 and was long known as Marasmius alliaceus, a designation it attributes to Elias Magnus Fries. It gave its name to the section Alliacei of Marasmius until a 2005 paper separated that group into the genus Mycetinis. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it is found in European woods, especially beech woods, from early summer to autumn, growing on fallen leaves and rotting wood. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*
