## Summary

Heterobasidion annosum (Fr.) Bref. 1888 is a basidiomycete in the family Bondarzewiaceae, order Russulales. Wikipedia describes it as the most economically important forest pathogen in the Northern Hemisphere, causing the disease annosus root rot in conifers. MushroomExpert.Com likewise describes it as a destructive root and butt rot fungus parasitic on conifers, well known to foresters as "annosus rot". *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by Fries in 1821 under the name Polyporus annosum. Robert Hartig later linked it to conifer disease in 1874, and it was renamed Fomes annosus by H. Karsten. Wikipedia states that the current name, Heterobasidion annosum, was given by Brefeld in 1888. MushroomExpert.Com cites the authority as (Fries, 1821) Brefeld, 1888, and lists Fomes annosus, Fomitopsis annosa and Polyporus annosus as synonyms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as parasitic on the wood of conifers (rarely on hardwoods) and saprobic on deadwood, causing a white pocket rot of the roots and butt. It says the fruiting body is annual or perennial, grows alone or gregariously at the bases of trees, and appears year-round. It reports the species as widely distributed in North America but rare in the central Rocky Mountains, and describes it as especially destructive in conifer plantations and in thinned second-growth conifer forests. Wikipedia says basidiospores, the primary infective propagules, are released in summer and infect trees, usually conifers, through damage such as freshly cut stumps. It adds that the fungus then spreads between neighbouring trees through root grafts. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
