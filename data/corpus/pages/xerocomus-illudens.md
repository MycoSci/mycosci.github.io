## Summary

Xerocomus illudens (Peck) Singer is a bolete in the family Boletaceae. Peck described it in 1898, and Singer later transferred it to Xerocomus. MushroomExpert.Com characterizes it as a relatively small bolete from eastern North America with a wide-meshed reticulum on the upper stem, no blue staining of surfaces or flesh, and a blue-green reaction of the cap surface to ammonia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was first described scientifically by Charles Horton Peck in 1898, and that Rolf Singer transferred it to the genus Xerocomus. MushroomExpert.Com lists Boletus illudens as a synonym. Wikipedia adds that some mycologists keep Xerocomus as a separate genus while taxonomic authorities usually lump it into Boletus, and that recent multi-loci studies have supported recognizing a core Xerocomus group. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies can be used in mushroom dyeing, producing colors such as beige (with yellow, green, or gray tints) or light gold, depending on the mordant used. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes X. illudens as mycorrhizal with oak, with fruit bodies growing singly, scattered, or in groups on the ground in forests of oak or oak-pine, fruiting from July to October. It gives the North American range as eastern Canada south to South Carolina and west to Alabama and Minnesota, and says the species has also been recorded in India (Ramna Forest, Burdwan District, West Bengal) and in the Himalayas. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks, hickories, and possibly other hardwoods, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall, and widely distributed in North America east of the Rocky Mountains; its illustrated and described collections are from Illinois, Indiana, and Georgia. A GBIF description record lists a distribution of Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
