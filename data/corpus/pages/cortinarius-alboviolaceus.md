## Summary

Cortinarius alboviolaceus (Pers.) Zaw., the silvery-violet webcap or pearly webcap, is a species of Cortinarius in the family Cortinariaceae. Wikipedia reports it from Europe and North America. It is described as having a dry, silky, pale lilac to silvery cap that is bell-shaped to umbonate, gills that go from pale purplish to rusty brown, and a rusty brown spore print. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says the species was first described in 1801 by Persoon, who called it a common mushroom in European forests. Persoon characterized it by a pale, bell-shaped cap, pale purple gills and a club-shaped stem. Kuo notes that North American field guides have long listed the name across the continent. He suggests these reports may in fact cover a group of alboviolaceus-like species that needs contemporary study. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, especially birch. It grows alone or gregariously in summer and fall. Kuo treats its North American distribution as that of a species group, not a confirmed single species. Mushroom World separately states that it grows with deciduous trees but is also found with conifers, often on acidic soil. GBIF description records list its distribution as Belgium (Brussels-Capital, Flemish and Walloon regions) and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, gbif_desc]*
