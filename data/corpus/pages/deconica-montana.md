## Summary

Deconica montana (Pers.) P.D. Orton (1960) is a species in the family Strophariaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a common "little brown mushroom" with a small brown cap (0.5–2 cm across) and a straight, thin stipe. It is reported from many regions around the world and is often associated with mosses. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was formerly placed in Psilocybe as Psilocybe montana, and that it was formerly the type species of that genus. Molecular studies in the late 2000s found that Psilocybe was polyphyletic, made up of two distinct clades. Because the name Psilocybe was attached to P. montana, a proposal was made to conserve Psilocybe with P. semilanceata as the type, which left the genus name Deconica available. The Nomenclature Committee for Fungi unanimously accepted the proposal in 2009. This account comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is commonly found in exposed situations such as dune-meadows, heaths, tree-less tundra and open Pinus forests, usually on nutrient-poor, well-drained soil. It describes the species as saprobic, possibly also parasitic, and often associated with mosses, including Brachythecium albicans, B. mutabulum, Campylopus introflexus, Ceratodon purpureus, Dicranum scoparium, Eurhynchium hians, E. praelongum, E. speciosum, Rhacomitrium canescens, Pohlia species and Polytrichum piliferum. It lists reports from many regions, including Britain, California, Greenland, Mexico, Norway, Switzerland (alpine tundra and subalpine regions) and Idaho and Montana (in moss at high elevations). It also lists a report from vegetation-covered flat roofs in Chemnitz, Germany. GBIF distribution records list Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. The habitat details come from a single independent source (Wikipedia); GBIF supplies only distribution. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
