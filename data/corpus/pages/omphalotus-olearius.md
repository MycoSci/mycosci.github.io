## Summary

Omphalotus olearius (DC.) Singer, 1948, is a gilled fungus in the family Omphalotaceae, known in English as the jack-o'-lantern mushroom or European jack-o'-lantern. Wikipedia describes it as a poisonous orange gilled mushroom with bioluminescent gills. MushroomExpert.Com treats the name as strictly European and describes it from Italian material. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states the species was originally described from France (de Candolle, 1815). It adds that the name olearius often appears in older North American field guides. According to that source, mating, morphological and DNA studies (summarized in Kirchmair & Pöder, 2002) support reserving olearius for the European species and using illudens for the eastern North American species. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing in large clusters on the stumps or buried roots of hardwoods, especially olive trees. It gives late summer and fall as the season and central and southern Europe as the range, especially along the Mediterranean coast, on the deadwood of oaks and olive trees. Wikipedia likewise reports growth on decaying stumps, buried roots or the bases of hardwood trees, particularly decaying olive roots. It gives July–October as the season, in southern Europe including southern France and the Iberian Peninsula. Wikipedia also notes a report from the Western Cape Province, South Africa. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
