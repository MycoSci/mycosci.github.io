## Summary

Mycena alphitophora (Berk.) Sacc. is an agaric in the family Mycenaceae, combined into Mycena in 1887. Wikipedia describes it as having small, white, delicate fruit bodies with powdery coatings on the surfaces of both the cap and stipe. GBIF descriptive records list its distribution as Belgium, Brazil, Germany, Denmark, France, Great Britain, the Netherlands, Norway and Terceira Island. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus alphitophorus by Miles Joseph Berkeley in 1877, based on specimens collected in 1873 from the Devonshire Marsh, a peatland in Bermuda. Pier Andrea Saccardo transferred it to Mycena in 1887, and William Alphonso Murrill placed it in Prunulus in 1916. Jakob Emanuel Lange's Mycena osmundicola, published in 1914, is treated as a synonym. P. Manimohan and K.M. Leelavathy defined the varieties distincta and globispora from southern India in 1989. Wikipedia classifies the species in section Saccharifera of Mycena. This account rests on a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
