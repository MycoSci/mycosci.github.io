## Summary

Amanita caesarea (Scop.) Pers. is a species of Amanitaceae, commonly called Caesar's mushroom. Scopoli described it in 1772 as Agaricus caesareus, and Persoon placed it in Amanita in 1801. Wikipedia describes it as having an orange to red cap, yellowish gills and stipe, and a white cup-like volva at the stipe base. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the common name comes from the mushroom being a favourite of the Roman emperors, who took the name Caesar as a title. It also states that the Italian name ovolo refers to the resemblance of the very young mushroom to an egg, and that the Albanian name kuqëlorja comes from its colour (kuqe, 'red'). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Giovanni Antonio Scopoli first described the species in 1772 as Agaricus caesareus, and that Persoon later placed it in Amanita in 1801. Wikispecies lists Agaricus caesareus Scop., 1772 as the basionym. Its synonyms there include Amanita aurantia, Venenarius caesareus (Murrill, 1913) and Volvoamanita caesarea (Beck, 1922). *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia, the only source to address the point, states that A. caesarea was first domesticated in 1984. It gives no details of method or scale. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is found in North Africa and southern Europe, particularly in the hills of northern Italy, and is also distributed in the Balkans and Hungary. It says the mushroom is thought to have been introduced north of the Alps by Roman armies because it is most frequently found along old Roman roads. In Europe it is reported to inhabit primarily oak and pine forests, growing singly or in groups from early summer to mid autumn. In warmer climates it fruits in higher oak woodlands, sometimes mixed with conifers. These habitat statements come from a single source. *[wikipedia]*
