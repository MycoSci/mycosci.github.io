## Summary

Callistosporium luteo-olivaceum (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Singer is a species of agaric fungus in the family Callistosporiaceae. It was described in 1859 as Agaricus luteo-olivaceus and transferred to Callistosporium by Rolf Singer in 1946. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small decomposer of conifer deadwood with a golden yellow to olive yellow cap, yellow gills and a white spore print. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was originally described in 1859 as Agaricus luteo-olivaceus by Miles Joseph Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis, and that Rolf Singer transferred it to Callistosporium in 1946; it notes an extensive synonymy. MushroomExpert.Com adds that the original description was from "New England" (Berkeley & Curtis 1859). It also reports that Vizzini et al. (2020) found three phylogenetically separate groups (North American, European and Asian collections) within the collections they studied. The authors treated these as lineages of a single species but left open that future work may separate them, in which case the North American species would keep the name luteo-olivaceum. According to the same page, Callistosporium graminicolor was separated by Lennox (1979) and re-affirmed as a phylogenetic species by Vizzini et al. (2020); it is found in the Pacific Northwest. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (single-source description) reports it as saprobic on well-decayed deadwood of conifers, occasionally on wood so decayed that the mushrooms appear terrestrial, and sometimes on buried wood. It usually grows gregariously and fruits in summer, fall, and winter in warm climates. That page describes it as widely distributed in North America and Europe, and reported from Central America, the Caribbean, South America, eastern Asia, and Oceania; the collections it illustrates are from Illinois, Maryland, and North Carolina. Wikipedia describes it as rare but widely distributed in temperate and tropical areas of Europe and North America, and reports a 2014 record growing in pine forests in Western Himalaya, Pakistan. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
