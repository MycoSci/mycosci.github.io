## Summary

Chlorophyllum rhacodes (Vittad.) Vellinga, 2002, the shaggy parasol, is a large lepiotoid agaric in the family Agaricaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it by its large size, shaggy scaly cap, white spore print and flesh that turns pinkish orange when sliced. Wikipedia notes that the name "shaggy parasol" covers three closely related species, C. rhacodes, C. olivieri and C. brunneum. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com says the epithet derives from Vittadini's mis-transliteration of the Greek ρακωδης, "ragged" or "tattered", which it calls an apt description of the mushroom. It also reports that Vellinga and Pennycook (2010) argued Vittadini may instead have meant ραχωδης, "bush", because he found it under an old, half-dead hazel bush; the author of that page finds this unconvincing. Wikipedia gives a different gloss, rhakos 'piece of cloth', and says the original spelling "rachodes" was erroneous. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Vittadini first published the species in 1835, spelling the epithet "rachodes" (MushroomExpert.Com; Wikipedia). According to Wikipedia, it was formerly placed in Macrolepiota or Lepiota, and was moved to Chlorophyllum on molecular phylogenetic evidence of a closer relationship to Chlorophyllum molybdites than to Macrolepiota procera. The variety Macrolepiota rhacodes var. brunneum was also raised to species rank as Chlorophyllum brunneum. Both sources say the spelling "rachodes" is an error, and MushroomExpert.Com cites Article 60.1 of the International Code of Nomenclature as allowing the correction. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered or gregariously, often in troops or fairy rings, in lawns and disturbed ground such as roadsides, gardens and field edges. It often appears near conifers, especially spruces, from spring through fall. That page calls it widely distributed in North America but more common from the Rocky Mountains eastward, and its described collections are from Illinois and California. Mushroom World, a separate source, says it grows solitary or scattered in woods, often with conifers. Wikipedia says it prefers more shade than Macrolepiota procera and dislikes open pastures and fields. GBIF lists distribution records for Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, mushroomworld, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

- **Chlorophyllum brunneum** — *unknown* — Has a rimmed basal bulb on the stem, whereas C. rhacodes has a gradually swollen bulb. Microscopically it has consistently truncated spores and longer, narrower cheilocystidia. *[mushroomexpert]*
