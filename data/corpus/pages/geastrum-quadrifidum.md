## Summary

Geastrum quadrifidum Pers. (1794), the rayed earthstar, is an earthstar in the family Geastraceae with a type locality in Germany. Wikipedia describes it as a small species whose outer layer splits into usually four or five rays, and as a saprobe of soil and litter that is widely distributed but not common. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet quadrifidum is derived from Latin and means "four-forks". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Persoon published the first official description in 1794 and later sanctioned the name in his 1801 Synopsis Methodica Fungorum, as Geastrum quadrifidum var. minus, a variety now treated as a synonym. It says the species had earlier been described as Lycoperdon coronatum by Schaeffer (1763) and Scopoli (1772), and later as Geaster coronatus by Schröter (1889). The epithet coronatus is not to be used because the sanctioned name exists. This history comes from a single encyclopedia source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to Wikipedia (single source), G. quadrifidum is a saprobe that decomposes leaf litter and similar detritus. Fruit bodies are generally found in coniferous woodland, in summer and autumn. In Mexico it was found in tropical thorn forest and pine-oak forest in summer. In Britain, all collections have been made in beech forest on calcareous soil. Wikipedia also says it has a wide distribution but is not common, and that it is on the Regional Red Lists of several European countries. *[wikipedia]*
