## Summary

Phaeotremella translucens (H.D. Gordon) M. Yamada, Endoh & Degawa (2022) is a species of fungus in the family Tremellaceae, originally described as Tremella translucens. According to the one independent source available (Wikipedia), it forms small, pustular, gelatinous fruit bodies up to 2 mm across and is parasitic on Lophodermium ascocarps on decaying pine needles. It was originally described from Scotland. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Tremella translucens was first published in 1938 by Hugh Douglas Gordon, based on collections from Scotland on decaying pine needles. David Minter recollected specimens in Scotland in the 1970s and established that the species was associated with ascocarps of Lophodermium species. He transferred it to the genus Pseudostypella on morphological characters. Robert Bandoni later moved it to his new genus Sirotrema. DNA-sequence (cladistic) analysis has since shown it belongs in Phaeotremella. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as a parasite of Lophodermium species growing on decaying pine needles, typically those still attached to brash or branches. The same source says it was originally described from the United Kingdom and has been recorded in Europe from Austria, Bosnia, Ireland, Norway, Poland, Russia and Spain, and elsewhere from Canada, New Zealand and Japan. No season is stated. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*
