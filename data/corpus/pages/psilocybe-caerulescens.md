## Summary

Psilocybe caerulescens Murrill (1923) is a species in the family Hymenogastraceae, described from Alabama and recorded on soil. English Wikipedia gives "landslide mushroom" ("derrumbe" in Spanish) as a common name. The descriptive text available for this species comes from a single independent source, Wikipedia. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Murrill first reported the species in 1923 from near Montgomery, Alabama, on sugarcane mulch. It has not been re-documented from that locality since. Wikispecies gives the place of publication as Mycologia 15(1): 20, 1923, and lists five infraspecific names, including varieties published by R. Heim in 1957 and 1973. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia, the only source that covers this, states that the Nahuatls of Necaxa (Puebla) use P. caerulescens and P. mexicana as sacred mushrooms. It also says the species is an important sacred mushroom among the Mazatecs. It reports that the Totonacs of Veracruz used it extensively in the past, though that tradition is now mostly extinct. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (a single-source account) describes the species as growing gregariously or cespitosely, rarely solitarily, from June through October. It grows on disturbed ground often devoid of herbaceous plants, and often in sunny locations. It is said to prefer muddy orangish brown soils with much woody debris. The same source reports it from South Carolina in September 2008, calls it common and widespread in northern Georgia, and says it was found in northern Georgia in 2000. *[wikipedia]*
