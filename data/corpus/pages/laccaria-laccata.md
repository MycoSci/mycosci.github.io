## Summary

Laccaria laccata (Scop.) Cooke, the deceiver, is a small, highly variable mushroom in the family Hydnangiaceae. It is reported across Europe and North America, south into Mexico and Costa Rica. It is the type species of the genus Laccaria and is mycorrhizal with trees. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin adjective laccatus, 'varnished' or 'shining'. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the deceiver gets its common name from its variable appearance. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that it was first described by Giovanni Antonio Scopoli in 1772 as Agaricus laccatus, and was given its current binomial name by Mordecai Cubitt Cooke in 1884. MushroomExpert.Com gives the official name of the widespread North American variety as Laccaria laccata var. pallidifolia Peck (1890). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as found in scattered troops in wooded areas and on heathland, often in poor soil, and mycorrhizal with members of the Pinaceae, Fagaceae and Betulaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers, growing alone or gregariously, in spring, summer and fall, and widely distributed in North America. GBIF descriptive records list it as terrestrial and ectomycorrhizal, once noted on Quercus humboldtii. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
