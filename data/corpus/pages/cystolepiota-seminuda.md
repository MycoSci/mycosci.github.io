## Summary

Cystolepiota seminuda (Lasch) Bon, 1976, is a member of the Agaricaceae, recorded in many countries, mostly in Europe. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a diminutive lepiotoid mushroom with free gills, a white spore print, and a white cap and stem covered with a powdery, granular material that rubs off easily. The same source calls it widespread and common but not often collected. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com notes that Lepiota seminuda is a former name, and that Cystolepiota sistrata, as applied by many authors, is a synonym. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it can be found on humus, often along forest roads. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously in hardwood and conifer forests, in humus or from well-decayed wood, in late summer and fall, and widely distributed throughout North America (the described collection is from Illinois). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
