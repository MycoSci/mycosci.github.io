## Summary

Xylaria longipes Nitschke (1867), commonly known as dead moll's fingers, is a club-shaped fungus in the family Xylariaceae that grows on dead hardwood. Wikipedia describes it as inedible. MushroomExpert.Com gives a description and ecology but states that it contains no edibility or toxicity information. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives Xylaria as from the Latin xulon, 'wood', and aria, 'pertaining to'. It gives longipes as from longus, 'long', and pes, 'foot', a reference to the long stem, which distinguishes it from Xylaria polymorpha (dead man's fingers). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the species was first described by Theodor Rudolph Joseph Nitschke in the first volume of his Pyrenomycetes Germanici (1867). In 1958 R. W. G. Dennis coined the binomial Xylosphaera longipes, resurrecting Dumortier's 1822 genus Xylosphaera. MycoBank and Index Fungorum reject that name and prefer Nitschke's. The variety X. longipes var. tropica was described from Mexico in 1989. Index Fungorum lists it as synonymous with the nominate variety, but MycoBank lists it as taxonomically independent. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a saprotroph growing directly from dead hardwood, including fallen branches and stumps, and causing soft rot. It reports the species from Europe, Asia and North America. In Europe it favours sycamore wood, and North American collections have favoured maple and beech. It can grow singly or in groups. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on decaying hardwood logs and sticks, especially beech and maple debris, growing alone or gregariously, from spring through fall. It calls the species common in northeastern and midwestern North America and occasionally reported elsewhere on the continent. The collections it describes are from Illinois. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
