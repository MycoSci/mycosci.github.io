## Summary

Peniophora quercina (Pers.) Cooke, 1879, known in English as Oak Crust, is a wood-decay fungus in the family Peniophoraceae (order Russulales). Wikipedia describes it as forming resupinate fruit bodies that are waxy and lilac when wet and crusty and pink when dry, with the edges curling up to show a dark underside. It is the type species of the genus Peniophora. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific name quercina refers to Quercus, the generic name for oak. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, early descriptions came from Carl Ludwig Willdenow, who named the species Lichen carneus in 1787, and Jean Baptiste François Pierre Bulliard, who named it Auricularia corticalis in 1790. The sanctioned name is Thelephora quercina, given by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon in 1801 and sanctioned by Elias Magnus Fries in the first volume of his Systema Mycologicum. Samuel Frederick Gray moved Persoon's species to Corticium as Corticium quercinum in 1821. In 1879 Mordecai Cubitt Cooke transferred it to his newly described genus Peniophora and declared it the type species; Cooke's name is the one currently used. This account comes from a single independent source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species typically grows on dead wood, either still attached to the tree or fallen, where it causes white rot. It favours oak but is also found on other deciduous trees such as beech. It is described as a pioneer species on dead wood, meaning it can be the first to colonise. It is reported as very common in Europe, can be encountered all year, and produces spores in late summer and autumn. It has also been recorded in Amur, in eastern Asia. The same source reports that it has been identified in living sapwood, where it is latent, and that in inoculation and branch-colonisation studies it grew mainly around wounds and at the ends of partially living branches. These statements come from a single independent source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
