## Summary

Echinoderma asperum (Pers.) Bon (1991), the freckled dapperling, is a species in the family Agaricaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a large, brownish, white-gilled mushroom with a warty or scaly cap, and gives a cap up to about 10 cm wide with erect pyramidal scales on a paler ground. That description comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name is the Latin adjective "asper" (feminine "aspera", neuter "asperum"), meaning "rough". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described by Persoon as Agaricus asper. Quélet moved it to Lepiota, where it was long known as Lepiota aspera. Marcel Bon placed it in Cystolepiota in 1978, then created the genus Echinoderma in 1991 for this and similar brownish warty species. Wikipedia also says the same species was described by Weinmann in 1824 as Agaricus acutesquamosus and by Lasch in 1828 as Agaricus friesii. Most authorities now treat these names as synonyms, but Moser separated the acutesquamosum form from the asperum form as different species, on the basis that the latter has forking gills and the former does not. This account rests on a single independent source, Wikipedia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it appears during autumn in deciduous woodland, or in parks and gardens where wood chip mulch has been used. The same source says it has been recorded widely in northern temperate zones, varying between common and quite rare in Europe and North Africa, and that it occurs in North America and has been reported in Japan, Australia and New Zealand. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*
