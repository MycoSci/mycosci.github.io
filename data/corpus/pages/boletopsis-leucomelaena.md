## Summary

Boletopsis leucomelaena (Pers.) Fayod, 1889 is a species in the family Bankeraceae, order Thelephorales, with occurrence records from many countries. Wikipedia describes it as a hydnoid fungus and says it is less common than the similar Boletopsis grisea. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with spruce and says the genus is confusing to identify. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was originally described in 1801 as Boletus leucomelas by Christian Hendrik Persoon in Synopsis Methodica Fungorum, and that the Swiss mycologist Victor Fayod transferred it to Boletopsis in 1889. MushroomExpert.Com adds that the name Boletopsis subsquamosa, which appears in many North American sources, has been applied to both B. grisea and B. leucomelaena, and that Donk (1969) and Niemelä & Saarenoksa (1989) regard that name as misapplied. It also reports that a 2008 study by Watling and Milne compared ITS sequences from six North American Boletopsis collections with European material and found none matching B. leucomelaena. MushroomExpert.Com criticises the study's basis for concluding anything about North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species favours relatively rich habitats and is characteristically found in spruce-dominated forests. For Finland it names two forest types: spruce-dominated grass-herb forests with intermixed birch, aspen, hazel and other deciduous trees and shrubs, and moist heath forests dominated by Norway spruce. Wikipedia describes both as shady with moist microclimates. It also states that the species appears to tolerate soils on calcareous bedrock, though whether it is truly calciphilous is uncertain. Wikipedia describes it as rare or very rare throughout Europe, occurring mainly in lowlands up to submontane mixed spruce and fir forests. It says that in the Nordic countries fruiting begins in late July, with most fruiting from late August through October. MushroomExpert.Com independently describes it as mycorrhizal with spruces, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall. It says the illustrated collection came from a spruce forest in Colorado. The two sources differ on North America: Wikipedia says the species occurs in the Pacific Northwest, while MushroomExpert.Com calls its North American distribution uncertain. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
