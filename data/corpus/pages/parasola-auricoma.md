## Summary

Parasola auricoma (Pat.) Redhead, Vilgalys & Hopple, the goldenhaired inkcap, is a species in the family Psathyrellaceae. Wikipedia describes small, umbrella-shaped, short-lived fruit bodies that grow in grass or woodchips, and reports the species from Europe, Japan and North America. It was first described in 1886 as Coprinus auricomus and transferred to Parasola in 2001. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1886 by the French mycologist Narcisse Théophile Patouillard as Coprinus auricomus. It was transferred to Parasola in 2001, when molecular phylogenetics was used to sort the coprinoid genera (Coprinus and the segregate genera Coprinopsis, Coprinellus and Parasola) into natural monophyletic groups. In a 2010 study of type material, Laszlo Nagy and colleagues designated Patouillard's plate 453, which contains the original description, as the lectotype. They also determined that Pseudocoprinus besseyi and Coprinus elongatipes, both described in 1946 by Alexander H. Smith and Lexemuel Ray Hesler, were conspecific with P. auricoma. Wikipedia states that, per MycoBank, hansenii (described by Jakob Emanuel Lange in 1915) is a facultative synonym based on a different type. It adds that P.D. Orton and Roy Watling disagree and suggest it is a forgotten species that requires reanalysis. Wikipedia also says the placement of P. auricoma within Parasola is somewhat controversial. Several molecular studies have confirmed its inclusion in the Parasola clade, but its relationships to other members have not been fully resolved because of limited sampling. This account rests on a single independent source (Wikipedia); MushroomExpert.Com independently lists the same authorship (Patouillard, 1886; Redhead, Vilgalys & Hopple, 2001). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Two independent sources describe P. auricoma as saprobic. MushroomExpert.Com says it grows scattered or gregariously on soil, in grass, or (more frequently) in wood chips, in early summer and sometimes again in fall (and over winter in warmer climates), and is widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia says the fruit bodies grow singly or in groups, often in large numbers, at road sides in deciduous forests or on grassy areas. It reports that in Europe fruiting is most common in spring and summer, while in North America it is more common in late summer and autumn after rains, and that the species is also recorded from Japan and, per one report, Bogotá, Colombia. The two sources differ somewhat on North American fruiting season, and no attempt is made here to reconcile them. A GBIF-hosted description of Pakistani material records specimens scattered in grassland under herbaceous plants (Malakand, 460 m, August 2014; Swat, 1023 m, July 2017). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
