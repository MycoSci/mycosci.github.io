## Summary

Buchwaldoboletus lignicola (Kallenb.) Pilát is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, first published as Boletus lignicola and moved to its current genus in 1969. Wikipedia describes it as growing on wood while actually parasitising the fungus Phaeolus schweinitzii, with a convex yellow to rusty brown cap and yellow to yellow-brown pores and stipe. It is recorded from Europe and North America, and a GBIF-held description also lists Asia. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name comes from the Latin words lignum 'wood' and the verb colere 'to inhabit'. This derivation comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Franz Joseph Kallenbach originally described the species in 1929 as Boletus lignicola. Albert Pilát gave it its current name in 1969. He first placed it in Pulveroboletus, then erected the new genus Buchwaldoboletus. Wikipedia lists his reasons as its occurrence on wood rather than in the ground, decurrent and arcuate pores, yellow mycelium at the base of the stipe, blueing flesh, and lack of hyphal clamps. Wikipedia also lists earlier placements in Xerocomus (Rolf Singer, 1942), Gyrodon (Paul Heinemann, 1951) and Phlebopus (Meinhard Moser, 1955). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states the species is found on wood but is actually parasitic on the fungus Phaeolus schweinitzii, and that it is found only where that fungus grows. Microscopic tests are said to have shown the parasitism. Wikipedia says the two occur with coniferous trees such as Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris), eastern white pine (P. strobus) and European larch (Larix decidua), and less commonly with deciduous trees such as wild cherry (Prunus avium). Wikipedia reports records across Europe from the far northern subarctic south to Switzerland, and in North America from Ontario and Quebec south to Pennsylvania, and says it is considered endangered in the Czech Republic. A GBIF-held description additionally lists Hainan and Heilongjiang provinces in China, Nepal, Korea and Indonesia. The habitat and host details rest on a single source, Wikipedia. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
