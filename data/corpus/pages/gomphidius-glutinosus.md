## Summary

Gomphidius glutinosus (Schaeff.) Fr. is a gilled mushroom in the family Gomphidiaceae, order Boletales, with a slimy cap and a slimy veil. Wikipedia places it in Europe and North America. Wikipedia also says opinions differ on its suitability for the table, with some guides holding it in high regard and others viewing it with caution. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name is derived from the Greek 'γομφος' (gomphos), meaning "plug" or "large wedge-shaped nail", and that the specific epithet glutinosus is the Latin adjective "sticky". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Jacob Christian Schäffer first described the species as Agaricus glutinosus in 1774, in his series on the fungi of Bavaria and the Palatinate. Elias Magnus Fries gave it its current genus and binomial in 1838. Samuel Frederick Gray's Cortinaria viscida (1821) was concluded by Orson K. Miller Jr. to be the same species. In 1971 Miller described two varieties from Idaho specimens, var. purpureus and var. salmoneus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports the species from Eurasia and North America, up to 2,900 m elevation. It fruits in autumn under conifers, in both natural woods and plantations, including balsam fir, Norway spruce and grand fir. Fruiting bodies are single, scattered, or occasionally clumped, and fruiting runs as late as December in northern California. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially spruces and firs, growing alone or scattered in summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. Kuo gives its range as northern and montane North America, and also Europe, Asia and northern Africa. Mushroom World says it grows in grass or needle litter beneath spruce. Wikipedia adds that it was long thought to be ectomycorrhizal, but there is now evidence that many, perhaps all, Gomphidiaceae are parasitic on ectomycorrhizal boletes. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
