## Summary

Amanita rubescens Pers. (1797), the blusher, is placed in the family Amanitaceae. Wikipedia describes it as having flesh that turns pink when bruised or exposed to air, and as native to Europe and Asia. MushroomExpert.Com notes that material called by this name in eastern North America is probably not the same species as the European original. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that both the scientific and common names stem from the tendency of the flesh to bruise pink. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the common name "blusher" stems from the tendency of the flesh to bruise pink. MushroomExpert.Com likewise says the species is sometimes called the "Blusher" in field guides because of its tendency to discolor pinkish red to rose. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes A. rubescens as common throughout much of Europe and Asia, growing on poor soils as well as in deciduous and coniferous woodlands, and appearing from June through November in the United Kingdom. It also reports a record from South Africa, where the fungus is thought to have been accidentally introduced with trees imported from Europe. Mushroom World says it grows solitary or scattered on soil in woods. MushroomExpert.Com, describing eastern North American material, says it is mycorrhizal, primarily with oaks but also under pines and other conifers, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall (or over winter along the Gulf Coast). That source cautions that the eastern North American version is probably not the same as the European species. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, mushroomexpert]*
