## Summary

Tricholomopsis decora (Fr.) Singer, 1939, known as Prunes and Custard or Decorated Mop, is a gilled mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes a yellow to brownish-yellow cap covered with small brownish to grayish scales, yellow gills, and a white spore print. GBIF descriptions list it for Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. MushroomExpert.Com also treats North American material as possibly a species group rather than a single species. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com notes that several North American species may be going under the European name Tricholomopsis decora. It cites a 2015 DNA study (Olariaga and collaborators) that found separation between North American and European collections, and says further research may determine whether two or more species are justified. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the wood of conifers, especially (but not exclusively) hemlocks. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall. That source calls it widely distributed in North America and also known from Europe and Australasia, with the illustrated collections from Colorado, Kentucky and Ohio. Wikipedia says it grows in conifer forests in North America and Britain. Mushroom World says it typically grows on conifer logs in late summer and fall. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
