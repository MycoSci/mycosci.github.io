## Summary

Craterellus lutescens (Fr.) Fr. 1838 is a species in the family Hydnaceae, known in English as Yellow Foot or Golden Chanterelle and recorded in Europe, North America and Asia. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a chanterelle-like mushroom with a perforated brown cap 2–6 cm across, an orange-yellow stem and an almost completely smooth undersurface. It also notes that similar species differ on one or more of those three features. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) reports it from cold conifer bogs in northern Michigan, growing in wet moss on the ground and on decaying logs. It describes the species as apparently mycorrhizal, associated with conifers, growing gregariously, often in moss or on well-decayed mossy logs, in summer and fall. It says the species was originally described from Europe and, in North America, is distributed in the northeast, the upper Midwest, the Appalachians and Mexico. It adds records from the Caribbean, Central America and western Asia. Wikipedia separately says the species is usually found in wetlands and can commonly be found in large colonies in some coniferous forests, under spruce, mountain fir trees, or pinewoods near the seashore. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
