## Summary

Porodaedalea pini (Brot.) Murrill, 1905, is a species of fungus in the family Hymenochaetaceae, commonly called pine conk or pine bracket. It is a plant pathogen of conifers that causes the tree disease known as "red ring rot" or "white speck", a rot of the heartwood. It is recorded widely across the Northern Hemisphere. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com notes that the species is usually called Phellinus pini in field guides, and lists Phellinus pini as a synonym. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as parasitic on the heartwood of living conifers and also saprobic on the dead wood of conifers, very rarely reported on hardwoods, causing a white pocket rot, growing alone, gregariously, or in shelving clusters, and very widely distributed in North America wherever conifers are present. Wikipedia reports that it infects a wide range of conifers, including jack pine, lodgepole pine, Sitka and white spruce, Douglas-fir, balsam and true fir, western hemlock, and tamarack, entering through branch stubs, stumps, damaged roots, dead branches, and other wounds. Wikipedia also states that the disease is most common in the Pacific Northwest and is widespread in the temperate zone of the Northern Hemisphere. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
