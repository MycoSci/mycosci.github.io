## Summary

Lactarius blennius (Fr.) Fr., the slimy milkcap or beech milkcap, is a medium-sized milkcap in the family Russulaceae. Wikipedia describes it as found commonly in beech forests in Europe, and as slimy when wet and exuding copious milk. It is recorded in many countries, mainly in Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from a Latin adjective blennius, meaning "slimy". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Elias Magnus Fries first described the species as Agaricus blennius in 1815 and gave it its current binomial in 1838. The same source lists Agaricus viridis (Schrader, 1794), Galorrheus blennius, Lactarius viridis and Lactifluus blennius as synonyms. It also reports that Lactarius fluens was proposed as a variety of L. blennius but is today normally regarded as a separate species, and that Lactarius albidopallens began as a form of L. blennius before being classified as a separate species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as very common in broad-leaved woodland, favouring beech, and most associated with Fagus sylvatica (European beech). It has also been observed growing in association with oak species. It forms an ectomycorrhizal association with trees, can grow on a wide variety of subsoil types but is more typical of acidic soil, and is found between late summer and late autumn. Wikipedia states that its distribution coincides with that of beech. This is a single-cluster account (Wikipedia; the iNaturalist text is a derivative of it). *[wikipedia]*
