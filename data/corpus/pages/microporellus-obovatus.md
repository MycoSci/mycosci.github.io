## Summary

Microporellus obovatus (Jungh.) Ryvarden, 1972, is a poroid fungus in the family Polyporaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a pan-tropical polypore with a zoned brownish cap, a fairly well-developed stem, and a whitish pore surface of very small pores (6–8 per millimeter). The same source describes the cap as 4–8 cm across, and usually shallowly to deeply vase-shaped. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on decaying hardwood logs and sticks, causing a white rot, and growing alone, gregariously, or in small clusters. It says the fungus is annual, found in summer and fall in temperate areas and year-round in tropical and subtropical areas. The same source calls it widely distributed in tropical and subtropical North America and occasional in the lower Midwest and lower eastern United States, and reports it throughout the tropics in Central America, South America, Africa, Asia and Australasia. The collection illustrated and described there is from Missouri. Wikipedia separately lists it from French Guiana, Guadeloupe, and various locales in Africa, India, and the United States. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
