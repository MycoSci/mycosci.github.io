## Summary

Amanita regalis (Fr.) Michael, 1904, the royal fly agaric or king of Sweden Amanita, is a species in the family Amanitaceae. GBIF records it from Sweden, Norway and Denmark. Wikipedia describes it as poisonous, containing ibotenic acid and muscimol. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin word regalis, meaning "royal". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the taxon was first described as Agaricus muscarius β regalis by Elias Magnus Fries in his Systema Mycologicum (1821). Saccardo treated it as a variety of A. muscaria in 1887, and Edmund Michael was the first to consider it a distinct species. In 1941 Gilbert moved it to Amanitaria as A. muscaria var. regalis. Singer first treated it as a subspecies of A. muscaria (1949) and listed it as a distinct species in the fourth edition (1986). A Japanese group using molecular phylogenetics concluded it should be considered a grouping within A. muscaria, but as of 2012 Index Fungorum and MycoBank listed it as Amanita regalis. This account comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as rare and typically found growing on the ground in mountainous forests, both deciduous and coniferous. It is mycorrhizal, and has been shown experimentally to form mycorrhiza with birch, Scots pine, Mountain pine and Norway spruce. Fruit bodies have been reported in fairy rings. It is reported as more common in northern than southern Europe, and in North America only from Alaska, where it is usually found above the tree line. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*
