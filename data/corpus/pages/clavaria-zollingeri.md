## Summary

Clavaria zollingeri Léveillé (1846) is a coral fungus in the family Clavariaceae, described from Java, and Index Fungorum currently lists it as Clavulinopsis zollingeri. Wikipedia describes a widely distributed saprobic species with purple to pinkish-violet, branched, brittle fruit bodies up to about 10 cm tall. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as growing in moss under hardwoods in eastern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikispecies gives the etymology as named after Heinrich Zollinger. Wikipedia says the same and describes Zollinger as a Swiss botanist who collected the type specimen in Java. *[wikispecies, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the species was first described by the French mycologist Joseph-Henri Léveillé in 1846 from a type specimen collected in Java. Léveillé regarded the dichotomous branching as the main character separating it from the otherwise similar Clavaria amethystina. Wikispecies lists the holotype as held at BPI and cites Léveillé's 1846 'Description des Champignons de l'herbier du Muséum de Paris'. Wikipedia reports that Charles Horton Peck published a collection from Stow, Massachusetts as Clavaria lavendula in 1910, which is now a synonym. Wikipedia also reports that a 2006 ribosomal DNA analysis of clavarioid fungi by Dentinger and McLaughlin found C. zollingeri most similar genetically to Clavulinopsis laeticolor. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the fruit bodies grow solitarily, in groups or in clusters on the ground, in grassy spots usually near hardwood trees or with mosses, and that the species is saprobic. It also says they are found in woodland litter or in grasslands. MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as saprobic, almost always found in moss under hardwoods, alone or in groups, in summer and fall in eastern North America. A GBIF description record gives the substrate as on moss. Wikipedia lists records from Australia, New Zealand, North America, South America and Asia, including Brunei, India and Korea. It says the North American distribution is restricted to the northeast, with isolated records as far south as northern Arkansas. It calls the species rare in Europe and says it is on the Red Lists of Denmark and Great Britain. Wikispecies records the type collection as on trunks in Java, Indonesia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc, wikispecies]*
