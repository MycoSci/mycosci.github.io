## Summary

Craterellus ignicolor (R.H. Petersen) Dahlman, Danell & Spatafora, 2000, is a fungus in the family Hydnaceae, also known as the flame trumpet or Flame Chanterelle. It is recorded in the US, Canada, Costa Rica, Mexico, Guatemala, Belize and Spain. MushroomExpert.Com characterizes it by its small size, fairly well developed false gills that often develop pinkish shades with maturity, and a yellow-orange cap that develops a central perforation and becomes vase-shaped. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, North American mycologists recognized this fungus as distinct for just over a hundred years before it had its own species name. Peck called it Cantharellus lutescens in 1873 and later (1892) treated it as Cantharellus infundibuliformis var. luteolus. Smith called it Cantharellus lutescens in 1953, then in 1968 decided it was Cantharellus minor. In 1975 Ron Petersen described it as a new species of Cantharellus, designating a type collection from Great Smoky Mountain National Park, Tennessee. In 2000, DNA analysis by Dahlman and collaborators showed it belongs in Craterellus rather than Cantharellus. Wikipedia gives a briefer version of the same account. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks, beech and birches. It grows gregariously or in clusters in moss or sphagnum in damp, shady areas, in summer and fall, and is apparently widely distributed in eastern North America; the illustrated and described collections are from Illinois and Michigan. Wikipedia, a separate source, describes fruiting bodies growing amongst moss in shaded coniferous or hardwood forests, scattered or in groups or clusters in summer and fall. It says they can be either mycorrhizal or saprotrophic. The two sources differ on forest type and on whether the fungus is only mycorrhizal. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
