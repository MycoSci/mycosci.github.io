## Summary

Russula atropurpurea (Krombh.) Britzelm., 1893, is a species in the family Russulaceae, recorded in GBIF occurrences from many countries across Europe, North America and Asia. Wikipedia describes a dark reddish-purple cap, sometimes almost black at the centre. MushroomExpert.Com describes a purple to pinkish-purple cap with a darker centre. The two sources differ on other characters, and MushroomExpert.Com notes that the epithet has a long, twisted history of application. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described as Agaricus atropurpureus by Julius von Krombholz in 1845 and placed in Russula by Max Britzelmayr in 1893. It says the binomial R. atropurpurea (Krombh.) Britzelm. is accepted as incorrect and that mycologists cannot agree on a replacement. MushroomExpert.Com separately says the epithet atropurpurea has a long, twisted history of applications, and that some North American field guides use the name for a different species, which it treats as Russula vinacea. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species appears in late summer and autumn, is mycorrhizal with oak (Quercus), and favours acid soil. It says the species is occasionally found with beech (Fagus) or pine (Pinus). MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and other hardwoods, growing alone or gregariously in summer and fall, and widely distributed east of the Great Plains in the United States and Canada. It notes that in mixed woods the host trees can be difficult to sort out. The two sources may be describing different concepts of the name. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
