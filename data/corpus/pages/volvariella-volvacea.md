## Summary

Volvariella volvacea (Bull.) Singer, 1951, is a species in the family Pluteaceae, known in English as paddy straw mushroom or straw mushroom. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a robust Volvariella with a grayish brown, silky-streaked cap, free gills that turn brownish pink, a brownish pink spore print, and a thick sack-like volva at the stem base. It is described as saprobic, and sources place it on paddy straw in tropical and subtropical Asia and as an apparent introduction in North America. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Cultivation

A GBIF-hosted description (a Sri Lankan molecular study) states that commercial cultivation is seen in Asian countries including China, India, the Philippines and Thailand, and that the species is commercially grown in Sri Lanka. Wikipedia states that it is grown on rice straw beds, takes four to five days to mature, and is most successfully grown in subtropical climates with high annual rainfall. It also says no record of cultivation before the 19th century has been found. The Wikipedia and iNaturalist text is one cluster, so this is two independent sources. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

The GBIF-hosted description says it grows solitary on paddy straw with moisture and is reported from tropical and subtropical regions, listing China, India, Indonesia, Madagascar, Nigeria, the Philippines, Sri Lanka and Thailand. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says it is apparently not native to North America but has been introduced by human activity. There it is saprobic, growing gregariously or in clusters in woodchips, greenhouses, gardens, compost piles and similar places. It can appear year-round depending on climate, usually in summer when outdoors. It is apparently widely distributed in introduced settings in North America, but more common east of the Great Plains. The collection MushroomExpert.Com describes is from Illinois. *[gbif_desc, mushroomexpert]*
