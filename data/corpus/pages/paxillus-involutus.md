## Summary

Paxillus involutus (Batsch) Fr., the brown roll-rim or common roll-rim, is a gilled basidiomycete in the family Paxillaceae (order Boletales). Wikipedia says it is more closely related to the pored boletes than to typical gilled mushrooms. It was formerly eaten in parts of Europe but is now documented as deadly poisonous. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the generic name is derived from the Latin for 'peg' or 'plug', and that the specific epithet involutus, 'inrolled', refers to the cap margin. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the common name 'roll-rim' comes from the cap's rolled rim. It also lists other common names, including naked brimcap, poison paxillus, inrolled pax, poison pax and brown chanterelle, and says Gray called it the 'involved navel-stool' in 1821. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Pierre Bulliard described the species in 1785 as Agaricus contiguus. The 1786 combination Agaricus involutus by August Batsch is taken as the first valid description. James Bolton's Agaricus adscendibus (1788), Samuel Frederick Gray's Omphalia involuta (1821) and Rabenhorst's Rhymovis involuta (1844) are listed as synonyms. Elias Magnus Fries erected the genus Paxillus in 1838 and made this the type species. The genus was later placed in Paxillaceae by René Maire. Wikipedia also notes that genetic and mating studies suggest P. involutus may be a species complex. A multi-gene analysis of European isolates separated it into four lineages, corresponding to P. obscurosporus, P. involutus sensu stricto, P. validus and a fourth, unidentified species. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that the species was widely eaten in Central and Eastern Europe until World War II, although English guidebooks did not recommend it. In Poland it was often eaten after pickling or salting. It is still consumed in parts of Poland, Russia and Ukraine. According to the same source, guidebooks did not firmly warn against it until 1990, and one Italian guidebook recommended it as late as 1998. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes P. involutus as ectomycorrhizal with a broad range of coniferous and deciduous trees. It says the species is more common in coniferous woods in Europe and is also closely associated with birch (Betula pendula). Within woodland it prefers wet or boggy ground and avoids calcareous soils. It also grows on lawns and old meadows, and fruit bodies are generally terrestrial, though they may occur on woody material around tree stumps. It fruits in late summer and autumn. In California, David Arora noted a larger form associated with oak and pine that appears in late autumn and winter. The species has been introduced with European trees to Australia, New Zealand, South Africa and South America. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with a wide variety of hardwoods and conifers, apparently also able to live as a saprobe on wood, found in woods and urban settings, growing alone, scattered or gregariously in summer and fall, and widely distributed in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
