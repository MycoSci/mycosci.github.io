## Summary

Hydnellum ferrugineum (Fr.) P. Karst. is a species of tooth fungus in the family Bankeraceae, commonly known as the mealy tooth. Wikipedia describes it as fruiting on the ground, singly or in clusters, in coniferous forest, usually in poor or sandy soil. It was first described by Fries as Hydnum ferrugineum in 1815 and transferred to Hydnellum by Karsten in 1879. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet ferrugineum as Latin for "rust-colored". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries first described the species as Hydnum ferrugineum in 1815. Petter Karsten gave it its current binomial when he transferred it to Hydnellum in 1879. Wikipedia lists later transfers to Calodon by Karsten in 1881 and to Phaeodon by Joseph Schröter in 1888. It also reports that in 1964 Kenneth A. Harrison described Hydnellum pineticola from collections with Pinus resinosa in Michigan and Pinus banksiana in Nova Scotia, and that Index Fungorum treats this name as a synonym of H. ferrugineum. Other names Wikipedia treats as synonyms are Bulliard's 1791 Hydnum hybridum, Secretan's Hydnum carbunculus (1833) and Banker's 1906 Hydnellum sanguinarium. Wikipedia quotes Banker on the difficulty of identifying old dried Hydnellum specimens that lack notes on fresh characters. These naming details come from a single independent cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that H. ferrugineum is found mainly in coniferous woodland, often near pines but occasionally with spruce. It prefers sandy soil with low organic matter and nutrients, grows singly or in clusters, and is more likely to be found in older-growth forests. The same source says it forms an unusual ectomycorrhizal association with Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris) and a tough mycelial mat in the humus and upper soil that can cover several square metres. It reports the species in North America including Mexico, describes it as widespread but generally uncommon in Europe, and says it has been collected in India and North Africa. Wikipedia also says the species is provisionally classified as endangered in Britain and is protected there and in Montenegro. A GBIF record lists distribution entries for Flanders, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. No supplied source states a fruiting season. Apart from the GBIF distribution entries, the habitat details come from one independent cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
