## Summary

Suillus brevipes (Peck) Kuntze, 1898, is a bolete in the family Suillaceae, recorded mainly in North America. Wikipedia describes it as having a sticky, chocolate to reddish-brown cap, a short whitish stipe, and yellowish pores on the underside of the cap. Both Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as mycorrhizal with pines. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Latin brevipes, meaning "short-footed". MushroomExpert.Com says the epithet reflects the tendency of the stem to be short and squat, but cautions that this is not always the case and that squattiness should not be the principal means of identifying the species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia (a single source for this account), the species was first described scientifically as Boletus viscosus by Charles Frost in 1874. In 1885 Charles Horton Peck, who had found specimens in pine woods of Albany County, New York, noted that the name was a homonym and renamed it Boletus brevipes. Otto Kuntze assigned the current name in 1898. William Alphonso Murrill later placed it in Rostkovites as Rostkovites brevipes in 1948, a genus Wikipedia says is now considered synonymous with Suillus. MushroomExpert.Com likewise gives the original description as from "New England" (Frost 1874) and the later one from the Albany, New York area (Peck 1885). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Both MushroomExpert.Com and Wikipedia describe the species as mycorrhizal with pines that have needles in bundles of two or three. Wikipedia names lodgepole and ponderosa pine as especially frequent associates. MushroomExpert.Com names lodgepole, red and jack pine. Both say it grows alone or scattered on the ground, with groups also noted, in late summer and fall. Wikipedia describes it as occurring over most of North America, including Hawaii, from Canada to Mexico. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as widely distributed from the northeast to the Midwest, the Rocky Mountains, the southwestern United States and the West Coast, but probably absent in the southeastern United States. It also reports the species from Mexico and Oceania. Wikipedia alone reports introductions with planted pines in Puerto Rico, Argentina, India, New Zealand, Japan and Taiwan. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
