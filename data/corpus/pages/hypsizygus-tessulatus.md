## Summary

Hypsizygus tessulatus (Bull.) Singer (1947) is a gilled mushroom in the family Lyophyllaceae, known in English as the beech mushroom. Wikipedia describes it as growing on hardwood in the wild and as being commercially cultivated. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a saprobic species with whitish to buff, convex caps and a white to buff spore print. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the mushroom is often found on beech trees, "hence the common name" beech mushroom. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) notes that field guides and technical treatments have long confused H. tessulatus with its close relative Hypsizygus ulmarius. It cites Redhead (1986) as separating the two on spore size and growth habit. Kuo lists Hypsizygus marmoreus and Hypsizygus elongatipes as synonyms, and says the name H. marmoreus is often used for a cultivated, commercially available version. He also notes that "Hypsizygus tessellatus" is a frequently encountered misspelling. Kuo's reference list cites Bulliard (1791) and Singer (1947). Wikipedia separately mentions a "radical alternative view" based on ITS DNA barcoding that all members of the genus are the same species. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states the species is cultivated locally in temperate climates in Europe, North America and Australia and sold fresh in supermarkets. It describes two commercial variations from Japan. Buna-shimeji is the brown, wild-type coloration, also called brown beech mushroom or brown clamshell mushroom. Bunapi-shimeji is a white UV-induced mutant of it, also called white beech mushroom or white clamshell mushroom, and its original strain is registered by Hokto Corporation. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the species is native to East Asia and common in northern North America, and is found singly and scattered on hardwood, often far above the ground, and often on beech trees. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and usually growing in clusters of two or three, and fruiting in fall. It says the species is widely distributed in eastern and northern North America and sometimes reported from the Rocky Mountains and the West Coast, with the illustrated collections from Michigan and Québec. Citing Redhead (1986), it says the species is most often found on aspens, cottonwoods and sugar maple. The two sources differ on whether it grows singly or in clusters. GBIF's description records list distribution entries for Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
