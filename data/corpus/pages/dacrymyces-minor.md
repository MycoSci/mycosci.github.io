## Summary

Dacrymyces minor Peck (1878) is a member of the family Dacrymycetaceae, described from New York. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as essentially a smaller version of Dacrymyces stillatus, with gelatinous, cushion-shaped, yellow to orangish yellow fruiting bodies about 0.5–2.5 mm across. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com credits the original description to Peck (1878) and says the species was originally described from New York. It lists Dacrymyces deliquescens var. minor as a synonym. By traditional definitions the species is treated as a smaller version of Dacrymyces stillatus, but the same page reports a 2009 DNA analysis by Shirouzu and collaborators finding the two species paraphyletic, meaning neither represents a coherent phylogenetic species. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to MushroomExpert.Com, it is saprobic, growing scattered to gregariously on the wood of hardwoods, and is found year-round. That source describes it as widely distributed in North America and also found in Central America, South America, Europe, Oceania and Asia. The collection it illustrates and describes is from Illinois. *[mushroomexpert]*
