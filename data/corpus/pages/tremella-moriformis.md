## Summary

Tremella moriformis Sm. & Sowerby (1812) is a species in the family Tremellaceae with a type locality in Great Britain, known in English as Mulberry Brain. Wikipedia describes its fruit bodies as gelatinous, dark purple-black and pustular, up to 0.5 cm across but sometimes larger and brain-like where they merge, and emerging from the perithecia of their host. The same source describes it as a parasite on Diaporthe species. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the Latin epithet "moriformis" means "mulberry-shaped", with reference to both the shape and colour of typical fruit bodies. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described from Surrey in 1812 by the English naturalists James Edward Smith and James Sowerby. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as a parasite on Diaporthe species and possibly other ascomycetous hosts, on dead herbaceous stems and on dead, attached or fallen wood of deciduous trees. The fruit bodies emerge from the perithecia of the host. The same source lists reports from the United Kingdom, Belgium, France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Portugal, Spain, Canada and the US, and the GBIF description record carries a distribution entry for Denmark. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
