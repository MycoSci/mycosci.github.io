## Summary

Marasmius oreades (Bolton) Fr. (1836) is a mushroom in the family Marasmiaceae, known as the fairy ring mushroom, fairy ring champignon or Scotch bonnet. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a saprobe of grass that often fruits in rings on lawns and meadows. It gives the cap as pale tan, 1-5 cm across, with a white spore print. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

MushroomExpert.Com says the mushroom is often called the "fairy ring mushroom" because it frequently fruits in rings on lawns, in meadows, and in other grassy places. It adds that many other mushrooms also create fairy rings, so the name causes confusion and leads to other ring-forming mushrooms being mislabeled. Wikipedia and iNaturalist likewise note that these common names cause confusion. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, inaturalist]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on grass in lawns, meadows and other grassy areas, and frequently among coastal grasses in dunes. It grows gregariously in troops, arcs or rings. Fruiting is in summer and fall, or year-round in warmer climates, and the species is widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia describes the same habitat and adds that it occurs in Europe, with a May-November season in the UK. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
