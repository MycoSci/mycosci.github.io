## Summary

Cerrena unicolor (Bull.) Murrill, 1903, the mossy maze polypore, is a poroid fungus in the family Cerrenaceae. It is a saprobic species that causes white rot. It is recorded across Europe, Asia, South America and North America. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Jean Bulliard described the fungus in 1785 as Boletus unicolor, at a time when all pored fungi were typically placed in Boletus. William Alphonso Murrill transferred it to Cerrena in 1903. Wikipedia adds that the species has acquired a long synonymy through re-description under many names and transfers among polypore genera. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as saprobic on deadwood of hardwoods, very rarely or rarely on conifers, causing a white rot. Both say it is found year-round. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as annual, growing in overlapping clusters, and sometimes lacking a cap when on the undersides of logs. Wikipedia reports it causing canker rot and decay in paper birch (Betula papyrifera) and sugar maple (Acer saccharum). MushroomExpert.Com mentions horntail wasps laying eggs in hardwood logs, often beech. MushroomExpert.Com says it is widely distributed in North America but rare or absent in the Southwest. A GBIF description record gives the substrate as a decomposing trunk (originally in Spanish: 'Tronco en descomposición'). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
