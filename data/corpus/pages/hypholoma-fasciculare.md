## Summary

Hypholoma fasciculare (Huds.) P. Kumm. 1871, in the family Strophariaceae, is known as the sulphur tuft or clustered woodlover. Wikipedia describes it as a common saprotrophic woodland mushroom that grows in large clumps on stumps, dead roots or rotting trunks of broadleaved trees. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both record the taste as bitter, and Wikipedia describes the mushroom as poisonous. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet derives from the Latin fascicularis, 'in bundles' or 'clustered', referring to its habit of growing in clumps. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Sources differ on the preferred host wood. Wikipedia says it grows on the dead wood of both deciduous and coniferous trees, and is more commonly found on decaying deciduous wood. It calls the species widespread and abundant in northern Europe and North America, and says it can appear anytime from spring to autumn. MushroomExpert.Com (a single source) describes it as saprobic, growing in clusters on decaying logs and stumps of conifers and, rarely, hardwoods, fruiting in fall and winter and sometimes in spring. For North America it says the species is more common along the West Coast and in montane or northern areas. A primary-literature source describes it as a white-rot basidiomycete in decaying wood. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_1]*
