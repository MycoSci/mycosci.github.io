## Summary

Aphroditeola olida (Quél.) Redhead & Manfr. Binder, 2013, is placed in the Hygrophoraceae (Agaricales) and is the only species in the monotypic genus Aphroditeola. Wikipedia describes the genus as producing pink cantharelloid fruit bodies with forked lamellae and a fragrant odor described as candy-like, cinnamon-like or pink bubble gum-like. A 2025 paper reports it for the first time in Russia, from the Komi Republic. A 2021 paper lists it as new to the mycobiota of the Hutsulshchyna National Nature Park in Ukraine. *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name Aphroditeola alludes to the Greek goddess Aphrodite, because of the combination of pretty pink coloration and perfume-like odor. It states that the specific epithet olida is from the Latin olidus, meaning "smelling" or "rank". This derivation comes from a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that in the last century the species was classified in Hygrophoropsis, a genus in the Boletales. It gives the difference as Hygrophoropsis having dextrinoid basidiospores while Aphroditeola lacks these, and says Aphroditeola is phylogenetically placed in the Agaricales near the Hygrophoraceae. It also notes that other literature and web sites use the synonymous names Hygrophoropsis morganii or Hygrophoropsis olida, or the misapplied name Hygrophoropsis rufescens. According to Wikipedia, the name Hygrophoropsis morganii rests on an incorrect presumption that the species was named Cantharellus morganii before Cantharellus olidus. These points come from a single source (Wikipedia). The supplied sources do not say who described the species from what material. *[wikipedia]*
