## Summary

Agaricus arvensis Schaeff. (1774), the horse mushroom, is a member of the Agaricaceae. Sources describe it as a large, white-capped Agaricus with a ring on the stalk, and it is known from ecological studies as a fairy-ring-forming fungus in grassland. It is recorded across Europe and parts of North America and Asia. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, lit_1, lit_2]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet arvensis means "of the field". *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that it is frequently found near stables and in meadows, may form fairy rings, and is often found growing with nettles. It says the fungus is associated with cypress on the North American West Coast and with spruce further east, and that in Britain it appears from July to November. Mushroom World states that it grows on soil (often heavily fertilized) or on vegetable debris. Two ecological studies (Journal of Ecology, 1984; New Phytologist, 1988) describe its fairy rings in grassland vegetation that included Agrostis capillaris. A 1914 developmental study reports young fruiting bodies collected in forest mould in a dense spruce forest near Pontarlier, France. Its author noted some question over whether that material was A. arvensis or A. xanthodermus. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*
