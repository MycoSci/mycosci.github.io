## Summary

Calocybe gambosa (Fr.) Donk, 1962, is a species in the family Lyophyllaceae, commonly known as St George's mushroom. It has been recorded in GBIF occurrence data from many countries, mostly in Europe. Wikipedia describes it as growing mainly in European grasslands. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet derives from a Latin term for "club footed", in relation to the species' bulky stipe, and that the genus name derives from the Ancient Greek kalos "pretty" and cubos "head". This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

According to Wikipedia, the English common name comes from Saint George's Day (23 April), around which the species first appears in the United Kingdom. The same source gives other regional names tied to fruiting time: Maipilz in Germany (fruits in May), Vårmousseron in Denmark (early May) and marzolino in Italy (appears in March). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described as Agaricus gambosus by Elias Magnus Fries in his 1821 Systema Mycologicum. Paul Kummer later named it Tricholoma gambosum in 1871, and Marinus Anton Donk reclassified it as Calocybe gambosa in 1962. The source does not say what material the description was based on or why the transfers were made. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as common in grasslands in Europe, often in areas rich in limestone, and names the Swedish islands Öland and Gotland as places where it is common. It reports huge fairy rings on the South Downs in southern England that appear to be several hundred years old. The same source gives a United Kingdom season of April to June, earlier in warmer countries and later in cooler ones, and says it appears in March in Italy. Mushroom World, a separate source, says it grows mainly in fields, grass verges and roadsides, and less frequently appears in mixed woods. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
