## Summary

Podaxis pistillaris (L.) Fr. (1829) is an agaric in the family Agaricaceae, recorded in the site catalog as the Desert Shaggymane. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as looking like a shaggy mane that survives in arid conditions. Its interior is not made of deliquescing gills but of poorly formed, contorted, gill-like plates that erode into a very dark brown to nearly black spore powder. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the common name comes from the species' superficial resemblance to the shaggy mane, Coprinus comatus, whose deliquescing gills it lacks. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia lists Lycoperdon pistillare L. (1771) and Scleroderma pistillare (L.) Pers. (1801) as older synonyms. MushroomExpert.Com says the original species was named from India by Linnaeus in 1771. The GBIF description records the original locality note "Habitat in India orientali. Koenig." and a lectotype designated by Priest & Lenz (Austral. Syst. Bot. 12: 114, 1999): Herb. Linn. No. 1287.7 (LINN). MushroomExpert.Com cites a study of Podaxis collections (Conlon and collaborators, 2016) that supported the idea that several Podaxis species need clarification. It says the three North American specimens studied formed a group clearly separate from the African groups. It adds that much more sampling worldwide will be needed to know whether the North American form is the same as the original species. Wikipedia separately notes that Australian collections appear more subglobose in spore shape than those from the United States, raising the possibility that they are not the same species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia reports that in Australia the species was used by many desert tribes to darken the white hair in old men's whiskers and for body painting. It also mentions reports of use as a fly repellent, and says the fungus can be used to dye textiles to a tan or reddish hue, with an alkaline base such as ammonia (urine in former times). These are single-source statements, and Wikipedia itself hedges the extent of Aboriginal use ("presumably"). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it thrives in deserts and semi-deserts of Australia and North America, that large numbers may appear after soaking rains, and that it is often found on termite mounds in South Africa. It also says that in Hawaii it is frequently encountered along roadsides and in disturbed areas on the dry sides of the islands. MushroomExpert.Com says it is found in the deserts of western North America and in Mexico. It describes the ecology as presumably saprobic, growing alone or scattered in arid, desert settings including wasteland, fields and urban locations, from fall through spring, and fairly common in the deserts of the southwest and California. That description is based on a collection from southern California. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
