## Summary

Clitocybe robusta Peck (1897) is a species in the family Tricholomataceae, originally described from New York. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a medium-sized to large white species that grows in clusters, with a pale yellowish spore print and often a sickly, foul odor. The cap does not become deeply vase-shaped and is often distorted by the clustered growth. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com lists Clitocybe alba as a synonym. It also notes that, according to some authors, Clitocybe nebularis var. alba is a synonym, and that some European authors treat Clitocybe robusta as merely a whitish form of Clitocybe nebularis. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing in dense clusters under hardwoods or conifers, often on disturbed ground (road banks, paths and similar), in summer and fall. It is described there as widely distributed in North America, with the described and illustrated collections from Colorado and Illinois. The GBIF description record lists distribution as Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
