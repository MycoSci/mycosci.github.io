## Summary

Tricholoma murrillianum Singer (1942), published in Index of Fungi 1: 72, is a member of the Tricholomataceae with occurrence records from the US, Canada and Mexico. It is known as the Western matsutake and is an ectomycorrhizal member of the matsutake species complex. Before 2017 the name T. magnivelare was widely applied to it, and molecular work has since restricted T. magnivelare to eastern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com reports that the name Tricholoma magnivelare was long used for the western North American matsutake. It says Trudell et al. (2017) found that T. magnivelare occurs only in eastern North America, and that T. murrillianum ranges from the Rocky Mountains to the West Coast. Wikipedia likewise says the species was previously included with T. magnivelare and was separated from it in 2017 on molecular evidence. MushroomExpert adds that the western, eastern and Mexican (T. mesoamericanum) matsutakes are not easily separated on physical features. It says their ranges do not appear to overlap and their ITS sequences separate them clearly. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia describes a commercial matsutake harvest in western North America that grew over the last four decades as Japanese production of T. matsutake declined. It gives a price peak in the 1990s of up to $600 per pound for Western matsutakes, followed by a large decline in prices. It says the harvest still drives some rural Pacific Northwest economies, and that Pacific Northwest harvests have declined in recent years, possibly through deep raking and over-harvesting. It also says illegal raking of wild patches can cause serious ecological damage. This is a single-source account (Wikipedia) and covers matsutake harvesting in general as well as this species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes T. murrillianum as mycorrhizal, primarily with conifers (lodgepole pine and other true pines, Douglas-fir, hemlock and fir). It says the species is also found with tanoak and madrone on the West Coast. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, in western North America from the Rocky Mountains westward, in fall and winter. It is often found buried nearly to its apex in needle duff, forming a "mushrump". The illustrated and described collections are from Colorado and Oregon. Wikipedia gives a season of October to January and says the species occurs especially on the West Coast in sandy soil. It lists pines, Douglas-fir, tanoak, madrone and manzanita as associates. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
