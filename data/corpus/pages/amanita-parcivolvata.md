## Summary

Amanita parcivolvata (Peck) E.-J. Gilbert, 1941, is a species in the family Amanitaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a red to orange cap, pale yellow gills and a pale yellow stem that lacks a ring. It describes the species as eastern and southeastern North American and associated with oaks. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Charles Horton Peck first described the species in 1900 as Amanitopsis parcivolvata, that Murrill placed it in the genus Vaginata in 1910, and that J. E. Gilbert placed it in Amanita in 1941. This account comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and often reported under pines in mixed woods. It is found growing alone or gregariously, commonly in grassy, disturbed-ground settings such as picnic areas, lawns, ditches and roadbanks, and also in woods, from spring through fall. That source places it in the eastern and southeastern United States, documented from New Jersey to Florida and Texas. Wikipedia describes it as occasional to common in the Southeastern United States, occurring primarily in Quercus forests and also observed in mixed conifer and oak forests. A GBIF description record lists a distribution of Vermont, with no further detail. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
