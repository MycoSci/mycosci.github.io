## Summary

Phallus ravenelii Berk. & M.A. Curtis (1873), Ravenel's stinkhorn, is a member of the Phallaceae, originally described from South Carolina. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a saprobic stinkhorn of eastern North America whose mature fruit body is covered in foul-smelling spore slime. MushroomExpert.Com says it is easily distinguished by a smooth cap surface rather than a pitted and ridged one. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the fungus is named after Henry William Ravenel, a botanist who first discovered it in 1846, although it remained undescribed until 1873. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Ravenel collected the fungus at the Santee River in South Carolina in 1846 and sent it, with collection notes, to Moses Ashley Curtis, who passed the specimens to the English mycologist Miles Berkeley. Berkeley described the species in 1873. Wikipedia says his description was brief and omitted the veil. It adds that Curtis Gates Lloyd later criticised the description as telling nothing of the species' leading characters. It also says Charles Horton Peck could not identify the fungus from Berkeley's description and had to obtain Ravenel's original notes before confirming its identity, and later wrote a full description. This account comes from a single encyclopedia source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as saprobic and found in almost any habitat with decaying wood: wood chips, rotten stumps and sawdust, as well as urban flowerbeds, parks, lawns, meadows, cultivated areas and woods. It says the fungus usually grows in groups and occasionally singly, and appears from August to October. MushroomExpert.Com independently gives a similar picture: saprobic, alone or gregarious in gardens, flowerbeds, meadows, lawns, woodchips, sawdust piles and cultivated areas, and also in woods, often near rotting wood or on well-rotted logs. It says the species usually appears in fall, and also over winter during warm spells and in warmer climates. For range, Wikipedia gives eastern North America from Quebec south to Florida and west to Iowa and Ohio, and also mentions Costa Rica. MushroomExpert.Com gives North America from Mexico to eastern Canada, with its illustrated collections from Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Massachusetts and Virginia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
