## Summary

Hemileccinum subglabripes (Peck) Halling is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, transferred to Hemileccinum in 2015 on the basis of DNA evidence. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods in northern and eastern North America, with a bald brown cap 3–10 cm wide and a slender yellow stem bearing tiny scabers that are best seen with a hand lens. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that Peck's species name subglabripes is Latin for "nearly smooth-stemmed", and links this to the stem, whose scabers are inconspicuous and tiny. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Charles Horton Peck first described the species as Boletus subglabripes, and that it was transferred to Hemileccinum in 2015 based on DNA evidence. MushroomExpert.Com lists Boletus subglabripes, Leccinum subglabripes and Boletus flavipes as synonyms. Wikispecies additionally lists combinations in Ceriomyces, Krombholzia and Suillus. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, including birch, aspen, oaks and hickories, appearing alone or gregariously in summer and fall. It says the species grows under oaks in the southern part of its range and under birch or aspen in its northern range. It is described as widely distributed in northern and eastern North America and also appearing in the northern Rocky Mountains. The illustrated and described collections are from Iowa, Illinois, Michigan and Minnesota. Wikipedia gives eastern North America, July to September, and association with hardwood trees. GBIF's description record lists a distribution of Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
