## Summary

Collybia cookei (Bres.) J.D. Arnold, 1935, the split-pea shanklet, is a species in the family Tricholomataceae and one of three species remaining in the genus Collybia. Its small fruit bodies have white to cream caps 2–9 mm across and stems that arise from a yellowish sclerotium. It is recorded from Europe, Asia and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the taxon was first described in 1928 by the Italian mycologist Giacomo Bresadola as Collybia cirrhata var. cookei. In a 1935 publication, Jean D. Arnold reported cultural studies with monokaryon isolates of several Collybia species to determine mating type. Attempts to produce hybrids or mycelial fusions between C. cirrhata var. cookei and C. cirrata failed. Arnold took this sexual incompatibility to show the two taxa were separate species and raised the variety to species rank as Collybia cookei. Joanne Lennox used the name Microcollybia cookei in 1979, but Microcollybia has since been folded into Collybia. Marcel Bon and Régis Courtecuisse considered it a variety of Collybia tuberosa in 1988. A 2001 ribosomal DNA analysis found C. cookei to be related to C. tuberosa and C. cirrhata, with the three forming a monophyletic group that makes up the genus Collybia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports that fruit bodies usually grow on the well-rotted, blackened remains of other mushrooms, naming Russula species, Meripilus giganteus and Inonotus hispidus. They occasionally grow on rich humus or well-decayed wood, and grow scattered, clustered or in groups. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently describes the species as saprobic and gregarious on the remains of decayed mushrooms or on humus, occasionally on well-rotted wood, found under hardwoods or conifers in summer and fall, and fairly widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia adds that the species is found in Europe (north to the Arctic Circle and the Lofoten Islands), Asia (Japan) and North America, and was first reported from Mexico in 1998. It also says the fungus is partial to mixed forest dominated by aspen and conifers in montane and subalpine environments. These last details rest on Wikipedia alone. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
