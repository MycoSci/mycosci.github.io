## Summary

Panaeolus cyanescens Sacc. (1887) is a mushroom in the family Bolbitiaceae, also treated under the name Copelandia cyanescens. Sources describe it as a coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) species whose fruiting bodies turn blue when bruised, and as a psilocybin-containing species. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc, lit_3]*

## Discovery and naming

The Argentine study in the GBIF descriptions says the species is also widely cited as Copelandia cyanescens, because authors disagreed on how to treat Copelandia. Some authors (Ola'h 1969, Gerhardt 1996) treat Copelandia species as a group within Panaeolus. Others (Pegler 1983, Singer 1986, Silva-Filho et al. 2018) treat it as a related but separate genus. The same source says recent studies (Hu et al. 2020; Asif et al. 2023) find the Copelandia species form a clade nested within Panaeolus, and on that basis they should be treated as Panaeolus. *[gbif_desc]*

## Cultivation

A 2014 Mycosphere study collected wild Philippine P. cyanescens, rescued its cell lines and tested mycelial growth on carabao, cow and horse dung media. Mycelium grew best on carabao and cow dung decoction gulaman at pH 7.5-8.0, incubated unsealed and in the dark. Carabao dung gave the highest mean yield, 7.16 g with 17.89% bio-efficiency, and cow dung gave 6.99 g with 17.49%. The authors describe the strains as having potential for cultivation for nutraceutical purposes. Wikipedia's external links also point to a home-cultivation method (TEK), but its content was not supplied. *[lit_2, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes P. cyanescens as a coprophilous (dung-inhabiting) species occurring in both the Neotropics and Paleotropics, and lists records from many countries across Africa, Asia, Oceania, the Caribbean, Central and South America, and the southern United States. An Argentine study states the fruiting bodies grow on cattle and horse dung, and describes the distribution as pantropical. It also reports a Chinese collection from Guizhou Province dated 4 August 1988. No source supplied here gives a fruiting season. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
