## Summary

Helvella maculata N.S. Weber (1975) is a member of the Helvellaceae, with its type locality in Idaho. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a western species with an elaborately ribbed and pocketed stem, a brownish, loosely lobed cap, and a fuzzy, whitish undersurface. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com cites Weber (1975) as the reference for the species. It gives the holotype as N. J. Smith 2124, from Bonner County, Idaho, held at the University of Michigan Herbarium as MICH 5635. The site examined a portion of that collection (three dried fruiting bodies) in October 2012. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) reports it under conifers (especially spruces and Douglas-fir) and hardwoods, growing alone or gregariously, and describes it as probably mycorrhizal. It is recorded from Alaska to California, inland to Idaho and Alberta. It fruits usually in fall, and also in winter or spring in coastal areas. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*
