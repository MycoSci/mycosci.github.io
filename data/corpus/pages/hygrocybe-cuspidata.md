## Summary

Hygrocybe cuspidata (Peck) Murrill, 1916, is a waxy cap in the family Hygrophoraceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a sharply pointed, scarlet-red cap that is sticky when fresh and young, and a dry stem ranging from reddish to yellow. That source treats it as a non-blackening counterpart of Hygrocybe conica and a red counterpart of Hygrocybe acutoconica. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, Peck's original description placed the species in Hygrophorus as Hygrophorus cuspidatus, and Murrill combined it into the Hygrocybe group in 1916. The same source reports continuing taxonomic disagreement. Boertmann (2000) is cited as not treating it as distinct from Hygrocybe acutoconica, since the two differ only in color, whereas Smith and Hesler (1963) treated them as distinct species, and the site author's own collecting experience supports keeping them separate. The source also reports disagreement over whether the species blackens on drying, and over the validity of the name's combination into Hygrocybe (citing Roody's 2003 combination as an example of an attempt it describes as illegitimate). These are one author's account of the literature, not an independent check. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as growing scattered or gregariously under oaks and other hardwoods, from late spring through fall, and as apparently widely distributed in North America. The illustrated and described collections are from Illinois. The same source says its precise ecological role is uncertain. This is a single-source description. *[mushroomexpert]*
