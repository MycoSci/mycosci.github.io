## Summary

Lepiota cristata (Bolton) P. Kumm., the stinking dapperling or stinking parasol, is a small agaric in the family Agaricaceae. Wikipedia describes it as common and widespread, reported from Europe, northern Asia, North America and New Zealand, and says it may be poisonous. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet cristata means "crested". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described as Agaricus cristatus by the British naturalist James Bolton in his 1788 work An History of Fungusses, Growing about Halifax. The type collection was made in 1787 from a garden in Warley Town, England. Paul Kummer moved it to the genus Lepiota in 1871, which gave the current name. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes L. cristata as saprobic, growing singly or in small groups in woodlands, gardens (especially shady and damp ones), garden waste, short grass, leaf litter, paths, ditches and other disturbed ground. It reports saprobic growth on the soil of broadleaved and conifer trees. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing scattered or gregariously, often in disturbed ground such as paths, ditches and lawns, but also on the forest floor under hardwoods or conifers, in summer and fall. Kuo's described collections are from Illinois. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
