## Summary

Lepista personata (Fr.) Cooke, 1871 is a species in the family Tricholomataceae, known in English as the Field Blewit. Wikipedia reports that the species was transferred to the genus Collybia in 2023, and Index Fungorum's current name for it is Collybia personata. GBIF holds occurrence records from about 40 countries, including the United Kingdom, Denmark, Sweden, the Netherlands and the United States. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia glosses the epithet of the synonym Lepista saeva, saevus, as an adjective meaning fierce, outrageous, angry or strong. It glosses personatus as a participle meaning disguised, pretended or false. It does not say why either epithet was chosen for this fungus. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Fries originally described the species in 1818 as Agaricus personatus. Cooke proposed Lepista personata in 1871. P. D. Orton later published Lepista saeva (1960), and H. E. Bigelow and A. H. Smith published Clitocybe saeva (1969), placing the fungus in the larger genus Clitocybe. Wikipedia adds that the species was moved to Collybia in 2023, together with Collybia nuda (formerly Lepista nuda). Wikipedia does not say what material any of these names were based on. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, a single source, states that the species fruits in open grasslands, parks, pastures, forest clearings and near forest edges. It says the fruiting is gregarious and forms fairy rings. The same source calls it widespread in Europe, with a season running from summer to the beginning of winter, and September to December in the United Kingdom. The GBIF description record lists a distribution of Vermont, with no further detail. No host tree or substrate is stated in either source. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
