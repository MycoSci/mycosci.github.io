## Summary

Tylopilus alboater (Schwein.) Murrill, 1909, the black velvet bolete, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae. Wikipedia describes a black to grayish-brown, velvety cap and a pore surface that is whitish when young and stains reddish, then black, when bruised; MushroomExpert.Com gives a similar description. Index Fungorum treats Neoporphyrellus alboater as the current name. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet alboater means "white and black". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1822 as Boletus alboater by Lewis David de Schweinitz from specimens he collected in North Carolina, and Elias Magnus Fries sanctioned the name in his 1821 Systema Mycologicum. Otto Kuntze transferred it to Suillus in 1898, and William Alphonso Murrill transferred it to Tylopilus in 1909. Jean-Edouard Gilbert moved it to Porphyrellus in 1931. Wikipedia reports that in 2025 it was transferred to the new genus Neoporphyrellus by Yan-Chun Li, Jin Li, Roy Halling and Todd Osmundson, after phylogenetic analysis showed Tylopilus as traditionally defined to be polyphyletic. Wikipedia adds that Murrill called the species the "blackish bolete". Wikipedia also says Charles Horton Peck described Boletus nigrellus in 1875 from Sand Lake, New York, and that Murrill reduced it to synonymy with T. alboater in 1916. Several later authorities have followed that synonymy, but Wikipedia says neither Index Fungorum nor MycoBank indicates it. Wikispecies lists the same names, dates and type localities (Boletus alboater, Suillus alboater, Boletus nigrellus, Porphyrellus alboater). *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the mushrooms can be used in mushroom dyeing. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a mycorrhizal species that fruits on the ground, solitarily, scattered or in groups, usually under deciduous trees and particularly oak, and that has also been recorded from coniferous and mixed forests. It gives a fruiting period of June to September in North America. MushroomExpert.Com independently describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks, growing alone or scattered in summer and fall, and widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains; its described collections are from Illinois. Wikipedia gives the North American range as Quebec south through New England to Florida and west to Missouri, Michigan and Texas, and also lists Mexico. In Asia, it lists records from China (Anhui, Fujian, Guangdong, Guangxi and Sichuan), Japan, Taiwan and Thailand. The Asian records and the June–September period rest on Wikipedia alone. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
