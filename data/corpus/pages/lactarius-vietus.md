## Summary

Lactarius vietus (Fr.) Fr., the grey milkcap, is a species of Russulaceae in the order Russulales. It is recorded across Europe, North America and parts of Asia. It is a brittle mushroom with a grey, often depressed cap and crowded, whitish gills that exude white milk. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet as the Latin vietus, meaning shrunken. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Elias Magnus Fries first described the species in 1821 as Agaricus vietus in Systema Mycologicum. In his 1838 Epicrisis systematis mycologici he moved it to Lactarius. Paul Kummer placed it in Galorrheus in 1871, and Otto Kuntze placed it in Lactifluus in 1891. Wikipedia describes both later names as obligate synonyms of Lactarius vietus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as fairly common, growing in moist areas under trees in autumn, often among Sphagnum moss. It strongly favours birch but has also been found under oak, and it forms an ectomycorrhizal relationship with the trees. It can also occur on rotting wood, including conifer and hardwood logs. Wikipedia says that it is not saprotrophic there and that its mycelium links with tree roots growing through or near the wood. Mushroom World, a separate source, likewise describes damp, mossy woodlands, particularly under birches. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
