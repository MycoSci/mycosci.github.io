## Summary

Mutinus ravenelii (Berk.) E. Fisch. (1888) is a stinkhorn in the family Phallaceae, order Phallales. MushroomExpert.Com describes the mature fruiting body as 4–8 cm high, hollow, spongy and pink to nearly red when fresh, with a brown spore slime near the apex and a foul odor while the slime is present. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, Miles Berkeley received dried specimens of this stinkhorn from his North American correspondent Moses Curtis in the mid 19th century. Later, Curtis sent specimens preserved in spirits. Berkeley then decided a new species had to be described, citing the red color, the 'more compact texture', the stubby stature and the perforated apex. The same source says Berkeley was wrong about the perforated apex, since both species develop a small hole regularly. It says the color and texture differences do separate this species from Mutinus caninus. It gives South Carolina, 'near the Santee River', as the place of original description. Wikispecies lists the original combination as Corynites ravenelii Berk. & M.A. Curtis (Trans. Linn. Soc. London 21: 151, 1853). It lists an Ithyphallus combination by E. Fischer dated 1886 and the Mutinus combination in Syll. fung. 7: 13 (1888). *[mushroomexpert, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously in gardens and cultivated areas, or in woods, in summer and fall. It gives the North American range as the southern Appalachians northward and westward to the Maritime Provinces, Washington and Alaska. It also reports the species from Mexico, says it is fairly widely distributed in Europe, and says it is apparently also found in New Zealand. Mushroom World, a separate site that links to MushroomExpert.Com, states that it is typically found in wood chips, mulch or decaying leaf litter, particularly in gardens, parks and forests, often in small clusters. *[mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*
