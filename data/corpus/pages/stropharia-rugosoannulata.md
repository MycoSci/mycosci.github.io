## Summary

Stropharia rugosoannulata Farl. ex Murrill (1922) is an agaric in the family Strophariaceae, with a type locality in Massachusetts. It is also known as wine cap, king stropharia, garden giant and burgundy mushroom. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a wine-red to reddish-brown cap that often fades to yellowish brown, purple-gray gills, a dark purple-brown to purplish-black spore print, and a thick ring that is radially split or "cogwheeled" on the underside. Wikipedia reports it from North America and Europe, and as introduced to Australia and New Zealand. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet means "wrinkled-ringed" and refers to the wrinkled annulus of the fruiting body. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the large size of the cap led to the additional colloquial name "godzilla mushroom". No supplied source explains the origin of the other common names. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing scattered or gregariously and sometimes in clusters, usually on woodchips, in gardens and in other cultivated areas. It is sometimes collected along stream beds where spring floods have occurred. He gives the season as spring through fall. He describes it in North America as widespread and fairly common east of the Great Plains, and occasionally reported in Washington and British Columbia. The collections he illustrates and describes are from Pennsylvania, Massachusetts and Illinois. Wikipedia states that it is found on wood chips across North America in summer and autumn, that it also occurs in Europe, and that it has been introduced to Australia and New Zealand. Mushroom World describes it as growing in small trooping groups, sometimes caespitose, on wood chippings or rotting straw at woodland margins. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
