## Summary

Boletus aereus Bull. (1789), the bronze bolete, is a species in the family Boletaceae, with its type locality in France. Wikipedia describes it as closely related to the European boletes B. reticulatus, B. pinophilus and B. edulis. It is reported mainly from central and southern Europe and North Africa. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species epithet is the Latin adjective aerĕus, meaning "made with bronze or copper". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

According to Wikipedia, Bulliard gave the species the common name le bolet bronzé (the bronze bolete) in 1789, noting that it was called the cep noir (black cep) in other countries. Wikipedia also lists ontto beltza (black fungus) in Basque, porcino nero (black piglet) in Italian, and vasilikό (the royal one) or kalogeraki (little monk) in Greek. It says the English name dark cep is in use and that the British Mycological Society also approved the name bronze bolete. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Pierre Bulliard described Boletus aereus in 1789. Before 1987 the name was written B. aereus Fr., because Elias Magnus Fries sanctioned Bulliard's description in 1821. Lucien Quélet transferred it to Dictyopus in 1886, and René Maire treated it as a subspecies of B. edulis in 1937. Moroccan collections under cork oak were described as B. mamorensis in 1978, on the basis of a rufous chestnut cap and a rooting stipe. Molecular studies by Dentinger and colleagues in 2010 and Loizides and colleagues in 2019 found these collections could not be genetically separated from B. aereus, and B. mamorensis is now treated as a later synonym. Wikipedia also says that Californian material reported as B. aereus in 1975 was established in 2008 as a separate species, B. regineus. These details come from a single independent source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only independent source with habitat text, says the species is found mainly in central and southern Europe and North Africa and is rare in colder climates such as England. Fruit bodies are described as appearing mostly during hot spells in summer and autumn, in mycorrhizal association with broad-leaved trees and sclerophyllous shrubs. The hosts it names are oak (Quercus), beech (Fagus), chestnut (Castanea), strawberry tree (Arbutus), treeheath (Erica) and rockrose (Cistus), with a stated preference for acidic soils. Roadsides and parks are given as common habitats, and cork oak (Quercus suber) is named as an important symbiont. The GBIF distribution record lists Denmark, Norway, Madeira Island and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
