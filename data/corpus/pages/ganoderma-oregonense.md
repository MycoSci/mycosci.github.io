## Summary

Ganoderma oregonense Murrill (1908) is a polypore in the family Polyporaceae, described from Oregon. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a wood-decaying bracket fungus of conifers in northwestern coastal North America. It has a lacquered, reddish-brown cap and causes a white rot. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reproduces W. A. Murrill's original description of the species. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) reports that the similar Ganoderma tsugae was until recently thought to coexist with G. oregonense in the Pacific Northwest. It says a study of hundreds of North American Ganoderma collections by Loyd and collaborators (2017, 2018) failed to support this, so that G. oregonense appears to be the only conifer-associated, lacquered-topped species in the area. Wikipedia separately notes it has been speculated that G. oregonense and G. tsugae might be a single species; this is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and sometimes parasitic. It grows alone or in groups on decaying conifer logs and stumps, or from the wounds of injured living trees, causing a white rot. It is annual and distributed in the Pacific Northwest and California, and the illustrated collection is from Oregon. That source gives the season as fall through spring. Wikipedia lists the range as California, Oregon, Washington, British Columbia, Yukon and Alaska. It says the fungus can be found year-round but is freshest from July to November. It also says the fungus prefers dead red fir but will also grow on dead or living Douglas fir, spruce, hemlock and pine, and that on living trees it is usually associated with wounds such as bear marks. The two sources differ on season; Wikipedia's host list is a single-source claim. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
