## Summary

Volvariella surrecta (Knapp) Singer, the piggyback rosegill, is an agaric in the family Pluteaceae. According to Wikipedia, it grows as a parasite on the fruit bodies of other gilled mushrooms, usually Clitocybe nebularis. The same source calls it rare but widely distributed, reported from Eurasia, northern Africa, North America and New Zealand. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet surrecta is Latin for "to arise". It also states that Berkeley's synonymous epithet loveianus honors the British naturalist and Reverend Richard Thomas Lowe. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first mentioned in the scientific literature as Agaricus surrectus by the English botanist John Leonard Knapp in his 1829 Journal of a Naturalist, where he described it and illustrated it in a woodcut. Seven years later Miles Berkeley described the fungus as Agaricus loveianus, unaware of Knapp's earlier publication. Berkeley's name was then used in the literature for over a century. In 1917 William Alphonso Murrill made the combination Volvariopsis loweiana, based on Berkeley's name. In 1942 John Ramsbottom found Knapp's image and description and recognised that it referred to the same species as Berkeley's A. loveianus, and made the combination Volvaria surrecta. Rolf Singer transferred it to Volvariella in 1951. The same source adds that molecular analysis places the species in the Volvariella pusilla group, in a subclade with V. hypopithys, and that Konrad and Maublanc had earlier proposed treating it as a subspecies of V. hypopithys. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia reports a single historical account: in 1867 Worthington George Smith stated that he had cultivated the species by partially burying fruit bodies under water-soaked rotting fir leaves in a bell-glass in a warm room. In his account a white mycelium grew over the leaves and formed small white pins, which grew into fully formed mushrooms about two weeks after starting. No other cultivation source was supplied. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that V. surrecta grows parasitically on the fruit bodies of Clitocybe species, usually C. nebularis. It has also been reported on Tricholoma species and on Melanoleuca brevipes. The mushrooms grow in clusters and fruit in summer and autumn. The host mushroom is sometimes malformed. The species is described as rare even though its main host is common, and the conditions needed for the parasite to fruit are not well known. Some authors have suggested that it may grow equally well as a parasite or a saprobe. It has been found on its host in birch woodlands, pine plantations, scrub, thickets of small trees or shrubs beside roads, and under brambles. No soil preference has been determined, and it has been found in sands, clay, gravels and peat. Wikipedia gives its range as North America north of Mexico, northern Africa, Europe, New Zealand, and Asia (Amur region of Russia, India and Korea). The GBIF record lists distribution entries including Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Madeira and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
