## Summary

Xylaria cubensis (Mont.) Fr. (1851) is placed in the Xylariaceae (Xylariales, Ascomycota) and has GBIF occurrence records from many countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a club-shaped Xylaria with a coppery brown surface that becomes blackish with maturity, growing directly from decaying hardwood logs. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was originally described from Cuba, "as the species name suggests". *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the original combination as Hypoxylon cubense Mont. (1840). MushroomExpert.Com cites Montagne 1840 and Fries 1851. It also notes that the asexual stage (anamorph) was previously recognized by some as a separate species, Xylocoremium flabelliforme, but that current taxonomic rules require the sexual and asexual stages to be treated as one taxonomic entity. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on decaying hardwood logs, growing directly from the wood, alone or gregariously, and causing a soft rot. It fruits from spring through fall, or over winter in warm climates. The same source calls it common in tropical and subtropical areas, the southeastern United States and the Appalachian Mountains, and absent in western and northern North America. Its examined collections came from North Carolina, Pennsylvania and Massachusetts. It says the anamorph appears in winter, spring and early summer. The GBIF description record, a taxonomic account of a Hainan (China) collection, describes the fungus as saprobic on dead tree trunk. That specimen was collected on dead wood of unknown plant roots, in November 2020, at 152 m elevation. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
