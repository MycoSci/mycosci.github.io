## Summary

Agaricus bernardii Quél. (1878) is a species in the family Agaricaceae, recorded in the site catalog from sand dunes and from many countries across Europe, North America, Asia and Oceania. Wikipedia gives its common names as salt-loving agaricus and salty mushroom, and describes a cap 5–15 cm across, whitish to buff, with gills that start pink and turn chocolate brown, and flesh that stains reddish when cut. The available literature on this species is thin, and most of it is not descriptive. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species was named after its original collector, G. Bernard. This is a single-source statement, and no other supplied source discusses the derivation of the name. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Lucien Quélet first described the species, as a Psalliota, from collections made at La Rochelle, a seaport on the Bay of Biscay in France. Pier Andrea Saccardo later transferred it to Agaricus. Wikipedia also says the infrageneric placement of the species is uncertain and has been treated differently by Heinemann (1978), Romagnesi (1986) and Wasser (1995). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes A. bernardii as saprobic, fruiting singly, scattered or in groups on the ground, in sandy soils, lawns and other high-salt habitats such as ocean coasts and salt marshes. It says the fungus was once primarily maritime but has spread inland to roadside verges where salt is applied to de-ice roads, that fruit bodies sometimes form underground, and that it can form fairy rings in grasslands or pastures. Mushroom World independently describes it as growing in saline habitats such as coastal grasslands, salt marshes and areas influenced by road salt. A 2020 food-engineering paper calls it a typical mushroom of Inner Mongolia, China, and a 1986 survey of Mexico City macromycetes reports it for the first time from Mexico. No supplied source gives a fruiting season or host trees. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, lit_1, lit_3]*
