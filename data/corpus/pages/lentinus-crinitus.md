## Summary

Lentinus crinitus (L.) Fr. (1825) is a species in the family Polyporaceae, order Polyporales, known in English as the fringed sawgill. Wikipedia describes it as a saprotrophic white rot fungus that grows on dead wood. The catalog holds occurrence records from many countries across the Americas, plus a few in Africa, Asia and Australia. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described by Carl Linnaeus and that Elias Magnus Fries reported and identified it in 1825. A GBIF-hosted nomenclatural description gives the original citation as Sp. Pl., ed. 2, 2: 1644 (1763), with the protologue habitat given as "Habitat in America meridionali. Rolander." The same record lists original material as Herb. Linn. No. 1279.1 (LINN) and an icon in Browne, Civ. Nat. Hist. Jamaica (1756), and states that no type was designated. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (a single source cluster, which includes the derivative iNaturalist text) describes a saprotrophic white rot fungus found on decaying logs in open areas, in tropical forest and in mixed oak-dominated forest. Its listed hosts are dead wood of Nerium oleander, Hevea, Hippomane, Quercus, Barringtonia, Nyssa, Salix and Rhizophora mangle. Wikipedia gives an altitude range of 50–2,800 m and says fruiting is most often April to July in the Northern Hemisphere and September to December in the Southern Hemisphere. A separate GBIF specimen-description record, in Spanish, gives the substrate as wood, including decaying and burned trunks. It records growth habits as clustered to gregarious, or solitary to anastomosed, and elevations from about 60 m to over 3,500 m. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
