## Summary

Coltricia perennis (L.) Murrill, 1903, is a polypore in the family Hymenochaetaceae, based on the older name Boletus perennis L. (1753). Mushroom World, an aggregator, describes it as an annual polypore that grows in soil rather than on dead wood, and lists its location as Europe and parts of North America. *[mushroomworld, wikispecies]*

## Discovery and naming

The basionym is Boletus perennis L., published in Species Plantarum 2: 1177 (1753). Wikispecies lists the typification as Linnaeus's Flora lapponica no. 522, Flora suecica no. 1086 and Vaillant's Botanicon parisiense, Tab. 12, fig. 7. GBIF records the original material as an illustration in Vaillant (1727) and states that no type was designated. Murrill made the combination in Coltricia in the Journal of Mycology 9(2): 91 (1903). Wikispecies also lists earlier homotypic transfers: Suillus (Roussel, 1796), Trametes (Fries, 1849), Polystictus (Karsten, 1879), Pelloporus (Quélet, 1886), Ochroporus (Schröter, 1888), Microporus (Kuntze, 1898) and Xanthochrous (Patouillard, 1900). None of the supplied sources gives the reasons for these transfers. *[wikispecies, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Mushroom World states that it grows in the soil rather than on dead wood, and that it prefers humus-rich sandy soil on woodland edges and on acidic heathland. GBIF gives the substrate as "Sobre mantillo" and quotes the original habitat statement "Habitat in sylvis super terram, subjectis caudicibus arborum putridis." Wikispecies gives the basionym's host/substratum and locality as "On soil and rotten wood: Sweden". The three sources are not identical in how they describe the substrate. None of them states a season or a specific host tree. *[mushroomworld, gbif_desc, wikispecies]*
