## Summary

Leccinum scabrum (Bull.) Gray, 1821, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the birch bolete, rough-stemmed bolete or scaber stalk. Wikipedia notes it was formerly classified as Boletus scaber. Wikipedia describes it as widespread in Europe, in the Himalayas in Asia, and elsewhere in the Northern Hemisphere, occurring only in mycorrhizal association with birch trees. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species occurs only in mycorrhizal association with birch, and describes it as a European species introduced to various areas of the world, mostly appearing in urban areas. In New Zealand it is said to associate solely with Betula pendula. It has also been found with ornamental birch planted outside its native range, such as in California. For North America, Wikipedia gives June to September, except on the West Coast, where it appears from September to November. Mushroom World and iNaturalist (whose text is a declared derivative of Wikipedia) give a fruiting season of June to October. Mushroom World, a single source, adds that it is often found growing in damp ground conditions. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, inaturalist]*
