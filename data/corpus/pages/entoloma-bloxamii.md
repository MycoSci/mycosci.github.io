## Summary

Entoloma bloxamii (Berk. & Broome) Sacc. (1887) is a species of gilled mushroom in the family Entolomataceae, known in English as the big blue pinkgill or Bloxam's Entoloma. Wikipedia describes it as having a European distribution, occurring mainly in agriculturally unimproved grassland, and as assessed globally 'vulnerable' on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species because its habitat is threatened. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species was named Agaricus Bloxami in honour of its collector, the naturalist and clergyman Andrew Bloxam. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally described from England in 1854 as Agaricus Bloxami by Miles Joseph Berkeley and Christopher Edmund Broome, and was transferred to Entoloma by Pier Andrea Saccardo in 1887. The same source reports that molecular work has shown E. bloxamii sensu lato to comprise several distinct blue taxa in Europe, including E. bloxamii sensu stricto, E. madidum (previously considered a synonym), E. atromadidum, E. ochreoprunuloides f. hyacinthinum and one as yet unnamed species. It also reports that collections from the west coast of North America, formerly referred to E. bloxamii or E. madidum, are distinct and are now referred to E. medianox. All of this comes from a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as rare but widespread in Europe. It says that, like many other European pinkgills, it occurs in old, agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns). It is usually associated with calcareous soils but may also be found in more acidic areas, and it is described as typical of waxcap grasslands. This habitat information comes from a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
