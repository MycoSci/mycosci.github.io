## Summary

Cuphophyllus flavipes (Britzelm.) Bon is a gilled mushroom in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the yellow foot waxcap. Wikipedia describes it as having a European distribution, occurring mainly in agriculturally unimproved grassland. It also reports that threats to that habitat have led to the species being assessed as globally "vulnerable" on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described from Germany in 1891 by the mycologist Max Britzelmayr as Hygrophorus flavipes, and was transferred to the genus Cuphophyllus by the French mycologist Marcel Bon in 1985. The same source states that recent molecular work, based on cladistic analysis of DNA sequences, has confirmed C. flavipes as a distinct species. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as widespread but uncommon throughout Europe. Like most other European waxcaps, it is said to occur in old, agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns). The same source adds that recent research suggests waxcaps in general are neither mycorrhizal nor saprotrophic but may be associated with mosses. That statement is about waxcaps generally, not this species specifically. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*
