## Summary

Suillus americanus (Peck) Snell is a bolete in the family Suillaceae, known in English as the American slippery Jack, American Suillus and chicken fat mushroom. Sources describe it as a slimy or sticky yellow-capped bolete with large angular yellow pores, associated with pines, especially eastern white pine (Pinus strobus). It is recorded across North America and, according to DNA-based work summarised by Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com, more widely across Eurasia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, mushroomworld]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Charles Horton Peck described the species from New York material collected as early as 1869 near Sand Lake, Albany, and Port Jefferson. Peck designated no type specimen, and one of the Sand Lake collections was lectotypified in 1986. Wikipedia says Édouard-Jean Gilbert later transferred it to Ixocomus, a genus since absorbed into Suillus. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both report that Klofac (2013) synonymized S. sibiricus with S. americanus and that Nguyen et al. (2016) supported this with ITS DNA data from Asian, European and North American collections. Both also list several varieties and forms, which Nguyen et al. read as expected variation in a widespread species. MushroomExpert.Com also lists S. himalayensis as a synonym. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe an ectomycorrhizal species associated with pines. Wikipedia says it is best known in North America with eastern white pine (Pinus strobus). MushroomExpert.Com lists 5-needled pines: eastern white, western white, limber, Siberian and Swiss pine. Wikipedia says it fruits on the ground, singly or in clusters, and MushroomExpert.Com says it typically grows gregariously, often densely. Both give the season as late summer into autumn. MushroomExpert.Com says it was originally described from New York, is widespread in North America wherever the host trees occur, and is also found in Europe and Asia where 5-needled pines occur. Wikipedia also cites records from Macedonian pine (Pinus peuce) forests in Montenegro at about 1,400–1,900 m elevation. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
