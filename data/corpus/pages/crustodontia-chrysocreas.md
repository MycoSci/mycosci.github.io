## Summary

Crustodontia chrysocreas (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) Hjortstam & Ryvarden is a crust fungus in the family Meruliaceae, order Polyporales. It was first described as Corticium chrysocreas, and the genus Crustodontia was circumscribed in 2005 to contain it. It is described as having a pantropical distribution. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

The species was originally described as Corticium chrysocreas by Miles Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis in 1873. Their description reads: "Subiculum bright yellow, thin; hymenium immarginate pallid, or yellow tinged with tawny." The genus Crustodontia was circumscribed in 2005 to contain this species. Wikipedia reports that molecular analysis places the genus in the Meruliaceae, in the phlebioid clade, and that it is closely related to Phlebia. This account comes from a single independent source cluster (Wikipedia, with iNaturalist reproducing its text). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fungus causes a white rot in the woody debris of live oak and other hardwoods, and that it also causes white heart rot in living trees. Wikipedia describes the distribution as pantropical, with collections from the United States, Costa Rica, the Caribbean Islands, Venezuela, equatorial Africa, Sri Lanka, China, Taiwan, Japan, Hawaii, Brunei, western Australia and New Zealand. It says the fungus is rare in Europe, with its northernmost recorded collection at 51.7°N in Belarus. A GBIF-listed specimen record describes a collection from the trunk of an angiosperm in Xianren Valley, Zixishan National Forest Park, Chuxiong, Yunnan Province, China (1 July 2021). *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
