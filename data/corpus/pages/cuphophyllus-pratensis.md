## Summary

Cuphophyllus pratensis (Pers.) Bon, 1985 is an agaric in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the meadow waxcap. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a dry, brownish orange to orangish buff cap and cream to orangish, distant gills that begin to run down the stem. Wikipedia describes a widespread, mainly temperate distribution. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet comes from Latin "pratensis", meaning growing in meadows. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the species has been shifted between several genera in the waxy cap family, and older field guides may place it in Hygrophorus, Camarophyllus or Hygrocybe. Wikipedia adds that it was transferred to Hygrocybe in 1914, and that molecular (DNA sequence) research found it does not belong in Hygrocybe sensu stricto but in Cuphophyllus. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says that in Europe, as with other waxcaps, it grows in old, unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns), while elsewhere it grows in woodland, and that recent research suggests waxcaps may be associated with mosses. MushroomExpert.Com describes its ecological role as uncertain and says it grows scattered to gregariously in hardwood or conifer forests or in grass, from late spring through fall (or over winter in warmer climates). Its illustrated collections are from California, Illinois and Ohio. Each of these habitat descriptions comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
