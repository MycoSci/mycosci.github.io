## Summary

Lycoperdon subcretaceum (Zeller) Jeppson & E. Larss. (2010) is a puffball in the family Lycoperdaceae, with occurrence records in 11 countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as found under conifers in the mountains of western North America. It gives the fruiting body as 2.5–4 cm high and 3–5 cm wide, covered in low, mostly pyramid-shaped warts and scales, with soft white flesh that turns brown and then becomes brown spore dust. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, the species was originally named Calvatia subcretacea by Zeller (1947). It has since been placed in Gastropila and Handkea. The same source says DNA sequencing (Bates 2004; Larsson & Jeppson 2010) supports placing it in Lycoperdon. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, usually growing alone or scattered in montane and subalpine conifer woods. It fruits in summer and fall and is distributed from the Rocky Mountains to the mountains of the West Coast. The collections illustrated and described there are from Colorado. This is a single-source description. *[mushroomexpert]*
