## Summary

Lactarius repraesentaneus Britzelm. (1885) is a milkcap in the family Russulaceae, known in English as the northern bearded milkcap or purple-staining bearded milkcap. Wikipedia describes it as a large orange-yellow-capped species with a bearded cap margin, whose white latex and cut tissues stain lilac to purple. Wikipedia reports the fruit bodies as poisonous, causing stomach aches. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet repraesentaneus as Latin for "well-represented". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Max Britzelmayr first described the species in 1885 from material collected in Bavaria. Killermann's 1933 variety Lactarius scrobiculatus var. repraesentaneus is treated as a synonym. Rolf Singer defined the subspecies speciosus in 1942 for forms with zonate matted fibers on the cap. A form, f. immutabilis, was described in 2011 from Le Sappey, Haute-Savoie, France, differing in the staining reaction of injured flesh. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with spruces (Engelmann spruce in the southern Rocky Mountains), fruiting in summer and fall, and widely distributed in northern and montane North America. Wikipedia reports fruit bodies scattered or in groups on the ground under spruce from August to September, at higher elevations in the northern USA and southern Canada. The same source says that in Europe, where it is fairly rare, it is found mainly under sallows (Salix capraea) in mountain-foot forests on non-calcareous soil, and that it has been collected under birch in Greenland. GBIF-hosted material examined records Montana and Wyoming collections among Salix glauca and krummholz Picea engelmannii. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
