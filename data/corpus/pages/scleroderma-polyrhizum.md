## Summary

Scleroderma polyrhizum (J.F. Gmel.) Pers. (1801) is a member of the family Sclerodermataceae, order Boletales, known in English as the star earthball or dead man's hand. Wikipedia describes a fungus that begins buried and later splits open into a rough, star-shaped body around a dark spore mass. MushroomExpert.Com describes a baseball-sized fruiting body with tough, thick skin and a hard, black interior that turns to dust in old age. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that the epithet (which it spells "polyrhizon") means "many-rooted", a reference to the rhizomorphs at the base of the fruiting body. This is the only source that discusses the name's derivation. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Johann Friedrich Gmelin first described the species in 1792 as Lycoperdon polyrhizum, and Christiaan Hendrik Persoon transferred it to Scleroderma in his 1801 work Synopsis methodica fungorum. Elias Fries's Scleroderma geaster (1829) is a synonym. In 1848 Joseph-Henri Léveillé treated the star-shaped opening of mature fruit bodies as a distinct characteristic and proposed the genus Sclerangium to contain the taxon. Wikipedia also reports that Gastón Guzmán's 1970 classification places the species in subgenus Sclerangium. MushroomExpert.Com says the species was originally described from Italy (citing Micheli 1729). It also advises treating Scleroderma bermudense, S. geaster, S. guzmanii and S. yunnanense as synonyms pending further research, and possibly S. anomalosporum, S. sinnamariense and S. xanthochroum as well. That site considers a 2021 preliminary investigation of the group (Ortiz-Rivero et al.) to be suspect. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies have been used in traditional Chinese medicine for detumescence and hemostasis, and that the species was featured on a Libyan postage stamp in 1985. Both statements come from that single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that fruit bodies grow singly, scattered, or in clusters, usually on hard clay or sandy soil, gravel, in lawns, or on bare soil, fruiting in late summer and fall, with blackened rays sometimes found in winter. MushroomExpert.Com describes growth alone, scattered, or gregariously in grass and disturbed-ground settings, often in urban settings, in summer, fall and early winter. Both sources call the species probably saprobic but possibly mycorrhizal. Wikipedia cites experimental evidence that spores inoculated onto Monterey pine (Pinus radiata) seedlings formed ectomycorrhizae. Wikipedia describes a wide distribution in North America including Mexico, with records from Africa, Asia (China and Japan), Europe, South America (Brazil) and Oceania. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as widely distributed in Europe and North America, and also reported from the Caribbean, Central America, northern Africa, Asia and Oceania, with its illustrated collections from Illinois and New Jersey. The GBIF description record lists only a distribution of Brazil and Madeira Island. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
