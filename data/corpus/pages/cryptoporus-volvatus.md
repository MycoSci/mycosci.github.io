## Summary

Cryptoporus volvatus (Peck) Shear, 1902, is a polypore in the family Polyporaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a polypore that decomposes the rotting sapwood of conifers. Its pore surface is covered by a flap of tissue, so it looks somewhat like a wood-inhabiting puffball (MushroomExpert.Com). It is recorded in the US, Canada, Mexico, Japan, Korea, China and several other countries. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Charles Horton Peck originally described the species in 1875 as Polyporus volvatus, and that Cornelius Lott Shear transferred it to Cryptoporus in 1902. Wikispecies gives the original publication as Peck, Ann. Rep. N.Y. St. Mus. nat. Hist. 27: 98 (1875), and the transfer as Shear, Bull. Torrey bot. Club 29: 450 (1902). MushroomExpert.Com says it was originally described from New York. None of the supplied sources says why the transfer was made. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of conifers, especially pines. It grows alone or gregariously, often on trees killed by fire, insects or other causes in the previous year. It is said to cause a soft, grayish brown rot in the outer few centimeters of the sapwood, and to be rarely found more than a year or two after the tree has died. That source gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. It reports the species as widely distributed in North America and also reported from South America and eastern Asia. Wikipedia separately describes it as an after effect of attack by the pine bark beetle. Both habitat descriptions are single-source statements. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
