## Summary

Armillaria ostoyae (Romagn.) Herink, 1973, is a species in the family Physalacriaceae, with records in Europe, North America and Asia. Sources describe it as a root-rot fungus of conifers, recognised by its clustered fruiting, the ring on its stem and the dark scales on its cap. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) states that the species was originally described from France by Romagnesi in 1970, and cites Herink 1973 for the combination. Wikipedia says a 2008 publication showed that Charles Horton Peck had described the species under the earlier name Armillaria solidipes in 1900. It adds that a proposal to conserve the name A. ostoyae was published in 2011 and approved by the Nomenclature Committee for Fungi. MushroomExpert.Com disagrees on the synonymy. It says the name ostoyae was for decades applied to a similar North American species, A. solidipes, and that recent research (Klopfenstein et al., 2017) supports treating the two as distinct phylogenetic species. See `disagreements`. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as parasitic and saprobic on the wood and roots of conifers and, less frequently, hardwoods (especially aspens and birches within conifer forests). It causes a root rot, typically grows in dense clusters, and fruits in summer and fall. That source calls it a conifer-consuming species of Eurasia, widespread in Europe. Wikipedia describes it as mostly common in the cooler regions of the northern hemisphere, found on coniferous host trees in British Columbia and the Pacific Northwest and also in parts of Asia. It also says the mushrooms appear in autumn. A study of four mountain forests in Central France (lit_3) recorded A. ostoyae in a beech stand, in a beech stand into which conifers had been introduced, and as the exclusive and pathogenic Armillaria species in a young pine stand. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, lit_3]*
