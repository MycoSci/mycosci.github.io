## Summary

Rubroboletus satanas (Lenz) Kuan Zhao & Zhu L. Yang, 2014, known as Satan's bolete or the Devil's bolete, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae. It was formerly placed in Boletus and is recorded mainly in Europe. The supplied sources conflict on whether it can be eaten (see disagreements), and it is classed as a poisonous mushroom that causes gastrointestinal illness. *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*

## Etymology

According to Wikipedia, Lenz knew of several reports of adverse reactions in people who had eaten the fungus. He apparently felt ill from undescribed "emanations" while describing it, and this is given as the reason for the sinister epithet. The Greek word satanas is derived from Hebrew śāṭān. This account comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

It was originally described as Boletus satanas by the German mycologist Harald Othmar Lenz in 1831. Genetic analysis published in 2013 placed it and several other red-pored boletes in the "dupainii" clade, distantly nested from the core Boletus group (including B. edulis), and it was transferred to the new genus Rubroboletus in 2014. Harry D. Thiers concluded that North American material matched the species description, but genetic testing has since shown that western North American collections are Rubroboletus eastwoodiae, a different species. Wikipedia reports that R. satanas is most closely related to R. pulchrotinctus. Wikispecies lists further synonyms, including Suillus satanas (Kuntze, 1898), Tubiporus satanas (Maire, 1937), Suillellus satanas (Blanco-Dios, 2015) and Boletus crataegi Smotl., 1952. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it fruits in summer and early autumn in warm, broad-leaved and mixed forests. It forms ectomycorrhizal associations with oak (Quercus) and sweet chestnut (Castanea) and prefers calcareous soils. Other frequently reported hosts are hornbeam (Carpinus), beech (Fagus) and lime/linden (Tilia). In Europe it mostly occurs in the southern regions and is rare or absent in the north. In the United Kingdom it is found only in the south of England, and in Scandinavia it occurs primarily on a few Baltic islands with highly calcareous soil. Past reports from the United States are said to be of R. eastwoodiae instead. This habitat account rests on a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
