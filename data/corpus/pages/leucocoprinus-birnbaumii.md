## Summary

Leucocoprinus birnbaumii (Corda) Singer, 1962 is a gilled mushroom in the family Agaricaceae, known in English as the plantpot dapperling or flower pot parasol. Wikipedia describes it as a small yellow species that is common in the tropics and subtropics and frequently appears in greenhouses and flowerpots in temperate regions. It is reported to be toxic if ingested. *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia says Corda described the species from a Prague greenhouse, where a garden inspector named Birnbaum found it growing among pineapple plants, and that he named it Agaricus birnbaumii on that basis. The same source says the earlier name luteus comes from the Latin for yellow or yellow-orange. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, James Bolton first described the species as Agaricus luteus in 1788, from a hothouse near Halifax. That name was illegitimate because Hudson had already used A. luteus in 1778 for an unrelated Russula. August Corda described it again in 1839 from a Prague greenhouse as Agaricus birnbaumii. Julien Godfrin treated it as Lepiota lutea in 1897, and many North American books used that name until the 1980s. Marcel Locquin moved it to Leucocoprinus luteus in 1945, and Rolf Singer made the combination Leucocoprinus birnbaumii in 1962. This account comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as a saprotroph on very decayed plant matter (humus or compost). It says the species is common in the tropics and subtropics. In temperate regions such as North America, Europe and Australia it is more often found in hothouses and plant pots than in the wild. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing alone, gregariously or clustered in flower pots and greenhouses. In warm conditions it also grows outdoors in gardens, lawns and other cultivated areas, often around stumps. Kuo also reports it in hardwood and conifer forests, especially on disturbed ground, outdoors in summer and indoors year-round. Wikipedia cites a 2019 study that recorded it on the stump of a dead lemon tree in an orchard near Damietta, Egypt, in autumn. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
