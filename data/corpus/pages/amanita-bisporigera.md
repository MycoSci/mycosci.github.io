## Summary

Amanita bisporigera G.F. Atk. (1906) is a species in the family Amanitaceae, known as the eastern North American destroying angel. Wikipedia describes it as a deadly poisonous white amanita of eastern North America, placed in section Phalloideae with other amatoxin-containing species. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia says that, as the species name suggests, A. bisporigera typically bears two spores on the basidia. It gives no fuller account of the name's derivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described in 1906 by George Francis Atkinson in a publication by Cornell University colleague Charles E. Lewis, with the type locality at Ithaca, New York, where several collections were made. In 1941 Édouard-Jean Gilbert transferred it to his genus Amanitina, which is now considered synonymous with Amanita. Amanita vernella Murrill (1944, Gainesville, Florida) is now thought to be synonymous after a 1979 examination of its type material showed mostly 2-spored basidia. Rodham Tulloss considers Amanita phalloides var. striatula Peck (1902) synonymous as well. Wikispecies gives the original place of publication as Botanical Gazette 41(3): 348. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports fruit bodies on the ground, solitary, scattered or in groups, in mixed coniferous and deciduous forests, appearing in summer and early fall. It says they are commonly found near oak and have been reported in birch-aspen areas in the west. It describes the species as most common in eastern North America, rare in western North America, widely distributed in Canada, and ranging south to Mexico. MushroomExpert.Com (same independence cluster as Wikipedia) describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and possibly other hardwoods, in summer and fall, and common from Texas to the north woods and maritime provinces. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
