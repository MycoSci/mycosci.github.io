## Summary

Athelia rolfsii (Sacc.) C.C. Tu & Kimbr. 1978, family Atheliaceae, is a corticioid fungus that Wikipedia describes as a facultative plant pathogen and the causal agent of "southern blight" disease in crops. It is known from soil, where it forms mycelium and hard sclerotia, and from the plant hosts it attacks. Wikipedia lists the same fungus under the name Agroathelia rolfsii, and it also gives the asexual-state name Sclerotium rolfsii. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1911 by Pier Andrea Saccardo from a specimen collected by Peter Henry Rolfs and sent by John A. Stevenson at the US national mycological collection. Rolfs had first taken the unnamed fungus to be the cause of a tomato blight in Florida, and it was later found to cause disease on multiple hosts. The specimens sent to Saccardo were sterile, consisting only of hyphae and sclerotia, and Saccardo placed the species in the form genus Sclerotium as Sclerotium rolfsii. In 1932 Mario Curzi found that the spore-bearing (teleomorph) state was a corticioid fungus and placed the species in Corticium. The same source says that uncertainty over classification, while the broadly defined Corticium was being partitioned, led to placements in Pellicularia, then Botryobasidium, and finally Athelia. This account comes from a single source, Wikipedia. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only source here that describes its ecology, states that the fungus occurs in soil as a saprotroph but can also attack living plants. It says the host range is almost indiscriminate, and it particularly attacks seasonal crops because its sclerotia remain in the soil. The same source says it mostly occurs in warm soils (above 15 °C) and can be a serious pest of vegetables in tropical and subtropical regions, including Florida, where it was first recognized. It also states that the fungus causes a root rot of cassava. *[wikipedia]*
