## Summary

Suillus collinitus (Fr.) Kuntze is a pored bolete in the family Suillaceae. Wikipedia describes it as having a brown, viscid cap up to 11 cm across, a yellowish stem with no ring, and distinctive pink mycelial threads at the stem base. The same source describes it as an ectomycorrhizal associate of pines, commonly occurring in southern Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet collinitus is derived from Latin and means "smeared" or "greased". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Boletus collinitus by Elias Magnus Fries in 1838, and Otto Kuntze transferred it to the genus Suillus in 1898. The same source says S. fluryi is a synonym. It reports that Dutch mycologist H.S.C. Huijsman described the variety aureus in 1969 (as S. fluryi var. aureus) from a Swiss collection, and that Giampaolo Simonini and colleagues described the variety velatipes in 1998 from Italian collections. This account rests on a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes S. collinitus as an ectomycorrhizal fungus that associates with several two-needled pines: Aleppo pine (Pinus halepensis), European black pine (P. nigra), stone pine (P. pinea) and Scots pine (P. sylvestris). It states that the fungus favours limestone soils and is thermophilic, commonly occurring in southern Europe. It is rarely seen in more northern regions such as the British Isles and Poland. The same source reports it as the most frequent fungus associated with Aleppo pine in the Mediterranean region, and says the variety velatipes was originally found in Italy in poor acidic soil with Aleppo pine. It also reports that fruit bodies are not typically produced in young plantations. These statements come from a single source; iNaturalist copies Wikipedia and is not independent. No source supplied a fruiting season. *[wikipedia]*
