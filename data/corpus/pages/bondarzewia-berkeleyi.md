## Summary

Bondarzewia berkeleyi (Fr.) Bondartsev & Singer (1941), known in English as Berkeley's polypore, is a polypore in the family Bondarzewiaceae (Russulales). Sources describe it as a parasitic butt rot of oaks and other hardwoods. It forms a large rosette of overlapping caps, 20–50 cm or more across, at the base of the host tree. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Elias Magnus Fries described the species as Polyporus berkeleyi in 1851 and that it was moved to Bondarzewia in 1941. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) adds that Fries was studying an unnamed collection from North Carolina that he found in the herbarium of the English mycologist Miles Joseph Berkeley. Kuo reports that Fries called it the "most noble of all the polypores known to me" (1851, Kuo's translation). This detail comes from a single source. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as parasitic on hardwoods, especially oaks, causing a white, stringy butt rot. It is also saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods. It grows alone or gregariously at the bases of trees in summer and fall. Kuo gives the range as widely distributed in North America east of the Great Plains, occasionally reported from Mexico, with the original description from North Carolina. Wikipedia reports fruit bodies from July to October in the United States. It gives oak as the primary host and also records maple. It cites a North Carolina host survey in which the fungus almost always grew on oaks. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com are independent of each other; the other sources here copy Wikipedia. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
