## Summary

Hygrophorus hypothejus (Fr.) Fr. is a waxcap in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the herald of the winter or olive-brown waxy cap. Wikipedia describes it as having a slimy, yellowish to olive-brown cap and yellow decurrent gills, and appearing in late autumn in coniferous forests, often with the first frosts. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name is derived from the Ancient Greek words hypo and theios, glossed as "sulphur yellow underneath". *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the name "herald of the winter" arose because the mushroom appears in autumn with the onset of the first overnight frosts. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries described the species in 1821 as Agaricus hypothejus, then placed it in the genus Hygrophorus in 1838. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows in coniferous forests, appearing in October and November and occasionally December, often in large groups and clusters that can be hard to spot among pine needles. Mushroom World states that it grows scattered or in groups on soil under conifers, common under 2-needle pines, from late autumn to winter. Wikipedia describes it as native to Europe; Mushroom World lists its location as Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
