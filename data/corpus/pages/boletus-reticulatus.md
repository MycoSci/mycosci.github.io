## Etymology

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the British Mycological Society approved the name 'summer bolete' for Boletus reticulatus. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Jacob Christian Schäffer described the species as Boletus reticulatus in 1774 in his series on the fungi of Bavaria and the Palatinate. Jean-Jacques Paulet described it in 1793 as Le grand Mousseux (Tubiporus aestivalis). Elias Magnus Fries followed Paulet and used Boletus aestivalis in 1838. Schäffer's name took precedence, and both names were used in the literature for many years. Wikipedia places the species in Boletus section Boletus, alongside B. aereus, B. edulis and B. pinophilus. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species occurs in deciduous forests of Europe, forming a mycorrhizal relationship with oak (Quercus), and that it is less host-specific than other porcini mushrooms. It is reported from woods throughout Europe after hot and humid weather, from the start of summer until the end of autumn. Wikipedia names southern and western France and the Tosco-Emiliano Apennine in Italy as areas where it is particularly common. It also records the species in Ukraine, Crimea and several regions of Russia. It has been recovered in southern Africa, where Wikipedia says it was likely introduced, growing under Pinus patula. *[wikipedia]*
