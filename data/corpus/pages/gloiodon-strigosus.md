## Summary

Gloiodon strigosus (Sw.) P. Karst. (1879) is a fungus in the family Bondarzewiaceae, order Russulales. Wikipedia describes it as a wood-inhabiting species with a hydnoid (spined) hymenium that grows on dead wood. It reports the species from the boreal-hemiboreal zones of Europe, North America and Asia, and says it is considered rare in Europe, where it is mainly found in Fennoscandia. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia and Wikispecies, the species was first described by Olof Swartz in 1810 as Hydnum strigosum. Petter Adolf Karsten made the combination Gloiodon strigosus in 1879. Wikispecies lists later homotypic transfers to Leptodon (Quélet, 1886), Sclerodon (Karsten, 1889), Mycoleptodon (Patouillard, 1900), Steccherinum (Banker, 1906) and Pleurodon (Ricken, 1918). The supplied sources do not give the reasons for these transfers. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only supplied source with ecological detail, describes G. strigosus as a saprotrophic fungus on dead trunks of deciduous trees, causing white rot. The fruiting bodies usually grow on fairly hard wood of moss-covered trunks. In Europe it is mostly associated with Populus, but it is also found on other deciduous trees such as Alnus and Salix. Wikipedia says it favours older forest habitats with high humidity and a stable stand of deciduous trees. GBIF's distribution descriptions list Norway, Sweden and Vermont. No source supplied here states a fruiting season. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
