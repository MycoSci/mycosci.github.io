## Summary

Chromosera cyanophylla (Fr.) Redhead, Ammirati & Norvell (2012) is a species in the family Hygrophoraceae and the type species of Chromosera. Wikipedia gives its range as Europe and Asia and states that earlier North American reports belong to two other species. A 2014 study in Nova Hedwigia describes it as a rare fungus. *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_2]*

## Discovery and naming

A 2012 Mycotaxon paper notes that some authors had incorrectly cited as basionyms names that Fries treated in 1863 but had actually first published in 1861. Because of these citation errors the intended new combinations were not validly published, so the paper proposes them again as new. Chromosera cyanophylla is one of the combinations listed. *[lit_2]*

## Habitat and ecology

A 2014 Nova Hedwigia study reports it on decaying to strongly decayed fallen trunks and stumps of conifers, mostly Abies alba and Picea abies and rarely Pinus cembra. Wikipedia describes it as growing in small groups on conifer wood. The same study says it is known from the hemiboreal to boreal zone of Scandinavia, up to 500 m a.s.l. there, and from 350 to 2000 m a.s.l. in other parts of Europe. In the core of Central Europe it prefers mixed montane old-growth forests of Fagus, Abies and Picea, mostly long-protected nature reserves. In the Alps it is rarely found in montane to subalpine spruce woods, Green Alder scrubs with conifer dead wood brought down by avalanches, and subalpine Larch-Arolla Pine woods. A 2015 Czech Mycology inventory of Boubínský prales virgin forest (Czech Republic) lists it among several very rare fungi there. *[lit_1, wikipedia, lit_3]*
