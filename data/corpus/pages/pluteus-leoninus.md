## Summary

Pluteus leoninus (Schaeff.) P. Kumm. 1871 is a member of the Pluteaceae, known in English as the lion shield. Sources describe it as a wood-growing species with a bright tawny to olivaceous yellow cap and pale gills that turn pink as the spores ripen. It is recorded from Europe, North Africa, Asia and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name leoninus (meaning leonine) refers to the colour of the cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com reports that North American collections have traditionally been called Pluteus flavofuligineus. It cites preliminary DNA evidence (Justo et al., 2008) in support of using the name P. leoninus for them. Of four North American collections studied, two showed no molecular differentiation from European specimens. The other two represented a distinct molecular lineage without morphological differentiation, so the author suggests a cryptic species or two may remain to be found. Wikipedia notes that many authorities treat Pluteus fayodii as a synonym of P. leoninus, but that Species Fungorum treats them as distinct. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it occasionally grows on dead wood in Europe and North Africa, on stumps and wood debris of broad-leaved trees and sometimes of conifers. MushroomExpert.Com (describing North American material) calls it saprobic, growing alone or scattered on decaying hardwood logs and debris or terrestrially, causing a white rot. It gives the season as late spring, early summer and fall east of the Rocky Mountains, and over winter on the West Coast. GBIF description text lists the world distribution as Europe, Asia, North America and North Africa, and states that it occurs in all provinces of China. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
