## Summary

Caloboletus inedulis (Murrill) Vizzini, 2014 is a bolete in the family Boletaceae. Wikipedia describes it as native to North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a pale cap, a yellow pore surface that bruises blue, and a stem that is whitish to pinkish red, sometimes with a reticulum. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was known as Boletus inedulis until 2014. It was then transferred to Caloboletus, together with several related boletes including Caloboletus calopus, after changes in the phylogenetic framework of the Boletaceae. MushroomExpert.Com cites the original name to Murrill, 1938 and the current combination to Vizzini, 2014. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes the species as mycorrhizal with hardwoods (especially oaks) or with eastern hemlock, usually growing gregariously in summer and fall. It says the species is widely distributed in North America east of the Great Plains and is also documented from Central America. The collections that guide illustrates and describes are from Illinois and Indiana. A GBIF description record lists Vermont under distribution. The habitat details come from a single independent source. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
