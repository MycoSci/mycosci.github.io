## Summary

Stereum ostrea (Blume & T. Nees) Fr., 1838, is a crust fungus in the family Stereaceae (Russulales), known in English as false turkey-tail and golden curtain crust. It is described as a stemless, shell- or fan-shaped, zoned bracket with a smooth, pore-free underside, which is how it is told apart from true turkey tail (Trametes versicolor). GBIF records it from many countries, including the US, Australia, New Zealand, Japan and Brazil. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the name ostrea comes from the word 'oyster' and describes the fungus's shape. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com say the name 'false turkey-tail' arises because the fungus mimics Trametes versicolor (turkey tail). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the fungus is native to the island of Java, Indonesia. It also says the name has been misapplied to the North American species Stereum fasciatum, Stereum lobatum and Stereum subtomentosum. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the dead wood of hardwoods. It grows densely gregarious but usually individually, often from gaps in the bark, and causes a white rot of the heartwood. It is reported in spring, summer, fall and winter and is widely distributed in North America. Wikipedia likewise describes it as growing on hardwood bark, especially oak, and decaying parts, all year round, and gives Indonesia as its native range. A GBIF record from Colombia gives the substrate as dead wood ('Sobre madera muerta'). *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
