## Summary

Ischnoderma benzoinum (Wahlenb.) P. Karst. (1881), the Benzoin Bracket, is a polypore in the family Ischnodermataceae that has been recorded in many countries, mostly in Europe and North America. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as the conifer-associated counterpart of Ischnoderma resinosum: an annual polypore with a pore surface that bruises brown and a cap zoned in shades of brown. That page also gives it as stalkless, with a cap 4–15 cm across. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that North American mycologists generally treat this species and Ischnoderma resinosum as synonyms, and that its author was not aware of any contemporary molecular study addressing the question. It reports, citing Pouzar (1990), that the definitive morphological difference between the two is tube colour at maturity: brown in I. benzoinum, creamy to slightly brownish in I. resinosum. It also says I. benzoinum is usually a bit thinner than I. resinosum and has somewhat darker flesh. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the deadwood of conifers, annual, and causing a white rot. It grows alone, gregariously, or in overlapping clusters, and usually appears in fall. The same page says its North American distribution is uncertain because it is usually treated as Ischnoderma resinosum there, and that the illustrated and described collections are from Ohio and Pennsylvania. The GBIF description record lists a distribution of Belgium (Flemish and Walloon regions), Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
