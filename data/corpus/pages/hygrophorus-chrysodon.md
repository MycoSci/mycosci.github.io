## Summary

Hygrophorus chrysodon (Batsch) Fr., 1838, is a species in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the Gold Dust Waxy Cap or Gold-Flecked Woodwax. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a white, slimy waxy cap with yellow to golden granules along the cap margin and at the stem apex, and says the yellow can fade or spread over the whole cap with age. The same source reports a bright yellow reaction to KOH on the cap, stem or flesh, which it calls one of the best ways to identify the species. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet chrysodon is Greek for 'golden tooth', and that it refers to the gold-hued granules or hairs on the cap (especially near the edge), stipe and gills. This derivation comes from a single encyclopedia source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Reports differ by region and each rests on a single source. MushroomExpert.Com (North American collections, illustrated from Colorado and California) describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, rarely reported with hardwoods on the West Coast. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, in summer and fall, and over winter in warmer climates. A GBIF-hosted description from a European study describes it as gregarious or in small groups in mixed broadleaf-conifer forests, mainly with Abies and Fagus, below 2000 m elevation, appearing late summer to fall (August–November). Wikipedia gives a broader statement: on the ground, with moss, under conifers or other hardwood trees, throughout the Northern Hemisphere. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc, wikipedia]*
