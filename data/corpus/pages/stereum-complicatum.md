## Summary

Stereum complicatum (Fr.) Fr. 1838 is a crust fungus in the family Stereaceae (Russulales), recorded across many countries in GBIF. MushroomExpert.Com gives its distinguishing features as orange colors, a smooth underside lacking pores, and small, often fused fruiting bodies. It describes the species as very close in appearance to Stereum hirsutum, which it says is yellow and brown and slightly larger. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com lists Stereum rameale as a synonym. It states that S. complicatum, S. hirsutum, S. gausapatum, S. styracifluum and S. versicolor are members of the Stereum hirsutum group, considered forms or varieties of S. hirsutum by some mycologists. It also says specimens intergrading between classic S. complicatum and S. hirsutum are not uncommon. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the dead wood of hardwoods, especially oaks. It grows densely gregariously, often from gaps in the bark, with the fruiting bodies fusing together laterally, and causes a white rot of the heartwood. The same source gives the fruiting season as spring, summer, fall and winter, and says it is widely distributed in North America but apparently absent in the Rocky Mountains. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*
