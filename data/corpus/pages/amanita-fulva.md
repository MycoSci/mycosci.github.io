## Summary

Amanita fulva Fr. (1815), the Tawny Grisette, is a ringless member of the family Amanitaceae. Wikipedia describes it as found frequently in deciduous and coniferous forests of Europe and possibly North America. MushroomExpert.Com calls it a classic European species and says North American fulva-like collections are probably genetically distinct and currently unnamed. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described by Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1774. Wikispecies lists the basionym as Agaricus fulvus Schaeff. (1774), with the combination Amanita fulva made by Fries in 1815. Wikipedia also says that A. fulva and the grisette (A. vaginata) were historically placed in the genus Amanitopsis because they lack a ring. It calls that distinction now insufficient to warrant a separate genus and places the species in section Vaginatae following Bas. Wikipedia adds that the name A. fulva has previously been misapplied to other North American taxa, such as A. amerifulva. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as forming mycorrhizae, generally with oak, birch, spruce, pine, chestnut and alder. It says the species is often found with birch in Scandinavia, while collections from southern Europe are usually from oak, chestnut and pine forests. It also says the species grows in acidic soils and fruits from summer to late autumn (May to November in the United Kingdom). Mushroom World describes it as growing solitary or scattered on soil in mixed woodlands, favouring birch. MushroomExpert.Com gives the ecology as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously, in summer and fall, and widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains. That page also says the North American fulva-like entities are probably genetically distinct from the European species, so its ecology note may not describe A. fulva in the strict sense. Wikipedia likewise says North American collections may belong to a different, undescribed species. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, mushroomexpert]*
