## Summary

Psilocybe strictipes Singer & A.H. Sm. (1958) is a species in the family Hymenogastraceae, with Oregon given as its type locality in the catalog. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as growing in lawns and grassy meadows. Both also record it from the Pacific Northwest and Chile. ## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the epithet "strictipes" comes from the Latin words stricti (narrow) and pes (foot). This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

The species was described by Singer & A.H. Smith in 1958. Wikispecies cites the paper as "New species of Psilocybe", Mycologia 50(1): 141–142. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo, 2022) lists Psilocybe callosa in the sense of many authors, including Guzmán 1983, as a synonym. It also reports that Watling & Gregory (1987) speculate the species is often erroneously labeled as Psilocybe semilanceata. In the same account, Læssøe & Petersen (2019) are said to express doubt that the two species are truly distinct. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes growth in lawns and grassy fields, never directly from dung, with fruiting in late summer to fall. It lists Chile, England, Scotland, France, Germany, the Netherlands, Poland, Slovakia, Sweden, Siberia and the Pacific Northwest. MushroomExpert.Com (Kuo) describes it as saprobic and growing gregariously in lawns and meadows, or occasionally in forests. Kuo notes that the illustrated collection is from North Carolina and that it is also recorded from Chile. He says it is possibly distributed throughout Europe and North America, especially the Pacific Northwest. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
