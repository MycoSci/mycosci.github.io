## Summary

Trametes elegans (Spreng.) Fr. (1838) is a polypore in the family Polyporaceae, known in English as the white maze polypore. Wikipedia describes it as a common wood-decay fungus with a pantropical distribution on hardwood hosts. A description hosted on GBIF, which cites Ryvarden 2016, says it is easy to recognize because its hymenophore is irregular and can range from pores to lamellae. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia notes that the species has also been called Lenzites elegans and Daedalea elegans, and says it has recently been suggested to be a complex of three species: T. elegans, T. aesculi and T. repanda. This is a single-source statement, and the supplied sources do not say who made the suggestion or on what evidence. A GBIF-hosted description notes that Flora do Brasil 2020 lists the species as Lenzites elegans (Spreng.) Pat. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says it grows alone on dead wood, on hardwood hosts. It says the species prefers rotting wood and leaf litter in tropical forests, and that it is most common in tropical hardwood forests. It lists West Africa, Australia, New Zealand, Japan and the southern United States among the places where it occurs. A GBIF-hosted description citing Ryvarden 2016 says it grows on deciduous wood and is widespread and common in all tropical areas. The same description lists Brazilian records (from Flora do Brasil 2020, under the name Lenzites elegans) from Amazonas, Bahia, Mato Grosso, Pará, Paraná, Pernambuco, Rio Grande do Sul, Rondônia, Santa Catarina and São Paulo, and reports new records for Maranhão and Tocantins. It also lists a Colombian specimen from Ibagué, Tolima, collected at 1150 m a.s.l. Wikipedia and the iNaturalist entry are one independent source, and the GBIF description is the other. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
