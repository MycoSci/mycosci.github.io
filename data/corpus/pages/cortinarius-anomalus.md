## Summary

Cortinarius anomalus (Fr.) Fr., the variable webcap, is a member of the Cortinariaceae. Wikipedia describes a medium-sized mushroom with a grayish-brown cap up to 5 cm wide, gray-violet gills and a whitish stem with pale yellow belts below. MushroomExpert.Com notes that the name is probably applied to a large group of species worldwide, not a single one. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet anomalus is derived from the Latin word for "paradoxical". This is a single-source statement, and no other supplied source discusses the derivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus anomalus by Elias Magnus Fries, and Fries later transferred it to Cortinarius in 1838 in his Epicrisis Systematis Mycologici. Wikipedia adds that Friedrich Otto Wünsche placed it in Dermocybe as Dermocybe anomala. MushroomExpert.Com notes that Kauffman (1918) suggested Peck's Dermocybe simulans may represent this species. It adds that if North American members of the anomalus group prove phylogenetically distinct from the European species, Peck's name might return to use. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports it growing solitarily or in scattered groups on the ground in deciduous, mixed or, more rarely, coniferous forests. It says the fruit bodies appear late in summer and in autumn, and that the species occurs throughout the temperate zone of the Northern Hemisphere. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers, growing alone or gregariously in fall, and apparently widely distributed in North America. That source also says the wide range of reported hosts and ecosystems is a reason to suspect that several species are involved. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
