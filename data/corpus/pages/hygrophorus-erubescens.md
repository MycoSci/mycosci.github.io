## Summary

Hygrophorus erubescens (Fr.) Fr. is a member of the Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the blotched woodwax. Wikipedia describes a sticky, light pink to white cap that can be dotted with darker pink or red marks and bruises yellow. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as one of several Hygrophorus species that develop pink to reddish spots on the gills, and gives conifers as its preferred association. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name is derived from the Latin erubescens, meaning "reddening" or "blushing". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries described the species as Agaricus erubescens in his 1821 work Systema Mycologicum, and it became Hygrophorus erubescens when Hygrophorus was raised to genus rank. Wikipedia places it in subsection Pudorini of Hygrophorus, together with H. pudorinus and H. purpurascens. MushroomExpert.Com cites the authority as (Fries, 1821) Fries, 1838, and notes that Hesler & Smith (1963) described H. erubescens var. gracilis as a slender, long-stemmed variety. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species fruits from August to October in coniferous forests, particularly spruce, on chalky soils, singly or sometimes in large troops. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, growing scattered or gregariously in late summer and fall, or over winter in warmer climates. It says the species is apparently widely distributed in North America but much more common from the Rocky Mountains westward, and its illustrated collections are from Colorado and California. Wikipedia also reports that the species is classified as extinct in the British Mycological Society's 2006 list of threatened fungi, having not been documented in Great Britain since 1878. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
