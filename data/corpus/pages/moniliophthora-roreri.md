## Summary

Moniliophthora roreri (Cif.) H.C. Evans, Stalpers, Samson & Benny (1978) is a basidiomycete in the family Marasmiaceae. It causes frosty pod rot of cacao (Theobroma cacao), described as one of the most serious problems for cacao production in Latin America. It forms part of what is called the cacao disease trilogy, alongside witches' broom disease (caused by M. perniciosa) and black pod rot (caused by Phytophthora sp.). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to the English Wikipedia article, the species was first described as an anamorphic ascomycete, Monilia roreri Cif., because no recognizable fruiting body or sexual stage was known and it resembled species of Monilia. Later, dolipores and septal pore caps, which are features of basidiomycete fungi, were found in its septa, and the species was reclassified as Moniliophthora roreri. More recently, M. roreri and M. perniciosa were shown to be sister species within Marasmiaceae. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Only one independent source (English Wikipedia; the iNaturalist text is a copy of it) describes where this fungus occurs, and it treats the species as a pathogen of cacao pods. The main hosts named are plants in the Malvaceae genus Theobroma (T. cacao, T. gileri, T. bicolor and T. grandiflorum) and the closely related genus Herrania. Infection begins when conidia land on the surface of pods and penetrate directly through the epidermis. Spores are described as dry and powdery and are spread by water movement, wind, or movement of pods, with disease spread highest during periods of high rainfall. The same source says the disease was confined to Ecuador, Colombia and western Venezuela before the 1950s. It has since spread into Peru (1988) and Central America (Costa Rica 1978, Nicaragua 1979, Honduras 1997, Guatemala 2002, Belize 2004, Mexico 2005), and was confirmed in El Salvador (2009) and Bolivia (2012). *[wikipedia]*
