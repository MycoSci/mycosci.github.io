## Summary

Battarrea phalloides is a species of mushroom in the family Agaricaceae and the type species of the genus Battarrea. Wikipedia describes it as having a woody, shaggy or scaly stem up to about 40 cm tall, topped by a spore sac that holds a powdery rust-brown spore mass. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as looking like a puffball stuck on a long, shaggy spike. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet phalloides means phallus-like and refers to the similarity of the volva to the genus Phallus. It also states that the genus Battarrea was named after the Italian mycologist Giovanni Antonio Battarra. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first mentioned in the scientific literature in 1784, when Thomas Jenkinson Woodward described a new species found by Mr. Humphreys of Norwich. James Dickson named it Lycoperdon phalloides in 1785, with Suffolk, England as the type locality. Persoon transferred it to Battarrea in his 1801 Synopsis Methodica Fungorum. Wikipedia also records long-running uncertainty over whether the European B. stevenii is a separate species. MushroomExpert.Com treats B. stevenii as a synonym and says both names represent phylogenetically incoherent entities. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is found in dry, sandy locations and may grow solitary to scattered on dry, sandy hedgebanks. In Mexico it has usually been collected in arid and semiarid areas and on coastal dunes. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as presumably saprobic, growing alone or scattered in dry, sandy soil (coastal back dunes, deserts, sagebrush areas). It says fruiting is in spring and early summer, with bodies persisting for many months, and in fall or, in warm climates, over winter. In North America it is found primarily in arid regions and on the West Coast. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
