## Summary

Sistotrema brinkmannii (Bres.) J. Erikss. 1948 is a species in the family Hydnaceae, order Cantharellales, with GBIF occurrence records from many countries across Europe, the Americas, Asia and Oceania. Wikipedia describes it as a resupinate wood-rotting basidiomycete. It gives basidia of about 10–20 × 5–8 μm and spores of about 4–5 × 2–2.5 μm. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states the fungus is commonly called "chain chlamydospore fungus" because bulbils are formed by chains of its cells that resemble chlamydospores. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia (one independent source; iNaturalist copies it) reports the fungus from soil, moss, debris and rotten wood, including seedling roots of Pinus banksiana and ectomycorrhizae. It also reports the fungus in both outdoor and indoor air, and as a decayer of window joinery in houses. GBIF description records add a specimen from Ailaoshan National Nature Reserve, Yunnan, China, on a fallen angiosperm trunk in broad-leaved forest (collected 13 January 2018), and a substrate record of a leaf of Geonoma weberbaueri. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
