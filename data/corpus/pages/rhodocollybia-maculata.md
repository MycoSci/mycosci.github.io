## Summary

Rhodocollybia maculata (Alb. & Schwein.) Singer, 1939, is a species in the family Omphalotaceae, and GBIF holds occurrence records from many countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small to medium-sized mushroom with a whitish cap that develops cinnamon spots and stains with age, and very crowded, attached gills. It also notes that many varieties have been described on morphological grounds, the best known being var. scorzonerea, which has yellowish gills that are less crowded and a yellowish cap and stem. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, decomposing the deadwood or litter of conifers, fruiting in spring (in warmer climates), summer and fall, and widely distributed in North America; its described collections are from Illinois, Michigan and Québec. Wikipedia likewise says it is widespread in North America and often appears in decomposing conifer duff. A separate description in GBIF, of a collection from Jeollabuk-do, Republic of Korea, records it on soil in mixed forest under Carpinus laxiflora, Pinus densiflora and Quercus sp. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
