## Summary

Clavulinopsis aurantiocinnabarina (Schwein.) Corner, 1950, is a clavarioid fungus in the family Clavariaceae, known in English as orange spindle coral or orange worm coral. Wikipedia describes it as forming cylindrical, orange fruit bodies that grow on the ground in woodland litter. It was originally described from the United States and is part of a species complex that has not yet been resolved. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was originally described from Pennsylvania in 1832 by the American mycologist Lewis David de Schweinitz, and that English mycologist E.J.H. Corner placed it in the genus Clavulinopsis in 1950. MushroomExpert.Com lists Clavaria aurantiocinnabarina as a previous name and gives the same authorities and years (Schweinitz, 1832; Corner, 1950). According to Wikipedia, initial molecular research based on DNA sequences indicates that C. aurantiocinnabarina is part of a complex of related species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species occurs singly or in small clusters on the ground, is presumed to be saprotrophic, and typically grows in woodland. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as presumably saprobic, growing scattered, gregariously, or in loose clusters under hardwoods, often in low-lying areas, in summer and fall. Both sources say its distribution is uncertain because of confusion with similar, closely related species. Kuo says the North American distribution is probably limited to eastern North America and that it appears fairly common from roughly Ohio to Virginia and Connecticut. Wikipedia says the species complex is widespread in eastern North America and that C. aurantiocinnabarina sensu lato has also been reported from Central America and the Caribbean, Brazil, China, and Malaysia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
