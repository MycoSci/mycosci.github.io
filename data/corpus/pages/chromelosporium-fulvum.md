## Summary

Chromelosporium fulvum (Link) McGinty, Hennebert & Korf (1975) is a member of the Pezizaceae, order Pezizales. A 1990 review describes this "peat mould" as a cosmopolitan fungus commonly occurring in greenhouses and on mushroom beds, and discusses its detrimental effects on mushroom beds (lit_1). A 2012 Iranian report identifies it as the asexual form of Peziza ostrachoderma (lit_2), and a 2020 report describes it causing rot in houseplants (lit_3). *[lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*

## Habitat and ecology

Sources record it in greenhouses and on mushroom beds (lit_1). In Iran it was isolated from soil and sterile vermiculite in pots in a Shiraz University greenhouse (lit_2). In a 2019 houseplant outbreak, a red mycelium was seen on the pot substrate surface and on diseased Saintpaulia tissue, and similar symptoms were reported on geraniums and cyclamen (lit_3). Each of these is a single-source observation. *[lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*
