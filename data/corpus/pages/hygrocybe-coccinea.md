## Summary

Hygrocybe coccinea (Schaeff.) P. Kumm. (1871) is a waxcap in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as Scarlet Hood, Scarlet Waxcap or Scarlet Waxy Cap. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe a small scarlet mushroom with a sticky cap, thick, widely spaced gills and a white spore print. It is recorded from many countries across Europe and North America, among others. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet coccinea is Latin for "scarlet". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus coccineus by the German mycologist Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1774. It was later transferred to Hygrophorus by Elias Magnus Fries (Wikipedia gives 1838) and to Hygrocybe by Paul Kummer in 1871. This account rests on a single independent source. MushroomExpert.Com lists Hygrophorus coccineus as a synonym and cites Schaeffer 1774 and Kummer 1871. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as widespread in unimproved grasslands in Europe from August to October, and says that in western North America it may be found under redwoods or in mixed woodland in winter. Wikipedia also reports a record under Rhododendron and oak (Quercus) in Sagarmatha National Park, Nepal. It adds that specimens first identified as H. coccinea in Australia have been reclassified as H. miniata or H. kandora. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as growing scattered or gregariously in a variety of woodland ecosystems, in summer and fall or over winter in warmer climates. It says the precise ecological role is uncertain and that the illustrated collections are from California and Michigan. The grassland and woodland accounts come from different sources and regions and are not reconciled here. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
