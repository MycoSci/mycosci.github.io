## Summary

Boletopsis grisea (Peck) Bondartsev & Singer (1941) is a fungus in the family Bankeraceae, order Thelephorales. Wikipedia describes the fruit bodies as gray, fleshy polypores that grow on the ground in a mycorrhizal association with Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris), and reports the species from Europe and North America. The same source states that it is a threatened species in Europe, recorded from 15 countries and appearing on five Regional Red Lists. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as a species of Polyporus by the American mycologist Charles Horton Peck in 1874, from a type collection made at Copake, New York. Appollinaris Semenovich Bondartsev and Rolf Singer transferred it to Boletopsis in 1941. No supplied source gives the reasons for the transfer. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, the only independent source with habitat text, states that it grows on the ground on nutrient-poor acidic soils. It is mycorrhizal with Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris), with lichens and heath plants as understorey. It gives the range as Europe, Canada and the United States, with North American fruiting recorded August–October in the east and September–November in the west. *[wikipedia]*
