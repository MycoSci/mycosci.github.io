## Summary

Imleria badia (Fr.) Vizzini, 2014, the bay bolete, is a pored mushroom in the family Boletaceae. Wikipedia describes a chestnut to dark brown cap and yellowish pores that stain dull blue-grey when bruised. It is recorded in Europe and North America, and a second source, MushroomExpert.Com, also lists Mexico. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species name is the Latin adjective badia, meaning "chestnut brown", and that both the common and scientific names refer to the bay- or chestnut-coloured cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the common name derives from the colour of the cap, likened to the coat of a bay horse. It lists bay-brown bolete and bay-capped bolete as alternate common names of similar derivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Elias Magnus Fries first named the fungus in 1818 as Boletus castaneus ß badius, a subspecies of Boletus castaneus. He later treated it as a variety of B. castaneus in 1828 and then gave it species status. It was later moved through several genera: Rostkovites (Karsten, 1881), Viscipellis and Ixocomus (Quélet, 1886 and 1888), Suillus (Kuntze, 1898) and Xerocomus (Gilbert, 1931). Wikipedia says the stickiness of its wet cap distinguishes it from other Xerocomus species, so it was left in Boletus until Alfredo Vizzini placed it in its own genus, Imleria, in 2014. It also reports that genetic analysis published in 2013 relates the species to B. pallidus and B. glabellus in an informal 'badius clade'. Wikipedia also says the North American Boletus limatulus (Frost, 1874) was redescribed as a variety of this species by Wally Snell in 1945. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as common in coniferous and less commonly mixed woodlands in Europe. In the British Isles it gives the fruiting season as August to November. In North America it gives a range from eastern Canada west to Minnesota and south to North Carolina, fruiting July to November, and it also reports the species in central Mexico. Wikipedia says fruit bodies appear singly or scattered on the ground or on decaying tree stumps. It says they are commonly found under white pine, spruce and hemlock, and also under deciduous trees, especially beech, and that the species does not occur on calcareous soils. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo, 2023) independently describes it as mycorrhizal with conifers, especially pines and eastern hemlock, and sometimes reported under beech (especially in Europe) and birch. It says the fungus grows alone, scattered or gregariously, often from or near well-decayed, mossy stumps, in summer and fall. It gives the range as northeastern North America, the upper Midwest and the Appalachians, also Mexico and Europe. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
