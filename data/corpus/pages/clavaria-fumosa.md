## Summary

Clavaria fumosa Pers. is a coral fungus in the family Clavariaceae, known in English as smoky spindles. It forms unbranched, more or less cylindrical, off-white to grayish or dirty yellowish-pink clubs 2–14 cm tall, whose tips darken to reddish brown or black with age. Wikipedia describes it as common in Britain and Ireland and widely distributed in mainland Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the generic name comes from the Latin clava, meaning "club", and that the specific epithet fumosa means "smoky". *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a saprobic fungus growing on soil in unimproved grassland and in leaf litter along woodland edges, less common in dense woodland, usually in clusters, with solitary specimens rare; in Britain and Ireland fruiting bodies appear from June to November. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing in dense clusters, usually in woods and frequently among grass or moss, in summer and fall, in northeastern North America, the Great Lakes region and the southern Appalachians. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
