## Summary

Amanita onusta (Howe) Sacc. is a species in the family Amanitaceae, known in English as the gunpowder Lepidella or loaded Lepidella. Sources describe small to medium fruit bodies with a pale gray cap covered in darker gray warts or patches, a rooting stem base and a strong odor. It is recorded from eastern North America, from Nova Scotia to Mexico. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the Latin epithet onustus as meaning "charged, load-carrying, burdened", a regular adjective derived from onus, "burden". It also gives "loaded Lepidella" as a common name. This is a single-cluster claim. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was first described in 1874 by Elliot Calvin Howe as Agaricus onustus, and that Saccardo transferred it to Amanita in 1891. It adds that Cornelis Bas's 1969 monograph placed it in stirps Microlepis, subsection Solitariae, section Lepidellus. MushroomExpert.Com says it was originally described from "deeply shaded grassland" in Yonkers, New York. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Sources differ somewhat in detail. Wikipedia says it grows solitary or scattered on the ground in mixed oak, hickory and chestnut forests from southern New England to Texas, prefers sandy or loose soils, and ranges north to Nova Scotia and south to Mexico. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods or conifers in diverse ecosystems. His southern Appalachian collections were under eastern hemlock and his Midwestern collections under oaks. He reports it usually growing alone or scattered, from summer through fall, and widely distributed from northeastern North America to the Midwest and Jalisco. A GBIF description record lists a distribution in Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
