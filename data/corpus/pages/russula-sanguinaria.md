## Summary

Russula sanguinaria (Schumach.) Rauschert, the bloody brittlegill, is a bright blood-red Russula in the family Russulaceae that grows in association with coniferous trees and is described as inedible. It was previously widely known as Russula sanguinea. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that both specific epithets, sanguinaria and sanguinea, derive from the Latin word sanguis ('blood'), a reference to the mushroom's colour. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that it was first described as Agaricus sanguinarius by Heinrich Christian Friedrich Schumacher in 1803, and redescribed under its current binomial by Stephan Rauschert in 1989. It also notes that Agaricus sanguinea was described by Bulliard and renamed Russula sanguinea by Elias Magnus Fries. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it appears in summer and autumn, is widespread in the northern temperate zones, and is mycorrhizal with softwood trees, often Pinus (pine) in coniferous woodland, on sandy soils. *[wikipedia]*
