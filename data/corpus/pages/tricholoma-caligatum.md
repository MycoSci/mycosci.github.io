## Summary

Tricholoma caligatum (Viv.) Ricken, 1914, is a mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae. Wikipedia describes it as a large species with a distinct sheathing ring on the stem, found in mycorrhizal association with various trees in the Mediterranean. Application of the name to Central European and North American collections is controversial, and those collections likely represent different species. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the meaning of caligatum as Latin 'boot-wearing'. It says this refers to the mushroom looking as if it is wearing a boot, because of dark fibrils on the lower portion of the stem. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the species is sometimes called the European Matsutake because it allegedly tastes like the true matsutake (Tricholoma matsutake), which is prized in Japan. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was originally described in 1834 as Agaricus caligatus and was transferred to Tricholoma in 1914. Wikispecies gives the basionym as Agaricus caligatus Viv., I Funghi d'Italia (1834). Wikipedia notes considerable controversy over applying the name to Central European and North American collections, which likely represent different species. It cites Christensen & Heilmann-Clausen for the view that North American reports likely represent a different species. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) likewise says that what many North American authors have called T. caligatum is erroneously named, and that the true T. caligatum is a southern European and north African species associated with pines. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the species is regularly consumed along the Mediterranean coast. It says the species is highly valued in Cyprus, where it is considered a delicacy pickled and preserved in brine or vinegar. This is a single-cluster claim. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a strictly Southern species, locally common in Mediterranean woods. It says the species is associated with several conifers (Pinus pinea, P. brutia, P. halepensis, P. nigra and P. pinaster), and also with evergreen oaks, strawberry trees and terebinth bushes. It says the species appears in coastal and high-altitude woods in autumn and winter. MushroomExpert.Com describes the true T. caligatum as a southern European and north African species associated with pines. That source's own description covers North American hardwood-associated material that it says is not the true species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
