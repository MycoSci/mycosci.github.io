## Summary

Boletus pinophilus Pilát & Dermek (1973) is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the pine bolete. Wikipedia describes it as having a red-brown to maroon cap and a large, bulbous stipe covered with coarse reddish reticulation, and as forming ectomycorrhizal associations with pines and some other trees. Its range is given as Europe and western Asia, with introduced populations under exotic pine plantations elsewhere. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet as a mix of Latin pinus 'pine' and Ancient Greek philus 'loving'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Carlo Vittadini first recognised the pine bolete as a distinct taxon in 1835, describing it as Boletus edulis var. pinicola. Antonio Venturi raised it to species status as B. pinicola in 1863, and Pier Andrea Saccardo treated it as a variety of B. aestivalis in 1910. Albert Pilát and Aurel Dermek published the current name in 1973 as a replacement name, because B. pinicola as authored by Venturi was invalid, that name having been previously applied to the fungus now known as Fomitopsis pinicola. Wikispecies lists the same replaced synonym, Vittadini's 1835 name, and Pilát's 1973 nomen novum paper. Wikipedia adds that in 2008 western North American populations were formally described as a separate species, B. rex-veris. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that B. pinophilus forms ectomycorrhizal relationships with pines (Pinus), including stone pine, black pine, Corsican pine, cluster pine, Monterey pine, Scots pine and P. uncinata. It also names European silver fir (Abies alba) and European spruce (Picea abies) as hosts. It says the species prefers poor, acidic, sandy soils associated with coniferous forests and is not confined to conifers, and may also fruit under chestnut, oak and beech, and possibly birch and hornbeam. Fruiting bodies are described as occurring singly or in small groups through summer and autumn, and as early as April in Italy. Wikipedia also reports the species in exotic pine plantations in eastern North America, Mexico, Chile, South Africa and New Zealand. Mushroom World independently describes it as growing solitary or scattered on soil, particularly with Scots pine in Britain, on poor, acidic, sandy soils of coniferous forests. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*
