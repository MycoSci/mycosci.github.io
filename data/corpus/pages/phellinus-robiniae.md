## Summary

Phellinus robiniae (Murrill) A. Ames, 1913, is a polypore in the family Hymenochaetaceae. Index Fungorum treats Fulvifomes robiniae as the current name. Wikipedia gives the common name "cracked cap polypore". MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a tough, perennial bracket with a brown to blackish, cracked and furrowed cap and a brown pore surface. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was formerly placed in Phellinus and was moved to Fulvifomes when that genus was resurrected on morphological and molecular phylogenetic evidence. MushroomExpert.Com (2010) said it may more properly belong to Fulvifomes, citing Wagner & Fischer (2002), but that a formal transfer had not yet been made. The same page treats Phellinus rimosus, in the sense of some authors, as a synonym. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as parasitic on the heartwood of living trees in the genus Robinia (primarily black locust and New Mexican locust) and saprobic on their dead wood, causing a white rot. It grows alone or gregariously, is perennial, and is described as widely distributed where the host trees occur (below the Great Lakes, and in the Southwest). That page says it is occasionally reported on other hardwoods, and directs readers to Phellinus everhartii if the host is oak and to Phellinus weirianus if the host is walnut. Wikipedia says the fungus primarily infests black locust, aided by openings caused by Megacyllene robiniae infestation, but also grows on trees such as Carya, oak and Acacia. Wikipedia also says it is sympatric with most of its hosts. The two sources differ on oak: Wikipedia lists it as a host, while MushroomExpert.Com points oak-hosted material to a different species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
