## Summary

Infundibulicybe geotropa (Bull.) Harmaja, published in 2003, is a species in the family Tricholomataceae, known in English as the trooping funnel or monk's head. Wikipedia describes it as a large funnel-shaped mushroom with a sturdy cream or buff colour. It is recorded in GBIF occurrence data from many countries, chiefly in Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet derives from the Ancient Greek words γῆ (gē, "earth") and τρόπος (tropos, "turn"). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Pierre Bulliard first described the species as Agaricus geotropus in 1792. Lucien Quélet renamed it Clitocybe geotropa in 1872, and it was long known by that name. Harri Harmaja later proposed that I. geotropa and twelve other Clitocybe species be split off into a new genus, Infundibulicybe, which gives the current binomial. This account comes from a single independent source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is found in mixed woodlands, especially grassy clearings, in autumn. It is often gregarious and can form fairy rings. It is described as abundant and widespread in Europe (August to November) and less common in North America. One fairy ring at Belfort, eastern France, is reported to be over half a mile in diameter and is estimated at 800 years old. Wikipedia describes it as thought to be the largest known fairy ring. All of this is from one independent source; no host tree or substrate is specified. *[wikipedia]*
