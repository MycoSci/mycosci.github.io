## Summary

Simocybe centunculus (Fr.) P. Karst. 1879 is a species in the family Crepidotaceae, known in English as the Dingy Twiglet. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a tiny, olive to olive-brown mushroom with a cap of 1-2.5 cm, a brown spore print, and a centrally attached stem of 1-3 cm. That source says it is widely distributed and fairly common in North America but almost never treated in field guides. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com reports that DNA studies (citing Moncalvo et al., 2002 and Aime et al., 2005) placed Simocybe centunculus and the other members of Simocybe next to Crepidotus in the Crepidotaceae. It gives the naming reference as (Fries, 1821) Karsten, 1879. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the dead wood of hardwoods, often on stumps but also on fallen logs, and on old decorticated (barkless) wood as well as wood with bark still attached. It fruits in summer and fall, occasionally in late spring or in winter during warm spells, and is described as widely distributed in North America. The GBIF description record lists its distribution as Belgium (Brussels-Capital, Flemish and Walloon Regions), DK, and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
