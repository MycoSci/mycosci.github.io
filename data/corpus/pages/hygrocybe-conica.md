## Summary

Hygrocybe conica (Schaeff.) P. Kumm. 1871 is a waxcap in the family Hygrophoraceae. All parts of the fruit body blacken with age or bruising. Sources describe it as a widespread species that is probably a complex of several closely related taxa, recorded across Eurasia, North America and elsewhere. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the UK recommended English name 'blackening waxcap' was given because all parts of the basidiocarp blacken with age. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first described from Bavaria in 1774 by Jacob Christian Schäffer as Agaricus conicus, and that Paul Kummer transferred it to Hygrocybe in 1871. It also reports that molecular work confirms placement in Hygrocybe sensu stricto, but that the name is currently applied to at least eleven closely related but genetically distinct taxa. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) likewise doubts that all of these mushrooms are one phylogenetic species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultural and culinary history

A 2021 survey paper (IOP Conference Series) reports that, under the local name Kulat Siau, H. conica is foraged by some local people in Central Kalimantan, sold at the local market, and processed into various dishes. This is a single-source account from one region. *[lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

Sources give different habitats by region. Wikipedia reports summer and autumn fruiting and says that in Europe it is typical of waxcap grasslands, and is also found in dunes and road verges. It says that in North America it is often found in conifer woodland. MushroomExpert.Com describes Midwestern collections under hardwoods, especially oaks, with reports from other North American authors under conifers. It gives a season of early summer through fall, and winter in warmer climates. A 2021 study reports that in Central Kalimantan the mushroom is mostly harvested in secondary peat forest. A 2019 Hoehnea paper records var. conica from Atlantic Rain Forest, including seasonal semideciduous forest in Paraná, Brazil. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_1, lit_2]*
