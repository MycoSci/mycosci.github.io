## Summary

Rhizomarasmius pyrrhocephalus (Berk.) R.H. Petersen, 2000, is a member of the Physalacriaceae and the type species of Rhizomarasmius; it was formerly placed in Marasmius as Marasmius pyrrhocephalus. It is described as a small mushroom with an orange cap 1–2.5 cm wide and a tough, velvety-haired, dark stem that is usually deeply rooted, with a white spore print. Sources describe it as known mainly from eastern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus Rhizomarasmius was separated from Marasmius partly because of its rooting stipe, and that this is the reason for the prefix "rhizo". No supplied source discusses the derivation of the species epithet. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia says the genus Rhizomarasmius was split from Marasmius because of morphological differences such as the cystidia and the rooting stipe, with this species as its type species. MushroomExpert.Com says older sources treat the mushroom as a species of Marasmius, that Petersen (2000) suggested it deserves separate treatment, and that DNA results (Moncalvo and collaborators, 2002) upheld that suggestion. The same page lists Marasmius pyrrhocephalus, Marasmius longipes and Marasmius elongatipes as synonyms. This account rests on Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com only. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as known mainly from eastern North America, growing from May to October on leaf litter and wood. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on leaf litter and woody debris in hardwood forests, especially oak-hickory woods, growing alone or gregariously in spring, summer and fall, and widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains; it also quotes Gilliam (1975) that it is "one of the first agarics to appear in the spring and one of the last to cease fruiting in the fall". A GBIF description record lists a distribution of Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
