## Summary

Hypomyces lateritius (Fr.) Tul. & C. Tul. 1860, the ochre gillgobbler, is a parasitic ascomycete in the family Hypocreaceae that grows on species of Lactarius. Its whitish to buff coating covers the gills of the host mushroom and darkens to brick red or reddish brown when very old. It is recorded across North America and Europe. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com gives the species as originally described from Sweden by Fries in 1823, with the combination in Hypomyces credited to L. R. Tulasne & C. Tulasne in 1860. Wikipedia's synonym list gives the original name as Sphaeria lateritia Fries (1823) and lists several later synonyms. Wikipedia also notes that Hypomyces camphorati is sometimes treated as a separate species, that specimens on Lactarius camphoratus tend to have a more yellowish subiculum and slightly larger ascospores, and that more research is required to determine whether H. lateritius is a single species or a species complex. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Parasitic on Lactarius species. MushroomExpert.Com restricts the hosts to Lactarius in the Deliciosi section, while Wikipedia lists a broader set of hosts: L. camphoratus, L. chelidonium, L. controversus, L. deliciosus, L. indigo, L. rufus, L. salmonicolor, L. sanguifluus, L. semisanguifluus, L. tabidus, L. trivialis and L. vinosus. MushroomExpert.Com gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates. Wikipedia says it can be found wherever its Lactarius hosts occur: in North America from Alaska to Mexico, in Europe from the Iberian Peninsula to Ukraine, in Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Western Siberia, and reportedly in New Zealand and South Africa. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as widely distributed in Europe and throughout Canada, the United States and Mexico. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
