## Summary

Coprinopsis lagopus (Fr.) Redhead, Vilgalys & Moncalvo (2001) is a species of fungus in the family Psathyrellaceae, known in English as the hare's foot inkcap. Its fruit bodies are short-lived: they last only a few hours before the gills dissolve into a black ink, a process called deliquescence. It has been used for many decades as a model system for studying mushroom physiology and genetics. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the name 'harefoot mushroom' is due to the vague resemblance of the young fruit body to the paw of a white rabbit. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was known as Coprinus lagopus until 2001, when advances in understanding of phylogenetic relationships among the coprinoid species led to a major reorganization of that genus. Wikipedia also reports that the species varies greatly in size, and that this led some authors to name its dwarf fruit bodies as new species; it gives Coprinus radiatus, proposed by George Edward Massee, as an example. Both statements come from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

The two independent descriptive sources differ in emphasis. Wikipedia states that it grows solitarily or in groups in soil as well as on wood chips, compost heaps, vegetable refuse, horse dung, or cattle dung, from autumn to mid-winter, with a widespread distribution throughout the world. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously on decaying wood or woody debris in forests or, rarely, in urban settings. It sometimes appears terrestrial, but close inspection usually reveals lignin-rich soil containing woody material. Kuo gives the season as spring, summer and fall (or winter in California) and calls it widely distributed and common in North America. GBIF description records list distribution entries for Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
