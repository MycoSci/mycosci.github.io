## Summary

Cortinarius torvus (Fr.) Fr., 1838 is a species in the family Cortinariaceae (Agaricales), with the English name Stocking Webcap. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a variable lilac-brown cap, purplish and well-spaced gills, a white "boot" sheathing the stem that often forms a turned-over ring, and a strong, sickly sweet odor. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the name was originally applied to material from Europe's beech forests. It adds that in North America there seem to be at least two mushrooms that potentially qualify as Cortinarius torvus, so the name may cover more than one species there. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (the only source with ecological text) describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, including beech and both red and white oaks, growing scattered to gregariously in spring, summer and fall, and probably widely distributed in eastern North America. It distinguishes two North American forms: a northern beech-maple associate that appears in fall, and a more southerly oak-associated form with a more unpleasant odor that appears in spring and summer. The name was originally applied to European beech forest material. A GBIF description record lists the distribution as Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
