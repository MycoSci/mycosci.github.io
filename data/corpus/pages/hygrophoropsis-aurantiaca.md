## Summary

Hygrophoropsis aurantiaca (Wulfen) Maire ex Martin-Sans, the false chanterelle, is a fungus in the family Hygrophoropsidaceae (order Boletales). Franz Xaver von Wulfen described it in 1781. It is a yellow–orange, funnel-capped mushroom with thin, forked gills, and it has been recorded across many countries in Europe and North America. Sources disagree about its edibility; see the disagreements section. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet as the Latin word aurantiacus, meaning "orange". It says the genus name refers to a resemblance to the genus Hygrophorus. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Wulfen described the species as Agaricus aurantiacus in 1781, from fir forests around Klagenfurt in October. James Sowerby later illustrated it as Agaricus subcantharellus. Johann Friedrich Gmelin placed it in Merulius in 1792, and Elias Fries placed it in Cantharellus in 1821. Bernhard Studer-Steinhäuslin concluded in 1900 that it could only be classified in Clitocybe, citing its white spores, decurrent gills and lack of a ring. It was raised to genus level in a 1929 publication by Emile Martin-Sans, with authorship attributed to René Maire. All of this comes from a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that in Europe and North America it grows in both hardwood and conifer forests and in heathland, in summer and autumn. It fruits from the ground or from decaying wood, on burned areas in forests, and sometimes on woodchips used in gardening and landscaping. Wikipedia also describes it as a saprophyte that causes a brown rot on wood. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as saprobic on forest litter and woody debris, usually under conifers and occasionally on well-rotted wood. It is found alone, scattered or gregarious, in summer and fall (and over winter in warm climates), and is widely distributed in North America. Kuo's described collections are from Michigan, Ohio and Québec. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
