## Summary

Tropicoporus linteus (Berk. & M.A. Curtis) L.W. Zhou & Y.C. Dai (2015) is a member of the Hymenochaetaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a tropical American mushroom and notes that its former name Phellinus linteus has been applied more widely, including to an East Asian mushroom. A GBIF-hosted taxonomic treatment reports that, among Brazilian Tropicoporus, it differs mostly by its pileate basidiomata, the absence of binding-like hyphae in the context, and the presence of setae. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Polyporus linteus was named by Miles Joseph Berkeley and Moses Ashley Curtis and first reported from a specimen from Nicaragua in 1860. Shu Chün Teng renamed it Phellinus linteus in 1963, and Li-Wei Zhou and Yu-Cheng Dai renamed it Tropicoporus linteus in 2015. The same source says the name Phellinus linteus was later applied to several different mushrooms: in subtropical South America, records on Cordia americana are actually Tropicoporus drechsleri, and an East Asian mushroom has also carried the name. This account rests on a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that Tropicoporus linteus grows on oak and tamarind and is known from Nicaragua, the United States (Florida) and Brazil. A GBIF-hosted taxonomic treatment lists Brazilian specimens from Alagoas, Bahia, Ceará and Piauí states, all recorded as collected on dead wood. No supplied source gives a season. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
