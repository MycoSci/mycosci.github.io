## Summary

Coprinopsis marcescibilis is a species of agaric fungus in the family Psathyrellaceae. It was first described by Max Britzelmayr in 1893 as Agaricus marcescibilis, and the current combination in Coprinopsis dates to 2008 (Örstadius & E. Larsson). Wikipedia describes it as small and saprobic, with a grey-brown cap 10–25 mm across that fades to cream or whitish as it dries. *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia and iNaturalist (whose text is a declared derivative of Wikipedia, so these count as one source) state that Max Britzelmayr described the species in 1893 as Agaricus marcescibilis. iNaturalist adds that Rolf Singer moved it to Psathyrella in 1951; the Wikipedia text supplied does not repeat that date. Wikipedia explains that molecular phylogenetics showed the traditional genus Coprinus to be polyphyletic, and that Redhead and colleagues (2001) restricted Coprinus and segregated the remaining taxa into Coprinus sensu stricto, Coprinopsis, Coprinellus and Parasola. In a multilocus rDNA (ITS and LSU) phylogeny, Larsson and Örstadius (2008) found Agaricus marcescibilis within the Coprinopsis clade. Wikipedia says it shared that genus's short-celled, cutis-type pileipellis and lack of clamp connections, and on this basis they proposed the new combination Coprinopsis marcescibilis. *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia, which is the only source with habitat text, describes it as a saprobic fungus occurring singly or in small groups on nutrient-rich soils and on decaying wood or bark, especially in urban green spaces. It cites one collection, made in late July along a sidewalk edge in Szczytnicki Park, Wrocław, Poland, from fertile soil mixed with decaying pine wood and bark. Wikipedia calls the distribution broad but scattered: Europe (including Poland, Germany and the Czech Republic), North America and North Africa (Morocco), and Asia, with the first Korean record in 2008. The GBIF descriptions record lists Denmark, Norway and Sweden. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
