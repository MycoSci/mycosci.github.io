## Summary

Heliocybe sulcata (Berk.) Redhead & Ginns 1985 is the type and sole species of the genus Heliocybe, in the family Gloeophyllaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a very small gilled mushroom with a distinctively pleated (sulcate) cap, growing on decaying wood and associated with a brown rot. The same source calls it fairly widely distributed in North America but rarely collected or reported. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the genus name Heliocybe derives from the Greek helios (sun) and cybe (head), meaning "the sun-head", and was coined for the sun-like pattern on the cap and the species' affinity for sun-baked habitats. MushroomExpert.Com says the species epithet sulcata comes from the cap being "sulcate" (ridged and grooved). *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that in older classifications the species was known as Lentinus sulcatus or Panus fulvidus, and that phylogenetic evidence supports placing the brown-rot genera Neolentinus, Heliocybe and Gloeophyllum in an order separate from the Polyporales, where Lentinus and Panus are classified. According to Wikipedia, Heliocybe has also been placed in synonymy with Neolentinus, but other authors keep it distinct. MushroomExpert.Com lists Lentinus sulcatus, Neolentinus sulcatus and Pleurotus sulcatus as synonyms. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Sources describe the habitat differently. Wikipedia says the species typically fruits on decorticated, sun-dried and cracked wood, such as fence posts and rails, vineyard trellises in Europe, branches in slash areas, sagebrush or naio branches in the rain-shadow areas of Hawaii, or in open pine forests. It says that in North America it occurs in the Mountain states and as far east as Texas and Kansas, from April to September. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic on the well-decayed wood of hardwoods (especially quaking aspen), causing a brown rot, growing alone or in small groups in summer and fall. It says the species appears more common from the Rocky Mountains southward into Mexico, and that its described collection is from Minnesota. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
