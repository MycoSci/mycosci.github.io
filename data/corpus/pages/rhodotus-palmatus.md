## Summary

Rhodotus palmatus (Bull.) Maire, the wrinkled peach, is a saprobic mushroom in the family Physalacriaceae. Wikipedia describes it as an uncommon species with a circumboreal distribution. It says declining European populations have put it on over half of the European fungal Red Lists of threatened species. MushroomExpert.Com describes a rubbery, peach-to-orange cap that is often marked with a network of whitish ridges and veins. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet comes from the Latin palmatus, meaning "shaped like a hand", and suggests it may refer to the resemblance of the cap surface to the lines in a palm. This is a single-source account, and Wikipedia itself hedges it with "possibly". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus palmatus by Jean Bulliard in 1785, and Fries later included it under that name in his Systema Mycologicum. René Maire transferred it to the newly described genus Rhodotus in 1926. Wikipedia also says Claude Gillet called it Pleurotus subpalmatus in 1876, and that a 1986 paper treated Pleurotus pubescens (Peck, 1891) as a synonym. Wikipedia reports that placement of the genus at family level was disputed, with proposals including Amanitaceae, Tricholomataceae and Entolomataceae, before molecular phylogenetics placed it in the Physalacriaceae. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes R. palmatus as saprobic, growing scattered or in small clusters on rotting hardwoods such as basswood, maple and especially elm, and on horse chestnut in Europe. It says the species prefers low-lying logs in periodically flooded, shaded areas, and often grows on dark-stained, relatively undecayed wood, especially the dried-out upper parts of trunks that have lost their bark. Wikipedia gives the fruiting season as spring to autumn in the United States and autumn to winter in Britain and Europe. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently describes it as saprobic on the wet, well-decayed wood of hardwoods, growing alone, scattered, or in troops or loose clusters, from late spring through fall. It reports the species as widely distributed in North America east of the Great Plains, and also in Europe and Asia, with the illustrated collections from Illinois. Kuo adds that he finds it most often in stream beds and lowlands. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
