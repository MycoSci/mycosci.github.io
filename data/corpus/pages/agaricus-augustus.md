## Summary

Agaricus augustus Fr. (1838), known as The Prince, is a species in the family Agaricaceae, recorded in GBIF occurrence data across Europe, North America and other regions. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a large agaric with a scaly brown cap, a ringed stem and a strong almond-like odour. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet augustus is a Latin adjective meaning noble. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as saprotrophic and terrestrial, fruiting on humus-rich soil in deciduous and coniferous woods, in gardens and on roadside verges, and also on disturbed ground. It gives the range as Europe, North Africa, Asia and North America, with fruiting in late summer and autumn in Europe and from April to December in North America. MushroomExpert.Com, describing a British Columbia collection, calls it saprobic and says it grows scattered or gregariously under conifers, usually along roadsides or paths or in lawns and gardens. It gives the season as summer and fall, or winter and spring in warmer climates, and says the species is fairly common in California and the Pacific Northwest. A separate 2025 record (lit_2) describes it as ectomycorrhizal with Areca catechu and Delonix regia in scrub jungles of southwest Karnataka. That is a single-source claim and differs from the saprotrophic description above. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, lit_2]*
