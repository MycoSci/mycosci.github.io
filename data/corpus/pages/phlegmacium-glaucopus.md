## Summary

Phlegmacium glaucopus (Schaeff.) Wünsche, 1877, is a species in the family Cortinariaceae, commonly known as the blue-foot webcap. GBIF holds occurrence records from many countries, including Norway, the United States, Switzerland and France. Wikipedia describes the species as relatively difficult to identify. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus glaucopus by Jacob Christian Schäffer in 1774, and was reclassified as Cortinarius glaucopus in 1821 by the British mycologist Samuel Frederick Gray. Wikipedia says its taxonomic history is complicated by numerous proposed variants and forms. It places the species in Cortinarius subgenus Phlegmacium, section Glaucopodes, and says a 2022 genomic study reshaping the family Cortinariaceae led to its transfer to the genus Phlegmacium. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that in Tlaxcala, Mexico, the species is collected in June and sold in the market. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species appears from August onwards into autumn in deciduous and coniferous forests, often in profuse numbers, and can be found in fairy rings. It says the species occurs in Western North America (United States and Canada) and is common in the Rocky Mountains, rare east of the Great Plains, and rare in the British Isles. A GBIF-hosted description, based on a collection from Xinjiang, China, found in a forest with Picea, gives the habitat as solitary to gregarious on soil in coniferous or deciduous forests, in summer. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
