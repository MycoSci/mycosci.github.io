## Summary

Retiboletus ornatipes (Peck) Manfr. Binder & Bresinsky is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, known in English as the ornate-stalked bolete. It was originally named Boletus ornatipes and was transferred to Retiboletus in 2002. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as having a yellow, prominently reticulate stem and a yellow to brown cap, with surfaces that do not bruise blue. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species epithet ornatipes means "ornate-stemmed". *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was originally named Boletus ornatipes by the American mycologist Charles Horton Peck in 1878 and was transferred to Retiboletus in 2002. Wikispecies also lists Suillus ornatipes, attributed to Kuntze in 1898, as a synonym. According to MushroomExpert.Com, DNA results from Binder & Bresinsky (2002) supported placing it, along with Retiboletus griseus and a few other mushrooms, in a separate genus. That study also found support for at least three ornatipes-like species. MushroomExpert.Com adds that, according to Zeng and collaborators (2016), the northern ornatipes-like species is the true Retiboletus ornatipes. *[wikipedia, wikispecies, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods, especially oaks. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, often in moss, in summer and fall, and is widely distributed in North America east of the Great Plains. Wikipedia separately reports it under oak and beech in eastern North America from July to September. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
