## Summary

Disciotis venosa (Pers.) Arnould, 1893, is a cup fungus in the family Morchellaceae, and a close relative of the morels. MushroomExpert.Com says this relationship is not apparent without a microscope or DNA sequencing. Wikipedia describes brown, brittle cups that flatten with age and develop vein-like folds on the inner surface. It is recorded from Europe and North America. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet venosa means "veined" and refers to the veins on the inner cup surface. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the fungus was first described as Peziza venosa by Christian Hendrik Persoon in 1801, from collections made near Klagenfurt, Austria. MushroomExpert.Com likewise gives Persoon 1801 and an Austrian origin. Wikipedia also reports that Bruno Perco described the form Disciotis venosa f. radicans from collections made in Italy. MushroomExpert.Com notes that no contemporary DNA-informed study of Disciotis collections has been published. It therefore says it is uncertain whether European and North American material is the same phylogenetic species or several species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports growth on the ground among mossy or needle-covered soil around conifers. It also reports a preference for banks and slopes over flat ground. It says fruit bodies typically appear around the edges of melting snowbanks, and that in North America it fruits between April and June and in Europe typically from March to May. MushroomExpert.Com describes the ecology as uncertain, possibly saprobic and/or at least facultatively mycorrhizal. It says the fungus is found primarily under hardwoods, alone, scattered, or gregariously, in spring and usually during morel season. It describes the species as widely distributed in Europe and North America, less common or absent in the southern areas of both continents, and also documented from India. The two sources differ on whether conifers or hardwoods are the usual associate; each statement is a single-source claim. MushroomExpert.Com's illustrated collections are from Illinois, Michigan and Ohio. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
