## Summary

Gymnopus dryophilus (Bull.) Murrill, 1916, is a species in the family Omphalotaceae, long known as Collybia dryophila. Sources describe it as a small, very common woodland mushroom of Europe and North America. It has a tan to reddish-brown cap, crowded whitish gills and a white spore print. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the Greek epithet dryophilus as meaning "lover of oak trees". MushroomExpert.Com likewise translates the name as "oak-loving". Both note that the fungus also grows with other trees. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both record that the species was until recently most frequently known as Collybia dryophila. MushroomExpert.Com cites the name as (Bulliard, 1790) Murrill, 1916. Wikipedia says it has been contended that G. dryophilus is a complex of several species, and that G. brunneolus, G. earleae and G. subsulphureus should be split from it, but these are not generally recognized at present. MushroomExpert.Com describes mating studies that found at least 11 interbreeding groups in the "Collybia dryophila complex" and proposed separating at least five named species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as very common in Northern Hemisphere temperate woodlands, generally saprophytic but occasionally attacking living wood. It says the fungus fruits from April to December, grows in arcs and fairy rings in oak and pine woods, and also grows in clusters on wood chip mulch. Despite the "oak-loving" epithet, Wikipedia says it is also found with other broad-leaved trees and with conifers. MushroomExpert.Com (a single field guide) describes it as saprobic, growing alone, scattered, gregariously or loosely clustered from litter or twigs in almost any hardwood, conifer or mixed forest in North America. It fruits in spring, summer and fall, and in winter in warmer climates. GBIF descriptive records list the habit as terrestrial and the substrate as leaf litter. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
