## Summary

Tylopilus plumbeoviolaceus, known in English as the Violet Gray Bolete, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae, published as (Snell & E.A. Dick) Snell & E.A. Dick in 1941. Sources describe the fruit bodies as violet when young, fading to brown at maturity, with a white pore surface that turns pinkish. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states the specific name is coined from the Latin adjectives plumbeus ('leaden' or 'lead-colored') and violaceus ('purple'). *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the taxon was first named in 1936 as Boletus felleus forma plumbeoviolaceus by Walter H. Snell and his graduate student Esther A. Dick, from specimens found in the Black Rock Forest near Cornwall, New York. The first collections were young specimens that yielded no spores. Later mature fruit bodies showed that the rosy pore color took time to develop. Snell and Dick raised it to species rank as Boletus plumbeoviolaceus in 1941, citing differences in physical characters and spore size from B. felleus. Rolf Singer transferred it to Tylopilus in 1947. Wikispecies gives the same type locality. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as a mycorrhizal species whose fruit bodies grow singly, scattered or clustered from mid-summer to autumn in deciduous forests, often under beech or oak, and sometimes in mixed hardwood-conifer forests under hemlock. It notes a preference for sandy soil. MushroomExpert.Com (a separate source) describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks, growing scattered or gregariously in summer and fall, and widely distributed east of the Rocky Mountains. Its illustrated collections come from Missouri, Kentucky, Ohio and Georgia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
