## Summary

Brevicellicium olivascens (Bres.) K.H. Larss. & Hjortstam is a crust-forming (corticioid) fungus in the family Hydnodontaceae, order Trechisporales. Wikipedia describes it as forming thin, olive-tinted patches only 0.1–0.3 mm thick on decaying wood. The same source reports it from Europe, North America, Asia and Oceania, and it is most frequently recorded in Europe. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

A single source, Wikipedia, glosses the epithet olivascens as 'becoming olive', referring to the olive-tinged colour of the fresh crust. The same source glosses the genus name Brevicellicium as 'short cells' in its lead section and as referring to the short basidia in its taxonomy section. No second independent source discusses the derivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Giacomo Bresadola first described the species in 1892 as Odontia olivascens, from specimens on decayed wood at Trento, Italy. In 1978 Karl-Henrik Larsson and Erik Hjortstam studied Bresadola's fungus and transferred it to their new genus Brevicellicium, of which it is the type species. This account comes from one source (Wikipedia); no primary literature was supplied. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as saprotrophic on decaying wood of broadleaf trees and woody vines, appearing as a patch on the underside of decaying wood. The same source gives Trento, Italy as the site of Bresadola's original specimens, records it widely across Europe from Italy and France up to Scandinavia, and reports identification in North America, Asia and Oceania in forest surveys. A GBIF distribution record lists Belgium (including the Brussels-Capital and Flemish regions), Denmark, Norway, Sweden, the Azores islands of Flores, Pico and São Miguel, and Vermont.
