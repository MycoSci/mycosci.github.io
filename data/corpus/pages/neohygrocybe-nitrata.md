## Summary

Neohygrocybe nitrata (Pers.) Kovalenko (1989) is a species of gilled mushroom in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the nitrous waxcap. According to Wikipedia, it has a European distribution and occurs mainly in agriculturally unimproved grassland. The same source reports that it has been assessed as globally "vulnerable" on the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species because of threats to its habitat. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the recommended English name "nitrous waxcap" is based on the species' smell, and describes the basidiocarps as having a distinctly nitrous smell. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first described in 1801 by Christiaan Hendrik Persoon as Agaricus nitratus. Josef Herink transferred it to Neohygrocybe in 1958, but that combination was not validly published. Alexander Kovalenko validly combined it in 1989. The same source says recent molecular work (cladistic analysis of DNA sequences) has confirmed it as a distinct species that does not belong in Hygrocybe sensu stricto. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as widespread but generally rare throughout Europe. It says that, like other waxcaps, it occurs in old, agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns). It also notes that recent research suggests waxcaps in general are neither mycorrhizal nor saprotrophic but may be associated with mosses. This is a statement about waxcaps as a group, not specifically about this species. All of this comes from a single independent source; no season, host or substrate is stated. *[wikipedia]*
