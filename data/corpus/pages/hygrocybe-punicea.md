## Summary

Hygrocybe punicea, the crimson waxcap, is a gilled mushroom in the family Hygrophoraceae, described by Fries and transferred to Hygrocybe by Kummer in 1871. Wikipedia describes it as mainly European and typical of unimproved grassland, and assessed as globally vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as probably the largest common Hygrocybe in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the Latin "puniceus" as meaning "blood red". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described in 1821 by Elias Magnus Fries as Agaricus puniceus, and Paul Kummer transferred it to Hygrocybe in 1871. Wikipedia also states that molecular work has confirmed its placement in Hygrocybe sensu stricto. MushroomExpert.Com lists Hygrophorus puniceus as a synonym. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states the species is widespread but generally rare in Europe, occurring like most European waxcaps in old, agriculturally unimproved, short-sward grassland (pastures and lawns). It says records from East Asia, North America and Australia need further research to establish whether they are the same species, and that North American material is described as occurring in woodland. MushroomExpert.Com (single source for this) describes the North American ecological role as uncertain, often under coast redwood on the West Coast and under beech and hemlock in eastern North America, growing scattered or gregariously in summer and fall, or over winter in warmer climates. Wikipedia reports recent research suggesting waxcaps are neither mycorrhizal nor saprotrophic but may be associated with mosses. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
