## Summary

Lepista nuda (Bull.) Cooke (1871), the wood blewit, is a species in the family Tricholomataceae. Index Fungorum lists its current name as Collybia nuda, and Wikipedia reports that the species was reassigned to Collybia in 2023. GBIF holds occurrence records from a wide range of countries, including many in Europe as well as the US, Australia, Japan and Brazil. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia (the only supplied source that tells this history), Pierre Bulliard described the species in Herbier de la France in 1790 as Agaricus nudus. English naturalist James Bolton gave it the name Agaricus bulbosa in 1791. In 1871 Paul Kummer placed it in Tricholoma, and in the same year Mordecai Cubitt Cooke placed it in Lepista. Some authors then accepted Lepista while others kept the species in Tricholoma. In 1969 Bigelow and Smith reviewed Lepista and reclassified it as a subgenus of Clitocybe, and Harri Harmaja has called for Lepista to be sunk into Clitocybe. A 2015 genetic study found Collybia, Lepista and the core Clitocybe clade to be closely related but polyphyletic, and found that the wood blewit is not closely related to the type species of Lepista, L. densifolia. Wikipedia says the species was reassigned to Collybia in 2023. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as saprotrophic on decaying leaf litter and found in both coniferous and deciduous woodlands. In the United Kingdom it reports fruiting from September through December. Wikipedia also cites a fairy ring under Norway spruce (Picea abies) and Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris) in southeast Sweden. It says that in Australia, where the species appears to have been introduced, it has developed a relationship with some eucalyptus species and gorse. Mushroom World, an aggregator, describes it as growing in trooping groups, often in rings, in leaf litter in deciduous and mixed woodland and under hedgerows during autumn and winter. A GBIF description record documents a collection from Tolima, Colombia, at 3000 to 3100 m a.s.l., made on 25 Nov 2005. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, gbif_desc]*
