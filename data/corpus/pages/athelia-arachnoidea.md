## Summary

Athelia arachnoidea (Berk.) Jülich (1972) is a corticioid fungus in the family Atheliaceae. Wikipedia describes it as forming thin, white, cobwebby basidiocarps and typically occurring saprotrophically on leaf litter and fallen wood, though it can also be a facultative parasite of lichens. Its asexual state, Fibularhizoctonia carotae, is a plant pathogen that causes "crater rot" of stored carrots. *[wikipedia, lit_1]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Corticium arachnoideum Berk., published in Ann. Mag. Nat. Hist. 13: 345 (1844). It lists Jülich's 1972 monograph of the Athelieae (Willdenowia Beiheft 7) as the primary reference for the combination in Athelia, and it lists Terana arachnoidea (Kuntze, 1891) among the synonyms. A 1996 Mycologia paper reported that A. arachnoidea is the sexual state of Rhizoctonia carotae, the carrot cold-storage pathogen. The relationship was confirmed by ITS sequence data. The paper proposed the genus Fibularhizoctonia for Rhizoctonia species with clamp connections. None of the supplied sources says why the fungus was transferred between genera. *[wikispecies, lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as typically saprotrophic on leaf litter and fallen wood, and also as a facultative parasite of lichens. A 1996 Mycologia paper reports a collection made during winter on dead leaves of deciduous trees in Oregon. Wikispecies lists it as a lichenicolous fungus on epiphytic lichens and algae, recorded in the Sonora area over the thallus of Physcia aipolia. It gives the world distribution as Europe, northern Africa (Tunisia) and North America. A 1988 study in the Sheffield area followed its lesions on the lichen Lecanora conizaeoides over two years. It found the fungus active only during the winter months. Each of these descriptions comes from a single source or study, and they have not been cross-checked against one another. *[wikipedia, lit_1, wikispecies, lit_2]*
