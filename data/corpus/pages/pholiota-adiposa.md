## Summary

Pholiota adiposa (Batsch) P. Kumm. is a species in the family Strophariaceae. Wikipedia describes it as a slimy, scaly, yellow-brown mushroom that fruits in clusters on wood, and MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a yellow, slimy mushroom that grows in clusters on deadwood. MushroomExpert.Com treats it as an aggregate species group because attempts to separate species within it have given varying results. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the name adiposa comes from the slimy, oily appearance of the cap. This is the only supplied source that discusses the derivation. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that August Batsch originally described the species in 1786 as a member of Agaricus, and that Paul Kummer transferred it to Pholiota in 1871. MushroomExpert.Com gives the type locality as Schöndorf, Germany, and says adiposa is the oldest name in its group. It also says that morphological, mating-compatibility and DNA studies have given varying results on whether P. limonella and P. aurivella are separate from P. adiposa. It calls a 2021 DNA-based study (Tian & Matheny) one that found specimens identified as adiposa, limonella and aurivella from across the globe to be the same phylogenetic species. On that basis it suggests treating the group as an aggregate for now. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states that P. adiposa is commercially cultivated and that there is interest in improving cultivated yields. It reports that in growth media the fungus grows at 10–30 °C (optimum 25 °C) and pH 5–9 (best at pH 6), with mycelial growth severely suppressed below 5 °C or above 35 °C. No cultivation method is described in the supplied sources. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the fungus grows in clusters, either parasitically on live tree stems or as a saprotroph on dead wood. It gives beech species, poplar and willow as substrates. It says fruiting occurs from August to November, most commonly in September and October, and that the fungus grows above ground. It reports documentation in North America, Europe and Asia. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and possibly parasitic, growing alone or more often in clusters on deadwood, frequently on birch and alder. It is also documented on a wide variety of hardwoods and occasionally on conifers. That source gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in warm climates, and describes the species group as widespread across Europe, Asia, Oceania, North America, South America and Africa. Its illustrated collections are from Illinois, Kentucky and Missouri. iNaturalist, a declared derivative of Wikipedia, restricts its statement to Europe and European beech. The GBIF description record lists distribution only as Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, inaturalist, gbif_desc]*
