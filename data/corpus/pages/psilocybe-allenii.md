## Summary

Psilocybe allenii (Borov., Rockefeller & P.G. Werner, 2012) is an agaric in the family Hymenogastraceae, described from material collected in Seattle, Washington. Wikipedia reports it from northwestern North America, from British Columbia to Los Angeles, California, most commonly within 10 miles of the Pacific coast. It grows on rotting wood, especially landscaping wood chips. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Etymology

According to Wikipedia, the specific epithet allenii honors John W. Allen, who collected the original material and provided the impetus for the study. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that Jan Borovička received material collected in Seattle, Washington, which was microscopically similar to Psilocybe cyanescens but lacked the wavy cap margin of that species. DNA sequencing showed a 5-base-pair difference in the ITS region from P. cyanescens. That difference, together with the macroscopic differences, was judged enough to describe a new species. The original description was published in Czech Mycology in 2012, and the primary-literature abstract says the species is distinguished from P. cyanescens by its convex to hemispheric pileus, not wavy at maturity, and by its ITS rDNA sequence. Wikipedia adds that the taxon was known in the San Francisco Bay Area for several years before the description, and that the type material was collected by John W. Allen on the University of Washington campus in November 2009. *[wikipedia, lit_1]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that fruitbodies grow scattered, in groups, or more rarely in clusters, on woody debris such as wood chips used in landscaping. Favored substrates are given as hardwood mulches made of oak, eucalyptus, Douglas fir and alder. The range runs from British Columbia south to Los Angeles, California, mostly within 10 miles (16 km) of the Pacific coast, though it has been collected 100 miles (160 km) inland. Fruiting is reported in cold weather, generally late September to January. These habitat details come from a single source cluster (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*
