## Summary

Rhizoctonia solani J.G. Kühn (1858) is a fungus in the family Ceratobasidiaceae, order Cantharellales. Wikipedia describes it in its wide sense as a facultative plant pathogen with a wide host range and worldwide distribution, causing plant diseases such as root rot, damping off and wire stem. The name is currently applied to a complex of related species, and the fungus is more typically encountered as hyphae and sclerotia than as fruit bodies. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the species epithet refers to Solanum tuberosum (potato), from the diseased potato tubers on which Julius Kühn first observed the fungus. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the German plant pathologist Julius Kühn observed and described the fungus in 1858 on diseased potato tubers; the disease it causes was well known before the fungus was described. The GBIF description record quotes the protologue, in which Kühn writes that he believed the fungus had not yet been described and names it Rhizoctonia solani. It describes dark brown threads emerging from the rind of the potatoes. In 1956 the Dutch mycologist M.A. Donk published the name Thanatephorus cucumeris for the spore-bearing teleomorph, based on Hypochnus cucumeris, originally described from diseased cucumbers in Germany. Wikipedia reports that following changes to the International Code of Nomenclature the practice of giving separate names to teleomorph and anamorph was discontinued, and that R. solani is now understood as a complex of at least 25 anastomosis groups (AGs) and sub-groups. The GBIF description record treats R. solani as the asexual morph name of Th. cucumeris, cites Stalpers et al. (2021) for treating the two names as synonymous, and says many further synonyms need re-examination under a phylogenetic species concept. It also records that the name is conserved with a conserved type, a dried culture from the Netherlands, 1995 (CBS 739.95). Based on ITS sequence data, it suggests that AG-5 is the anastomosis group associated with the name R. solani. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes R. solani in its wide sense as a soil-borne plant pathogen with a wide host range and worldwide distribution. It survives in soil for many years as sclerotia, overwintering in plant debris, soil or host plants. The same source says it prefers warm, wet weather, that outbreaks in crops typically occur in early summer with symptoms mostly appearing in late summer, and that poorly drained or compacted soils favour it. Hosts named there include potato, soybean, sugar beet, cucumber, maize, rice, cereals, tomato, cabbage and turfgrass. Wikipedia also states that it is one of several Rhizoctonia species forming mycorrhizal associations with orchids, including both pathogenic and non-pathogenic strains. These statements come from a single independent source cluster, and they refer to the species in the wide sense, as a complex. *[wikipedia]*
