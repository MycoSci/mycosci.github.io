## Summary

Amanita flavoconia G.F. Atk. (1902) is a species of Amanitaceae described from New York. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both describe it as common and widespread in eastern North America, with an orange to orange-yellow cap bearing yellow warts or patches. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia gives the specific epithet flavoconia as meaning 'yellowish and conical'. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that George Francis Atkinson first described the species in 1902 from a specimen found in woods north of Fall Creek, Cayuga Lake Basin, New York. Gilbert placed it in Amplariella in 1941, and Murrill favoured Venenarius in 1948. Both segregate genera have since been folded back into Amanita. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes a mycorrhizal mushroom growing solitarily or in groups on the ground in broad-leaved and mixed woods, in summer to fall. It is noted for preferring hemlock and is also associated with high-elevation red spruce forests. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and other hardwoods and reported with hemlock and other conifers. It grows alone, scattered or gregariously, in summer and fall (or over winter along the Gulf Coast), and is common east of the Rocky Mountains. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
