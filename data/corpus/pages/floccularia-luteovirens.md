## Summary

Floccularia luteovirens (Alb. & Schwein.) Pouzar, 1957, is a species in the family Agaricaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a bright yellow mushroom when young and fresh, with large, soft scales on the cap and stem and a prominent ring. The same source treats it as a western North American species that also occurs in Europe and Asia. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says the current official name for the North American species is "Floccularia straminea var. americana f. americana", but that Floccularia straminea has long been reduced to synonymy with Floccularia luteovirens. It says the separation of the North American mushroom from Europe's Floccularia luteovirens (Mitchel & Smith 1976) rested on subtle morphological differences that the present-day European concept of the species accounts for. In the absence of contemporary DNA-based evidence, the author judges Floccularia luteovirens the best name for the North American mushroom. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says that in North America this is a western species, fairly commonly encountered in aspen stands and spruce-fir forests, especially in grassy areas. It describes the fungus as mycorrhizal and terrestrial, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in grassy areas under aspens or conifers. It fruits in late summer and fall in the Rocky Mountains and is also distributed in Europe and Asia. The illustrated and described collections are from Colorado. This is a single-source description. *[mushroomexpert]*
