## Summary

Amanita persicina (D.T. Jenkins) Tulloss & Geml (2015), the peach-colored fly agaric, is a member of the Amanitaceae. Until about 2015 it was treated as a variety of Amanita muscaria. Wikipedia describes it as poisonous and psychoactive, and says that pending further research it should not be eaten. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the fungus was formerly treated as Amanita muscaria var. persicina. DNA evidence indicated it is better treated as a distinct species, and Tulloss & Geml elevated it to species rank in 2015. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as growing solitary or gregariously and as mycorrhizal with conifers (pine) and deciduous trees (oak) in North America. It says the species often fruits in the fall but sometimes in spring and summer, and is common in the southeastern United States from Texas to Georgia and north to New Jersey. Mushroom World similarly describes it as commonly found in sandy soils with pine and oak in the southeastern United States. A Guatemalan study (Revista Científica) reports the first record of the species in the Maya lowlands of Guatemala, from a survey of mycorrhizal fungi in Pinus caribaea stands at Poptún, Petén. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, lit_1]*
