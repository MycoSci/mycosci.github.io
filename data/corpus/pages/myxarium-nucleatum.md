## Summary

Myxarium nucleatum Wallr. (1833) is a jelly fungus in the family Hyaloriaceae, recorded in the UK as crystal brain. Its fruit bodies are described as watery white, pustular or lobed and gelatinous, with small white mineral inclusions visible to the naked eye. Wikipedia describes it as common in Europe, typically on dead branches of broadleaf trees, and says it is not clear whether collections from North America and elsewhere are the same species. *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Karl Friedrich Wilhelm Wallroth described the species in 1833 from material growing on hawthorn in Germany. He placed it in his new genus Myxarium because of its visible white inclusions, which he interpreted as spores. Wallroth noted its similarity to Tremella nucleata but described Myxarium nucleatum as a separate species with the same epithet. Lewis David von Schweinitz had earlier described Tremella nucleata from the United States, and Burt later transferred it to Exidia. Publications summarized by Donk (1966) and Reid (1970) debated whether the European and American taxa are conspecific, and the consensus, based on morphological similarity, was that they are. Wikipedia adds that molecular (DNA cladistic) analysis places M. nucleatum in a clade separate from Exidia, and that at least three similar Myxarium species occur in northern Europe, with a single American collection appearing distinct. This account comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species typically fruits in autumn and winter. It is often found in association with old pyrenomycetes. It is presumed to be a wood-rotting species, typically found on dead attached or fallen branches. It was originally recorded on hawthorn, and is also known from many other broadleaf trees and shrubs, including beech, ash, sycamore and ivy. In its current, wide sense it is reported from Europe, North, Central and South America, and New Zealand, but the same source says it is unclear whether collections outside Europe represent the same species. The GBIF description record lists distribution entries for Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
