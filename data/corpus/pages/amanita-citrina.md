## Summary

Amanita citrina (Pers., 1797) is a species in the family Amanitaceae, commonly called the false death cap. Sources describe it as a pale yellow to whitish-capped mushroom with a ring and a volva at the base of the stem. It is recorded from Europe and North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia states that the common name 'false death cap' arises because the species is often confused with the related Amanita phalloides, the death cap. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the basionym as Agaricus citrinus Schaeff., 1762. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) reports that the name is nomenclaturally unsettled: North American authors have long applied 'Amanita citrina' to a North American mushroom, while the correct name for the European species is given there as Amanita bulbosa var. citrina, and Amanita citrina f. lavendula is offered as the best North American name. A 2020 Phytotaxa study reports that A. citrina var. intermedia is not conspecific with A. mappa (= A. citrina) and should be raised to an autonomous species. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert, lit_3]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that it grows in silicate soil in summer and autumn, in deciduous and coniferous woodlands in Europe, and in oak and pine forests in North America. This is a single-cluster description. *[wikipedia]*
