## Summary

Pleurotus ostreatus (Jacq.) P. Kumm., 1871, is a member of the Pleurotaceae (Agaricales) and has been recorded in dozens of countries. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as growing on wood in shelf-like clusters, with whitish gills running down a stubby, nearly absent stem and a whitish to lilac spore print. Wikipedia gives the cap as fan- or oyster-shaped and 2–30 cm wide, and MushroomExpert.Com gives 3–15 cm. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the Latin pleurotus ("side-ear") refers to the sideways growth of the stem relative to the cap. It states that ostreatus refers to the cap shape resembling the bivalve of the same name. This derivation comes from a single source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the English common name "oyster" refers to the cap's resemblance in shape to the bivalve, and adds that the oyster reference may also derive from the slippery texture of the mushroom. This is a single-source account. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultivation

Wikipedia states that commercial cultivation began in Germany as a subsistence measure during World War I and that the species is now grown commercially around the world. A 2003 substrate trial (Journal of Industrial Microbiology & Biotechnology) tested eight lignocellulosic by-products. Yields were 183.1, 151.8, 111.5, 87.8, 49.5, 23.3, 13.0 and 0.0 g on composted sawdust of Triplochiton scleroxylon, rice straw, banana leaves, maize stover, corn husk, rice husk, fresh sawdust and elephant grass respectively. Biological efficiency ranged from 61.0% on composted sawdust to 0.0% on elephant grass. The authors concluded that rice straw appeared to be the best alternate substrate. *[wikipedia, lit_3]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing in shelf-like clusters on dead logs and living trees, primarily hardwoods but sometimes conifers, and causing a white rot. It reports fruiting from late fall (October) through early spring (early April) in North America, and describes the illustrated collections as from California and Illinois. Wikipedia describes it as a saprotroph and primary wood decomposer in temperate and subtropical forests worldwide, especially on deciduous trees and beech in particular, and as a white-rot fungus. Wikipedia states it appears year round in the United Kingdom, and that it is absent from the Pacific Northwest of North America, where P. pulmonarius and P. populinus replace it. Wikipedia also says it is often seen on dying hardwood but appears to be saprophytic rather than parasitic. A GBIF description record gives the substrate as a decomposing trunk ("Tronco en descomposición") and lists distribution entries including Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Madeira Island and Vermont. Mushroom World gives its location as Europe, North America and Asia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert, gbif_desc, mushroomworld]*
