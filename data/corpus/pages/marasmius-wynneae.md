## Summary

Marasmius wynneae Berk. & Broome (1859) is a species of gilled mushroom in the family Marasmiaceae, known in English as the Pearly Parachute. English Wikipedia describes it as found in European woods. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

According to English Wikipedia, the species was named for Mary Lloyd Wynne, not her husband, because the original entry explicitly mentions Mrs. Wynne. The original description spelled the name wynnei. Wikipedia treats wynneae as the correct form because a Latinized name dedicated to a woman takes the ending -ae. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

English Wikipedia states that the species was described in 1859 by Miles Joseph Berkeley and Christopher Edmund Broome in the British Annals and Magazine of Natural History. It says the original collection was made on the Coed Coch estate in Denbighshire, Wales, which then belonged to the Wynne family. This account comes from a single independent source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

English Wikipedia describes this saprobic mushroom as growing in clusters on humus and litter in beech woods or other broad-leaved woods, with white mycelium that can bind leaf litter together in sheets. It gives the season as August to January in Europe. It calls the species common in western temperate Europe, especially under beech, and says it has been reported from Algeria, Morocco and Texas. These habitat statements come from that one source only. *[wikipedia]*
