## Summary

Leratiomyces percevalii (Berk. & Broome) Bridge & Spooner, 2008, is a member of the Strophariaceae, known in English as mulch maid or mulch maids. Wikipedia describes it as a medium-sized saprobic mushroom with a yellowish-orange cap up to 8 cm wide and gills that are whitish, then purplish gray to purple-blackish. MushroomExpert.Com adds that it has a dark purple-brown to blackish spore print and that the stem base develops reddish brown discolorations. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was originally named in England, based on a collection made in sawdust, and cites Berkeley & Broome (1879) and Bridge & Spooner (2008). The same source says West Coast collectors often call this mushroom "Stropharia riparia", a name Alexander Smith published in 1979 for collections from riparian habitats under alders, aspens and cottonwoods. Kuo considers it likely that S. riparia should be reduced to synonymy with L. percevalii, but keeps it separate in his key in case a distinct riparian species exists. This treatment comes from a single source. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing scattered or gregariously in waste places, grassy areas and woodchips on the West Coast, in summer, fall and winter. Kuo also suggests it may be an "import" like the closely related Leratiomyces ceres. Wikipedia (whose text iNaturalist reproduces) describes it as common in urban areas and near trails and roads, as well as under conifers in western North America. The season and the association with woodchips and urban waste places come from MushroomExpert.Com alone. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
