## Summary

Pholiota flammans (Batsch) P. Kumm. is a saprobic agaric in the family Strophariaceae, known in English as the flaming scalycap or flame scalecap. The fruit bodies are bright yellow and covered in scales, and are described as growing alone or in clusters on conifer wood. It is recorded from Europe, North America and Asia. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet flammans is a Latin adjective meaning flaming. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia reports that the species was first recognised in 1783 by August Batsch as Agaricus flammans, and was later sanctioned by Elias Magnus Fries in his Systema Mycologicum. In 1871, when Pholiota was recognised as an independent genus, Paul Kummer renamed it Pholiota flammans. Lucien Quélet proposed the synonym Dryophila flammans in 1886. W. A. Murrill called it Hypodendrum flammans in 1912, without explaining the transfer. Wikipedia also says that P. kauffmaniana, described by A. H. Smith, is now considered synonymous with P. flammans. Wikipedia is the only independent source for this history. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes the species as saprobic, found on dead and decaying stumps and trunks of coniferous trees. Fruit bodies appear in tufts or singly, from summer to autumn. It reports the species from Europe (British Isles to Russia) and North America (southern Canada and the United States), and says it has also been collected in Asia, including India and China. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) independently gives the ecology as saprobic, growing alone or in clusters on conifer wood in summer and fall, and widely distributed in northern North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
