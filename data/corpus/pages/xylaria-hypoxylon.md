## Summary

Xylaria hypoxylon (L.) Grev. (1824) is a fungus in the family Xylariaceae, order Xylariales, known in English as the candlesnuff fungus. It is recorded in GBIF from many countries. Wikipedia describes fruit bodies with erect black branches with whitened tips, growing on decaying hardwood. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as extremely variable in appearance and notes that DNA work has shown collections under this name to include several distinct species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet is derived from the Greek words hypo, meaning "below", and xylon, meaning "wood". This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies lists Clavaria hypoxylon L. (1753) as the original combination, with the current combination by Greville in 1824 (Flora Edinensis). MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was first named by Linnaeus and later sanctioned by Fries, from Sweden. It reports that a DNA study of collections called Xylaria hypoxylon from around the globe (Peršoh et al. 2009) revealed several phylogenetically distinct species, some not closely related. It also reports that Stadler and collaborators (2014) fixed the concept of the "real" X. hypoxylon by designating type collections from Sweden. According to MushroomExpert.Com, misidentifications and potential cryptic species appear to account for other versions of the name. Wikipedia likewise mentions an X. hypoxylon complex whose validity has been debated. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as saprobic on the deadwood of hardwoods, growing gregariously to densely gregariously, from spring through fall. Wikipedia independently states that the fruit bodies typically grow in clusters on decaying hardwood. By MushroomExpert.Com's strict definition, evidence so far supports the true Xylaria hypoxylon in Europe and the West Coast of the United States, in temperate climates but not subtropical or tropical areas. The same source says that eastern North American collections may represent Xylaria vasconica or Xylaria longiana, and that the species has been (mis)reported as widely distributed elsewhere. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
