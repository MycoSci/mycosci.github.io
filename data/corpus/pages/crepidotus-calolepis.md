## Summary

Crepidotus calolepis (Fr.) P. Karst. (1879) is a species in the family Crepidotaceae, based on Agaricus calolepis. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a stemless, semicircular or fan-shaped mushroom with scattered fine brown fibrils on the cap. Its gills are whitish when young and become dull brown with maturity. The GBIF description record lists distribution in Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was originally described from Sweden (Fries 1873) and neotypified from Estonia (Senn-Irlet 1995). It also notes that many authors, especially in North America, treat it together with Crepidotus mollis, with C. calolepis designated as a variety, Crepidotus mollis var. calolepis. According to that source, the difference is the presence of brown fibrils on the cap of C. calolepis, whereas the surface of C. mollis is virtually bald. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously on the deadwood of oaks and other hardwoods. In Europe it is said to grow mainly on aspen, and in North America on various trees. The same source describes it as widely distributed in Europe and North America and gives California and Illinois as the origin of its illustrated and described collections. No source supplied here states a fruiting season. *[mushroomexpert]*
