## Summary

Lactarius pubescens Fr. (1838), the downy milk cap, is a species in the family Russulaceae and is recorded in many countries, mainly in Europe and North America. Wikipedia describes it as a medium to large agaric with a creamy-buff, hairy cap, whitish gills and a short stout stem, growing under or near birch. *[wikipedia]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet pubescens is derived from Latin and means "becoming downy". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first named Agaricus pubescens by Heinrich Schrader in 1794, and Elias Magnus Fries gave it its current name in 1838. Wikispecies lists the Schrader name as illegitimate (nom. illeg.), with a competing older name, Agaricus pubescens Scop., 1770. Wikipedia also records that it has been treated as a variety of Lactarius controversus, and as a subspecies and a variety of Lactarius torminosus. Wikipedia cites a 2004 phylogenetic analysis as placing it closest to L. scoticus and L. tesquorum. This is a single-source account (Wikipedia, with Wikispecies for the nomenclatural detail); neither source explains why the transfers were made. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies are found scattered or in groups on the ground in wet areas under birch and other hardwoods, from August to October, and elsewhere that the fungus grows solitarily or in scattered groups on sandy soil under or near birch. It describes the species as common across temperate Europe and reported from eastern North America, the Pacific Northwest, Arizona, California, Colorado, Idaho and western Canada, with occasional frequency. It is also reported from Greenland and, in 1997, from Rome, Italy. A GBIF-supplied description of Montana (U.S.A.) collections lists specimens taken under Betula sp. and under Populus tremuloides. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
