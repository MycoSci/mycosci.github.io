## Summary

Aureoboletus auriporus (Peck) Pouzar, 1957, is a bolete in the family Boletaceae. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a mushroom of eastern North America's oak forests, with a pinkish brown, sticky cap and a bright yellow pore surface that does not bruise but may develop reddish spots with age. It was first described by Charles Horton Peck in 1872 as Boletus auriporus. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was described by the American mycologist Charles Horton Peck in 1872 as Boletus auriporus, and that Zdenek Pouzar transferred it to Aureoboletus in 1957. Wikispecies also lists the intermediate combinations Ceriomyces auriporus (Murrill, 1909), Xerocomus auriporus (Singer, 1940) and Pulveroboletus auriporus (Singer, 1947). It also lists Aureoboletus novoguineensis Hongo (1973) among the synonyms. None of the supplied sources says what material the original description was based on or why the transfers were made. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) describes it as mycorrhizal with oaks and possibly other hardwoods. It grows alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall. It is reported as widely distributed in eastern North America, through Texas into Mexico, Central America and South America. The collections illustrated and described there are from Illinois. GBIF description records list the habitat as Andean forest and Quercus humboldtii forest, and give a distribution entry of Vermont. The altitude entries in the GBIF records (2400–3600 and 2450–3100) have no stated units or context. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
