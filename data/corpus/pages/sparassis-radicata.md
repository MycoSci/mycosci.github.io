## Summary

Sparassis radicata Weir (1917) is a species in the family Sparassidaceae, known in English as the western cauliflower mushroom, and was described from Idaho. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as resembling a large clump of tightly packed, noodle-like branches, and reports it from the Pacific Northwest and northern California. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com says the Latin name reflects the fact that the mushroom usually has a radicating, underground portion, a long, fleshy "root" that is often overlooked when the mushroom is picked. For the genus name, Wikipedia states that Sparassis comes from the Greek sparassein, meaning to tear. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to MushroomExpert.Com, Sparassis radicata was originally described by Weir in 1917 as a western, conifer-loving, rooting morphological species. Several western authors (e.g. Arora 1986, States 1990) used the name Sparassis crispa for it. A later mating study (Martin & Gilbertson 1976, using southwestern specimens) found it to be the same biological species as European and Asian S. crispa specimens. More recent DNA evidence (Zhao et al. 2013, Hughes et al. 2014), as summarised by MushroomExpert.Com, supports S. radicata being a distinct phylogenetic species limited to the Pacific Northwest and northern California. Wikipedia notes that S. crispa and S. radicata look very similar and that some authorities treat them as conspecific. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes S. radicata as pathogenic and saprobic, found at the bases of trees and growing from dead or dying roots. It reports the species only under conifers, especially Douglas-fir (the ecology entry also names pines), causing a brown rot or butt rot. The fruiting body is annual but often recurs yearly in the same location, and the page gives fall and winter as the season. The species was originally described from Idaho and is reported as distributed in the Pacific Northwest and northern California. The collections illustrated and described on that page are from California and Oregon. This is a single-source description. *[mushroomexpert]*
