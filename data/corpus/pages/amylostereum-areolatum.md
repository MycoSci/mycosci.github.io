## Summary

Amylostereum areolatum (Chaillet ex Fr.) Boidin is a crust fungus in the family Echinodontiaceae, order Russulales, known in English as the patchy duster. The available literature describes it as the symbiotic fungus of the woodwasp Sirex noctilio, a pest of pine plantations in the Southern Hemisphere. It is also studied as a source of chemical cues used by a parasitoid wasp that hunts the woodwasp. *[wikipedia, lit_1, lit_3]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was originally called Thelephora areolata in 1828 and was given its current name by the French mycologist Jacques Boidin in 1958. No supplied source gives the type material or the reasons for the transfers. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

One study (Physiological Entomology, 2006) reports the fungus growing naturally within pine logs, as a symbiont of the woodwasp Sirex noctilio. A 1998 study of isolates from Lithuania, Sweden, Denmark and Great Britain reports that A. areolatum isolates there were spread by the woodwasp Sirex juvencus. A 2001 study sampled isolates from South Africa, South America, Australasia and Europe. It reports the fungus and S. noctilio as causing extensive damage to pine plantations in the Southern Hemisphere. These are single-study statements, not independently corroborated descriptions of habitat, and no source gives a fruiting season. *[lit_1, lit_2, lit_3]*
