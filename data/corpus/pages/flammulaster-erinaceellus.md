## Summary

Flammulaster erinaceellus (Peck) Watling is a species in the family Tubariaceae (Agaricales), recorded in the site catalog under the vernacular name powder-scale pholiota. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small brown gilled mushroom with a finely scaly, often powdery cap that turns red and then quickly black with KOH. The sources supplied place it on decayed hardwood in eastern North America. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com credits the original description to C. H. Peck, from New York material (citing Peck 1878), and gives Watling's 1967 combination in Flammulaster. Wikipedia likewise says Roy Watling transferred the species to Flammulaster in 1967. MushroomExpert.Com lists Flammulaster erinaceella as an alternate spelling and Phaeomarasmius erinaceellus as a synonym. It also states, citing Vellinga (1986), that the mushroom appearing in some European literature as Flammulaster erinaceella is actually Flammulaster muricatus. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic, growing alone, gregariously, or in small loose clusters on well-decayed, decorticated deadwood of hardwoods, causing a white rot, from spring through fall. It says the species is probably widely distributed east of the Great Plains, and that the collections it illustrates and describes come from Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, and Missouri. Wikipedia independently says fruiting occurs on barkless areas of logs in the woods in eastern North America. A GBIF description record lists a distribution of Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
