## Summary

Ganoderma tsugae Murrill (1902), the hemlock varnish shelf, is a flat polypore in the family Polyporaceae. It is described as a laccate (lacquered-looking) Ganoderma found almost exclusively on the wood of eastern hemlock in northern and montane areas east of the Rocky Mountains. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com notes that G. oregonense and G. tsugae were until recently thought to coexist in the Pacific Northwest, but that a study of hundreds of North American Ganoderma collections by Loyd and collaborators (2017, 2018) failed to support this idea. G. oregonense is described there as a similar species of the Pacific Northwest and California with larger spores. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as parasitic and saprobic on the wood of eastern hemlock 'and perhaps other conifers', causing a white rot (usually a butt rot) of the heartwood. It grows alone, scattered, or gregariously, is annual, and fruits spring through fall. It is distributed in the northern Midwest, northeastern North America, and the Appalachian Mountains, where eastern hemlocks occur; the illustrated collections are from Michigan and Ohio. Wikipedia likewise says it tends to grow on conifers, especially hemlocks. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
