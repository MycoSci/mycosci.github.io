## Summary

Tricholoma magnivelare (Peck) Redhead, 1984, is the American matsutake, a gilled mushroom in the family Tricholomataceae. Since recent molecular work the name is restricted to the matsutake of eastern North America, which is mycorrhizal with conifers. MushroomExpert.Com describes a whitish to brownish cap with brown scales, a brown-scaly stem with a flaring ring, white gills and spore print, and a strong spicy odor reminiscent of cinnamon. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was originally described from New York (Peck 1873). Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com both report that the name Tricholoma magnivelare was formerly applied to all North American matsutake, including the western ones. Wikipedia says molecular data since the early 2000s showed separate species within that group, leaving the name only on those in the eastern United States and Canada. MushroomExpert.Com attributes the restriction to eastern North America to Trudell et al. 2017 and treats the western North American and Mexican matsutake as separate species. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com reports the species as mycorrhizal with hard pines (those with needles in bundles of 2 or 3), including jack pine, red pine and pitch pine, and also with eastern hemlock. It grows alone, scattered, or gregariously. That source gives its distribution as the upper Midwest and northeastern North America, southward through the Appalachian Mountains, with fruiting in fall and early winter. It also describes the species as occurring in northern and montane (Appalachian) areas. Wikipedia independently describes it as growing in coniferous woodland east of the Rocky Mountains, in association with various pines, commonly jack pine. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
