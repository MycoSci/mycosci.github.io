## Summary

Arrhenia acerosa (Fr.) Kühner (1980) is a small agaric in the family Hygrophoraceae, known in English as the moss oysterling. Wikipedia describes its fruit bodies as sessile, fan-shaped to kidney-shaped and up to 15 mm across. It has been documented from Sweden, Estonia and Norway, and a 2010 survey of Hatila Valley National Park (Artvin) reported it as a new record for Turkey. *[wikipedia, lit_1]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that the species was first named Agaricus acerosus by Elias Magnus Fries in 1821, was later moved through several genera, and was placed in Arrhenia by Robert Kühner in 1980. Because Fries did not designate a holotype, Petersen and Knudsen (2020) selected a neotype from a 1943 collection made near Bokhultet in Småland, Sweden (Lundell & Nannfeldt exsiccata no. 1761). Wikipedia reports that this neotype matches Fries's original description in habitat and morphology. It also reports that ITS sequence data group the neotype with specimens from Estonia and Norway. All of this comes from a single source (Wikipedia). *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species grows on decaying wood fragments, or directly on bare soil among twigs and wood chips, along earthy forest paths. It gives the neotype locality as Småland, Sweden, with further specimens documented in Valga County, Estonia, and Vestfold County, Norway. iNaturalist's short entry says only that it is found in Europe, where it grows on mosses. That substrate differs from Wikipedia's description, and only iNaturalist gives it. *[wikipedia, inaturalist]*
