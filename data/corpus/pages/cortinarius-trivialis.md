## Summary

Cortinarius trivialis J.E. Lange (1940) is a species in the family Cortinariaceae, with Denmark as its type locality. Wikipedia describes it as an inedible fungus. MushroomExpert.Com describes the North American form as a slimy-capped species associated with aspen, with a long stem sheathed in whitish slime that breaks up into concentric "belts". *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com credits the name to J. E. Lange, 1940. It notes that the original European species is now regarded as "a collective species" (Knudsen & Vesterholt, 2008), and that it is unresolved whether any North American version is the same as the European species. It also says some mycologists, notably Alexander Smith, consider C. trivialis a variety of Cortinarius collinitus that lacks purple shades in the slime veil. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

According to MushroomExpert.Com (a single source, describing the North American form): mycorrhizal with quaking aspen and other hardwoods; growing scattered or gregariously; summer and fall (or over winter in coastal California); northern and western North America. It says one version is associated with aspen and another occurs under coast live oak in California. *[mushroomexpert]*
