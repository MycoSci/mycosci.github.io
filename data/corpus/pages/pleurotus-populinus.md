## Summary

Pleurotus populinus O. Hilber & O.K. Mill. (1993) is the aspen oyster mushroom, placed in the family Pleurotaceae. GBIF records it from the United States, Canada and several other countries. It was described as a new species in the Pleurotus ostreatus complex, occurring primarily on aspen in North America. *[lit_1, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

MushroomExpert.Com's author, Michael Kuo, says he calls it the "aspen oyster" because it appears to be limited to the wood of quaking aspen and closely related trees (aspens and cottonwoods in the genus Populus). This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

A 1993 Canadian Journal of Botany study of mating compatibility, using strains from 170 North American collections in the Pleurotus ostreatus complex, found three intersterile species in the United States and Canada. A third intersterility group, occurring primarily on aspen, was intersterile with all other known species and was described as the new species Pleurotus populinus. The authors report that the three species differ in morphology, growth characteristics, geographic distribution and host range. They also report that, apart from mating compatibility tests, no single type of character was completely reliable for telling these species apart in the field or in the lab. MushroomExpert.Com adds that it is a well-established biological species that is also a phylogenetic species, supported by DNA evidence. Wikipedia says the species was first described in 1993 by Oswald and Orson K. Miller with a provisional name, and that it was republished in 1997. *[lit_1, mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com (Kuo) describes it as saprobic, growing in shelf-like clusters on dead and living wood of Populus species, primarily quaking aspen but also plains cottonwood, and causing a white rot. It gives the season as spring, summer and fall. It says the species is widely distributed in northern and montane North America, throughout the range of the host trees, and that the collections it illustrates and describes are from Colorado. Wikipedia describes growth on rotting stumps, logs and limbs of hardwoods, with aspen and black cottonwood as preferred substrates. It places the species in the northern United States and Canada and in mountainous regions of western North America, and gives the fruiting season as late spring and summer (June and July). The two sources differ on season length. The Canadian Journal of Botany study reports it as occurring primarily on aspen. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, lit_1]*
