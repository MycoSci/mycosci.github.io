## Summary

Hydnum albidum Peck (1887), the white hedgehog, is a fungus in the family Hydnaceae, described from New York. One source describes it as native to North America, and the supplied collections come from eastern Canada, the eastern United States and Veracruz, Mexico. It is a small, white to cream-coloured species with spines rather than gills under the cap. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

Peck described Hydnum albidum from New York in 1887. The holotype was collected in thin woods on the ground at Sandlake, Rensselaer County, around July 1886. In the protologue Peck separated it from H. repandum by its smaller basidiomes and spores and its white colour. DNA could not be sequenced from the holotype, so a modern collection from Kennedy State Forest, Cortland County, New York, was designated as an epitype. Several other historical Peck collections labelled H. albidum had much larger spores. Partial ITS sequences from two of them matched a species more closely related to H. repandum. The name H. repandum var. album has been widely applied in North America, but the same source interprets the American concept of that variety as H. subtilior rather than H. albidum. *[gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

The sources record it from eastern Canada and the eastern US (Nova Scotia, Vermont, New York, Tennessee, North Carolina) and from Veracruz, Mexico. The holotype was found on the ground in thin woods. Other examined collections were made on humus, in soil among leaf litter, or on roadside embankments, growing with or under Abies, Betula, Quercus, Fagus, Acer, Picea, Tsuga and Pinus. They were collected at 80–1525 m elevation, in June to September. *[gbif_desc]*
