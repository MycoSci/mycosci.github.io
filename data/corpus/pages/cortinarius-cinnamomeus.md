## Summary

Cortinarius cinnamomeus, the cinnamon webcap, is a species in the family Cortinariaceae, authored (L.) Gray, 1821. Wikipedia describes it as a brown-fruited mushroom with caps up to 6 cm wide and stems up to 12 cm long. Its closely crowded gills are yellow when young and turn brown with age. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states that Linnaeus first described the fungus in 1753 as Agaricus cinnamomeus. It lists Dermocybe cinnamomea, Flammula cinnamomea and Gomphos cinnamomeus as names that reflect differing approaches over the years to the taxonomy of Cortinarius species. This account comes from a single source. *[wikipedia]*

## Cultural and culinary history

Wikipedia states that the fruit bodies are used in mushroom dyeing to produce a brown color. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is common in damp places in coniferous forests. It says the species grows in Europe and apparently throughout the temperate zone of the Northern Hemisphere, and that it has also been collected from Yunnan Province, China. The same source reports that it colonizes the root systems of the sedges Carex flacca and Carex pilulifera, forming ectomycorrhizal-like structures that lack a Hartig net. A GBIF record lists the distribution as Norway, Sweden and Vermont. No season or substrate beyond the above is stated in the supplied sources. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
