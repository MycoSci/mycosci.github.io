## Summary

Hydnum umbilicatum Peck (1902) is a tooth fungus in the family Hydnaceae, described from New York and commonly called the depressed hedgehog. One supplied source describes it as widespread in eastern North America. *[gbif_desc]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikipedia states the species was described in 1902 from New York by Charles Horton Peck. The GBIF description text says Peck's description was accompanied by a colour plate and cites it as Peck (1901). The holotype (NYS-F-3258) was collected by C. H. Peck on the ground in thin woods at Sandlake, Rensselaer County, New York, in September, ca. 1901. The same text says DNA sequences could not be obtained from the type collection, and an epitype was designated from a 2014 collection by T. J. Baroni in Cortland County, New York. *[wikipedia, gbif_desc]*

## Habitat and ecology

The GBIF description text says the species is widespread in eastern North America at low and high elevations, mostly in conifer-dominated forests or mixed woods that include conifers. The listed specimens come from Michigan, Massachusetts, New York, Tennessee, North Carolina, Newfoundland and Labrador, and Quebec. The specimen labels record it scattered singly or gregarious, on soil or hardwood leaf litter, and under or with trees including Pinus, Tsuga, Abies fraseri and Quercus. The epitype was collected on humus in a wet, boggy area with Tsuga and Betula alleghaniensis. *[gbif_desc]*
