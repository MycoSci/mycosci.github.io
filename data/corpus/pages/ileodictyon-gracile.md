## Summary

Ileodictyon gracile Berk. (1845) is a species in the family Phallaceae, described from Western Australia and known in English as the smooth cage fungus. Wikipedia describes it as a saprotrophic fungus whose fruit bodies are balls of interlaced, latticed branches, with a foetid spore-bearing slime on the inner surfaces of the branches. *[wikipedia, wikispecies]*

## Discovery and naming

Wikispecies gives the original publication as Berkeley, London Journal of Botany 4: 69 (1845), with the holotype at K(M) (Drummond 177) and the type locality as on soil in Western Australia. It lists later combinations and rank changes: Clathrus gracilis (Schlechtendal, 1862), Clathrus cibarius f. gracilis (E. Fischer, 1893), Ileodictyon cibarium var. gracile (E. Fischer, 1898) and Clathrus cibarius var. gracilis (Cheel, 1909). It also lists Protubera canescens Beaton & Malajczuk (1986) as a heterotypic synonym. None of the supplied sources says why these transfers were made. *[wikispecies]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikispecies records the type locality as on soil in Western Australia. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) gives the ecology as saprobic, growing alone or gregariously in woods or in cultivated areas, and found year-round. Kuo notes that this description is based on photos sent to him and on the literature, not on his own collections. He lists the species as originally described from Australia and also found in New Zealand, Japan, South Korea and South Africa. Wikipedia says it is native to Australia and has been found in Asia (China, India, Japan, Korea), possibly as a result of introduction. It also says the species is known in Europe from England, Italy, Portugal and Spain, where it is certainly introduced. The sources differ on New Zealand: Wikipedia says the species is not known with certainty from there, while MushroomExpert.Com lists it as found there. *[wikispecies, mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*
