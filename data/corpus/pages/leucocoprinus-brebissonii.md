## Summary

Leucocoprinus brebissonii (Godey) Locq. is a species of mushroom-producing fungus in the family Agaricaceae, also known as the skullcap dapperling. Wikipedia describes it as small and delicate, and says it was long thought to occur only in Europe but has since been observed in the Pacific Northwest. It was first described by Louis-Luc Godey in 1874 and moved to Leucocoprinus by Marcel Locquin in 1943. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, Louis-Luc Godey described the species as Lepiota brebissoni, first published in 1874, and Marcel Locquin reclassified it as Leucocoprinus brebissonii in 1943. In 1953 Tsuguo Hongo documented a species from Ōtsu, Japan as Leucocoprinus otsuensis (previously Lepiota otsuensis). He noted it was very similar to L. brebissonii but distinguishable by the squamules on the cap. Wikipedia says these may now be considered synonyms, although some sources list Lepiota otsuensis as the current name. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia states that the species is found in Europe and North America from summer to fall, growing in the soil in woods with deciduous trees. It also says Godey recorded it in 1874 in the Cerisy Forest, Calvados region of France, during summer, noting it as solitary and rare.
