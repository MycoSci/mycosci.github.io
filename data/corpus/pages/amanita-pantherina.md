## Summary

Amanita pantherina (DC.) Krombh., family Amanitaceae, is known as the panther cap, false blusher and panther amanita. GBIF occurrence records come mostly from Europe, with others from North America, Asia and southern Africa. Wikipedia describes it as a species with poisonous and psychoactive properties. *[wikipedia]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia says the name 'false blusher' comes from the species' similarity to the true blusher (Amanita rubescens), and that unlike A. rubescens it does not turn red or pink when the flesh is damaged. The same source gives 'panther amanita' as a name 'due to its similarity to' A. rubescens. It does not explain the origin of 'panther cap'. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia describes it as an uncommon, ectomycorrhizal species found in deciduous woodland (especially beech), less frequently in coniferous woodland, and rarely in meadows, across Europe and western Asia in late summer and autumn. The same source says it has been recorded in South Africa, where it is thought to have been accidentally introduced with imported trees. Mushroom World (an aggregator) says it grows solitary or in small groups on soil under conifers or in mixed hardwood-conifer forests. MushroomExpert.Com (Michael Kuo) says the western North American mushroom it illustrates is probably an unnamed species or group of species currently passing under this European name. It describes that mushroom as mycorrhizal mainly with conifers (bishop pine, Monterey pine, Douglas-fir, spruces, occasionally hardwoods), fruiting in fall and winter along the West Coast from California to the Pacific Northwest, and reported from the southwestern Rocky Mountains. *[wikipedia, mushroomworld, mushroomexpert]*
