## Summary

Amanita brunnescens G.F. Atk. (1918) is a species in the family Amanitaceae, with its type locality in New York. Wikipedia calls it a native North American mushroom, and MushroomExpert.Com describes it as an eastern North American species. Both sources say its main distinguishing feature is a stem that ends in an abrupt basal bulb that is split or "cleft" and discolors reddish brown. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that Atkinson named the species after the fact that it bruised brown. This is a single-source statement. *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was originally presumed by Charles Horton Peck to be Amanita phalloides. It was then described and named by George F. Atkinson of Cornell University. This comes from a single independent source cluster. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with various hardwoods and conifers, growing alone, scattered, or gregariously in summer and fall. It calls the species widely distributed and common east of the Rocky Mountains. GBIF's description record lists only "Vermont" as a distribution entry. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
