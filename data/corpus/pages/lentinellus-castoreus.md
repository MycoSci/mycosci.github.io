## Summary

Lentinellus castoreus (Fr.) Kühner & Maire, 1934, belongs to the family Auriscalpiaceae in the order Russulales. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as a small, stem-less, shelving mushroom with saw-toothed gills and a fan- to kidney-shaped cap 1.5–4 cm across, often bearing a brown velvety patch of tomentum. That source says it is hard to identify with confidence and is separated from Lentinellus ursinus by a Melzer's or Lugol's reaction on dried flesh, and from Lentinellus vulpinus by microscopic hyphal features. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

MushroomExpert.Com states that the epithet castoreus means "beaver", and groups the species with other stem-less, shelving Lentinellus species that have saw-toothed gills and mammalian Latin names. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com credits the name to E. M. Fries (1838) and R. Kühner & R. C. J. E. Maire (1934). It states that the species was originally described from Switzerland (Secretan 1833) and Sweden (Fries 1838), and lists Lentinellus angustifolius as a synonym. This is a single-source account. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes the species as saprobic, growing in groups or in shelf-like clusters on the wood of hardwoods. It gives the season as summer and fall, or over winter in tropical climates. That source calls it widespread in Europe, North America, Central America, the Caribbean, South America, and Oceania, and its illustrated and described collections are from Illinois and Indiana. The GBIF description record lists distribution as BR, NO and SE. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc]*
