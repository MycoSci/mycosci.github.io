## Summary

Craterellus cornucopioides (L.) Pers., 1825 is a fungus in the family Hydnaceae, recorded in GBIF from many countries. Wikipedia and MushroomExpert.Com describe it as a funnel- or deeply vase-shaped fungus with a black to dark gray inner surface. MushroomExpert.Com's account is of the European species and questions whether the name applies in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*

## Where the common name comes from

Wikipedia offers one possible origin for the name "trumpet of the dead": growing mushrooms were seen as trumpets being played by dead people under the ground. It says the name is translated as trompette de mort in French and trompeta de la mort in Catalan. This is a single-source suggestion, described there only as a "possible origin". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com states that the species was first named and described by Linnaeus (1753), and cites it as "(Linnaeus, 1753) Persoon, 1825". A GBIF description record lists the original material as an "icon" in Vaillant, Bot. Paris.: 57, t. 13, f. 2, 3 (1727) and says no type was designated. Wikipedia says Craterellus fallax and C. konradii have been defined as separate species, but that DNA studies now show C. konradii should be considered part of C. cornucopioides. MushroomExpert.Com, citing Matheny and collaborators (2010), treats C. fallax as a distinct species, separated from C. cornucopioides by spore print colour and genetics. *[mushroomexpert, gbif_desc, wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia says the fungus is found in woods in Europe, East Asia, North America and Australia. It reports growth mainly under beech, oak or other broad-leaved trees, especially in moss in moist spots on heavy calcareous soil, and says it grows in groups. For Europe it gives June to November, and August to November in the United Kingdom. For Australia it describes rainforest gullies, January to May. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as mycorrhizal with hardwoods and, less frequently, with conifers. It grows scattered, gregariously or (usually) in tightly packed clusters, often in mossy areas, in summer and fall, and is widely distributed in Europe. The two sources disagree about North America. MushroomExpert.Com, citing Matheny et al. (2010), says C. cornucopioides does not occur there and is replaced by Craterellus fallax in eastern North America and by a probably unnamed species on the West Coast. Wikipedia lists North America within the range. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
