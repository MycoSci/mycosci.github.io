## Summary

Hericium coralloides (Scop.) Pers., 1794, is a toothed fungus in the family Hericiaceae (Russulales). MushroomExpert.Com describes it as fruiting from dead hardwood logs and stumps, with a branched fruiting body 8–20 cm across. Its short spines, mostly about 1 cm long, hang in rows along the branches, and the flesh and spore print are white. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

MushroomExpert.Com gives the basionym authority as Scopoli, 1772, with the combination by Persoon, 1794. It says that in older treatments the species will be found as Hericium ramosum. It also says that, confusingly, Hericium americanum will be found in those treatments as Hericium coralloides. Older literature and records under this name may therefore refer to a different species. *[mushroomexpert]*

## Habitat and ecology

MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and possibly parasitic, growing alone or gregariously on fallen hardwood branches, logs and stumps, sometimes in large patches. It gives the season as late summer and fall, or over winter and in spring in warmer climates. Its collections are from Illinois, Michigan and Minnesota, and it calls the species apparently widely distributed in North America. English Wikipedia says it grows on dead hardwood trees throughout North America, Eurasia and Australia, and gives July to October in the east and November to March in the west. Both statements are single-source claims. GBIF's description record lists distribution entries including Belgium, Denmark, Norway, Sweden and Vermont. *[mushroomexpert, wikipedia, gbif_desc]*
