## Summary

Collybia tuberosa (Bull.) P. Kumm., the lentil shanklet, is a small whitish agaric in the Tricholomataceae. Its stems arise from a reddish-brown sclerotium, and it grows on the decaying remains of other mushrooms. Wikipedia describes it as inedible. *[wikipedia, inaturalist, mushroomexpert]*

## Etymology

Wikipedia states that the specific epithet tuberosa is derived from the Latin word for "tuberous". It also records the common names "lentil shanklet" and "appleseed coincap". *[wikipedia]*

## Discovery and naming

According to Wikipedia, the species was first described as Agaricus tuberosus by the French naturalist Jean Bulliard in his Herbier de la France. Persoon treated it as Agaricus amanitae subsp. tuberosus in 1799, and Samuel Frederick Gray placed it in Gymnopus in 1821. Gray called it the "tuberous naked-foot" in his 1821 Natural Arrangement of British plants. This account comes from a single encyclopedia source. *[wikipedia]*

## Habitat and ecology

Wikipedia reports fruit bodies growing solitarily or in dense clusters on the decomposing, often blackened remains of other mushrooms. Hosts include agarics (particularly Lactarius and Russula), boletes, hydnums and polypores. It says it is not known whether the fungus is strictly parasitic or saprobic. It reports the species from Europe, North America and Japan, most commonly in summer and autumn. MushroomExpert.Com describes it as saprobic and gregarious on decayed mushrooms (especially Lactarius and Russula species) or occasionally on humus. It gives the setting as under hardwoods or conifers, in summer and fall (or winter in warm climates), and widely distributed in North America. *[wikipedia, mushroomexpert]*
