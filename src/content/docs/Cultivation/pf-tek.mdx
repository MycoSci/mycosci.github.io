PF-Tek is the method that taught a generation of home cultivators to grow mushrooms. It uses a sterilizable substrate of brown rice flour (BRF) and vermiculite packed into half-pint jars, capped with a dry vermiculite layer that doubles as a contamination barrier. You sterilize with boiling-water steam (no pressure cooker required), inoculate with a spore or culture syringe, let the jars colonize into solid "cakes," then birth those cakes into a humid fruiting chamber. It is slow and low-yield compared to grain-and-bulk, but it is the most forgiving entry point in the hobby — and the best place to learn sterile technique before you scale up.

<Badge text="Difficulty: Beginner" /> <Badge text="Where it fits: your first grow, before grain spawn" variant="success" /> <Badge text="Timeline: 4–8 weeks jar to harvest" variant="note" />

<Aside type="danger">
Never eat a mushroom you cannot identify with certainty, and never eat anything grown from a print or syringe of unverified origin. Cultivation does not remove the misidentification risk — a contaminated or mislabeled culture can produce something you should not consume. When in doubt, throw it out. See [Contamination Identification](/docs/lab/contamination-id) and [Spore Prints, Syringes & Culture Storage](/docs/lab/cultures-and-storage).
</Aside>

## Why PF-Tek for beginners

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="No pressure cooker">
    BRF cakes can be steam-sterilized in a stockpot. That removes the single biggest equipment barrier and the pressure-cooker safety risk for newcomers.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Self-isolating jars">
    Each jar is its own sealed unit. One contaminated jar does not doom the batch the way one bad spot can ruin a whole tub of bulk substrate.
  </Card>
  <Card title="The dry verm layer">
    The dry vermiculite top is a physical and moisture barrier that keeps airborne mold and bacteria away from the inoculation point. It is a big part of why this tek is so forgiving.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Visible feedback">
    Colonization is visible through the glass, so you learn to read healthy mycelium versus contamination before you ever open a jar.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## What you need

```
Substrate (per half-pint / ~236 mL jar):
  2 parts   vermiculite (fine/medium grade)
  1 part    brown rice flour (finely ground)
  approx.   water to field capacity (see hydration test)

Dry top layer:
  ~1.3 cm (1/2 in) of plain dry vermiculite per jar

Hardware:
  half-pint wide-mouth canning jars + lids
  hammer + small nail (4 holes per lid)
  micropore tape or foil
  large stockpot with lid + a rack/trivet
  spore or culture syringe (10–12 cc covers ~6–10 jars)
  70% isopropyl alcohol, lighter/torch, still-air box optional
```

<Aside type="tip">
Wide-mouth half-pints are worth it — narrow-mouth jars make birthing the cake far harder. At roughly 1–1.5 cc of inoculant per jar (about 0.25–0.5 cc per hole, 4 holes), a 10 cc syringe realistically covers 6–10 jars.
</Aside>

## Step 1 — Mix and fill the jars

The goal is substrate at *field capacity*: moist enough to support growth, dry enough that no free water pools. Over-wetting is the most common beginner mistake and invites bacterial contamination.

<Steps>

1. Combine 2 parts vermiculite to 1 part BRF by volume in a clean bowl. Mix dry first.

2. Add water slowly while stirring until the mix is uniformly damp. Hydration test: squeeze a handful — it should clump and release at most a drop or two, never a stream. If you can wring water out, it is too wet; add vermiculite.

3. Loosely fill each jar to about 1.3 cm (1/2 in) below the shoulder. Do not pack it — mycelium needs air gaps to breathe. Wipe the inner jar walls clean above the fill line so the dry layer seats cleanly.

4. Top each jar with ~1.3 cm of plain dry vermiculite. This is the contamination barrier. Keep it dry and keep it off the substrate-to-wall contact ring.

5. Hammer 4 evenly spaced nail holes through each lid for inoculation. Cover the lid with foil (or micropore tape) to keep water out during sterilization.

</Steps>

## Step 2 — Steam sterilize (no PC needed)

BRF cakes have a low enough nutrient load and a protective dry layer, so atmospheric steam works where grain would demand a pressure cooker. You are pasteurizing-plus, not achieving full 121 °C sterility — the tek tolerates this because of the barrier design.

<Steps>

1. Place a rack or trivet in the stockpot so jars sit off the bottom. Add water to roughly 2.5 cm (1 in) — below the rack, not touching the lids.

2. Load jars, foil intact. Bring to a rolling boil, then hold a steady steam for 75–90 minutes. Keep the lid on. Check the water level so it never boils dry.

3. Kill the heat and let the jars cool, lid on, to room temperature — typically overnight. Inoculating warm jars cooks your spores and pulls non-sterile air in as they cool.

</Steps>

<Aside type="danger">
A pressure cooker is optional for PF-Tek, but if you use one, treat it as a serious hazard. Never force a hot lid open, let pressure return to zero naturally before opening, keep the vent and gasket clean and unobstructed, and never leave it unattended. A blocked vent can cause an explosion. If you pressure-sterilize, 15 PSI for about 60 minutes is sufficient for half-pint BRF jars. More detail in [Pasteurization & Sterilization](/docs/cultivation/pasteurization-sterilization).
</Aside>

## Step 3 — Inoculate

<Aside type="danger">
Flame-sterilizing a needle and handling 70% isopropyl near an open flame is a real fire hazard. Alcohol vapor is flammable. Cap the alcohol and set it aside before lighting the flame, let the needle cool away from the flame before it touches anything wet, and never spray alcohol toward a lit lighter or torch.
</Aside>

Work clean. A still-air box helps but is not mandatory for PF-Tek if your room is calm and surfaces are wiped down. Review [Sterile Technique & Working Clean](/docs/lab/sterile-technique) first.

<Steps>

1. Wipe your work surface and your hands with 70% isopropyl. Shake the syringe hard to distribute spores or culture evenly.

2. Flame the needle until glowing, let it cool ~10 seconds, then wipe the lid with alcohol.

3. Inject ~0.25–0.5 cc into each of the 4 holes, tilting the needle to run inoculant down the inside glass. About 1–1.5 cc total per jar.

4. Re-cover the holes with micropore tape (breathable) or leave the foil. Label each jar with the species and date.

</Steps>

## Step 4 — Colonize

<Steps>

1. Incubate in the dark at 24–27 °C (75–81 °F) for most common temperate species. Stable temperature beats a perfect temperature — avoid swings.

2. Expect tiny white fuzz at injection points within 3–7 days, then radial growth. Full colonization of a half-pint usually takes 2–4 weeks depending on species and inoculation rate.

3. Watch through the glass. Bright white, rhizomorphic or fluffy mycelium is healthy. Any green, black, grey, pink, orange, or slimy/wet zones — or a sour, foul smell — mean contamination.

</Steps>

<Aside type="danger">
Do not open suspected contaminated jars indoors, especially anything blue-green and powdery (likely *Penicillium*) or dusty dark green/yellow (possible *Aspergillus*). Aspergillus spores are a serious inhalation hazard and can cause lung infection, particularly in anyone immunocompromised. Bag the sealed jar, dispose of it outside, and do not try to "rescue" it. See [Contamination Identification](/docs/lab/contamination-id).
</Aside>

Once a jar looks fully white, give it an extra 5–7 days to let the mycelium consolidate and toughen the cake before birthing.

## Step 5 — Birth, then dunk & roll

"Birthing" is popping the cake out of the jar. "Dunk and roll" rehydrates it and coats it in dry vermiculite to seed even pinning and protect the surface.

<Steps>

1. Wash and alcohol-wipe your hands. Remove the lid and the loose dry vermiculite top. Tap the inverted jar against your palm — a fully colonized cake slides out intact.

2. Rinse loose verm off under cool tap water.

3. Dunk: submerge the cake in cool, clean water for 12–24 hours, weighting it down so it stays under. This restores moisture spent during colonization. Cool water also gives a mild thermal shock that can encourage pinning.

4. Roll: drain the cake, pat off surface water, and roll the wet cake in a tray of plain dry vermiculite until evenly coated. This coating buffers humidity and reduces side-pinning and aborts.

5. Place each cake on a small stand (a jar lid or bottle cap) inside the fruiting chamber so air reaches all sides.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip">
Cakes can typically be re-dunked between flushes (2–4 flushes total) as yield tapers. Each flush is smaller; when a cake stops producing or shows contamination, retire it.
</Aside>

## Step 6 — Fruiting in a shotgun fruiting chamber (SGFC)

The SGFC is a clear tote drilled with a grid of ~6 mm (1/4 in) holes spaced ~5 cm (2 in) apart on all sides and the bottom, sitting on a bed of moist perlite. The perlite holds humidity; the holes provide passive fresh-air exchange driven by evaporation.

<Tabs>
  <TabItem label="Targets">
    - Humidity: 90–95% RH
    - Temperature: 21–24 °C (70–75 °F) for many common species; tune per species
    - Fresh air: fan/mist 4–6 times daily
    - Light: indirect ambient, ~12 hours; mushrooms grow toward it, but they do not need direct sun
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Daily routine">
    - Mist the chamber walls (not the cakes directly) until beaded
    - Fan with the lid for 30–60 seconds to flush stale, CO2-rich air
    - Replenish perlite moisture as it dries
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Troubleshooting">
    - Long thin stems, tiny caps means too much CO2 / not enough fresh air; fan more
    - Cracked dry caps means humidity too low
    - Wet slimy patches or sour smell means bacterial contamination, often from over-wet conditions
    - No pins after 1–2 weeks means it needs more fresh air and a light cycle, or a re-dunk
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

Pins appear as tiny dark dots that elongate into primordia. Harvest just before or as the caps open and the veil breaks or tears — that is the cleanest point for spore-mess control and peak quality. Twist and pull gently at the base.

## PF-Tek vs grain + bulk

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="PF-Tek strengths">
    Forgiving, cheap, no PC, self-isolating jars, excellent for learning to read mycelium and practice sterile technique.
  </Card>
  <Card title="PF-Tek limits">
    Low yield per unit effort, slow, nutrient-poor substrate, and cakes dry out and contaminate more readily than a colonized bulk tub.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Grain + bulk strengths">
    Much higher yields, faster colonization, and the standard path once you can work clean. See [Grain Spawn](/docs/cultivation/grain-spawn) and [Bulk Substrates](/docs/cultivation/bulk-substrate).
  </Card>
  <Card title="When to graduate">
    Once you can take a PF-Tek run from syringe to harvest without contamination, move to grain-to-bulk and the monotub — see [Fruiting Conditions & the Monotub](/docs/cultivation/fruiting-and-monotubs).
  </Card>
</CardGrid>
