<Aside type="caution" title="Safety Note">
  Oyster mushrooms are widely cultivated and safe when cooked, but always
  confirm decurrent white gills, lilac-gray spore print, and absence of a true
  stem ring. Avoid orange look-alikes such as _Omphalotus_ species, which grow
  on wood but have orange gills and cause severe gastrointestinal distress.
</Aside>

## 2. Discovery & Naming

- **Discoverer / Describer:** First described by Nikolaus Joseph von Jacquin (1775) as _Agaricus ostreatus_.
- **Year Described:** 1775; transferred to _Pleurotus_ by Paul Kummer in 1871.
- **Type Locality:** Austrian forests documented in Jacquin’s _Florae Austriaceae_.
- **Type Specimen ID:** Naturhistorisches Museum Wien, Jacquin exsiccatum.
- **Etymology:** “Ostreatus” references the oyster-like shell shape of the cap.
- **Historical Notes:** Commercial cultivation scaled in Europe during World War II as a protein-rich food that could grow on straw and sawdust; now a global crop.

## 3. Morphological Characteristics

{/* prettier-ignore-start */}

<Tabs syncKey="morphology">
  <TabItem label="Macroscopic" icon="eye">

    ### Cap

    - **Shape:** Shell- or fan-shaped with inrolled margin when young, flattening with age
    - **Size:** 5 – 20 cm across; overlapping shelves often form large clusters
    - **Color:** Lead-blue to slate-gray in cool weather, fading to tan or cream as temperatures rise
    - **Surface:** Smooth, satiny; becomes slightly greasy in humid conditions

    ### Gills

    - **Type:** Crowded lamellae with numerous short gills
    - **Attachment:** Decurrent, running well down the stem or point of attachment
    - **Color:** White to pale lilac when fresh, yellowing slightly with age

    ### Stem

    - **Present:** Often rudimentary; off-center to lateral
    - **Dimensions:** 0.5 – 4 cm long × 1 – 3 cm thick when present
    - **Characteristics:** Short, firm, sometimes absent on side-attached clusters; no ring or volva

    ### Flesh

    - **Color & Changes:** White, thick, and pliant; does not bruise markedly
    - **Odor:** Mildly anisic or farinaceous
    - **Taste:** Pleasantly umami; becomes slightly chewy with age

    ### Spore Print

    - **Color:** Lilac-gray to grayish white

  </TabItem>

  <TabItem label="Microscopic" icon="microscope">
    - **Spore Dimensions:** 7 – 11 × 3 – 4 µm
    - **Spore Shape:** Cylindrical to subellipsoid, smooth, hyaline
    - **Ornamentation:** Smooth; walls thin to slightly thickened
    - **Basidia / Cystidia:** Basidia 4-spored; cheilocystidia abundant, clavate
  </TabItem>

## 4. Chemical Profile

- **Toxic Compounds:** None; considered safe when cooked.
- **Medicinal Compounds:** Rich in beta-glucans (pleuran), lovastatin-like statins, and ergothioneine.
- **Nutritional Snapshot:** ~33 kcal per 100 g fresh weight; 3.3 g protein; notable B vitamins, copper, and potassium.
- **Secondary Metabolites:** Produces pleurotolysin (pore-forming protein) and nematotoxic droplets used for predation on nematodes.
- **Chemical Tests:** KOH on cap cuticle yields pale yellow tint; guaiac test turns greenish.

## 6. Ecology & Habitat

- **Habitat:** Saprotrophic on dead hardwood; rarely parasitic on weakened trees.
- **Substrate:** Poplar, beech, alder, maple, occasionally conifers in urban plantings.
- **Symbiosis / Trophic Type:** White-rot decomposer specializing in lignin breakdown.
- **Ecological Role:** Recycles cellulose and lignin, creating habitat for invertebrates and other fungi.
- **Distribution:** Cosmopolitan; native to temperate Northern Hemisphere, widely introduced via cultivation.
- **Altitude Range:** Sea level to ~2,000 m.
- **Population Status:** Stable globally with robust cultivation industries.

## 7. Environmental Tolerances

- **Temperature Range:** Fruits between 5 – 21 °C; mycelial growth optimum ~24 °C.
- **Moisture Needs:** Requires high humidity (>85 % RH) for primordia; tolerates short dry periods once mature.
- **pH Range:** Prefers neutral to slightly alkaline substrates (pH 6 – 8).
- **Light:** Indirect light (500–1,000 lux) triggers primordia formation.
- **USDA Zones:** Hardy in zones 4 – 10 when grown on logs or beds.

## 8. Culinary Information

<Aside type="tip" title="Culinary Overview">
  Harvest when caps are still slightly inrolled. Slice thick clusters to promote
  even cooking. Excellent sautéed, grilled, or smoked; stems can be minced for
  dumpling fillings or stocks.
</Aside>

<Steps>
  <ol>
    <li>Brush debris from caps; avoid soaking to maintain texture.</li>
    <li>Sear in a hot pan with butter or oil until edges crisp.</li>
    <li>Finish with garlic, thyme, or tamari to enhance umami depth.</li>
