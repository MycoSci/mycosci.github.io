Reishi is a varnished, woody bracket fungus prized for two millennia in East Asian medicine rather than as
food. The name *Ganoderma lucidum* has been applied loosely worldwide, so the taxonomy is genuinely tangled
(see below).

<Aside type="caution" title="Safety">
  Non-toxic but very bitter and woody — used as a decoction or extract, not eaten. Possible effects include
  dry mouth, mild GI upset, and rash; it can increase bleeding risk for people on anticoagulants. Treat it
  as a supplement, not a food.
</Aside>

## Taxonomy & naming

First described by **William Curtis (1781)** as *Boletus lucidus*, reclassified by **Fries (1821)** as
*Polyporus lucidus*, and finally placed in the new genus *Ganoderma* by **P. Karsten (1881)**, with this
species as the type. The genus name combines Greek *ganos* ("brightness, sheen") and *derma* ("skin"); the
epithet *lucidum* is Latin for "shining" — both nod to the varnished cap.

<Aside type="note" title="The reishi taxonomy problem">
  The name *G. lucidum* has been used as an umbrella term for many "reishi" worldwide. Modern molecular work
  shows the true European *G. lucidum* (sensu stricto) is distinct from the famed red reishi of China and
  Japan, which is now treated as ***Ganoderma lingzhi***. European *G. lucidum* is actually closer to North
  American species like *G. tsugae* and *G. oregonense*. Many commercial "*G. lucidum*" products are really
  *G. lingzhi*.
</Aside>

## Morphology

<Tabs syncKey="morphology">
  <TabItem label="Macroscopic">
    ### Fruit body
    - **Shape:** Kidney- to fan-shaped bracket, often with a lateral stem
    - **Size:** 5–15 cm across
    - **Surface:** Hard, woody, with a glossy "varnished" reddish-brown crust and a paler margin

    ### Pores & flesh
    - **Pore surface:** White to cream, ~4–5 pores per mm, bruising brownish
    - **Flesh:** Corky, pale buff; resinous odour; intensely **bitter**
    - Spore print **brown**
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Microscopic">
    - **Spores:** 8–12 × 5–9 µm, ellipsoid with truncate ends and a double wall (a distinctive ornamented inner wall)
    - **Hyphal system:** Trimitic, with clamp connections; cystidia absent
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Chemistry

- **Bioactives:** Beta-glucan polysaccharides (immunomodulatory), ganoderic acids (bitter triterpenoids), the LZ-8 protein, ergosterol, and adenosine — several hundred compounds in total.
- **Spot tests:** No reaction with KOH or iron salts on the flesh.

## Ecology & growing conditions

- **Mode:** White-rot saprotroph (sometimes a weak parasite) on dead or dying hardwood — oak, maple, and buried roots in warm temperate forests.
- **Range:** Native to Europe; widely cultivated in Asia and North America. Locally uncommon in the wild, abundant in cultivation.
- **Conditions:** 15–30 °C (optimum ~25–30 °C), high humidity (>80% RH), slightly acidic substrate (pH ~5). Diffuse light is needed to form a normal cap — grown in the dark it makes antler-like forms.

## Cultural significance

Recorded in the oldest Chinese herbal, the *Shen Nong Ben Cao Jing* (~1st–2nd c. AD), and in Japan's
*Nihon Shoki* (720 AD), where it is called *mannentake* ("10,000-year mushroom"). Wild reishi was rare
enough to be reserved for royalty and tied to legends of immortality; it remains a common motif in Chinese
and Taoist art.

<Aside type="note" title="Field summary">
  A shiny reddish-brown varnished cap with a white decurrent pore surface and a brown spore print, growing
  on hardwood, indicates **reishi (*Ganoderma lucidum* sensu lato)**.
</Aside>
