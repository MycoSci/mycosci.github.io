The fly agaric is the archetypal toadstool — a scarlet, white-warted cap as culturally iconic as it is
chemically active. It forms ectomycorrhizal partnerships with birch and conifers across the Northern
Hemisphere and has been carried around the world with planted trees.

<Aside type="danger" title="Safety">
  *Amanita muscaria* is poisonous raw and can cause severe gastrointestinal distress, delirium, and
  neurological symptoms from ibotenic acid and muscimol. Never eat red-capped, white-warted mushrooms.
  Traditional parboiling/detoxification is unreliable and still carries real risk.
</Aside>

## Morphology

<Tabs syncKey="morphology">
  <TabItem label="Macroscopic">
    ### Cap
    - **Shape:** Hemispherical when young, becoming convex to flat with age
    - **Size:** 8–20 cm across, occasionally larger in moist habitats
    - **Colour:** Scarlet to orange-red with detachable white warts (universal-veil remnants); cuticle viscid when wet, the margin striate when mature

    ### Gills
    - **Attachment:** Free from the stem; crowded, with many short gills
    - **Colour:** White to cream, occasionally with pale yellow tints in age

    ### Stem
    - **Form:** Central, 8–20 cm × 1.5–3 cm, swelling to a bulbous base
    - **Features:** White, with a skirt-like ring; base ringed with concentric warted volval remnants

    ### Flesh & spore print
    - White flesh bruising slowly yellowish; mild, faintly fungal odour
    - Spore print **white**
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Microscopic">
    - **Spores:** 9–13 × 6.5–8.5 µm, broadly ellipsoid to subglobose, smooth, **non-amyloid**
    - **Basidia:** 4-spored with clamp connections; cheilocystidia sparse
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Chemistry

- **Toxins:** Ibotenic acid (neuroexcitatory; nausea and delirium) and muscimol (a potent GABA-A agonist producing sedation and altered perception). Ibotenic acid decarboxylates to muscimol on drying.
- **Pigments:** The red cap colour comes from betalain-type pigments (muscaflavin / muscapurpurin group).
- **Spot tests:** KOH on the cap cuticle gives a yellowish reaction.

## Discovery & names

- Described by **Linnaeus (1753)** as *Agaricus muscarius*; transferred to *Amanita* by **Lamarck (1783)**.
- **Etymology:** *muscaria* refers to its old use as a fly poison — caps were steeped in milk to stupefy houseflies.
- **Culture:** Deeply woven into Siberian shamanism, European folklore, and modern pop culture.

## Ecology & growing conditions

- **Partners:** Ectomycorrhizal with birch, spruce, fir, pine, and occasionally oak — an obligate symbiont sheathing host-tree roots.
- **Habitat:** Boreal and temperate woodland and parkland with suitable hosts; sea level to subalpine (~2,500 m).
- **Conditions:** Fruits in cool, moist weather (roughly 5–18 °C) on acidic to neutral soils (pH ~4–6.5), following autumn rains.
