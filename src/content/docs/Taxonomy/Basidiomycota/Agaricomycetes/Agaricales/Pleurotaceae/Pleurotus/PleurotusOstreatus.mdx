The oyster mushroom is a fast, forgiving white-rot fungus that fruits in shelving clusters on dead hardwood.
It is one of the most-produced mushrooms in the world and a common first crop for new cultivators.

<Aside type="caution" title="Safety">
  Widely cultivated and safe when cooked, but confirm decurrent white-to-lilac gills, a lilac-grey spore
  print, and the absence of a true ring. Avoid orange, wood-growing look-alikes such as *Omphalotus*
  (jack-o'-lantern), which have true orange gills and cause severe gastrointestinal illness.
</Aside>

## Morphology

<Tabs syncKey="morphology">
  <TabItem label="Macroscopic">
    ### Cap
    - **Shape:** Shell- or fan-shaped, inrolled when young, flattening with age; overlapping shelves form large clusters
    - **Size:** 5–20 cm across
    - **Colour:** Lead-blue to slate-grey in cool weather, fading to tan or cream as temperatures rise; smooth and satiny

    ### Gills & stem
    - **Gills:** Crowded, **decurrent**, running well down the point of attachment; white to pale lilac, yellowing slightly with age
    - **Stem:** Often rudimentary, off-centre to lateral; short and firm; **no ring or volva**

    ### Flesh & spore print
    - White, thick, and pliant; mildly anisic/farinaceous odour
    - Spore print **lilac-grey** to greyish white
  </TabItem>
  <TabItem label="Microscopic">
    - **Spores:** 7–11 × 3–4 µm, cylindrical to subellipsoid, smooth, hyaline
    - **Basidia:** 4-spored; cheilocystidia clavate and abundant
  </TabItem>
</Tabs>

## Chemistry

- **Bioactives:** Rich in beta-glucans (pleuran), ergothioneine, and small amounts of natural statins (lovastatin).
- **Predation:** Produces pleurotolysin and nematotoxic droplets — the mycelium can trap and digest nematodes for nitrogen.

## Discovery & names

- First described by **Jacquin (1775)** as *Agaricus ostreatus*; transferred to *Pleurotus* by **P. Kummer (1871)**.
- **Etymology:** *ostreatus* refers to the oyster-shell shape of the cap.
- **History:** Cultivation scaled up in Europe during the World Wars as a protein source that grows on straw and sawdust; now a global crop.

## Ecology & growing conditions

- **Mode:** Saprotrophic white-rot decomposer of dead hardwood (poplar, beech, alder, maple), occasionally a weak parasite on stressed trees.
- **Role:** Breaks down lignin and cellulose, recycling deadwood and creating habitat for invertebrates and other fungi.
- **Conditions:** Fruits between roughly 5–21 °C (mycelial optimum ~24 °C) at high humidity (>85% RH); indirect light triggers pinning.

<Steps>
1. Harvest while cap margins are still slightly inrolled for the best texture.
2. Brush off debris rather than soaking; sear hot in butter or oil until the edges crisp.
3. Finish with garlic, thyme, or tamari to bring out the umami.
</Steps>
